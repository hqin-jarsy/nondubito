# Great Lives 036 — Wittgenstein full-edition revision

Reviewed: 2026-09-20. Batch 12, movement 3. Original Chinese/English titles and all article URLs retained.

## Scope and reading

Read the original Chinese and English in full before revision. Both revised sources retain nine sections and 54 paragraphs: silence and return; the first book; value and silence; criticism and return to everyday language; the second book; revising oneself; the continuing usefulness of both books; comparison with Kant; reported final words and the imagined bridgehead.

The edition uses concrete examples—cat and table, passing salt, making a promise, asking for water, a family member defending an identity, a friend asking for help—to make the philosophical stakes readable without treating them as a complete exposition of Wittgenstein. The bridgehead is expressly imagined, not biographical evidence. Philosophical comparisons are the essayist's reading, not an identity between different thinkers' doctrines.

## Factual and interpretive changes

- The original combined the preface's summary with the final proposition and called both the book's last sentence. The opening now identifies the final injunction separately. German publication in 1921, English in 1922, return to Cambridge in 1929, death in 1951 and posthumous publication of the Investigations in 1953 are kept distinct.
- The Vienna house was a collaboration with Paul Engelmann. Teacher, gardener and architectural work are not proof that Wittgenstein ceased all thinking outside the university.
- Removed the unsupported word count and the claim that only one person in intellectual history constructed two incompatible philosophies. Revision is not the wholesale deletion of every earlier insight.
- The picture account concerns possible situations and truth conditions. A false proposition can still have sense. It is not an assertion that each everyday word photographs an object, nor the later verificationist or falsifiability criterion.
- Ethics and the meaning of life are not dismissed as worthless. Distinguish reporting a death from asking about its significance. Include the ladder at 6.54 without settling the scholarly debate on how to read it.
- Ramsey and Sraffa's criticism restores the role of interlocutors. The return is not explained by a single episode or solely by Vienna Circle misunderstandings.
- Corrected the claim that the Investigations has no numbering. Numbered remarks differ from the earlier nested proposition hierarchy. Language-games involve learned activities and shared practices, not unrestricted personal interpretation.
- Section 43's qualification—many cases, not all—is preserved. The water example concerns what an utterance does within an activity, not a mysterious change of substance or unrestricted meaning.
- Neither Socrates, Hegel, Marx nor Galileo becomes a cardboard foil. Kant's conditions of experience and Wittgenstein's limits of expression are different inquiries. Removed the assertion that Kant had finally won while Hegel failed to refute him.
- The final words are a reported message preserved by Malcolm, not a recording or proof that the later book was written to license that sentence. Removed the claim that both books necessarily find the sentence meaningless. A life need not pass an achievement audit before its owner can speak of it.
- The other figures at the imagined bridge have not all stopped thinking. The two books make unfinished inquiry visible without giving one person a monopoly on it.

## Source trail

1. [Tractatus, public-domain English edition](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php?title=Tractatus_Logico-Philosophicus_(English)): preface, picture theory and §§6.52–7. The opening Chinese is a rendering of proposition 7, not a quotation attributed to a named modern translator.
2. [Philosophische Untersuchungen, German original](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php?title=Philosophische_Untersuchungen): preface, §§23 and 43. Checked the primary German text; no modern translation service or wholesale modern translated passages used.
3. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein/): biographical chronology and the distinction between continuity and change across periods.
4. [Ryan Manhire (2025)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/phin.12453): scholarly discussion identifying the Malcolm account and the setting of the reported final message. This is not a claim to have directly inspected Malcolm's original memoir or to adopt every interpretive conclusion in Manhire's paper.
5. [Wien Museum](https://magazin.wienmuseum.at/wittgensteins-orte-der-kindheit): the sister's house, Engelmann and the collaborative planning history. No private motive is inferred from architectural details.
