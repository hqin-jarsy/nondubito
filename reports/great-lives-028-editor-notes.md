# Great Lives 028 — Huineng full-edition editorial notes

Reviewed 2026-09-20. Source: `essays/mingren/huineng.html`; full-copy data: `data/great-lives/movement-03d-huineng.json`. Original source title and URL retained. This report records content work, not deployment.

## Scope and substantive revision

Read the previous Chinese and English essays completely before editing. The eight-section route remains: wood seller and literacy; verse contest; emptiness; sudden/gradual teaching; banner; Buddha and raft; Kafka/Gregor; Caoxi and the imagined bridge. Both source narratives are now eight sections and 72 paragraphs, with counts **7 / 10 / 9 / 8 / 9 / 8 / 9 / 12**. The Chinese is edited as prose, and the English is independently phrased for English readers. Neither is a historical correction checklist.

- Biography and verse contest are transmitted religious narrative, not a contemporaneous transcript. Removed confident claims about knowing no scriptures, learning from no one, being the least educated, surpassing the entire canon, and a uniquely entitled non-Buddha sutra. Hearing scripture and receiving teaching remain central.
- Retained the two familiar poems and rendered each in four visible lines. The later Zongbao text (T2008) supplies 本來無一物; the Dunhuang text (T2007) has 佛性常清淨 and another verse beginning 心是菩提樹. This difference is included in every language rather than hidden only in notes.
- Emptiness no longer means that persons, pain, or the world were never real. An ordinary example of a harsh label is an explicitly limited doorway into Buddhist teaching, not a replacement definition of the doctrine.
- Northern/gradual and Southern/sudden labels are not presented as two founders' exhaustive self-descriptions. Sudden insight retains its disruption of scorekeeping; practice and conduct remain necessary. Socratic perplexity is not equated with Chan realization.
- The wind/banner encounter is identified with the later transmitted text. The quarrel reading is one possible reading, not a physical explanation or exhaustive interpretation. No-thought is explicitly distinguished from shutting down perception or thought. Kant is a comparison, not an equivalent theory outperformed in one sentence.
- Huineng does not abolish the Buddha's practice or deny the river. The raft is an aid to crossing rather than a permanent identity. Both halves of the Diamond Sutra instruction—non-abiding and bringing forth mind—remain present.
- Gregor remains a person in need of care. The thought experiment turns toward the responsibility of people outside his door, not the injured person's failure to think correctly. The apple wound, hunger, closed door, sister's violin, and lack of earning remain concrete. Non-self is not silently rewritten as a modern rights doctrine.
- The conclusion no longer makes Huineng or the people on the bridge disappear. Mud, ordinary clothes, flowing water, and a person making room for another retain the bridge's literary function. Washington's estate and the poor wood seller are not flattened into identical simplicity.

## Sources actually consulted

1. **CBETA T2007**, Dunhuang Platform Sutra. The online reader did not expose useful text in the web tool, so the public CBETA XML at `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cbeta-org/xml-p5/master/T/T48/T48n2007.xml` was queried directly. Read surrounding passages for the two Huineng verses, the nighttime teaching, no-thought/莫百物不思, 法無頓漸, self-cultivation, and 前念迷/後念悟. The appendix and apparatus were not read in full. Public-domain classical text; the article is not an adaptation of a modern translation.
2. **CBETA T2008**, Zongbao Platform Sutra. Direct XML at the analogous `T48n2008.xml` address. Read the familiar verse and transmission scene, 應無所住而生其心, banner scene, no-thought warning, sudden/gradual passage, and practical admonitions. Source notes link the stable CBETA reader pages for readers.
3. **Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Huineng**, `https://iep.utm.edu/huineng/`. Read displayed biography, historical-source discussion, central teachings, practice, and teaching-style sections. Used for the historical caution, traditional dates, and later lineage disputes; not as the source of a new blanket verdict that all narrative is fiction.
4. **Alagaddūpama Sutta, MN22**, Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation, `https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html`. Read the raft passage and its immediate context directly. No translation is quoted verbatim in the essay. SuttaCentral's JS-only response was not counted as a consulted translation.

Daily-life illustrations and comparative bridge scenes are the author's interpretation. They do not purport to quote Huineng. Verse renderings in English, French, German, Spanish and Korean were written directly; Japanese uses a reading of the familiar classical text. No translation service was used.
