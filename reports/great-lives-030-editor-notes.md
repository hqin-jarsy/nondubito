## Factual and interpretive changes

1. Hegel’s letter: retained its dated, specific encounter; removed invented window staging and the claim that absolute spirit means history literally stops. Quoted only a short phrase, paraphrasing the rest. Hegel/Napoleon comparison is explicitly the essayist’s interpretation.
2. Corrected the denial of noble birth. Corsican minor nobility and French recognition helped education, while distance from metropolitan power remains narratively important. Removed the unknowable claim that without revolution a certain military rank was his maximum.
3. Separated 1789 revolutionary events from the 1793 execution. Kept Toulon, Italy, Egypt and Brumaire, without pretending they happened in a single clean rupture.
4. Rosetta Stone: French discovery in 1799, British acquisition after surrender, not Napoleon carrying it back to France. Scholarly gains do not excuse appropriation.
5. Civil Code: legal consolidation rather than invention of every revolutionary reform; common civil rules rather than a guarantee of universal equality or political freedom. Included married women’s inequality and the distinct 1802 colonial slavery policies. Distinguished civil marriage from modern church–state separation, and civil code from a constitution restraining supreme political power.
6. Legal diffusion varied by territory. The survival of institutions is not proof of justice. Revision by later people, including changes to women’s legal position, gives the inheritance a concrete afterlife. No person is reduced to a disposable vessel for an institution.
7. Coronation: planned self-crowning rather than a spontaneous snatching from the pope. Beethoven: damaged surviving title page, Ries’s retrospective story, changing attitudes and the non-exclusive interpretation of Eroica’s wording; no torn dedication asserted as directly established fact.
8. Russia: multinational army, campaign total not Moscow arrival total; attrition before winter; Borodino and Russian resistance; Saint Petersburg as political capital; fires not given a simplistic single explanation. Entering Moscow stated by month because source chronologies differ by a day. Removed invented 6,000-kilometre supply-line figure and the claim that the Russians never fought.
9. Waterloo credits Wellington’s allied forces and Blücher’s Prussians. Alexander’s death at 32 no longer coexists with the original impossible claim that both conquerors fell in their forties.
10. Death: brief, proportionate description supported by Lugli et al., Virchows Archiv (2021), a medical re-evaluation of contemporaneous autopsy reports. Arsenic stories are not treated as equally established explanations. This is historical sourcing, not medical advice.
11. Napoleon retained choices: success made restraint difficult, not impossible. The Saint Helena image is marked as literary imagination and includes unnamed dead rather than turning them into the cost of a great man’s achievement.
