# 034 Lincoln — full-edition editorial notes

Reviewed: 2026-09-20. Nine sections, sixty paragraphs in the calibrated Chinese source and each full reading edition. Source titles retained. No translation service used.

## Editorial calibration

- Preserved the divided-house opening, inherited Union, war and emancipation, Reconstruction law and reversal, Washington comparison, Gettysburg, Marx comparison, assassination and imagined bridgehead.
- The North was not uniformly free in 1776. The three-fifths clause concerned apportionment and direct taxation, not a declaration of fractional humanity; its political exploitation of people denied a vote is made explicit.
- The House Divided speech opposes enduring division, but does not predict or wish for dissolution. The account retains Lincoln’s 1858 opposition to expansion without making him an immediate universal federal abolitionist.
- Secession is not equated to a person’s freedom to leave. Fort Sumter, Confederate decisions and Lincoln’s call for militia replace the implication that Lincoln alone chose war.
- The Greeley letter is contextualized by its ending and the earlier emancipation draft. The proclamation is a military measure with moral and political consequences, not merely a tactic. Territorial exceptions and dependence on implementation are explicit.
- Enslaved people’s escapes, intelligence and pressure on Union policy, Black enlistment, and abolitionist organizing restore agency beyond the presidential signature.
- Distinguishes Senate passage (1864), House passage (January 31, 1865), and state ratification (December 6, 1865). The punishment exception and later coercive labor are acknowledged without reducing abolition to a non-event.
- Reconstruction amendments and political life are not all credited to Lincoln. White supremacist violence predates 1877; troop withdrawal is described as a retreat from protecting remaining Republican governments, not removal of every federal soldier.
- Jim Crow is not slavery unchanged, and oppression is not a conserved natural quantity. Qin and Marx comparisons remain interpretive and do not make Stalin inevitable.
- Washington’s retirement and state secession are separated. Lincoln’s colonization schemes, earlier opposition to full equality, and limited later voting-rights position are retained as a development rather than sainthood.
- Gettysburg casualties are not all deaths. The commonly printed 272-word text is distinguished from variant manuscripts; the address articulates ongoing change rather than creating it alone.
- Marx’s 1864 congratulatory address supplies an actual connection. Both figures had ideas and political activity; neither is reduced to a brain/hands caricature.
- Lincoln was shot April 14 and died April 15. Lee’s April 9 surrender did not complete all surrenders; Reconstruction had already begun during the war. The bridgehead is explicitly imaginative, includes collective participants, and ends with a question rather than invented historical dialogue.
- Root review’s positive-prose edits replace the old draft’s correction-focused “heroic story” and “cement” lines with the continuing work of ratification and each dead person’s unfinished life.
