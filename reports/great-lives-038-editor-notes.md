- Begin with looking at an eye, then a painting, rather than with a psychological verdict. ## Historical and interpretive calibration

1. Institutional records differ on 1624 and 1626; use the 1620s and approximate ages. 1705 is within the Kangxi reign, not after it. The Ming's fall is not represented as a single simultaneous event everywhere.
2. Ming lineage and danger are retained without the claim that merely being surnamed Zhu meant an automatic death sentence. Monastic life is not reduced to a proved ruse. Reports of disturbed conduct, crying, laughter or appearing mute do not establish a modern diagnosis or sixty-one continuous years without speech.
3. Around 1680, departure from monastic life and painting/calligraphy as a livelihood; the 1684 use of Bada Shanren are drawn from museum accounts. No invented prior official career: the source says he did not take the official path.
4. The Met's 1699 *Fish and Rocks* contains seven fish and two large rock forms, not a universal single lonely fish. The Palace Museum's lotus, rock and waterbird supplies a second concrete example. Upward pupils remain open to viewing rather than a fixed diagnosis or the sole political meaning of every work.
5. The 1697 *Flowers on a River* scroll is nearly thirteen meters (the Nankai discussion records 1292.5 cm), with the artist's long poem. Its inscription mentions a request and work across months. This supports deliberate craft and human relationships without copying the source's more speculative psychological claims.
6. *Book of Jin* 49 supports Ruan Ji's treatment of Ji Xi and Ji Kang, qin and wine, journeys to a road's end, official service and political risk. “Blue eye” is explained as favorable regard, not a literal iris color. The anecdotes are identified as transmitted accounts.
7. Huineng/Chan is not equated with dispossession. Voluntary release of attachment and the coerced loss of a way of life are distinguished in every edition. Neither a complete cure nor permanent trauma is claimed for the artist.
8. The Michelangelo comparison is presented as an interpretive metaphor, not an authenticated quotation. The joined signature's crying/laughing reading is a reading, not a daily mood cipher. The bridgehead is explicitly literary imagination.
