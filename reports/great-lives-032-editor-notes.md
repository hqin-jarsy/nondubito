## Substantive calibration

1. Names: no longer asserts that she was not called Curie or that readers may remove a surname she used. Her Polish and French names, her own agency, and recognition beyond marriage are distinguished. The series' first woman is a limitation of this particular selection, not the first historically important woman.
2. The 1903 recognition episode retains Mittag-Leffler's intervention and Pierre's response, without making her research real only when her husband acknowledged it. 1911 remains the first second Nobel for any individual; awards do not confer human worth.
3. Warsaw: lost independence, Russification and exclusion from university are distinguished from the false claim that scientific learning was wholly impossible. Added the Floating University, practical training, family assistance and the sisters' reciprocal plan. Removed six Paris years, fainting from cold, and a simplistic comparison reducing Du Fu's career to corrupt examinations. Poverty is an obstacle, not a prerequisite for genius.
4. Pierre's 1895 institution is the Paris Municipal School of Industrial Physics and Chemistry, not the Sorbonne. Removed an unsupported story that the Sorbonne withheld a particular laboratory solely because she was a foreign woman. The discovery belongs to a collaborative history, including Becquerel's prior uranium rays, Pierre, and Bémont on the radium report.
5. The shed narrative retains repeated physical work, but describes uranium-extraction residues, radium chloride around 1902 and metallic radium with Debierne in 1910 separately. Removed hand-stirring of chemicals and the claim that she personally originated the physical phenomenon of radioactivity.
6. Pierre was 46 at his 1906 death, not 47. Marie's 1906 teaching/laboratory duties and 1908 full professorship are separated. Removed a long unverified diary quotation and the invented motive of proving that their work was not collaborative.
7. Chemistry prize scope calibrated; Langevin press campaign is not equated with every person in France. Arrhenius's request is identified rather than attributed loosely to an entire Nobel committee. Sexism and xenophobia remain clear without claiming male scientists could never face press scrutiny or asserting irrelevant allegations about Einstein.
8. Health: early insufficient protection, developing knowledge and Marie's later protective efforts replace total ignorance or deliberate saintly self-destruction. Aplastic anemia and the generally recognized significance of long exposure remain proportionate; no invented lifetime dosimetry, radium-filled glowing bones, or categorical single-agent medical history.
9. Wartime X-rays are Röntgen's discovery, distinct from radium and radioactive substances. Her contribution in equipment, transport, organization, training and work with Irène is substantial without appropriating another discovery. Treatment and harm depend on use, dose and protection; injury is not the price that makes a person valuable.
10. Notebook contamination and specialist preservation/consultation procedures follow the BnF's institutional accounts, including its 2026 update. Removed blanket claims about all possessions, liability waivers and compulsory protective suits. A half-life is not an expiry date or an exact safety forecast for a notebook.
11. Removed the supposed physical proof of a philosophical conservation law and the irrelevant Gödel appeal. Kafka and Qin comparisons now distinguish human transmission from material persistence. The imaginary bridge is explicit and allows her to stand as a person, not a disposable brick or a glow coming from her body.
