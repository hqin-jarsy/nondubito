# Great Lives 037 — Augustine, But Not Yet

Reviewed: 2026-09-20. Batch 13, movement 3. Stable article URLs retained; the Chinese/English title changes from a blanket verdict about being unclean to “奥古斯丁，但不要现在 / Augustine, But Not Yet”.

## Editorial approach

Read the original Chinese and English in full, then reconstructed the same nine-stage argument without treating theological propositions as editorial verdicts on human worth. The garden, postponed promise, pear theft, group laughter and remembered song provide everyday points of entry. Grace retains its religious meaning rather than being renamed therapy. Comparisons with Huineng, Wittgenstein, Freud, Dostoevsky, Kant, Husserl, Hegel and Marx are bounded comparisons, not assertions of identical systems or decisive historical refutation.

## Corrections

- He was 31 at the summer 386 conversion, not 32; born 13 November 354. The garden is a retrospective first-person account, not independently recorded dialogue.
- Confessions I–IX are retrospective, X concerns the present self and memory, XI–XIII explore Genesis/creation, with the time inquiry in XI. Removed the unqualified first-self-analysis claim. The Analects daily examination belongs to Zengzi.
- Confession addresses human readers as well as God and includes praise as well as acknowledgment of wrongdoing. It is not written only for himself.
- The pear episode includes companionship, fear of seeming timid and pleasure in shared mischief. Avoid treating all prohibition as mechanically creating desire.
- Original sin is a theological doctrine, not a neutral remainder or empirical proof that people are dirty or worthless. Created nature is good in Augustine. An infant’s jealousy does not experimentally prove the doctrine. Huineng is not made to assert that every remainder can be eliminated.
- Grace, will and predestination developed through controversy. Removed the simple importation of a later formula of irresistible grace. Dependence on grace is distinguished from the disappearance of human response and action.
- The song/recitation example explains memory, attention and expectation without asserting external time does not exist or conflating Augustine with Kant or Husserl.
- Pear stealing is not an illustration of Beyond the Pleasure Principle: pleasures of transgression and companionship are explicit. Freud’s repetition question is different. Augustine’s acknowledged conflicting wishes are not an automatic conscious/unconscious mapping.
- The two cities concern orientations of love, not a Rome/church or state/church map. Their citizens are intermixed in history; this does not mean each person belongs simultaneously to both. The city of God has present pilgrim life as well as awaited fulfillment.
- Include Augustine’s defense of religious coercion as a difficulty rather than treating self-examination as proof of consistent respect for other people’s freedom. Philosophical argument, institutional practice and religious hope are examined separately rather than put on a winners/losers list.
- Conversion did not end thought. Late revisions remain visible through Retractations. The final bridgehead is explicitly literary imagination; the reader need not accept inherited guilt in order to recognize the difficulty of change.
