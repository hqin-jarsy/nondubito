## Factual and interpretive calibration

1. Replaced the circulating “every block contains a statue” quotation with an explicitly signaled paraphrase of the documented poetic conception discussed by the Metropolitan Museum. No claim that an apocryphal conversational wording is a verified quotation.
2. Carving is not mechanical retrieval of a predetermined answer. Choices and possible failures remain the artist's responsibility. Socrates' questions need not reveal a complete truth already stored inside a person.
3. David: 1501–1504, not two years; height includes its carved base. Earlier working of the block limits later choices; removed unsourced cracks and the simplistic claim everyone considered it worthless. Distinguished the cathedral commission from its eventual civic location and political reception. The Beethoven comparison is a reader's analogy, not an artistic formula or proof from the “fate knocking” anecdote.
4. Ceiling: 1508–1512; standing posture, not lying on his back; assistants and craftsmen not erased. Removed speculative conspiracy staging. Creation of Adam is among the nine central Genesis scenes, not necessarily the exact central panel. The gap is an interpretive focus, not Gödel's theorem applied to painting or a claim that all contact destroys artistic tension.
5. Last Judgment: 1536–1541; Bartholomew near Christ in the central area, not the lower-right entrance to hell. His skin's face is generally identified as a self-portrait. Removed an invented personal declaration that the artist was hollow inside; readings of weariness or religious fear remain interpretations.
6. Late work: born 1475, died 1564 aged 88; Saint Peter's appointment in 1547 and a dome executed and adjusted by successors after his death. Rondanini Pietà retains the late labour, revisions, elongated bodies and unfinished state. Death interrupted work; the evidence does not establish that he no longer wanted to finish, disbelieved in bodies, or had reached a Buddhist doctrine of nothingness. The late strength of supporting another body is presented as a reading.
7. Unfinished Bach/Leonardo/Michelangelo works are not assigned three tidy and speculative psychological causes. Leonardo is not described as someone who finished nothing, and Michelangelo is not described as invariably completing projects.
8. Complaints in letters and poems do not prove a life without joy. Removed retrospective permanent-injury diagnoses and the claim suffering itself inevitably generated the work. Preserved family burdens, love for Tommaso de' Cavalieri, and the 1532 meeting without inventing private acts or diminishing intense affection to courtesy.
9. Leonardo's age difference is approximately 23 years, not an exact integer assertion. Both civic mural projects remained unfinished, while designs and studies influenced later artists. Breadth/depth are ways of approaching the artists, not total definitions of their lives.
10. Bridgehead is explicitly imagined. A sculptor's authority over marble does not authorize shaping another person into an imposed ideal. A neglected life may have its own path; the elderly man's right to exist is not conditional on making another work. Retained the ending about more stone than one lifetime can meet.

## Source trail and limits

The source-page reading notes and full-edition JSON contain the same six institutional links, with six localized display labels and default English titles:

- Galleria dell’Accademia: David's dates, dimensions, commission and placement.
- Metropolitan Museum, *Michelangelo: Divine Draftsman and Designer*, exhibition galleries: poetic conception, designs and revisions, civic rivalry, collaborations, Cavalieri, incomplete projects, architectural and biographical context.
- Vatican Museums: Sistine Chapel history and the Last Judgment entry, including dates and the usual self-portrait identification.
- Sforza Castle Museums: official Rondanini Pietà guide. Its indexed text was available and supports the unfinished final sculpture and changed bodily forms; the later direct PDF fetch returned 403. No claim of a complete PDF or image review.
- Rijksmuseum catalogue record for the Royal Academy's *Michelangelo, Leonardo, Raphael: Florence, c.1504*: civic project and the two unfinished murals.

Additional source checking included the catalogue record for the study of Sistine assistants and the digitized description of the artist's ceiling poem and standing self-caricature. These helped distinguish the working history from later legends. No modern poem translation was copied into the essay.
