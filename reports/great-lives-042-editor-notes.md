# Great Lives 042 — Homer, at the Threshold of Voice and Text

Reviewed: 2026-09-20. Batch 14, movement 4. The article URL remains stable; its Chinese/English title changes from a single “moment” of textualization to a threshold between voice and text.

## Editorial approach

The blind-poet image and the name’s etymology are not presented as a secure biography. Homer is neither reduced to one modern author nor declared a fictional brand invented by a guild. Formula, performance, memory, composition, editing and manuscript transmission remain parts of one layered history.

Parry and Lord’s work supports oral-formulaic technique, not the claim that every performance was wholly new. The long poems’ organization does not by itself prove that writing was the only possible support. Dictation to a scribe remains one hypothesis among disputed models; oral performance continued after writing, and no recoverable day cleanly divides voice from text.

The comparison with Confucius no longer assigns collective transmission to the East and individual genius to the West. Briseis remains visible as an enslaved woman rather than only a token in a male honor dispute. Priam’s supplication is both constrained negotiation and a moment in which a person exceeds the category of enemy. The essay preserves the ambiguity of *polytropos* and Plato’s entanglement with poetry.
