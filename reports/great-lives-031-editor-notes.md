## Principal factual and interpretive corrections

1. Childhood is attributed to the later *Secret History*, rather than narrated as verified eyewitness detail. Restored Qasar's participation in killing Begter and Hö’elün's condemnation. A fish dispute does not prove an unavoidable survival killing or erase family power relations.
2. Removed the claim of literally building from nothing or abolishing all kinship. Inherited standing, marriage and allies matter; reorganization of military loyalties coexisted with the central position of the ruler's dynasty.
3. Left the title's etymology open. Distinguished Genghis Khan's campaigns from later expansion into eastern Europe and the final conquest of southern China.
4. Distinguished commanders and different cities. Did not reproduce enormous medieval massacre totals as reliable counts, equate every city's fate, or make Genghis personally conduct every siege. The loss of people and the instrumental treatment of captives remain explicit.
5. Replaced the false claim of 'no construction' with script, command structures, taxation, requisition and use of local officials. The Yasa is not presented as an extant, complete modern-style law code; its written codification is disputed.
6. Religious accommodation is not conflated with modern freedom or a right to reject rule. Qiu Chuji's 1222 audience and his refusal to promise immortality are tied to his disciple's account, available in the University of Washington's primary-text collection.
7. Appanages and designation of Ögedei were arrangements for dynastic rule, not a plan to create four independent successor states. Distinguished Jochi's death, Batu, the later Ilkhanate under Hülegü and the Yuan under Kublai. Removed the erroneous Qin/Alexander succession anecdotes instead of replacing one formula with another.
8. Baghdad in 1258 is a later-generation event, not a city permanently erased. Roads and the relay network required work and local provision; no imaginary safe Beijing–Budapest guarantee. Plague is not reduced to one man's order. No Darwinian analogy dissolves moral distinctions or turns later rebuilding into retrospective justification of killing.
9. The location of Alexander's tomb is also lost. Burial legends are not verified facts. Huineng's emptiness is not nonexistence; an unidentified grave does not erase consequences or responsibility.
10. Kept the horse-and-dust bridgehead, explicitly imaginary, but shifted attention to people obscured by the heroic portrait. Conquest, philosophical inquiry and art are not treated as morally interchangeable instances of an abstract action.
