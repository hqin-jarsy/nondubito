## Editorial direction

The existing Chinese and English essays were read in full before revision. Their nine-section sequence and concluding imaginative gathering remain. The central reading is now the possibility of finding another response while suffering, not a claim that a person becomes unbreakable by choosing the right attitude. Du Fu, Li Bai and Bada Shanren remain substantial comparisons, not failed people used to certify Su Shi's success. Huineng remains a possible conversation partner, without treating Buddhist emptiness and everyday peace as an identical formula.

The revised Chinese and English source each has nine sections and 57 narrative paragraphs. Changes include:

- The 1079 poetry trial retains its political danger and the prison poems to Su Zhe. A risk of execution is not represented as a sentence already passed; unverified rescue dialogue is removed. The 1080 Huangzhou title and restrictions are distinguished from ordinary office.
- The intervening returns to office and disagreement with sweeping reversals of reform replace a claim of 22 uninterrupted years of exile. Huizhou (1094), Danzhou (1097), release from Hainan (1100) and death at Changzhou (1101) are separately situated.
- Ma Zhengqing, agricultural labour, material need and help from others remain part of Dongpo, rather than scenery for an entirely self-made spiritual victory.
- The Red Cliff guest, flute, changing/continuing perspectives, ear and eye, shared enjoyment, renewed cups and dawn are retained. The rain lyric continues beyond “who's afraid” to cold, sunlight and retrospect. Cold Food, lychees and the sea-crossing couplet prevent equanimity from being mistaken for uninterrupted happiness.
- Public work on waterways, famine relief and illness guards against claiming external circumstances do not matter. A victim's difficulty recovering never transfers responsibility away from the person causing harm.
- Du Fu's poems are made works with artistic and ethical judgment, not raw suffering or evidence that he broke. Li Bai's imprisonment and Yelang sentence are retained. Bada's surviving calligraphy and poetry displace an unsupported decades-long silence.
- Rounu remains the speaker of the home-and-heart answer as presented in Su Shi's lyric and preface; the woman's words are not made the poet's personal property. Early returns to Sichuan for mourning are distinguished from the final journey that did not reach Meishan.
- Death age is 64 elapsed years; the notes distinguish the traditional reckoning of 66 and the lunar birth year convention. The bridge is explicitly literary imagination. No body shape, hand gesture or invented eyewitness testimony is used as biographical evidence.
