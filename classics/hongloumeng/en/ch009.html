Chapter 9 Drawn by affection, two friends enter the family school; Roused by suspicion, unruly boys turn the classroom upside down

Chapter end

Now Qin Ye and his son were waiting for someone from the Jia household to bring word of the day chosen for starting school. Bao-yu, impatient to be with Qin Zhong, could think of nothing else, and fixed on the day after next. ‘Ask Master Qin to come here early that morning, so that we can set off together.’ He sent a messenger with these instructions.

Early on the appointed morning, when Bao-yu woke, Aroma had already packed his books, brushes and other writing things, putting everything neatly in order, and was sitting disconsolately on the edge of his bed. Seeing him awake, she had to help him wash and do his hair. Bao-yu noticed her gloomy look and asked with a smile, ‘Dear sister, what’s troubling you again? Surely you don’t blame me for going to school and leaving you all lonely?’

‘What a thing to say!’ Aroma said, smiling. ‘Studying is an excellent thing. You can’t drift along all your life. Where would that end? There’s only one thing: when you’re studying, keep your mind on your books, and when you’re not, spare a thought for home. Don’t go fooling about with the others. If your father catches you, it will be no laughing matter. Of course you want to work hard and do well, but it would be better to take on a little less. For one thing, if you bite off too much you can’t chew it, and for another, you must look after your health. That’s all I mean. Do try to understand.’

Bao-yu agreed to everything she said, point by point.

‘I’ve packed your thick furs too,’ Aroma went on, ‘and sent them out to the boys.

‘It’s cold at school. Do remember to put on extra things or change what you’re wearing. It isn’t like home, with someone to look after you. I’ve sent out charcoal for your foot-warmer and hand-warmer as well. You must make them keep those filled. Those lazy rascals are only too pleased to do nothing unless you tell them, and you’ll be frozen for no reason.’

‘Don’t worry,’ Bao-yu said. ‘I can manage all that for myself when I’m out. And don’t sit here in this room, bored to death. Go and laugh and play with Cousin Lin as often as you can.’

By now he was fully dressed, and Aroma urged him to go and pay his respects to Grandmother Jia, Jia Zheng, Lady Wang and the others. Bao-yu still had a few instructions for Skybright and Musk before he went out to see Grandmother Jia. She too, naturally, had some parting advice for him. Then he went to Lady Wang, and from her rooms out to the study to see Jia Zheng.

As luck would have it, Jia Zheng had come home rather early that day and was in the study chatting with his literary dependants. Bao-yu suddenly appeared, paid his respects and said he was going to school.

‘If you mention the word “school” again, you’ll make even me die of shame,’ Jia Zheng said with a cold laugh. ‘If you take my advice, you’ll simply go and play. That’s what you’re fit for. Mind you don’t soil my floor by standing on it, or my door by leaning against it!’

The literary gentlemen had all risen and were smiling.

‘Come, sir, why speak like that? Once our young friend starts today, in two or three years he’ll have made a name for himself. He certainly won’t go on behaving like a child as he did before. It’s nearly time for the morning meal. Do be on your way, young sir.’

Two of the older men took Bao-yu out by the hand.

‘Who is going with Bao-yu?’ Jia Zheng asked.

A couple of answering calls came from outside, and three or four strapping men promptly entered and made formal kneeling salutes. Looking at them, Jia Zheng recognised Bao-yu’s wet-nurse’s son, Li Gui.

‘You go to school with him every day,’ he said to Li Gui. ‘What books has he actually read? All he’s filled his head with is gossip and nonsense, and all he’s learnt is ever more elaborate mischief. When I have a moment to spare, I’ll flay you first and settle with that good-for-nothing afterwards!’

Terrified, Li Gui dropped on both knees, took off his cap and knocked his head audibly on the floor, saying ‘Yes, sir’ over and over. Then he ventured, ‘The young master’s got as far as the third volume of the Book of Poetry. Something about “The deer go yoo-yoo, lotus leaves and duckweed.” I wouldn’t dare lie, sir.’

The whole room burst out laughing. Even Jia Zheng could not help joining in.

‘He might read thirty volumes of the Book of Poetry, and it would still be like stopping his ears to steal a bell: nothing but an attempt to fool people. Go and give my respects to the old gentleman at the school. Tell him I said there is no need to go through the motions with the Book of Poetry or classical prose or any of that. What matters most is to explain the Four Books straight through first and have him learn them thoroughly by heart.’

Li Gui hastily assented. Seeing that Jia Zheng had nothing more to say, he withdrew.

Bao-yu had been standing alone outside the courtyard, not daring to make a sound as he waited. When they came out, he hurried away. Brushing the dust from their clothes, Li Gui and the others said, ‘Did you hear that, young master? It’s our skins he’s going to strip off first!

‘Other people’s servants get some credit out of following their masters about. Servants like us only get dragged in for the beatings and scoldings. You might have a little pity on us from now on.’

‘Dear brother, don’t feel so ill-used,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘I’ll treat you tomorrow.’

‘Oh, my little lord and master, who would dare expect a treat from you?’ Li Gui said. ‘If you’d only listen to a word or two we say, that would be enough.’

As they spoke, they reached Grandmother Jia’s rooms again. Qin Zhong was already there waiting, and she was talking to him. The two boys greeted each other and took their leave of her. Suddenly Bao-yu remembered that he had not said goodbye to Dai-yu,

and hurried to her room. She was sitting by the window in front of her mirror, doing her hair. When he said he was going to school, she smiled.

‘Splendid! You’re sure to pluck the cassia in the Moon Palace this time. I can’t come to see you off.’

‘Dear cousin, wait till I come back from school before you have supper,’ Bao-yu said. ‘And wait for me before you mix the rouge paste.’

He went on and on before he could bring himself to leave. Dai-yu hastily called him back.

‘Why don’t you go and say goodbye to your Cousin Bao-chai?’

Bao-yu smiled without answering and went straight off to school with Qin Zhong.

The Jia family’s charitable clan school was not far away, only a li or so. It had been founded by their first ancestor so that boys of the clan whose families were too poor to engage a tutor could study there. All members of the clan who held office or rank contributed silver towards its expenses in proportion to their salaries. They chose a man of years and virtue to be schoolmaster, with the sole duty of teaching and supervising the boys. Bao-yu and Qin Zhong now arrived, exchanged formal greetings with everyone in turn, and began their lessons.

From that day on, they went to school together and came home together, rose together and sat together, growing ever closer. Grandmother Jia was fond of Qin Zhong too, and often kept him to stay for three or five days, cherishing him as if he were her own great-grandson. Seeing that he was not well off, she also helped him with clothes, shoes and other things.

In less than a month Qin Zhong was quite at home in the Rong mansion. Bao-yu, however, was never one to keep within bounds: he simply followed his own inclinations. Another of his odd notions took hold of him, and he made a point of whispering to Qin Zhong, ‘We’re the same age, and we’re schoolfellows too. From now on, let’s forget about being uncle and nephew and treat each other as brothers and friends.’

At first Qin Zhong refused, but Bao-yu would not have it and insisted on calling him ‘brother’, or using his courtesy name, Jing-qing. Qin Zhong had no choice but to follow suit and abandon the proper forms of address.

Now although the pupils at this school were all boys of the clan or sons of its relatives, the proverb puts it well: ‘One dragon has nine kinds of offspring, and no two kinds are alike.’ With so many of them, dragons and snakes were bound to be mixed together; there were some low characters among them.

Both the newcomers were as lovely as flowers. Qin Zhong was shy and gentle, blushing before he spoke, his hesitant, modest manner like a girl’s. Bao-yu, for his part, had an inborn readiness to make himself small, to yield and defer, with considerate ways and endlessly affectionate talk. The two therefore grew still closer, and one could hardly blame those schoolfellows for becoming suspicious. Behind their backs one boy passed a word to another, until abuse and scandal filled the classroom and spread beyond it.

Since coming to live with Lady Wang, Xue Pan had discovered that there was a family school with plenty of young lads in it. This occasionally stirred in him the inclinations of Longyang, and he too made a pretence of coming to study. His attendance was fitful, fishing for three days and drying his nets for two. He gave Jia Dai-ru tuition gifts for nothing and made no progress whatever. All he wanted was to acquire male favourites.

Indeed, several of the younger pupils, tempted by Xue Pan’s money and the food and clothes he provided, had already been enticed into his keeping. We need not record the details. There were also two amorous young pupils whose family connections were unknown, and whose real names had not been ascertained. So charming and graceful were they that the whole school called them by a pair of nicknames: one was Xiang-lian, the other Yu-ai.

Everyone admired them in secret, with intentions that boded no good for the lads, but fear of Xue Pan’s power kept anyone from approaching them. When Bao-yu and Qin Zhong arrived and saw the two, they too could not help feeling drawn to them. Knowing that they were intimate with Xue Pan, however, they dared make no rash advance. Xiang-lian and Yu-ai in turn felt just as fond of Bao-yu and Qin Zhong. All four thus cherished feelings which had yet to declare themselves. Each day at school they sat apart, but their eight eyes sought one another out. A casual remark might carry a hidden meaning, a verse about mulberry trees allude to willows: from a distance they understood each other, while outwardly avoiding notice. Yet a few sly rogues had seen what was happening and would wink and make faces behind their backs, or cough loudly and raise their voices. This had been going on for some time.

It so happened that Dai-ru had business that day and had gone home early, leaving a seven-character line for the boys to match with the second half of a couplet before their next lesson the following day. He put Jia Rui in temporary charge of the school. Happily, Xue Pan no longer came very often even to put in an appearance, so Qin Zhong took the opportunity to exchange winks and signals with Xiang-lian. Pretending that they needed to relieve themselves, they went into the back courtyard for a private talk.

‘Do the grown-ups at home mind who you make friends with?’ Qin Zhong began.

Before he had finished, someone coughed behind them. Startled, both hastily turned. It was a schoolfellow called Jin Rong. Xiang-lian was rather quick-tempered, and embarrassment and anger together provoked him.

‘What are you coughing for? Aren’t we allowed to talk?’

‘If you’re allowed to talk, why shouldn’t I cough?’ Jin Rong said with a laugh. ‘Just tell me this: why can’t you say what you have to say out in the open? What are you up to, sneaking about like this? I’ve caught you now. No use denying it!

‘Give me my cut first, and I won’t say a word. Otherwise we’ll all join in.’

Qin Zhong and Xiang-lian flushed scarlet with agitation.

‘What have you caught us doing?’ they demanded.

‘I’ve caught you at it, all right,’ Jin Rong said, laughing. He clapped his hands and shouted in amusement, ‘Fine griddle cakes you’ve slapped together! Won’t any of you buy one to eat?’

Furious and distressed, Qin Zhong and Xiang-lian hurried back inside to complain to Jia Rui that Jin Rong had been bullying them for no reason.

Jia Rui was a thoroughly unscrupulous creature, always on the lookout for an advantage. At school he used his authority to serve his private ends, forcing the boys to treat him. Later he had attached himself to Xue Pan for the sake of a little money, wine and meat, allowing him to bully and tyrannise as he pleased. Far from restraining him, Jia Rui helped him in his villainy to win his favour. But Xue Pan’s affections drifted like duckweed: one day he loved this one, the next day that. Lately he had made new friends and cast Xiang-lian and Yu-ai aside. Jin Rong himself had once been an intimate, only to be dropped when Xiang-lian and Yu-ai appeared; now they too had been abandoned. Jia Rui was consequently left without anyone to speak for him or lend him a hand. Instead of blaming Xue Pan for throwing over old friends for new, he blamed Xiang-lian and Yu-ai for failing to help him with Xue Pan. Jia Rui, Jin Rong and their set were therefore already jealous of the pair. When Qin Zhong and Xiang-lian came to complain about Jin Rong that day, Jia Rui felt immediately resentful. He could hardly scold Qin Zhong, so he vented his displeasure on Xiang-lian instead, accusing him of making trouble and giving him a sharp dressing-down.

Xiang-lian had only brought a rebuff on himself. Even Qin Zhong felt abashed, and they went back to their respective seats. Jin Rong was more triumphant than ever, shaking his head, smacking his lips and continuing to make remarks. Yu-ai happened to hear and could not swallow his indignation. The two began a muttered quarrel across the intervening seats.

‘I plainly saw them in the back courtyard just now, kissing and feeling each other’s bottoms,’ Jin Rong insisted. ‘Just the two of them, breaking off grass stalks and drawing for long and short. Whoever got the longer one went first.’

He was too pleased with himself to care what nonsense he talked, and forgot that others were listening. Already he had angered someone else. And who do you suppose that was?

His name was Jia Qiang, and he was a great-great-grandson in the legitimate line of the Ning branch. His parents had died when he was very young, and he had grown up in Jia Zhen’s household. Now sixteen, he was even more handsome and graceful than Jia Rong. The two cousins were particularly close and were often together. There were many people and many tongues in the Ning mansion, and servants who felt slighted were especially adept at inventing slanders against their masters. Who could say what malicious tales had been set going this time? Jia Zhen, it seemed, had heard something of the unsavoury gossip and wished to avoid suspicion himself. He had now allotted Jia Qiang quarters of his own and told him to move out of the Ning mansion and set up a separate household. Jia Qiang was as clever as he was good-looking. Though nominally a pupil, he attended only to keep up appearances, and went on cockfighting, coursing, enjoying flowers and dallying with willows. With Jia Zhen’s indulgence above and Jia Rong’s support below, who in the clan would dare cross him?

Being so close to Jia Rong, how could he stand by and see Qin Zhong bullied? He wanted to step forward and take his part, but first he thought it over.

‘Jin Rong, Jia Rui and that lot are all friends of Uncle Xue’s, and I’ve always been on good terms with Uncle Xue myself. If I interfere and they tell old Xue, won’t it spoil our friendship? Yet if I do nothing, this sort of gossip makes things unpleasant for everyone. Why not find a way to bring them to heel, stop their mouths and save everyone’s face?’

Having decided this, he too pretended to need to relieve himself and went outside. He quietly beckoned over Bao-yu’s schoolboy attendant, Tealeaf, and put a few such suggestions into his head. Tealeaf was Bao-yu’s most useful boy, but young and ignorant of the world. Jia Qiang now told him that Jin Rong was bullying Qin Zhong and dragging his own master Bao-yu into it too: unless he was taught a lesson, next time he would be still more insolent and impossible to check. Tealeaf needed no excuse to throw his weight about. With this news, and Jia Qiang behind him, he rushed in to find Jin Rong. There was no ‘Master Jin’ now.

‘You there, Jin! What do you think you are?’

Jia Qiang stamped his boots, deliberately straightened his clothes and looked at the sun.

‘Time to go,’ he said, and told Jia Rui that he had business and must leave early.

Jia Rui dared not make him stay and had to let him go. Meanwhile Tealeaf grabbed Jin Rong.

‘Whether we bugger anyone or not, what the fuck has it got to do with you? We haven’t buggered your dad, have we? If you’re such a fine fellow, come out and have a go at your lord and master Tealeaf!’

All the boys in the room stared, dumbfounded. Jia Rui hastily shouted, ‘Tealeaf, none of your insolence!’

Jin Rong’s face had turned yellow with rage.

‘This is rebellion! Even a servant boy dares behave like this! I’ll have a word with your master.’

He wrenched his hand free and was about to seize Bao-yu and Qin Zhong and hit them. Before he could reach them, an inkstone came whizzing from behind his head. Who had thrown it was not yet clear. Luckily it missed, but landed on someone else’s desk: the desk shared by Jia Lan and Jia Jun.

Jia Jun too was a great-grandson of a close branch of the Rong family. His mother had also been widowed young and had remained a widow, bringing him up alone. He and Jia Lan were particularly good friends, which was why they shared a desk. Young as Jia Jun was, however, he had tremendous spirit and was a very mischievous, fearless boy.

Watching coolly from his seat, he had seen one of Jin Rong’s friends secretly come to his aid by throwing the inkstone at Tealeaf. It missed Tealeaf and landed on Jia Jun’s desk instead, right in front of him, smashing a porcelain water-pot for the inkstone to pieces and splashing black water all over a book. Jia Jun was not going to put up with that.

‘You filthy bastards! So we’re all fighting now, are we?’

As he cursed, he snatched up his own inkstone to throw back. Jia Lan, who preferred to stay out of trouble, hastily held it down and pleaded with him.

‘Dear brother, it’s nothing to do with us.’

But Jia Jun could not contain himself. Taking his book-box in both hands, he hurled it towards the other side. He was too small and weak to throw so far, and it fell short on Bao-yu and Qin Zhong’s desk. It struck with a clatter, scattering books, loose sheets, brushes and inkstones all over the desk, and smashed Bao-yu’s tea-bowl as well, spilling the tea. Jia Jun jumped out to grapple with the boy who had thrown the inkstone. Jin Rong had meanwhile seized a long, broad bamboo board. In such a cramped, crowded space, how could anyone escape when he began swinging it? Tealeaf had already taken a blow.

‘Why aren’t you coming to help?’ he yelled.

Bao-yu had three other boys with him: Chu-yao, Sao-hong and Mo-yu. Not one of them was any less mischievous. They all shouted at once, ‘Sons of whores! They’re using weapons!’

Mo-yu picked up a door-bar, while Sao-hong and Chu-yao each had a riding-whip. They charged in together. Jia Rui rushed from one to another, trying to stop this boy and reason with that, but no one would listen. The brawl went on unchecked.

Some of the unruly boys joined in for the fun of landing a few blows without risk to themselves. Others, more timid, hid to one side, while others stood on the desks, clapping, roaring with laughter and shouting encouragement to the fighters. In an instant the room was in uproar. Outside, Li Gui and several of the grown servants heard the commotion and hurried in, shouting together for everyone to stop. They asked what had caused it. Each voice told a different story: one said this, another that. Li Gui first gave Tealeaf and the other three a furious scolding and drove them out. Qin Zhong’s head had already met Jin Rong’s board, grazing the skin, and Bao-yu was rubbing it with the front of his jacket. When he saw that everyone had been silenced, he gave his orders.

‘Li Gui, pack up the books! Bring the horses. I’m going back to tell the old schoolmaster! We’ve been bullied. We didn’t say anything out of turn, but went politely to tell Master Rui. Master Rui blamed us instead. He let them abuse us and even put them up to beating our Tealeaf, and now Qin Zhong’s head is cut open too. What’s the use of studying here any more?

‘Tealeaf only did it because someone was bullying me. We’d be better off breaking up the school.’

‘Don’t be hasty, young master,’ Li Gui urged him. ‘The old gentleman’s gone home on business. If we trouble him with this little matter now, it’ll look as if we’re in the wrong. I say we should settle it here where it happened. Why disturb him?’ He turned to Jia Rui. ‘It’s all your fault, Master Rui. When the old gentleman’s away, you’re the head of the school, sir. Everyone takes their lead from you. If someone misbehaves, beat him if he needs beating, punish him if he needs punishing. How could you let things come to this without doing anything?’

‘I shouted at them, but none of them would listen,’ Jia Rui said.

‘I hope you won’t be angry with me, sir,’ Li Gui said, smiling, ‘but you aren’t always quite as proper as you might be. That’s why the lads won’t listen to you.

‘If this goes before the old schoolmaster, you won’t get off either, sir. You’d better find a way to sort it out, and quickly.’

‘What is there to sort out?’ Bao-yu said. ‘I’m going home, whatever happens!’

‘If Jin Rong stays, I won’t study here,’ Qin Zhong said, crying.

‘Why should that be?’ Bao-yu said. ‘Are other people allowed to come here and we aren’t? I’ll explain everything to everyone and have Jin Rong turned out.’ He asked Li Gui, ‘Which branch of the family is Jin Rong related to?’

Li Gui thought for a moment.

‘Better not ask. If we start going into whose relation he is, we’ll only make more bad feeling among the family.’

From outside the window Tealeaf called, ‘He’s the nephew of Mrs Huang in the east lane. As if that were some mighty pillar to lean on! And he thinks he can frighten us! Mrs Huang’s his father’s sister. All that aunt of yours does is hang about pestering our Mrs Lian, going down on her knees to borrow things to pawn. I’ve no respect for a lady or a mistress like that!’

Li Gui kept shouting at him to stop.

‘You little dog-fucked bastard! You’re the only one who knows anything, are you, spewing out all that filth?’

‘So that’s whose relation he is!’ Bao-yu said with a cold laugh. ‘Sister-in-law Huang’s nephew. I’ll go and ask her about it myself.’

He made to leave, calling Tealeaf in to pack his books.

As Tealeaf packed, he spoke up again, pleased with himself.

‘No need for you to go and see her yourself, Master Bao. Let me go to her house and say Her Old Ladyship has something to ask her. I’ll hire a carriage and bring her in, and you can question her in front of Her Old Ladyship. Wouldn’t that save trouble?’

‘Are you tired of living?’ Li Gui shouted. ‘Mind I don’t give you a good thrashing when we get home, and then report to the master and mistress that you’re behind all Bao-yu’s mischief! I’d only just managed to coax him halfway round, and here you are with another bright idea. You’ve made a riot of the school. Instead of finding some way to quiet things down, you want to make it worse!’

After that Tealeaf dared not say another word.

By this time Jia Rui too was afraid of the affair growing bigger. His own hands were far from clean, so he had to swallow his pride and plead first with Qin Zhong and then with Bao-yu. At first neither would give way. Eventually Bao-yu said, ‘Very well, I needn’t go home. Just make Jin Rong apologise, and that will do.’

Jin Rong refused at first, but could not hold out when Jia Rui began pressing him to apologise as well. Li Gui and the others had to coax him.

‘You started it, after all. How else can it be settled?’

Unable to resist any longer, Jin Rong bowed to Qin Zhong with clasped hands. Bao-yu was still not satisfied: he insisted on a kowtow. Jia Rui wanted only to quiet the affair for the moment. He took Jin Rong aside and urged him in a whisper, ‘As the saying goes, “Even an execution ends with a head touching the ground.” You’ve made the trouble, so you’ll have to swallow a little pride. One kowtow, and it’s over.’

Jin Rong had no choice. He went forward and kowtowed to Qin Zhong. For what followed, listen to the next chapter.
