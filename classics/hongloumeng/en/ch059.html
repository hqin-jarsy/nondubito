Chapter 59 By Willow-leaf Islet, Oriole is scolded and Swallow berated; In Crimson Rue Studio, generals are summoned and orders fly

Chapter end

Now, when Bao-yu heard that Grandmother Jia and the others had returned, he put on another garment and, leaning on his stick, went to the front of the house to pay his respects to them all. Worn out by their daily exertions, they wished to retire early. The night passed without incident, and at the fifth watch next morning they went to Court again.

The day for accompanying the funeral procession was drawing near. Faithful, Amber, Jade and Glass were all busy preparing Grandmother Jia’s things, while Silver, Cai-yun, Cai-xia and the others packed Lady Wang’s, checking everything over in the presence of the married women who were to go in charge before handing the things over to them.

The attendants going with them numbered six maids, older and younger, and ten women, old attendants and married servants, not counting the men. For several days they had been getting the animal-borne litters and travelling equipment ready. Neither Faithful nor Silver was to go; they would stay to look after the rooms. Several days beforehand, the curtains, hangings, bedding and furnishings were sent on ahead. Four or five married women, with several men, took charge of them and set out in a number of carts, going by another road to reach the lodgings first, arrange everything and wait for the party.

When the day came, Grandmother Jia took Jia Rong’s wife with her in one animal-borne litter, with Lady Wang following in another. Jia Zhen rode at the head of a party of household men to escort them. There were also several large carts for the older women and maids, with bundles of changes of clothing and other necessities. Aunt Xue and You-shi led everyone out beyond the main gate to see them off before coming back. Afraid there might be difficulties on the road, Jia Lian sent his own parents off to catch up with Grandmother Jia’s and Lady Wang’s litters, then followed with a party of household men, bringing up the rear.

At the Rong mansion, Lai Da put extra men on night duty and closed off both sets of halls and courtyards. Everyone coming or going had to use the small side gate on the west. At sunset he had the ceremonial gate shut and allowed no one through. The garden’s front, rear, eastern and western side gates were all locked as well, leaving open only the gate behind Lady Wang’s main rooms which the girls ordinarily used, and the eastern side gate leading to Aunt Xue’s quarters. These two lay within the inner courtyards and did not need to be locked. Inside, Faithful and Silver each shut up the principal rooms and took their maids and older women to sleep in the side rooms. Every day Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife came in with ten or so older women to keep watch at night. A number of young serving-men had also been posted in the passage hall to sit up and strike the watch-clappers. All the arrangements were thoroughly in place.

Early one morning Bao-chai woke from her drowsy spring sleep, lifted the bed-curtains and got up. Feeling a slight chill, she opened the door and looked out. The earth in the garden was damp and the moss green: a few drops of light rain had fallen during the fifth watch. She called Xiang-yun and the others to get up. As they washed and dressed, Xiang-yun said that both her cheeks were itching and she was afraid the skin eruption had come back. She asked Bao-chai for some rose medicinal powder.

‘I gave all I had left to my younger cousin the other day,’ Bao-chai told her. ‘Pin-pin has made up quite a lot. I meant to ask her for some, but as I haven’t had any itching this year I forgot.’

She told Oriole to go and fetch some. Oriole had just answered and was starting off when Rui-guan said, ‘I’ll come with you. I can see Ou-guan while I’m there.’

The two of them set out together from Allspice Court.

Talking and laughing as they walked, with something to say at every step, they reached Willow-leaf Islet almost before they knew it and followed the willow embankment. The leaves were just opening, a pale green, and the slender shoots hung like threads of gold.

‘Can you weave things out of willow twigs?’ Oriole asked with a smile.

‘What sort of things?’ said Rui-guan, smiling too.

‘What can’t you make?

‘Things to play with, things to use, anything. Let me pick some. I’ll weave a little flower-basket with the leaves still on, and we can put all sorts of flowers in it. That really will be something to play with.’

Putting off the powder errand for the moment, she reached up, drew down the green and parted the gold, and picked a quantity of tender shoots, which she told Rui-guan to hold. She herself went on weaving as she walked, gathering a spray or two of any flowers she saw along the path, until she had made a delicate openwork basket with an arched handle. The twigs were already clothed in their own green leaves, and with the flowers set among them it looked charmingly unusual.

‘Oh, give it to me, sister!’ cried Rui-guan in delight.

‘We’ll give this one to Miss Lin,’ said Oriole. ‘On the way back we can pick some more and make several, for everyone to play with.’

As she spoke they reached Naiad’s House.

Dai-yu was also at her morning toilette. At the sight of the basket she smiled.

‘Who made this fresh little flower-basket?’

‘I made it for you to play with, miss,’ said Oriole, smiling.

‘No wonder people praise your clever fingers,’ said Dai-yu, taking it from her. ‘This really is an unusual little thing.’

She examined it, then told Nightingale to hang it up. Oriole paid her respects to Aunt Xue before asking Dai-yu for the powder. Dai-yu at once had Nightingale wrap up a packet and give it to her.

‘I’m better now, and I mean to go out for a walk today,’ she added. ‘Tell your mistress when you get back that she needn’t come over to see Mother, and I wouldn’t dream of troubling her to come and see me either. Once my hair is done, Mother and I are both coming to you. We’ll have our meal brought over there as well, and make a lively party of it.’

Oriole promised to deliver the message and went out to Nightingale’s room to find Rui-guan. Ou-guan and Rui-guan were talking so happily that neither could bear to leave the other.

‘Miss Lin’s coming over too,’ said Oriole. ‘Why shouldn’t Ou-guan come with us now and wait for her there?’

‘That’s a good idea,’ said Nightingale when she heard this. ‘She’s a nuisance here, always getting into mischief.’

As she spoke she wrapped Dai-yu’s spoon and chopsticks in a Western handkerchief and handed the bundle to Ou-guan.

‘Take these ahead with you. Then you’ll have done an errand as well.’

Ou-guan took it and, all smiles, went out with the other two. They made their way along the willow embankment. Oriole picked more willow shoots and this time sat down on a rock to weave. She told Rui-guan to deliver the powder first and then come back, but the two girls were so absorbed in watching her fingers that neither could tear herself away.

‘If you won’t go, I shan’t do any more,’ Oriole kept telling them.

‘I’ll go with you,’ Ou-guan said at last, ‘and then we can hurry back.’

Off the two of them went. Oriole was still weaving when Mamma He’s young daughter, Swallow, came up.

‘What are you weaving, sister?’ she asked with a smile.

While they were talking, Rui-guan and Ou-guan returned.

‘What paper were you burning the other day, after all?’ Swallow asked Ou-guan. ‘My aunt saw you and wanted to report you, but she never managed it. Instead Bao-yu put all sorts of blame on her. She was so angry she told my mother every last detail. What quarrels did you store up in those two or three years outside that you still can’t settle them?’

Ou-guan gave a cold laugh.

‘What quarrels? They’re never satisfied, and then they blame us. In those two years outside, never mind anything else, just think what they made out of our rice and vegetables. Heaven knows how much they took home, more than their whole families could eat. And then there was the money they made every day buying this and that. Whenever we asked them to do the least thing for us out there, they complained to heaven and earth. Have they any conscience? You tell me.’

‘She’s my aunt,’ Swallow said with a laugh. ‘I can hardly take an outsider’s side against her. No wonder Bao-yu says, “A girl before she marries is a priceless pearl. Once she’s married, goodness knows where all her nasty ways come from. She’s still a pearl, but she’s lost her lustre and her lovely colour. She’s a dead pearl. When she gets older still, she isn’t even a pearl any more, but a fish’s eye.

‘“It’s plainly the same person, so how does she turn into three different things?” It’s nonsense, of course, but there’s something in it. I don’t know about other people. Take my mother and my aunt: the older those two sisters get, the more they set their hearts on money. At first they used to sit at home grumbling that they had no work and nothing coming in. Luckily this garden was made, and I was picked to come in and happened to be sent to the House of Green Delights. Apart from saving the cost of keeping me at home, there were four or five hundred cash left over every month, and even that wasn’t enough for them. Later they were both sent to Pear-tree Court to look after the actresses. Ou-guan took my aunt for her godmother and Parfumée took my mother. They’ve been comfortably off these last few years. Now the girls have moved in here, you’d think they could let go, but they’re still not satisfied. Isn’t it ridiculous? My aunt had hardly finished quarrelling with Ou-guan when my mother started on Parfumée over washing her hair. She wouldn’t even let Parfumée have her hair washed when she asked. Yesterday the monthly allowance came, so she couldn’t put it off any longer. She bought the things, then told me to wash my hair first.

‘I thought it over. I’ve money of my own, and even if I hadn’t, if I wanted my hair washed, I’d only have to ask Aroma, Skybright or Musk, any one of them, and it would be easy enough. Why should I take advantage of this? It didn’t seem right, so I wouldn’t. Then she made my little sister Xiao-jiu wash hers, and only after that did she call Parfumée. Sure enough, there was a quarrel. Next she wanted to blow on Bao-yu’s soup for him. Wouldn’t it make you die laughing? I told her all the rules as soon as she came in, but she won’t believe me. She has to pretend she knows everything, and all she gets for it is a snub. Luckily there are so many people in the garden that nobody can keep track of who’s related to whom. If anyone did remember, and found it was always our family quarrelling, how would that look?

‘And now you’ve come here to do this! Everything growing along here is in my father’s sister’s charge. Getting this patch meant more to her than coming into an estate to last for ever. She’s up early and to bed late every day, and wearing herself out isn’t enough: she has to make us come and watch it too. She’s terrified someone will spoil something, and then she’s afraid of keeping me from my duties.

‘Now Mother’s come in, the two old sisters-in-law watch over it so carefully they won’t let anyone touch a blade of grass. And here you are picking the flowers and snapping off her young shoots. They’ll be here any moment. Mind they don’t complain.’

‘Other people mustn’t go breaking and picking things at random, but I can,’ said Oriole. ‘Ever since they divided the plots up, every household has had its regular allowance. Never mind things to eat; I’m only talking about flowers and greenery to enjoy. Whoever has charge of them has to send round sprays of all the different kinds each day for the young ladies and their maids to wear, and some for the vases too. We’re the only ones who said, “Don’t send anything. We’ll ask you when we want some.” And we’ve never asked once. If I pick a few today, they can hardly have the face to say anything.’

She had scarcely finished when Swallow’s aunt did indeed appear, leaning on a stick. Oriole, Swallow and the others at once offered her a seat. Seeing all the tender willow shoots they had picked, and all the fresh flowers Ou-guan and the others had gathered, the old woman was far from pleased. She watched Oriole weaving, but could not very well say anything to her, so she addressed Swallow instead.

‘I told you to come and keep an eye on things. Now you’ve got so taken up with playing that you won’t go back. If they call for you, you’ll say I sent you on an errand. I’m your charm to make you invisible while you enjoy yourself!’

‘You tell me to do things, and then you’re afraid of the consequences,’ said Swallow. ‘And now you blame me. Am I to split myself into eight pieces?’

‘Don’t believe little Swallow, Auntie,’ Oriole said with a laugh. ‘She picked all this herself and pestered me to weave it for her. I keep telling her to go away, but she won’t.’

‘Do stop teasing,’ Swallow said, laughing. ‘You think it’s all a joke, but Auntie will take you seriously.’

The old woman was stupid and obstinate to begin with, and age had brought her close to dotage. Gain was all she cared for; she had no regard for anyone’s feelings. The sight was tearing her heart out, yet she had been unable to do anything about it. At Oriole’s words she took full advantage of her years, raised her stick and struck Swallow several times.

‘You little hussy! Here I am telling you off, and you still answer back! Your mother hates you till her teeth itch. She’d like to tear your flesh off and eat it. And you’re going to be a stubborn little hardhead with me too!’

Stung with shame and distress by the blows, Swallow burst into tears.

‘Oriole was only joking, and you really hit me! Why should Mother hate me? It’s not as if I’ve burnt the wash-water! What have I done wrong?’

Oriole had meant only to tease. Seeing the old woman had lost her temper in earnest, she hurried over to catch her arm.

‘I was joking,’ she said with a smile. ‘If you beat her for that, how am I to hold up my head?’

‘Miss, keep out of our affairs,’ said the old woman. ‘Am I not to discipline a child just because you’re here?’

Such stupid words made Oriole flush with resentment. She let go and gave a cold laugh.

‘You’ve all the time in the world to discipline her, yet you have to begin the moment I make a joke. Go on, then. I’ll watch you do it.’

She sat down and went on weaving her willow basket.

Just then Swallow’s mother came looking for her.

‘Why aren’t you fetching water? What are you doing over there?’ she called.

‘Come and look!’ the other old woman called back. ‘Your daughter won’t even listen to me now. She’s standing here telling me off.’

‘Sister, what’s happened now?’ said her mother as she approached. ‘So my girl’s got no mother in her eyes. Has she got no aunt either?’

Seeing Swallow’s mother arrive, Oriole had to explain once more how it had happened. But the aunt would let no one speak. She pointed out the flowers and willow on the rock.

‘Look at what your daughter’s been playing with, a great girl of her age! She brings people to spoil my things, and then how can I say anything to them?’

Swallow’s mother was still angry over Parfumée, and resentful of Swallow for not doing as she wished. She came up and slapped the girl’s face.

‘Little whore! How long have you been too grand for us? You’ve been learning from those brazen little sluts. So we’re not allowed to discipline any of you now? I can’t lay a hand on the one I’m godmother to, but you dropped out of my cunt. Am I to be afraid to discipline you as well?

‘If you hussies can go places I’m not allowed into, you ought to stay there and serve till you drop dead. What are you running out here for, chasing men?’

She snatched up the willow twigs and thrust them in Swallow’s face.

‘What do you call this? Your mother’s cunt, that’s what you’re weaving!’

‘We made that,’ Oriole said at once. ‘Don’t scold one person to get at another.’

The old woman deeply envied Aroma, Skybright and their companions. She had already learnt that the senior maids in the young masters’ and mistresses’ rooms carried themselves with more consequence, and had more power, than women like her. Whenever she met them she felt both fear and deference, which in turn made her angry and resentful, and she took it out on everyone around her. Now there was Ou-guan before her too, her elder sister’s enemy. Anger gathered from every quarter into a single blaze.

Crying, Swallow set off for the House of Green Delights. Her mother grew anxious. They would ask why she was crying, and she would tell them she had been beaten, and then there would be more trouble with Skybright and the rest.

‘Come back!’ she hurriedly called. ‘I’ve something to tell you before you go.’

Swallow had no intention of coming back. Frantic, her mother ran after her to pull her back, but Swallow looked round, saw her coming, and fled all the faster. In her haste her mother failed to watch her footing, slipped on the moss and fell, setting Oriole and the other two laughing. Oriole flung all the flowers and willow into the river in a fit of temper and went back to her rooms.

The old aunt was left in such anguish over the loss that she could only call on the Buddha and curse.

‘Spiteful little hussy! Spoiling flowers like that, you’ll have the thunderbolt after you!’

She went on picking flowers herself to send round to the different households, and we shall leave her there.

Swallow ran straight into the courtyard and almost collided with Aroma, who was on her way to Dai-yu’s to ask after her. She threw her arms round her.

‘Save me, miss! Mother’s beating me again!’

Seeing her mother coming, Aroma could not help losing her temper.

‘Every other day it’s the same, beating your goddaughter and then your own. Are you showing off how many daughters you’ve got, or do you really not know there’s such a thing as the law?’

In the few days she had been there, the old woman had found Aroma quiet and good-natured.

‘You don’t know anything about it, miss,’ she said. ‘Keep out of our business! You’re the ones who spoil them. What’s the use of interfering now?’

She went on chasing Swallow to hit her. Aroma turned back inside in anger. Musk was under the crab-apple tree airing hand-towels. Hearing the shouting and commotion, she said, ‘Leave her alone, sister. Let’s see what she does.’

At the same time she gave Swallow a look. Swallow understood and ran straight to Bao-yu.

‘Now we’re seeing things we never thought to see!’ everyone said with a laugh.

‘Do cool down a little,’ Musk told the old woman. ‘Don’t all of us count for anything? Can’t we ask you to spare someone and be listened to?’

The old woman saw her daughter run to Bao-yu and saw him take her hand.

‘Don’t be afraid,’ he said. ‘I’m here.’

Still crying, Swallow told him everything that had happened with Oriole and the others.

This made Bao-yu still more upset.

‘It would be bad enough if you only made trouble here,’ he said. ‘Must you offend our relations’ people as well?’

Musk turned to the old woman and the others.

‘No wonder our good sister says their affairs are none of our business. We’ve been ignorant enough to interfere where we shouldn’t. Well, now we’ll ask someone who has the authority to deal with it. Then our sister will be convinced, heart and soul, and learn the rules as well.’

She turned to a little maid.

‘Go and fetch Patience for us. If she’s busy, fetch Mamma Lin.’

The girl answered and went out. The other married women came up smiling.

‘Quick, sister, ask the young ladies to call that child back. If Miss Patience comes, you’ll be in trouble.’

‘Whoever this Miss Patience may be, she’ll have to listen to reason,’ said the old woman. ‘Where was it ever heard of that a mother disciplines her daughter and everyone else disciplines the mother?’

‘Who do you think Miss Patience is?’ the women asked with a laugh. ‘She’s Miss Patience from Mrs Lian’s rooms. If she’s feeling kindly you may get a word or two in. If she turns nasty, sister, you’ll have more than you can stomach, and plenty left to carry home!’

They were still speaking when the little maid returned.

‘Miss Patience is busy. She asked what I wanted and I told her. She says, “In that case, turn her out. Tell Mamma Lin to give her forty strokes of the board outside the side gate, and have done with it.”’

The old woman was naturally unwilling to leave. Tears streamed down her face as she begged Aroma and the others.

‘It was hard enough for me to get a place in here. And I’m a widow with no one at home. It’s just right for me to give my whole mind to serving you young ladies, with nothing to worry about outside. It’s convenient for you, and it saves some of the expense of keeping me at home.

‘If I go, I’ll have to light my own fire and fend for myself again, and before long I shan’t have anything to live on.’

At the sight of her like this, Aroma’s heart had already softened.

‘You want to stay here, yet you won’t keep the rules, you won’t listen to anyone, and you go about hitting people. Where did they find anyone as unreasonable as you? Bickering every day, making a laughing-stock of us and destroying all propriety.’

‘Why bother with her?’ said Skybright. ‘Send her off and have done. Who wants to stand here arguing with her?’

The old woman begged them all again.

‘I know I was wrong. Now you’ve told me, I’ll mend my ways. You’ll be doing a good deed and earning merit.’

Then she appealed to Swallow.

‘It all began with my trying to beat you. In the end I never managed to beat you, and now I’m the one who suffers! Say a word for me too.’

Seeing her so pitiable, Bao-yu could only let her stay, warning her not to make any more trouble. The old woman came up and thanked them one by one, then withdrew.

Just then Patience arrived and asked what had happened.

‘It’s all over,’ Aroma and the others told her at once. ‘There’s no need to mention it again.’

‘“Where you can spare someone, spare them,”’ said Patience with a smile. ‘And if we can save ourselves some trouble by letting things pass, so much the better. They’ve only been gone a few days, yet all I hear is that everyone, great and small, is rising in rebellion. Before one affair’s settled another starts. I don’t know which to attend to.’

‘I thought ours was the only rebellion,’ Aroma said with a laugh. ‘So there are others too?’

‘This is nothing,’ said Patience, smiling. ‘I was just reckoning them up with Mrs Zhen. In these three or four days there have been eight or nine affairs, big and small, altogether. Yours is one of the smallest, hardly worth counting. There are bigger ones that would make you angry and make you laugh.’

To learn what these matters were that Aroma asked her about, listen to the next chapter.
