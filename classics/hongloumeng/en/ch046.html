Chapter 46 An awkward person cannot escape an awkward business; Faithful vows never to be paired like mandarin ducks1

Chapter end

Only towards the end of the fourth watch did Dai-yu gradually fall asleep. We may leave her there for the moment.

Now Xi-feng, summoned by Lady Xing without knowing why, hurriedly changed her clothes and ornaments and went over in her carriage. Lady Xing sent everyone out of the room and spoke to her quietly.

‘I’ve sent for you about one thing only. There’s a difficult matter your father-in-law has entrusted to me, and I can’t decide what to do, so I wanted to consult you first. He’s taken a fancy to Grandmother’s Faithful and wants her in his rooms. He’s told me to ask Grandmother for her. I think it’s an ordinary enough thing to ask, but I’m afraid she won’t let her go. Can you think of a way?’

‘If you ask me,’ Xi-feng said at once, ‘I wouldn’t invite the rebuff.

‘Grandmother can’t even eat her dinner without Faithful. How could she bear to part with her? Besides, when we’re talking of one thing and another, she often says, “At his age, what does he want with one concubine after another in his rooms, spoiling the poor girls’ lives for nothing? Instead of looking after his health or doing his work properly, he spends his days drinking with his concubines.” Does that sound as though she’s pleased with him, Mother? We ought to be keeping out of the way, and here you are, poking a straw up a tiger’s nose! Please don’t be angry with me, but I daren’t go.

‘It plainly won’t do any good, and it will only make things unpleasant. At his age, when he does something improper, you ought to try to dissuade him. It isn’t as though he were still young and could do these things without its mattering. Now he has a whole crowd of brothers, nephews, sons and grandsons. If he carries on like this, how can he face them?’

Lady Xing gave a cold laugh.

‘Plenty of great families have three or four concubines. Why should ours be the one that can’t? He might not listen even if I did try to dissuade him. However fond Grandmother is of the maid, it won’t be so easy for her to refuse her own grown son, a man with a greying beard and an official post, when he asks to take the girl into his rooms. I only sent for you to talk it over, and the first thing you do is deliver a whole sermon on our faults. As if I’d ever ask you to go! Naturally I’ll speak to her myself. You say I don’t try to dissuade him. Don’t you know his temper? Before I could persuade him of anything, he’d have turned on me.’

Xi-feng knew Lady Xing’s foolish, obstinate nature. First came her own safety, which she sought by complying with Jia She in everything; next came the pleasure she took in grasping at money and possessions. She left every household affair, great or small, to Jia She. But wherever money passed through her hands, coming in or going out, she was exceedingly stingy, making his extravagance her excuse: ‘I have to economise where I can, to make up for it.’ She trusted none of the children or servants, and would not listen to a word from any of them. Hearing her now, Xi-feng knew she had grown contrary again and that advice would be wasted. She hastily put on a conciliatory smile.

‘What you say is perfectly right, Mother. How long have I lived? What do I know about the rights and wrongs of things? After all, to a mother or father, never mind a maid: even a priceless living treasure would go to their son before anyone else. How can one believe what’s said behind his back?

‘I really am a fool. When Lian does something wrong, you and Father are so angry you’d like to drag him in and beat him dead on the spot. Yet once you see him, you let it pass, and give him your own favourite things just as before. Naturally Grandmother must be the same towards Father. I say, Grandmother’s in a good mood today. If you want to ask her, do it today. I’ll go ahead and keep her laughing. When you arrive, I’ll find an excuse to slip away, taking the other people out with me, so you can speak to her alone. If she gives you the girl, so much the better. If not, no harm’s done, and no one else will know.’

Lady Xing was pleased again at this, and explained further.

‘My idea is not to ask Grandmother first. If she refuses, that’s the end of it. I mean to have a quiet word with Faithful beforehand. Of course she’ll be shy, but once I explain it carefully, she’ll naturally say nothing, and that will settle it.

‘Then I can speak to Grandmother. Even if she refuses, she won’t be able to hold out against the girl’s own wishes. As the saying goes, “You can’t keep someone who means to leave.” Then it’s bound to come right.’

‘You’re the one with the clever plans, Mother,’ said Xi-feng, smiling. ‘That’s a thousand times surer. Never mind Faithful: who wouldn’t want to better herself and get on in the world? Who would turn down being half a mistress and choose to stay a maid, only to be married off to some serving lad in the end?’

‘Exactly,’ said Lady Xing with a smile. ‘Not only Faithful. Which of the senior maids with duties of their own wouldn’t want it? Go ahead, but don’t let a word slip. I’ll come over after dinner.’

‘Faithful has always been a difficult creature,’ Xi-feng thought. ‘Even so, she might yet agree. If I go first and Mother follows, all will be well if the girl consents. But if she doesn’t, Mother is so suspicious she may think I’ve let the secret out and put her up to making difficulties. Then, finding my warning come true, she’ll turn her mortification into anger and take it out on me. I don’t want that. Better to go over together. Whether the girl agrees or not, she won’t be able to suspect me.’

Having thought it through, she smiled.

‘Just before I came, Aunt sent over two cages of quails. I told them to fry the birds, and meant to have them sent here in time for your dinner. But as I was coming through the main gate, I saw the lads carrying your carriage away. They said a joint had come apart and it was going to be repaired. Why don’t we go over together now in mine?’

Lady Xing sent for her people to help her change.

Xi-feng busied herself attending to her, and the mother- and daughter-in-law drove over together. Then Xi-feng said:

‘If I go with you to Grandmother’s, she may ask what I came over to see you for, and that would be awkward. You’d better go ahead. I’ll take off these clothes and join you later.’

Lady Xing thought this sensible and went by herself to Grandmother Jia’s. After chatting for a while, she left on the pretext of going to Lady Wang’s rooms, went out by the back door and passed Faithful’s bedroom. Faithful was sitting there at her sewing. Seeing Lady Xing, she hastily rose.

‘What are you making?’ Lady Xing asked with a smile. ‘Let me see. Your embroidered flowers get better and better.’

She took the work from Faithful’s hands, looked it over and praised it unceasingly. Then she put it down and surveyed the girl from head to foot. Faithful wore a half-worn lotus-mauve figured-silk jacket, a dark-blue satin waistcoat with piped edges, and a pale-green skirt. She had a wasp waist and slender back, an oval face, glossy black hair, a high nose and a faint scattering of freckles on either cheek. Such scrutiny made her feel awkward, and she wondered at it.

‘What brings Your Ladyship here at such an in-between hour?’ she asked with a smile.

Lady Xing gave a look, and her attendants withdrew.

Lady Xing sat down, took Faithful’s hand and smiled.

‘I’ve come especially to congratulate you.’

Faithful already had some inkling of what she meant. In spite of herself she blushed, lowered her head and said nothing. Lady Xing went on:

‘You know your master has no one dependable to attend to him. He thought of buying another girl, but you never know what sort of unclean goings-on the girls from those dealers have been mixed up in, or what’s wrong with them. You buy one, and within two or three days she’s up to all her filthy tricks again. So he wanted to choose a girl born into service in the household and take her into his rooms. But he couldn’t find a good one. Either her looks weren’t right or her disposition wasn’t right; if she had one merit, she lacked another. He’s been quietly looking them over for six months, and of all these girls, you’re the pick. Looks, conduct, gentleness, reliability: you have everything. He means to ask Grandmother for you and take you into his rooms. You’re not like a girl newly bought outside. As soon as you enter, you’ll have your bridal face-threading, and he’ll acknowledge you as his concubine. Think of the honour and respect!

‘And you’re a girl who wants to do well. As the saying goes, “Gold must be paid for with gold.” Who would have thought the master would set such store by you? Now all those high ambitions of yours can be fulfilled, and you’ll stop the mouths of the people who dislike you. Come with me and tell Grandmother!’

She took Faithful’s hand to lead her away. Faithful blushed, pulled her hand away and would not go. Taking this for shyness, Lady Xing said:

‘What is there to be ashamed of? You needn’t say a word. Just come with me.’

Faithful merely kept her head down and did not move. Seeing this, Lady Xing tried again.

‘Surely you don’t mean you’re unwilling? If you really are, you’re a foolish girl indeed. You could be a mistress, and you’d rather be a maid! In two or three years you’ll only be paired off with a serving lad and still be a slave.

‘Come over to us. You know what a good temper I have. I’m not the sort who can’t bear another woman about. And the master treats you girls so well. In half a year or a year, once you’ve borne a boy or a girl, you’ll stand beside me as an equal. Whoever you want to order about in the house, will any of them dare not stir? Here’s your chance to be a mistress, ready-made. Miss it, and it’ll be too late for regrets.’

Faithful kept her head down, still without speaking.

‘You’re usually so quick and forthright,’ Lady Xing went on. ‘Why all this hanging back? If there’s anything you’re not happy about, tell me. I’ll see that everything is just as you wish.’

Still Faithful said nothing. Lady Xing smiled again.

‘It must be that you have your father and mother, and you’re too shy to speak for yourself. You’re waiting for them to ask you. That’s reasonable enough. I’ll go and speak to them and have them ask you. Whatever you want to say, you can tell them.’

With that she went to Xi-feng’s rooms.

Xi-feng had long since changed. Finding herself alone with Patience, she had told her what was afoot. Patience too shook her head and smiled.

‘I’m not sure it will come off. From the things she says when we talk in private, I don’t think she’s likely to agree. But we can only wait and see.’

‘Her Ladyship is bound to come here to talk it over,’ said Xi-feng. ‘If the girl agrees, well and good. If she doesn’t, Her Ladyship will have made a fool of herself for nothing, and how will she like that in front of all of you? Tell them to fry the quails, add a few other dishes from whatever there is, and get dinner ready. Then go off somewhere for a stroll. Come back when you think she’s gone.’

Patience passed on these instructions to the older women and went leisurely into the garden, free to do as she pleased.

Meanwhile, Faithful saw Lady Xing leave. She must have gone to consult Xi-feng, and someone was bound to come and question her. Better to get out of the way. She found Amber.

‘If Her Old Ladyship asks for me, say I’m not well and haven’t had any breakfast. I’m going for a walk in the garden and shall be back soon.’

Amber agreed. Faithful too went into the garden and wandered from place to place until she happened to meet Patience. Seeing there was no one else about, Patience smiled.

‘Here comes the new concubine!’

Faithful blushed.

‘So you’ve all joined forces to plot against me! Just wait till I have it out with your mistress.’

Patience regretted her slip. She led Faithful under a maple tree, sat with her on a rock and told her outright the whole story, everything that had been said and done from Xi-feng’s going over to her coming back. Her face still red, Faithful gave a cold laugh.

‘We’ve always been friends, you and I. Think of Aroma, Amber, Su-yun, Nightingale, Cai-xia, Silver, Musk and Ebony; Kingfisher, who went away with Miss Shi; Ke-ren and Golden, who are dead; Snowpink, who left. With you and me, that makes a dozen or more. Ever since we were little, what was there we didn’t tell each other?

‘What was there we didn’t do together? Now we’re grown, we’ve all gone our separate ways to our separate duties, but in my heart it’s just the same. If I have something to say or something happens, I don’t keep it from you. I’m telling you this in confidence, so don’t tell Mrs Lian yet. Never mind the Elder Master wanting me for a concubine: even if Her Ladyship died this minute and he sent three go-betweens and all six marriage gifts to make me his lawful wife, I still wouldn’t go.’

Patience was about to laugh and reply when laughter rang out behind the rockery.

‘What a shameless girl! How can you say such things without your teeth setting on edge?’

Startled, the two of them jumped up and looked behind the rocks. It was none other than Aroma, who came out smiling.

‘What’s happened? Tell me.’

All three sat down on the rock, and Patience told Aroma what had just passed.

‘Really, we’ve no business saying it, but that Elder Master is too lustful. Let a girl be even passably good-looking, and he won’t leave her alone.’

Then Patience said:

‘If you don’t want to go, I can show you a way to settle it without any trouble.’

‘What way?’ asked Faithful. ‘Let me hear it.’

‘Just tell Her Old Ladyship you’ve already been given to Master Lian,’ Patience said with a smile. ‘Then the Elder Master won’t be able to ask for you.’

Faithful spat.

‘You wretch! How can you say that? Wasn’t your mistress talking that same nonsense the other day?

‘And now look how it’s come true!’

‘Neither of those two wants it,’ Aroma said, smiling, ‘so I’ll tell Her Old Ladyship to say she’s already promised you to Bao-yu. That’ll make the Elder Master give it up too.’

Faithful was angry, embarrassed and desperate all at once.

‘You two little bitches! May you both come to a bad end! Here I am in trouble, thinking you’re serious people, telling you about it so you can help me out, and you take turns making fun of me. You think your own futures are all settled, and you’ll both be concubines one day.

‘Well, I don’t believe everything in this world necessarily goes just as we wish. Restrain yourselves a little. Don’t be too pleased with yourselves!’

Seeing how upset she was, they hastily smiled and pleaded with her.

‘Dear sister, don’t take it to heart. We’ve been like real sisters since we were little. It was only a joke, with no one else about. Tell us what you mean to do, so we can stop worrying.’

‘What do I mean to do? I simply won’t go, and that’s all.’

Patience shook her head.

‘You may not want to go, but that doesn’t mean he’ll let the matter drop. You know his temper. You belong to Her Old Ladyship’s rooms, so he daren’t do anything to you now, but can you stay with her for ever? You’ll have to leave one day.

‘If you fall into his hands then, it’ll be worse.’

Faithful gave a cold laugh.

‘As long as Her Old Ladyship lives, I won’t leave her. If she dies, he’ll have three years’ mourning to observe, whatever else happens. A man can’t start taking concubines the moment his mother dies! Who knows what things will be like in three years? I’ll think about it then. Even if I’m driven into the last corner, I can cut off my hair and become a nun. Failing that, there’s still death. What if I never marry a man as long as I live? So much the cleaner!’

‘The little minx really has no shame!’ Patience and Aroma said, laughing. ‘Now she’ll say anything that comes into her head.’

‘Things have come to this,’ said Faithful. ‘What does it matter if I’m shameless for a moment? If you don’t believe me, wait and see. Her Ladyship just said she was going to find my father and mother. Let her go and look in Nanjing!’

‘Your parents are both in Nanjing looking after the house,’ said Patience. ‘They haven’t come up here, but she can find them in the end. And you still have your brother and sister-in-law here. It’s a pity you were born into service in this household, instead of being here on your own like the two of us.’

‘What if I was born here? “If an ox won’t drink, must you force its head down?” If I don’t agree, is he going to kill my father and mother?’

As they spoke, they saw Faithful’s sister-in-law coming towards them.

‘She couldn’t find your parents straight away,’ said Aroma, ‘so she must have spoken to your sister-in-law.’

‘That whore is a camel-dealer through nine kingdoms,’ said Faithful. ‘Could she hear of this without running to curry favour?’

Before the words were out, her sister-in-law was standing before them.

‘Nowhere I haven’t looked, and here you are, miss!’ she said, smiling. ‘Come with me. I want a word with you.’

Patience and Aroma hurriedly offered her a seat.

‘Please stay where you are, girls. I only came to have a word with our young lady.’

Aroma and Patience pretended to know nothing.

‘What’s so urgent?’ they asked with smiles. ‘We’re guessing riddles, with slaps as forfeits. Let us finish this one before she goes.’

‘What is it?’ Faithful asked. ‘Say it here.’

‘Come with me and I’ll tell you over there,’ her sister-in-law said, smiling. ‘They’re fine words, anyway.’

‘Is it what the Elder Lady told you?’

‘If you know already, miss, why make it difficult for me?

‘Come along. Let me tell you all about it. It’s the happiest news in the world!’

At this Faithful sprang up, spat as hard as she could straight in her sister-in-law’s face and pointed at her.

‘Clamp that cunt of a mouth shut and get out of here! That would be a sight better!

‘“Fine words”, indeed! Emperor Huizong’s hawks and Zhao Zi-ang’s horses: those are fine pictures. “Happy news”! First-rate pox with the pustules full of pus: that’s happy news too.2 No wonder you’re always envying families whose girls have become concubines, and who all use her position to throw their weight about. The whole family turns concubine! You’ve watched till your eyes are red with envy, and now you want to shove me into the firepit too. If I win favour, you’ll swagger about outside and promote yourselves to “Her Ladyship’s brother” without being asked. If I lose favour and come to grief, you’ll pull your filthy turtle necks into your shells and leave me to live or die as I can.’

She was weeping as she spoke, and Patience and Aroma held her back and tried to soothe her. Her sister-in-law could not bear the humiliation.

‘Whether you want it or not, you can say so decently. There’s no call to drag everyone else into it. As they say, “Don’t talk of shortness in front of a dwarf.”

‘You may abuse me, my lady, and I daren’t answer. But these two girls have done nothing to you. With “concubines” this and “concubines” that, how are they to hold their heads up?’

‘Don’t you say that,’ Aroma and Patience said at once. ‘She wasn’t talking about us. You’re the one dragging other people into it. Which of the masters or mistresses have you heard acknowledge us as concubines?

‘And neither of us has a mother or father or brothers in this household using us to throw their weight about. She knows who she’s abusing. We have no reason to take it to heart.’

‘She’s ashamed because I’ve cursed her,’ said Faithful. ‘She’s nothing to cover her face with, so now she’s trying to set you two against me. Luckily you both have some sense. I was so angry I didn’t make distinctions, and she seized the opening.’

Her sister-in-law knew she had made herself unwelcome and went off in a huff.

Faithful was so angry that she went on cursing, and it took Patience and Aroma some time to quieten her. Then Patience asked Aroma:

‘What were you hiding there for? We never saw you.’

‘I went to Fourth Miss’s rooms to look for our Master Bao,’ said Aroma. ‘But I was just too late. They said he’d gone home. I wondered why I hadn’t met him, and thought I’d try Miss Lin’s. Then I met one of her people, who said he hadn’t been there either. I was wondering whether he’d left the garden when you came along. I ducked out of sight, and you never noticed me. Then she came too. I slipped from behind the tree to the back of the rockery, and I saw you two come over to talk. Who would have thought your four eyes could miss me?’

She had scarcely finished when someone laughed behind them.

‘Their four eyes missed you? Your six eyes all missed me!’

All three jumped and turned round. It was none other than Bao-yu, coming towards them. Aroma was the first to laugh.

‘You’ve given me a fine search! Where have you been?’

‘I was coming out of Fourth Sister’s when I saw you ahead,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘I knew you were looking for me, so I hid to tease you. I watched you go past with your head down, go into the courtyard and come out again, asking everyone you met. I was laughing away, waiting for you to come close enough for me to give you a fright. Then I saw you skulking about too and knew you were going to play the same trick on someone else. I peeped round to see who it was, and there were these two, so I circled round behind you. When you came out, I took your hiding-place.’

‘Let’s look behind us again,’ Patience said, laughing. ‘Who knows? We might find two more.’

‘There are no more this time,’ Bao-yu said with a laugh.

Faithful knew by now that Bao-yu had heard everything. She simply lay forward over the rock and pretended to be asleep.

Bao-yu nudged her, smiling.

‘This rock is cold. Wouldn’t it be better to go back to the rooms and sleep?’

He drew her up as he spoke, and quickly invited Patience to come home with him for tea. Patience and Aroma both urged Faithful to come, and at last she rose. All four went to the House of Green Delights. Having heard everything they had said, Bao-yu was naturally unhappy. He lay silently on his bed and left the three of them to talk and laugh in the outer room.

Meanwhile, Lady Xing had asked Xi-feng about Faithful’s parents.

‘Her father is called Jin Cai,’ Xi-feng told her. ‘He and his wife both look after the house in Nanjing. They hardly ever come up to the capital. Her brother, Jin Wen-xiang, is the buyer for Grandmother’s rooms, and her sister-in-law is in charge of Grandmother’s laundry.’

Lady Xing sent for Jin Wen-xiang’s wife and explained everything carefully. Naturally Mrs Jin was delighted. She set off eagerly to find Faithful, expecting everything to be settled as soon as she spoke. Instead, Faithful had given her a tongue-lashing, and Aroma and Patience had added a few words of their own. She came back angry and humiliated.

‘It’s no use,’ she told Lady Xing. ‘She gave me a round of abuse.’

With Xi-feng there, she dared not mention Patience, but said only:

‘Aroma helped her attack me too. They said all sorts of ungrateful things I couldn’t repeat to Your Ladyship. You and the master had better buy someone else.

‘The little bitch plainly isn’t destined for such good fortune, and neither are we.’

‘What has it to do with Aroma?’ asked Lady Xing. ‘How did they know about it?’ Then she asked, ‘Who else was there?’

‘Miss Patience,’ Mrs Jin replied.

‘Why didn’t you slap her face and send her back?’ Xi-feng broke in. ‘The moment I leave the house, off she goes wandering! I come home and can’t find so much as her shadow.

‘She must have helped them say something too!’

‘Miss Patience wasn’t actually there,’ said Mrs Jin. ‘I thought I saw her at a distance, but I couldn’t be sure. It was only a guess.’

Xi-feng sent someone to fetch her.

‘Go and drag her here this minute. Tell her I’m home and Her Ladyship is here too. Ask her to come and lend a hand!’

Felicity hurried forward.

‘Miss Lin sent people over with written invitations three or four times before she went, ma’am. I went to call her as soon as you came in, but Miss Lin said, “Tell your mistress I need her for something.”’

Only then did Xi-feng let it drop, though she still made a show of saying:

‘Every day she needs her! What can there be to do?’

Lady Xing could think of no further way. After dinner she went home, and that evening told Jia She what had happened. He thought for a moment and immediately summoned Jia Lian.

‘There are other people looking after the house in Nanjing. It isn’t just that one family. Have Jin Cai brought up here at once.’

‘The last letter from Nanjing said Jin Cai had phlegm clouding his mind,’ Jia Lian replied. ‘They’d even given him money for a coffin. I don’t know whether he’s dead or alive by now. Even if he’s alive, he’s lost all awareness, so bringing him here would be no use. His wife is deaf too.’

Jia She roared at him and cursed.

‘You vile, low-bred bastard! You know everything, don’t you? Get out of here!’

Frightened, Jia Lian withdrew. Presently Jia She sent for Jin Wen-xiang. Jia Lian remained in attendance in the outer study, afraid either to go home or to face his father. He could only wait and listen. Before long Jin Wen-xiang arrived. The pages took him straight through the inner gate, and it was not until enough time had passed for five or six meals that he came out and left.

For the moment Jia Lian dared not ask what had happened. After a while he learnt that Jia She had gone to sleep, and only then came home. Not until Xi-feng told him that evening did he understand.

Faithful did not sleep all night. Next day her brother asked Grandmother Jia for permission to take her home for a visit. Grandmother Jia agreed and told her to go. Faithful wanted to refuse, but feared making her mistress suspicious, and reluctantly went out. Her brother had no choice but to repeat Jia She’s words, promising how honoured she would be and how she would manage affairs as a concubine. Faithful set her teeth and would not consent. Unable to do anything with her, he had to report back to Jia She, who flew into a rage.

‘I’ll tell you what to do. Have your wife tell her this, in my very words: “Since ancient times the moon goddess has loved a young man.” She must think I’m too old. I suppose she’s hankering after the young masters, most likely Bao-yu, though Lian may come into it too. If that’s what she has in mind, tell her to give up the idea now. Once she’s refused to come to me, who else will dare take her?

‘That’s one thing. The other is that she thinks, because the old lady’s fond of her, she’ll be married outside one day as someone’s proper wife. Tell her to think carefully. Whoever she marries, she won’t easily get out of my clutches. Unless she dies, or never marries a man all her life, I won’t admit defeat. Otherwise, she’d better change her mind now. She’ll find there are plenty of advantages.’

To every sentence Jin Wen-xiang answered, ‘Yes, sir.’

‘Don’t try to deceive me,’ Jia She said. ‘Tomorrow I’ll send your mistress over again to question Faithful. If you’ve told her and she refuses, you won’t be to blame. But if she agrees when she’s asked, look out for your head!’

Jin Wen-xiang hastily agreed over and over again and went home. He did not even wait to tell his wife and have her pass it on, but delivered the message to Faithful face to face. She was so angry that she could find nothing to say. After thinking for a moment, she said:

‘Even if I were willing to go, you’d have to take me to tell Her Old Ladyship first.’

Her brother and sister-in-law supposed she had thought better of it and were overjoyed. Her sister-in-law took her straight up to see Grandmother Jia.

As it happened, Lady Wang, Aunt Xue, Li Wan, Xi-feng, Bao-chai and the other girls were all with Grandmother Jia, along with several of the married women of standing who supervised work in the outer household. They were keeping her entertained. Faithful was delighted beyond measure. She pulled her sister-in-law with her and knelt before Grandmother Jia. Weeping as she spoke, she told how Lady Xing had come to her, what her sister-in-law had said in the garden, and what her brother had said that day.

‘Because I wouldn’t agree, the Elder Master went so far as to say I was in love with Bao-yu, or else waiting to be married outside. Even if I went up to heaven, I’d never get out of his clutches all my life. He’d have his revenge in the end. Well, my mind is made up. Here, before everyone, I say that all my life, never mind “Bao-yu”, even if it were “Bao-jin” or “Bao-yin”, “Bao the Heavenly King” or “Bao the Emperor”, I will not marry, whatever happens.3 Even if Your Old Ladyship forced me, I’d cut my own throat rather than obey! If I’m fortunate, I’ll die before you. If I’m not, if I’m destined to beg my bread, once I’ve served you to the end of your life, I still won’t go with my parents or my brother. I’ll either seek death or cut off my hair and become a nun.

‘If I’m not speaking from my heart, if I’m only putting you off with words until I can contrive something else, let Heaven and Earth, ghosts and spirits, the sun and the moon look into my throat! Let boils grow out of it and rot until I melt into a puddle of putrid paste here before you!’

She had slipped a pair of scissors into her sleeve before coming in. As she spoke, she loosened her hair with her left hand and cut into it with her right. The women and maids rushed to hold her back, but she had already cut off half a lock. They looked: fortunately she had so much hair that the scissors had not gone right through it. They hurriedly pinned it up for her again.

Grandmother Jia was trembling all over with rage. She could only repeat:

‘She’s the one dependable person I have left, and now they want to scheme her away from me too!’

Seeing Lady Wang beside her, she turned on her.

‘So you’ve all been deceiving me! So dutiful on the outside, and plotting against me in secret. If I have anything nice, you want it; if I have anyone good, you want her too. I’ve only this one little maid left. Naturally, when you see how well I treat her, you can’t bear it. Get her away, and then you can do what you like with me!’

Lady Wang hastily stood up, not daring to answer a word.

Seeing that Lady Wang too had come under blame, Aunt Xue found it awkward to intervene. At the first words of Faithful’s story, Li Wan had already taken the girls outside. Tan-chun was thoughtful. She considered that, unjustly treated as Lady Wang was, she could not dare defend herself. Aunt Xue was her own sister, so naturally she could not speak for her either. It would be awkward for Bao-chai to defend her aunt; Li Wan, Xi-feng and Bao-yu would none of them dare. This was just the moment when one of the girls was needed. Ying-chun was too meek, Xi-chun too young. So Tan-chun listened for a while outside the window, then went in and spoke to Grandmother Jia with a conciliatory smile.

‘What has this to do with Mother, Grandmother? Think about it. If an elder brother wants to take someone into his rooms, how is his younger brother’s wife to know? Even if she does know, she must pretend she doesn’t.’

Before she had quite finished, Grandmother Jia smiled.

‘Of course! I really am old and muddle-headed. Don’t laugh at me, Aunt Xue. Your sister is most dutiful to me, not like my elder daughter-in-law, who thinks of nothing but being afraid of her husband and only goes through the motions with her mother-in-law. I have treated her unfairly.’

Aunt Xue merely answered, ‘Yes,’ then added:

‘It’s not unheard of for an old lady to be partial to her younger son’s wife.’

‘I’m not partial!’ said Grandmother Jia. Then she went on, ‘Bao-yu, I blamed your mother unjustly. Why didn’t you remind me? Why stand there watching her suffer?’

‘Was I to take Mother’s side against Uncle and Aunt?’ Bao-yu asked with a smile. ‘There was only one lot of blame to go round. If Mother was here and wouldn’t take it, who was she to pass it on to? I’d have said it was my fault, but you wouldn’t have believed me.’

‘There’s sense in that too,’ Grandmother Jia said, laughing. ‘Go and kneel to your mother. Tell her, “Please don’t feel hurt, Mother. Grandmother is getting old. Bear with her for Bao-yu’s sake.”’

Bao-yu hurried over and knelt, about to speak, but Lady Wang quickly drew him up, smiling.

‘Get up, get up! You certainly mustn’t. Surely you aren’t going to apologise to me for your grandmother!’

Bao-yu rose at once. Grandmother Jia smiled again.

‘Xi-feng didn’t remind me either.’

‘Here I am, not blaming you,’ Xi-feng said, laughing, ‘and you turn round and find fault with me?’

Grandmother Jia and all the others laughed.

‘Now that is strange! Let’s hear what I’ve done wrong.’

‘Who told you to bring people up so well?’ said Xi-feng. ‘You make them as fresh and tender as young spring onions. How can you blame anyone for wanting them? Luckily I’m your granddaughter-in-law. If I were your grandson, I’d have asked for her long ago. Would I have waited till now?’

‘So it’s my fault, is it?’ Grandmother Jia asked, laughing.

‘Of course it’s your fault, Grandmother.’

‘Very well, I don’t want her either. Take her away with you!’

‘Wait till I’ve earned enough merit in this life to be born a man in the next,’ Xi-feng said. ‘Then I’ll ask for her.’

‘Take her and put her in Lian’s rooms,’ Grandmother Jia said, laughing. ‘See whether that shameless father-in-law of yours still wants her then!’

‘Lian isn’t good enough for her,’ said Xi-feng. ‘He’s only fit to muddle along with Patience and me, a pair of burnt rolls.’

Everyone laughed. Then a maid announced:

‘The Elder Lady is here.’

Lady Wang hurried out to meet her.
