Chapter 26 Words contrived at Wasp-waist Bridge convey a secret longing; Spring drowsiness at Naiad’s House gives voice to hidden feelings

Chapter end

After thirty-three days of convalescence, Bao-yu was strong again, and even the scars on his face had healed smooth. He returned to Prospect Garden, of which we need say no more. Now, during his illness Jia Yun had brought the household pages to keep watch, remaining there day and night. Crimson and the other maids were also attending Bao-yu, and after seeing one another for so many days they had gradually grown familiar. Crimson noticed a handkerchief in Jia Yun’s hand which looked very like the one she had lost. She wanted to ask him about it, but could hardly bring herself to do so. Then the monk and the Taoist unexpectedly arrived; none of the men was needed any longer, and Jia Yun went back to his tree-planting. She tried to put the matter out of her mind, but could not. She wanted to go and ask, yet feared that people would grow suspicious. While she was wavering, unable to settle to anything, a voice suddenly called outside the window.

‘Sister, are you in?’

Crimson looked out through a hole in the window-paper. It was Melilot, a little maid from the same courtyard.

‘I’m here. Come in.’

Melilot ran in, sat down on the bed and said with a smile, ‘What luck I’ve had!

‘I was washing things in the courtyard just now when Master Bao said some tea was to be sent to Miss Lin. Sister Hua gave it to me to take. Money had just come from Her Old Ladyship for Miss Lin, and she was sharing it out among her maids. When she saw me, she scooped up two handfuls for me too. I don’t know how much there is.

‘Will you keep it for me?’

She opened her handkerchief and poured out the money. Crimson counted it carefully, five by five and ten by ten, and put it away for her.

‘How have you really been feeling lately?’ Melilot asked. ‘If you ask me, you ought to go home for a couple of days and have a doctor see you. A couple of doses of medicine would soon put you right.’

‘What nonsense! There’s nothing wrong with me. Why should I go home?’

‘I’ve thought of something. Miss Lin is delicate and always taking medicine. Ask her for some. It would do just as well.’

‘Don’t be silly! You can’t take any medicine that happens to come along.’

‘But you can’t go on like this, hardly bothering to eat or drink. What will become of you?’

‘What does it matter? Better to die soon and have done with it!’

‘Why say things like that when there’s nothing the matter?’

‘What do you know of what’s on my mind?’ said Crimson.

Melilot nodded and thought for a moment.

‘Well, I can’t blame you. It’s hard to find a footing here. Take yesterday. Her Old Ladyship said everyone who’d looked after Master Bao through all those days of illness had worked hard. Now that he was well and the vows had all been fulfilled at the different temples, she wanted the people attending him rewarded according to their grades. They count us as too young to qualify, and I don’t complain of that. But why shouldn’t you be included? That’s what I can’t stomach. If Aroma got ten shares, I wouldn’t grudge her a thing. She deserves them. In all conscience, who would dare compare herself with her? Quite apart from all the care and trouble she takes, we couldn’t compete with her even if she didn’t. What makes me angry is Skybright, Mackerel and that lot, all reckoned among the best. They trade on their parents’ standing, and everyone makes up to them. Don’t you think it’s maddening?’

‘They aren’t worth getting angry about,’ said Crimson.

‘As the saying goes, “Though you build an awning a thousand li long, every feast must end.” Who stays with anyone all their life? Another three or five years, and we shall all be off about our own affairs. Who will care about anyone else then?’

These few words touched Melilot in spite of herself. Her eyes reddened, but she was ashamed to burst into tears for no apparent reason and forced a smile.

‘What you say is true. Only yesterday Master Bao was talking about how he would do up his rooms and have his clothes made, as though there were hundreds of years of fretting ahead of him.’

Crimson gave a couple of cold little laughs and was about to speak when a little maid, whose hair had not yet been allowed to grow, came in carrying embroidery patterns and two sheets of paper.

‘Here are two patterns for you to trace.’

She flung them down beside Crimson, turned and ran.

‘Whose are they?’ Crimson called after her. ‘Can’t you wait to finish speaking before you run off? Is someone keeping steamed buns for you? Are you afraid they’ll get cold?’

‘Sister Mackerel’s!’ came the reply from outside the window, followed by a thudding of running feet.

Crimson threw the patterns aside in a temper and searched the drawer for a brush. After a long hunt she had found nothing but worn-out ones.

‘Where did I put that new brush the other day? Why can’t I remember?’

She fell into a reverie as she spoke, then after a moment smiled.

‘Of course. Oriole took it the other evening.’

‘Go and fetch it for me,’ she told Melilot.

‘Sister Hua’s waiting for me to help her lift a chest. You’ll have to go yourself.’

‘She’s waiting for you, and here you sit chattering? If I hadn’t asked you to fetch the brush, she wouldn’t be waiting for you at all, I suppose. You little wretch!’

She went out herself, left the House of Green Delights and headed straight for Bao-chai’s courtyard. Near the Drenched Blossoms Pavilion she saw Bao-yu’s nurse, Nurse Li, coming towards her. Crimson stopped and asked with a smile, ‘Where have you been, Granny Li? What brings you this way?’

Nurse Li stopped and clapped her hands.

‘Just think! Now he’s taken a fancy to that tree-planting Cloud or Rain or whatever the boy’s called, and he’s made me go and fetch him. If they hear of it up at the main house, there’ll be trouble again.’

‘And you really did as he asked and went to fetch him?’ Crimson asked, smiling.

‘What else could I do?’

‘If the young man knows what’s good for him, he’ll say he can’t come in,’ Crimson said with a smile.

‘He’s no fool. Why shouldn’t he come?’

‘Well, if he is coming, you ought to have brought him with you. It won’t do to have him wandering about on his own, stumbling in anywhere.’

‘Have I the time to walk about with him? I’ve told him, and now I’ll send a little maid or one of the old women to bring him in. That will do.’

With that Nurse Li went on her way, leaning on her stick. Crimson stood lost in thought and made no move to fetch the brush. Presently a little maid came running up.

‘What are you doing here, Sister Lin?’ she asked, seeing Crimson standing there.

Crimson looked up and recognised Zhui-er.

‘Where are you off to?’

‘They’ve told me to bring Master Yun in.’

She ran on. Crimson had just reached the gateway by Wasp-waist Bridge when she saw Zhui-er leading Jia Yun towards her. As he walked, Jia Yun let his eyes sweep over Crimson. Crimson pretended to be talking to Zhui-er, but she too stole a glance at Jia Yun. Their eyes met. Crimson blushed involuntarily, turned away and went towards Allspice Court.

We shall leave her there. Jia Yun followed Zhui-er by a winding route to the House of Green Delights. She went in first to announce him, then came back to lead him inside. Looking about the courtyard, Jia Yun saw a few ornamental rocks and some plantains; over there, beneath a pine, two cranes were preening their feathers. Cages of every kind hung along a covered walk, filled with rare and beautiful birds.

At the far end stood a small projecting building of five rooms, its screens all carved in fresh designs. Above them hung a tablet inscribed in four large characters, ‘Delight in Red and Joy in Green’.

‘So that is why they call it the House of Green Delights,’ Jia Yun thought. ‘Those are the words on the tablet.’

Just then a laughing voice came through the gauze window.

‘Come in, come in! How could I have forgotten you for two or three months?’

Recognising Bao-yu’s voice, Jia Yun hurried into the room. He looked up to find gold and colour shining everywhere, patterns and inscriptions dazzling his eyes, but no sign of Bao-yu. Turning his head, he saw a large full-length mirror to his left. Two maids of about the same age, fifteen or sixteen, came out from behind it.

‘Please take a seat in the inner room, Master Yun.’

Not daring even to look them straight in the face, Jia Yun hastily assented. He passed through another green-gauze screen. There was a small bed decorated with filled-in lacquer, hung with scarlet curtains patterned with scattered flowers in gold. Bao-yu, in his everyday clothes with his slippers loosely on, was reclining on the bed with a book. At the sight of Jia Yun he tossed it aside and rose, all smiles.

Jia Yun hurried forward to pay his respects. Bao-yu invited him to sit, and he took a chair lower down the room.

‘When I saw you that month, I told you to come to the study,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘Then one thing happened after another, and I forgot all about you.’

‘It was only my bad luck,’ Jia Yun replied, smiling. ‘And then you fell ill, Uncle. Are you quite well again now?’

‘Quite well. I hear you worked hard for several days.’

‘It was no more than my duty. Your recovery is a blessing for our whole family.’

As they spoke, a maid brought him tea. Jia Yun went on talking to Bao-yu, but glanced sideways at her: a slender figure, a longish face, a silvery-pink jacket, a dark satin waistcoat and a finely pleated skirt of white damask. It was none other than Aroma. During Bao-yu’s illness, Jia Yun had spent two days in the inner quarters and learnt the names and faces of half the people of consequence there.

He also knew that Aroma’s position in Bao-yu’s rooms was different from the others’. Seeing her bring him tea, with Bao-yu sitting beside him, he rose hastily, smiling.

‘Sister, why should you pour tea for me? I’m no guest here in my uncle’s rooms. Let me pour it myself.’

‘Just sit down,’ Bao-yu said. ‘Must you stand on ceremony even with the maids?’

‘You may say so, Uncle, but how could I take liberties with the young ladies who serve you?’

As he spoke he sat down to drink his tea.

Bao-yu chatted with him about things of no importance: whose actors were good, whose garden was fine, who had pretty maids, who gave sumptuous dinners, who owned rare curiosities and who had unusual treasures. Jia Yun could only follow his lead. After a time, seeing that Bao-yu was growing listless, he rose to take his leave. Bao-yu made no great effort to keep him, merely saying, ‘Come whenever you have time.’ He told the little maid Zhui-er to show him out again.

Once they had left the House of Green Delights, Jia Yun looked about and saw no one near. He began to slacken his pace, drawing Zhui-er into conversation with one question after another.

‘How old are you? What’s your name? What work do your parents do? How many years have you been in Uncle Bao’s rooms? How much do you get a month? How many girls are there in his rooms altogether?’

Zhui-er answered each question in turn.

‘That girl you were speaking to just now,’ Jia Yun went on, ‘is she called Crimson?’

‘Yes, she’s called Crimson,’ said Zhui-er with a smile.

‘Why do you ask about her?’

‘She was asking you about a handkerchief just now. I happen to have picked one up.’

Zhui-er smiled.

‘She’s asked me several times whether I’ve seen her handkerchief. As if I had time to bother about such things! Today she asked again, and said she’d give me something if I found it for her. That was just now, at the gate of Allspice Court. You heard her yourself, Master Yun. I’m not making it up. Do give it to me, dear Master Yun, if you’ve found it. I want to see what she gives me in return.’

Now, when Jia Yun had come into the garden to plant trees the previous month, he had picked up a gauze handkerchief. He knew that someone in the garden must have dropped it, but had no idea who, and so had not dared to do anything rash. Hearing Crimson ask Zhui-er about it, he knew at last that it was hers, and felt delighted at his good fortune. With Zhui-er pressing him for it, he quickly formed a plan. He took a handkerchief of his own from his sleeve and said to her with a smile, ‘You can have it. But if she gives you a reward, you mustn’t keep it from me.’

Zhui-er agreed readily, took the handkerchief, escorted Jia Yun out and returned to look for Crimson. Of that, no more for the present.

After sending Jia Yun away, Bao-yu lay back listlessly on the bed and seemed to be drifting off. Aroma came over, sat on the edge of the bed and gave him a push.

‘Going to sleep again? It’s so dull in here. Why don’t you go out for a walk?’

Bao-yu took her hand and smiled.

‘I want to, but I can’t bear to leave you.’

‘Come along, get up!’ she said, smiling, and pulled him to his feet.

‘But where am I to go? Everything is so tiresome.’

‘You’ll feel better once you’re out. Drooping about like this only makes you more bored and restless.’

Still listless, Bao-yu could only do as she said. He drifted out of the room, played with the birds along the covered walk for a while, then went outside the courtyard and watched the goldfish in the Drenched Blossoms stream. Suddenly two young deer came darting down the slope like arrows. Bao-yu was wondering what could be the matter when Jia Lan appeared behind them with a little bow. Seeing Bao-yu ahead of him, he stopped and smiled.

‘You’re at home, Second Uncle! I thought you’d gone out.’

‘Up to mischief again? What do you want to shoot them for? They’ve done nothing to you.’

‘I’m not studying just now. What else is there to do? I’m practising riding and shooting.’

‘When you fall and knock your teeth out, perhaps you’ll stop practising.’

As he spoke, Bao-yu let his feet carry him on until he reached a courtyard gate. The bamboos rose thick as phoenix tails, murmuring softly like dragons. Looking up, he saw the three characters for ‘Naiad’s House’ on the tablet above the gate. He wandered in. Bamboo blinds hung to the ground, and not a human voice disturbed the stillness. At the window he caught a faint fragrance stealing through the green gauze. He put his face close and peered inside. Suddenly a long, soft sigh reached him, followed by the words:

‘“Day after day, thoughts of love leave me drowsy and dazed.”’

Something stirred and tingled in Bao-yu’s heart. Looking again, he saw Dai-yu stretching herself on the bed.

‘Why “Day after day, thoughts of love leave me drowsy and dazed”?’ he asked from outside, laughing. As he spoke, he lifted the door-curtain and came in. Dai-yu realised that she had let herself go. She blushed, hid her face in her sleeve and turned towards the wall, pretending to be asleep.

Bao-yu had just come over to turn her towards him when Dai-yu’s nurse and two old women followed him in.

‘Your cousin is asleep. Please come back when she wakes.’

They had scarcely spoken when Dai-yu turned and sat up.

‘Who’s asleep?’ she asked, smiling.

Seeing her up, the women smiled too.

‘We thought you were asleep, miss.’

Then they called to Nightingale, ‘Your young lady’s awake. Come and attend to her,’ and went away.

Sitting on the bed, Dai-yu raised a hand to smooth the hair at her temples and smiled at Bao-yu.

‘What do you mean by coming in when I’m asleep?’

The sight of her starry eyes still faintly misted with drowsiness and her fragrant cheeks flushed pink had already set his senses reeling. He sank sideways into a chair.

‘What were you saying just now?’ he asked with a smile.

‘I wasn’t saying anything.’

‘You’ll get a flick on the forehead! I heard every word.’

As they were speaking, Nightingale came in.

‘Nightingale, pour me a bowl of your best tea,’ Bao-yu said with a smile.

‘Where would we get good tea? If it’s good tea you want, you’ll have to wait until Aroma comes.’

‘Don’t mind him,’ Dai-yu said. ‘Fetch some water for me first.’

‘He’s a guest,’ Nightingale said, smiling. ‘Naturally I must pour his tea before I fetch your water.’

She went to pour the tea.

‘Good girl,’ Bao-yu said with a smile.

‘“If I shared the lovers’ bed-curtains with your loving mistress, How could I bear to fold the quilt and make the bed?”’

Dai-yu’s face fell at once.

‘Cousin Bao, what did you say?’

‘I didn’t say anything,’ he replied, still smiling.

Dai-yu began to cry.

‘So this is the latest fashion! You hear coarse talk outside and come and repeat it to me. You read filthy books and use them to make fun of me. I’ve become something for you gentlemen to amuse yourselves with.’

Still crying, she got off the bed and made for the door. Bao-yu panicked, not knowing what to do, and hurried after her.

‘Dear cousin, I deserved to die for saying it. Don’t tell on me.

‘If I ever dare do it again, may a boil grow on my mouth and my tongue rot away.’

At that moment Aroma came in.

‘Come back and get dressed at once. The Master wants you.’

Bao-yu felt as though a thunderbolt had burst over his head. He forgot everything else and hurried back to dress. Leaving the garden, he found Tealeaf waiting at the inner gate.

‘What’s it about?’ he asked.

‘Come along, sir. You have to go and see him anyway. You’ll find out when you get there.’

As he spoke, Tealeaf urged Bao-yu on.

They passed the great hall, Bao-yu still full of misgivings. Suddenly a burst of loud laughter came from the corner of a wall. He turned, and Xue Pan jumped out, clapping his hands.

‘If I hadn’t said Uncle wanted you, how could I have got you out so quickly?’

Tealeaf knelt down, laughing too. Bao-yu stood bewildered for some time before he understood that Xue Pan had tricked him into coming out. Xue Pan hurriedly bowed and clasped his hands in apology, begging him not to punish the boy.

‘I made him do it.’

There was nothing Bao-yu could do. He could only smile and ask, ‘Trick me if you like, but why bring my father into it? I shall tell Aunt and let her judge whether that’s a proper thing to do.’

‘My dear cousin, all I wanted was to get you out quickly. I forgot that I shouldn’t mention him. Another day you can trick me and bring my father into it. Then we’ll be even.’

‘Oh, oh, now you’re worse than ever!’ said Bao-yu. Then he turned to Tealeaf. ‘You treacherous little bastard! What are you still kneeling for?’

Tealeaf quickly kowtowed and got up.

‘I wouldn’t have disturbed you for anything less,’ Xue Pan said, ‘but tomorrow, the third of the fifth month, is my birthday. And who should turn up but Cheng Ri-xing from the antiques trade? Heaven knows where he found them, but he brought me fresh lotus roots this thick and this long, floury and crisp, watermelons this big, a fresh sturgeon this long, and a Siamese pig this size, smoked with aromatic cypress, a tribute gift from Siam. Tell me those aren’t four rarities! The fish and the pig are only hard to get because they cost so much. But how he managed to grow lotus roots and melons like that, I can’t imagine. I gave some to Mother at once, and hurried some over to your grandmother and my uncle and aunt. Now I’ve kept a little for myself, but I was afraid of using up my good fortune by eating it all alone. I thought and thought, and apart from me you’re the only person worthy of eating it. So I’ve specially asked you over. And by good luck the boy who sings has just arrived. How about spending the day enjoying ourselves?’

Talking as they went, they came to his study. Zhan Guang, Cheng Ri-xing, Hu Si-lai, Shan Pin-ren and the singer were all there. When Bao-yu entered, some paid their respects and others asked after him, and greetings were exchanged all round.

After tea, Xue Pan ordered the wine brought in. He had scarcely finished speaking before the pages were bustling about in all directions, and only after a good deal of activity was everything ready and everyone seated. Bao-yu found the melons and lotus roots as remarkable as promised.

‘My birthday present hasn’t even reached you, and here I am enjoying your hospitality already,’ he said with a smile.

‘So you are! What are you giving me tomorrow?’

‘What have I to give? Money, food, clothes, any of those things, none of them is really mine. Only a piece of my own calligraphy or a picture I’ve painted would count as something of my own.’

Xue Pan laughed.

‘Speaking of pictures, you’ve reminded me. Yesterday I saw an erotic painting at someone’s house, really beautifully done. There was a lot of writing on it too. I didn’t look closely at that, just at the signature. It was painted by “Geng Huang”. Absolutely marvellous!’

Bao-yu was puzzled. He had seen a fair number of old and new paintings and works of calligraphy, but where had this ‘Geng Huang’ come from? After thinking for some time, he suddenly began to laugh. He asked for a brush and wrote two characters on his palm.

‘Are you sure you read “Geng Huang” correctly?’

‘Of course I did!’

Bao-yu opened his hand for him to see.

‘Could it have been these two characters? They aren’t very different from “Geng Huang”.’

They all looked. The characters were ‘Tang Yin’.

‘Those must have been the characters,’ they said, laughing. ‘Perhaps your eyes were playing tricks on you for a moment.’

Xue Pan felt rather foolish, but laughed it off.

‘Who knows whether he’s “Sugar Silver” or “Fruit Silver”?’1

As they were speaking, a page announced that Mr Feng had arrived. Bao-yu knew this must be Feng Zi-ying, the son of Feng Tang, General of Divine Valour. Xue Pan and the others all called out, ‘Show him in!’ Before they had finished, Feng Zi-ying was already coming in, laughing and talking as he walked. Everyone rose from the table to offer him a seat.

‘Very nice!’ Feng Zi-ying said with a laugh.

‘No more going out, then? Just enjoying yourselves at home!’

Bao-yu and Xue Pan both smiled.

‘We haven’t seen you for ages. Is your honoured father keeping well?’

‘Thanks to your good wishes, my father is well enough. My mother caught a chill recently and has been unwell for a couple of days.’

Noticing some bruises on his face, Xue Pan asked with a laugh, ‘Who have you been fighting this time? You’re sporting a fine signboard!’

‘Ever since I injured Commandant Qiu’s son that time, I’ve made a point of not losing my temper,’ Feng Zi-ying replied, smiling. ‘Why should I be fighting again?

‘This mark on my face came from a hare-hawk catching me with its wing when we were out hunting on Iron-net Mountain the other day.’

‘When was that?’ Bao-yu asked.

‘We left on the twenty-eighth of the third month and only came back the day before yesterday.’

‘No wonder I didn’t see you at Mr Shen’s dinner the other day, on the third or fourth. I meant to ask about you, but somehow forgot. Did you go on your own, or was your honoured father there too?’

‘It was because Father was going that I had no choice but to go. Do you think I’ve gone mad with idleness? Why go looking for hardship when the few of us could be drinking wine and listening to songs? This time there was great good fortune in the midst of great misfortune.’

Seeing that he had finished his tea, Xue Pan and the rest urged him to join them at the table and tell his story at leisure. Feng Zi-ying stood up.

‘By rights I should stay and drink a few cups with you. But there’s something of the greatest importance I have to report to Father in person when I get back. I really can’t accept.’

Neither Xue Pan nor Bao-yu nor anyone else would hear of it. They caught hold of him and would not let him go.

‘This is extraordinary!’ Feng Zi-ying said with a laugh. ‘In all the years we’ve known one another, when have you ever treated me like this? I truly can’t stay. If you insist on my drinking, bring me a large cup and I’ll have two.’

They had to agree. Xue Pan held the wine-pot and Bao-yu the cups, and two large beakers were filled. Feng Zi-ying remained standing and drained them straight off.

‘At least finish telling us about this “good fortune in misfortune” before you go,’ Bao-yu said.

‘There’s no time to do it justice today. I mean to give a party specially, so that I can invite you over and tell you all about it. Besides, I have a favour to ask.’

Taking their hands in farewell, he made for the door.

‘Now you’ve got us all worked up, you can’t just leave it there,’ Xue Pan said. ‘When are you going to invite us? Tell us, and save us wondering.’

‘Ten days at most. Eight at the least.’

As he spoke, Feng Zi-ying went out, mounted his horse and rode away. The others returned to their places, drank a while longer and then dispersed.

When Bao-yu returned to the garden, Aroma, who thought he had gone to see Jia Zheng, was still worrying about what might have happened to him. Seeing him come home tipsy, she asked what had happened, and he told her everything.

‘Here was I waiting with my heart in my mouth, while you were off enjoying yourself. You might at least have sent someone with a message.’

‘I meant to send word. But when Mr Feng came, it went clean out of my head.’

They were still talking when Bao-chai came in with a smile.

‘You’ve been enjoying rare delicacies without us.’

‘They came from your family,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘Naturally you had the first taste.’

Bao-chai shook her head and smiled.

‘My brother did specially invite me to eat some yesterday. I wouldn’t, and told him to keep them for guests and presents. I know my lot in life is too humble, my share of good fortune too small, to deserve such food.’

A maid brought tea, and she drank it while they chatted. We need say no more of that.

Now Dai-yu, having heard that Jia Zheng had summoned Bao-yu and finding him gone all day, had also been anxious on his behalf. After supper, when she heard that he was back, she wanted to go and ask him what had happened. She strolled over, saw Bao-chai enter his courtyard and followed. At the Drenched Blossoms Bridge she came upon all kinds of waterfowl bathing in the pool. She could not tell what they were called, but their brilliant markings were so beautiful that she stopped to watch them for a while. When she went on to the House of Green Delights, she found the courtyard gate shut and knocked.

Unfortunately, Skybright had just quarrelled with Emerald and was in a bad temper. Bao-chai’s arrival had given her someone else to be angry with, and she was grumbling in the courtyard.

‘Whether she has anything to come for or not, she’s always here, sitting on and on, keeping us from our sleep till all hours!’

When she heard another knock at the gate, she grew angrier still. Without asking who it was, she called out, ‘We’ve all gone to bed. Come back tomorrow!’

Dai-yu knew the maids’ ways. They were used to playing together, and she thought perhaps the girl inside had failed to recognise her voice and was refusing to open because she took her for another maid. She therefore raised her voice.

‘It’s me. Aren’t you going to open the gate?’

But Skybright still did not recognise her.

‘I don’t care who you are,’ she snapped. ‘Master Bao’s orders are that no one is to be let in!’

Dai-yu stood outside, stunned with anger. She was about to call out and challenge her, but thought better of provoking a quarrel.

‘They say my aunt’s house is as good as my own, but I am a guest here all the same. Both my parents are dead. I have no one to turn to and depend on their household for a home. If I really lose my temper, I shall only make myself ridiculous.’

At these thoughts her tears began to fall. She could neither bring herself to go away nor bear to stay. While she stood there uncertain, laughter and voices came from within. She listened: they were Bao-yu’s and Bao-chai’s. Angrier than ever, she turned the matter over in her mind until she suddenly remembered what had happened that morning.

‘He must be angry because I threatened to tell on him. But I never did tell! You might have found that out before taking such a grudge against me. You won’t let me in today, but can you avoid seeing me tomorrow?’

The more she thought, the more she grieved. Heedless of the cold dew on the moss and the chill wind along the flower-path, she stood alone in the shadow of the flowers at a corner of the wall and began to sob bitterly.

Now Dai-yu was endowed with beauty beyond compare, with a loveliness seldom seen in this world. When she began to cry, the birds and crows roosting among the willows and blossoms nearby heard her and flew off in a sudden flutter, unable to bear the sound. Truly:

The flower’s soul is silent, sunk in listlessness;

Where are the birds startled from their deep, unknowing dreams?

A poem tells of it:

Rare in this world are Pin-pin’s gifts and grace;

Alone she leaves her embroidered bower, bearing her hidden fragrance;

Before a single sob has run its course,

Flowers lie fallen everywhere, and startled birds take flight.

Dai-yu was still weeping when she suddenly heard a creak. The courtyard gate opened, but who was coming out? To learn who it was, listen to the next chapter.
