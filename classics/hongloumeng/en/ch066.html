Chapter 66 A loving girl, shamed by love, returns to the underworld; Cold Liu the Second grows cold to the world and enters the gate of emptiness

Chapter end

Now Bao Er’s wife gave him a smack, laughing.

‘There was some truth in it to begin with, but you’ve made up this nonsense and let it run completely wild. You don’t sound like one of Master Lian’s men. With rubbish like that, you ought to be over with Bao-yu.’

Er-jie was about to ask another question when San-jie broke in with a smile.

‘What does this Bao-yu of yours do, apart from going to school?’

‘Don’t ask about him, ma’am,’ Joker said, grinning. ‘You might not believe me if I told you.

‘He’s grown to this age, and he’s the only one who’s never been to a proper school. From our ancestors down to Master Lian, who hasn’t spent ten years at his books by a cold window? But he doesn’t like studying. He’s the old lady’s darling. His father used to keep him in order, but now even he daren’t. All day long he’s half out of his wits, saying things nobody understands and doing things nobody can make out. People outside see such a handsome, fine-looking boy and naturally think he must be clever. Who’d guess he’s clear on the outside and muddy within? Put him in front of anyone, and he hasn’t a word to say. The only good thing about him is that, although he’s never been to school, he’s somehow managed to learn a few characters. He studies neither books nor arms, and he’s afraid of meeting people. All he likes is fooling about among the maids. He shows no firmness with us, either. Sometimes, if he sees us when he’s in a good mood, there’s no more high and low: we all tumble about together. If he’s not in the mood, we go our separate ways and he takes no notice. We can stay sitting or lying down when he comes, without so much as acknowledging him, and he doesn’t tell us off. So nobody’s afraid of him. We do as we please, and it all passes well enough.’

‘When your master is easy with you, that’s how you behave,’ San-jie said with a laugh. ‘When he’s strict, you complain.

‘There’s no pleasing you.’

‘We thought he was rather nice,’ said Er-jie. ‘So that’s what he’s really like. Such good looks wasted!’

‘Sister, why believe his nonsense?’ said San-jie. ‘We’ve seen him more than once or twice. There is something girlish about his manners, his talk, the way he eats and drinks, but that’s from always being kept in the women’s quarters. As for being muddle-headed, where does that come in? Don’t you remember when we were all in mourning together? The day the monks came in to walk round the coffin, we were all standing there, and he stood right in front of us, screening us from the others. People said he had no manners and couldn’t see what was wanted. But didn’t he tell us quietly afterwards, “You don’t understand, sisters. It wasn’t that I couldn’t see. I thought the monks were dirty, and I was afraid their smell would reach you.” Then he had some tea. You asked for tea too, and that old woman picked up his cup to pour it into. He stopped her at once: “I’ve drunk out of that and dirtied it. Wash it before you bring it back.” I watched those two little things for myself. With girls, whatever he does is quite all right. He just doesn’t fit other people’s rules, so they don’t understand him.’

Er-jie smiled at this.

‘From what you say, the two of you are already of one heart and mind.

‘Why shouldn’t we simply marry you to him?’

With Joker there, San-jie could not speak freely. She merely lowered her head and nibbled melon seeds.

‘In looks, manners and disposition you’d make a fine pair,’ Joker said, grinning. ‘Only he’s spoken for already, though nothing’s come out yet. It will certainly be Miss Lin in the end. She’s often ill, for one thing, and they’re both still young, for another, so it hasn’t come to that yet. In another two or three years the old lady has only to open her mouth, and nobody will say no.’

They were still talking when Long-er returned.

‘The master’s father has some business, a very important, confidential matter, and wants to send Master Lian to Ping-an Zhou. He’ll be leaving in three to five days. The journey there and back will take half a month. He can’t come today. He asks the old lady and Second Aunt to settle that matter soon, so he can make a decision when he comes tomorrow.’

With that he took Joker away.

Er-jie had the doors shut and went to bed early, but she spent the night questioning her sister. Jia Lian did not arrive until the following afternoon.

‘If you have proper business to attend to, why hurry over here again?’ Er-jie urged him. ‘You mustn’t let me keep you from it.’

‘There’s nothing much,’ said Jia Lian. ‘Only, as luck would have it, another errand has come up that will take me a long way from home. I leave after the end of the month and shan’t be back for half a month.’

‘Then go with an easy mind,’ said Er-jie. ‘You needn’t worry about anything here. My little sister has never been one to change her mind between morning and evening. If she says she’s mended her ways, then she has.

‘She’s chosen her man. All you need do is let her have him.’

Jia Lian asked who it was.

‘He’s not here at present,’ Er-jie said, smiling, ‘and no one knows when he’ll come. She certainly has an eye for a man. She says that if he doesn’t come for a year, she’ll wait a year; if he doesn’t come for ten years, she’ll wait ten. If he’s dead and can never come again, she’ll willingly shave her head and become a nun, eat no meat and say her prayers for the rest of her life.’

‘Who on earth is it, to have taken such a hold on her?’

‘It’s a long story,’ she said with a smile. ‘Five years ago our grandmother was having a birthday celebration. Mother took us to her house to wish her many happy returns. They’d invited a group of amateur players, and one of them, playing a young male lead, was called Liu Xiang-lian. San-jie took a fancy to him. Now she won’t marry anyone else. We heard last year that he’d got into some trouble and run away. I wonder whether he’s come back?’

‘That explains it!’ said Jia Lian. ‘I was wondering what sort of man it could be. So it’s him!

‘She certainly has a good eye. You don’t know Liu the Second. Handsome as he is, he’s the coldest creature, in face and heart alike. For most people he has neither affection nor regard. Bao-yu is the one he gets on with best. Last year, after beating up that Xue blockhead, he was ashamed to face us and went off somewhere for a time. I did hear later that he was back, but I don’t know whether it’s true. We have only to ask Bao-yu’s pages to find out. But suppose he hasn’t come back? He drifts about like duckweed. Who knows how many years it’ll be before he returns? Won’t she be wasting her time?’

‘This sister of ours does what she says,’ said Er-jie. ‘Whatever she asks, you had better agree to it.’

While they were talking, San-jie came in.

‘Don’t worry, brother-in-law. I’m not one to say one thing and mean another. What I say, I mean. If this Liu comes back, I’ll marry him. From today I’ll eat no meat and say my prayers, and do nothing but look after Mother until he comes and I can marry him. If he doesn’t come for a hundred years, I’ll devote myself to the religious life.’

She took a jade hairpin and struck it into two pieces.

‘If a single word is false, let me end like this hairpin!’

She went back to her room. And indeed, from then on she neither did nor said anything improper. There was nothing Jia Lian could do. He discussed the household affairs with Er-jie for a while, then went home to consult Xi-feng about his departure. He also sent someone to ask Tealeaf for news.

‘I really don’t know,’ Tealeaf said. ‘I don’t think he’s back. I’d certainly know if he were.’

They asked Xiang-lian’s neighbours too, but they also said he had not returned. Jia Lian could only report this to Er-jie. As his departure drew near, he announced that he was leaving two days before he actually meant to go, and spent those two nights at Er-jie’s before quietly setting off on his journey from there. The younger sister had indeed become another person. Seeing, too, how diligently and carefully Er-jie managed the household, he had no cause for anxiety.

He left the city early that morning and took the highway to Ping-an Zhou, travelling by day and lodging at night, drinking when thirsty and eating when hungry. He had been on the road only three days when he met a train of pack-animals coming towards him. Among them was a party of some ten mounted men, masters and servants together. As they drew nearer, he saw that they were none other than Xue Pan and Liu Xiang-lian. Greatly surprised, he urged his horse forward to meet them. They exchanged greetings and asked how each had fared since they last met, then went into a wineshop to rest and talk.

‘After that quarrel, we were all trying to bring you two together again,’ Jia Lian said, smiling. ‘But Brother Liu had disappeared without a trace. How do you come to be travelling together now?’

‘You wouldn’t think such a thing could happen!’ Xue Pan said with a smile. ‘My partners and I had bought our goods and set off for home in the spring. All went well on the road until the other day, when we reached the borders of Ping-an Zhou and ran into a band of robbers. They’d already made off with our things when Brother Liu came along from the other direction, scattered the robbers and recovered the goods. He saved our lives as well. He wouldn’t accept anything by way of thanks, so we’ve sworn to be brothers in life and death. Now we’re going to the capital together. From now on we’re as close as brothers born. We’ll part at the fork ahead. He has an aunt two hundred li to the south and is going to visit her. I’ll go on to the capital and put my affairs in order, then find him a house and a good wife, so we can all settle down.’

‘So that’s it,’ said Jia Lian. ‘You gave us some anxious days.’

The mention of a wife made him add eagerly:

‘I have just the match for our brother here.’

He told them how he had married You Er-jie and now wanted to find a husband for her younger sister, but said nothing of San-jie’s having chosen Xiang-lian herself.

He also warned Xue Pan not to tell the family yet. They would naturally find out once a son was born. Xue Pan was delighted.

‘You should have done it long ago. It’s all that cousin of mine’s fault.’

‘You’re forgetting yourself again,’ Xiang-lian put in quickly, smiling. ‘Hold your tongue.’

Xue Pan stopped short, then said:

‘Well, in that case we must certainly make this match.’

‘I’d always vowed to marry a woman of exceptional beauty,’ said Xiang-lian. ‘But since you gentlemen are kind enough to take the matter in hand, I won’t be so particular. Decide as you think fit; I shall abide by it.’

‘Words alone prove nothing,’ Jia Lian said with a smile. ‘Once you see her, Brother Liu, you’ll know that my young sister-in-law has no equal, in character or looks, in any age.’

Xiang-lian was delighted.

‘If that’s so, I’ll be in the capital by the middle of the month, once I’ve visited my aunt. Shall we settle it then?’

‘Our word makes it a bargain,’ Jia Lian said, smiling. ‘Only I don’t trust you, Brother Liu. You drift about like duckweed on the waves. If you stay away and never come back, won’t you be keeping the girl waiting for nothing?

‘You’ll have to leave a betrothal pledge.’

‘How could a man of honour break his word?’ said Xiang-lian. ‘I’ve always been poor, and here I am on my travels. What could I offer as a pledge?’

‘I have everything you need,’ said Xue Pan. ‘I’ll put something together for you to take back, Brother Lian.’

‘There’s no need for gold or silk,’ Jia Lian said, smiling. ‘It must be something of Brother Liu’s own. Its value doesn’t matter. I only want something to take back as proof of his good faith.’

‘In that case,’ said Xiang-lian, ‘I have nothing else to offer. This sword is for my defence; I can’t take it off. But in my baggage I have the Mandarin-duck swords, a treasure handed down in my family. I don’t presume to use them as I please. I merely carry them with me for safekeeping. Take them as my pledge, Brother Jia. Though I may drift like running water and fallen flowers, I could never abandon those swords.’

They drank a few more cups, then mounted their horses, took leave of one another and set off. As the saying goes:

The general does not leave his saddle; Each rides on to his own destiny.

In due course Jia Lian reached Ping-an Zhou, saw the military commissioner and completed his official business.

The commissioner told him that he must be sure to come again around the tenth month, and Jia Lian received his orders. Next day he hurried home, going first to see Er-jie.

Since his departure she had managed the household with the greatest care and propriety. The doors were kept shut each day, and nothing was heard of the world outside. Her younger sister had indeed proved a woman of iron resolve. Apart from attending to her mother and sister, she kept quietly to her proper place and lived as her means allowed. At night, alone with her coverlet and pillow, she was unused to the loneliness. Yet she had put all other men out of her mind and thought only of Liu Xiang-lian’s speedy return to marry her.

When Jia Lian came in and saw how things were, his delight knew no bounds, and he felt deeply grateful for Er-jie’s goodness. After the usual enquiries, he told them of his meeting with Xiang-lian on the road, took out the Mandarin-duck swords and handed them to San-jie. She saw dragons with open jaws and guardian kui dragons wrought upon them, among sparkling jewels. She pulled at the hilt: inside were two blades fitted together.

One was engraved with the character for a male mandarin duck, the other with that for a female. Cold and gleaming, they were like two streaks of autumn water. San-jie’s joy exceeded all her hopes. She quickly put them away and hung them by the bed in her chamber. Every day she looked at the swords and smiled to herself: now she had someone to depend on for life.

Jia Lian stayed two days, then returned to report on his father’s commission and see everyone at home.

Xi-feng was now much better and had come out to attend to household business again. Jia Lian told Jia Zhen of the match too. Jia Zhen had lately found a new companion and put the whole affair out of his mind. He was content to leave it to Jia Lian, though he feared the expense might be too much for him to bear alone, and felt obliged to give him another thirty taels of silver. Jia Lian took the money to Er-jie for the trousseau.

It was not until the eighth month that Xiang-lian reached the capital. He went first to pay his respects to Aunt Xue. There he met Xue Ke and learnt that Xue Pan, unused to the hardships of travel and unaccustomed to strange food and water, had fallen ill as soon as he returned home and was under a doctor’s care. Hearing that Xiang-lian had come, Xue Pan asked him into the bedroom. Aunt Xue, too, put the old affair out of her mind and thought only of his recent kindness. Mother and son thanked him most warmly.

They spoke of his marriage as well. Everything needed had been arranged; only the date remained to be chosen. Xiang-lian was deeply grateful.

Next day he went to see Bao-yu. The two were as happy together as fish in water. Xiang-lian asked about Jia Lian’s secret marriage to a secondary wife.

‘I’ve heard Tealeaf and the others talking about it,’ Bao-yu said with a smile, ‘but I haven’t seen anything myself, and I daren’t meddle. Tealeaf also told me Cousin Lian has been asking very earnestly about you. I wonder what he wants to say?’

Xiang-lian told him everything that had happened on the road.

‘Congratulations, congratulations!’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘Such a lovely creature is not easily found. She really is a beauty without equal in any age, a worthy match for you.’

‘If that’s so, there’s no shortage of men about their household,’ said Xiang-lian. ‘Why should they think only of me? Besides, he and I have never been particularly close. He has no reason to take such an interest in me. We were in a hurry on the road, yet he insisted over and over on settling it then and there. Since when did the bride’s family go chasing the bridegroom? I’ve begun to have my doubts. I regret leaving the swords as a pledge.

‘Then I thought of you. I could ask you all about it and get to the bottom of things.’

‘You’re usually so shrewd,’ said Bao-yu. ‘Why give your pledge and only then begin to doubt? You said yourself that all you wanted was an exceptional beauty. Now you’ve got one. Why not be satisfied, instead of raising more doubts?’

‘If you didn’t know about the marriage, how do you know she’s a beauty?’

‘They’re Cousin Zhen’s two young sisters-in-law, the ones his wife’s stepmother brought with her. I spent a month there, mixing with them. How could I not know? A pair of you-wu, real beauties, and You is their surname too.’

Xiang-lian stamped his foot.

‘This is no good! It can’t possibly go ahead. Apart from those two stone lions, I doubt there’s anything clean in your Ning mansion, not even the cats and dogs. I’m not going to be the cuckold left with someone else’s leavings.’

Bao-yu flushed. Ashamed of having spoken so heedlessly, Xiang-lian hastily bowed with clasped hands.

‘I deserve to die for talking such nonsense. Do tell me, though: what is her character like?’

‘You know so much about it, why ask me?’ Bao-yu said with a smile.

‘Perhaps even I’m not clean.’

‘I forgot myself for a moment, that’s all,’ Xiang-lian said, smiling. ‘Please don’t take it to heart.’

‘Why bring it up again?’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘That makes it sound deliberate.’

Xiang-lian bowed and took his leave. He considered going to Xue Pan, but Xue Pan was ill in bed, for one thing, and too hot-headed, for another. Better to ask for the betrothal pledge back. Having made up his mind, he went straight to find Jia Lian.

Jia Lian was at the new house. Hearing that Xiang-lian had come, he was overjoyed, hurried out to welcome him and invited him into the inner room to meet Old Mrs You. Xiang-lian merely bowed with clasped hands, addressed her as ‘Aunt’ and referred to himself as ‘your junior’. Jia Lian was astonished.

Over tea Xiang-lian said:

‘Our meeting on the road was unexpected, and everything was done in haste. Little did I know that my aunt had arranged a marriage for me in the fourth month. I have no answer to give her. If I followed your wishes and went against hers, brother, it would hardly be right. Had my pledge been gold or silk, I should not venture to ask for it back. But the swords were left me by my grandfather. I should be grateful if you would return them.’

Jia Lian was discomfited.

‘A pledge is a pledge,’ he protested. ‘It’s given precisely to prevent a change of mind. You can’t enter a marriage agreement and withdraw from it just as you please.

‘You must think it over.’

‘True as that may be,’ Xiang-lian said with a smile, ‘I willingly submit to blame or punishment. But in this matter I cannot do as you ask.’

Jia Lian was about to argue further, but Xiang-lian stood up.

‘Let us go and talk outside, brother. This is not a convenient place.’

San-jie had heard every word from her room. After all her waiting, he had come at last, only to go back on his promise. She knew he must have heard about her at the Jia household. He plainly despised her as a shameless, promiscuous woman and disdained to take her for his wife. If she let him go outside to talk to Jia Lian about breaking off the match, Jia Lian would surely have no way of stopping him. What humiliation for her! As soon as she heard that Jia Lian was going out with him, she took down the swords, hid the female blade against the inside of her arm and came out.

‘There’s no need to go outside and discuss it. Here is your pledge.’

Tears fell like rain. With her left hand she offered Xiang-lian the sword and its scabbard; with her right she bent back her arm and swept the other blade across her throat. Alas!

Peach blossom crushed, its red strewn on the ground; A mountain of jade fallen, never to be raised again.

Her fragrant spirit, her orchid nature, faded into darkness, no one knew where. Everyone rushed to save her, but it was too late. Old Mrs You wailed and cursed Xiang-lian. Jia Lian seized him and ordered that he be bound and taken before the magistrate. But Er-jie checked her tears and tried to dissuade him.

‘You’re making too much trouble. He didn’t threaten her or force her to die. She took her own life.

‘What good would it do to take him to the authorities? You’d only make a scandal and bring us shame. Let him go. Wouldn’t that save trouble?’

By now Jia Lian did not know what to do. He let go of Xiang-lian and told him to leave at once. Xiang-lian, however, would not move.

‘I never knew what a brave and virtuous wife she was,’ he wept. ‘How worthy of respect! How worthy!’

He held her body and wept bitterly. He waited until a coffin had been bought and watched her laid in it, then bent over the coffin and wept bitterly once more. Only then did he take his leave.

Outside, he had nowhere to go. Dazed and silent, he thought over what had just happened. San-jie had been so lovely, and her spirit so fierce. His remorse came too late. As he walked, one of Xue Pan’s pages came to fetch him home. Xiang-lian remained lost in thought. The boy took him to the new house, which was very neatly arranged. Suddenly he heard the chime of jade ornaments, and San-jie entered. In one hand she held the Mandarin-duck swords, in the other a scroll. Weeping, she addressed Liu Xiang-lian.

‘For five years I waited for you in the folly of my love. I never thought you would prove so cold in heart and face. With my death I have repaid that foolish love. Now, at Disenchantment’s command, I am going to the Land of Illusion to revise and annotate the register of all the spirits of love. I could not bear to part without seeing you once more. After this we can never meet again.’

She turned to go. Unable to bear losing her, Xiang-lian hurried forward to catch her and question her, but she said:

‘From the heaven of love I came; through the earth of love I go. In my former life I was led astray by love. Now, shamed by love, I am awake. You and I have nothing more to do with one another.’

With that, a fragrant breeze passed, and she vanished without a trace.

Xiang-lian started awake. Had it been a dream, or not? He opened his eyes. There was no Xue-family page, and this was no new house, but a ruined temple. Beside him a Taoist sat cross-legged, picking lice. Xiang-lian rose, bowed low and asked:

‘What place is this? Reverend master, what is your holy name?’

The Taoist laughed.

‘Even I don’t know what place this is, or who I am. I’ve only stopped here to rest my feet.’

At these words a sudden cold seized Xiang-lian, as though ice had entered his bones. He drew the male blade and with one sweep cut off the ten thousand strands of troubling attachment that were his hair. Then he followed the Taoist, no one knew where.
