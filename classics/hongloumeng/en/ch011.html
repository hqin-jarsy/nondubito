Chapter 11 The Ning mansion lays on a family feast to celebrate a birthday; At the sight of Xi-feng, Jia Rui is stirred to lust

Chapter end

Now it was Jia Jing’s birthday. Jia Zhen first had sixteen large presentation boxes packed with the finest delicacies and some unusual fruits, and sent Jia Rong with a party of household servants to take them to Jia Jing.

‘Watch carefully and see whether your grandfather likes them,’ he told Jia Rong. ‘Pay your respects, then come back. Tell him, “My father has obeyed your instructions and has not ventured to come. He has led the whole household in paying their respects to you from home.”’

Jia Rong took his instructions and set off with the servants.

Meanwhile the guests began to arrive. Jia Lian and Jia Qiang came first. They inspected the seating arrangements in the various rooms and asked whether there was to be any entertainment.

‘Our master had meant to invite the old master home today,’ the servants replied, ‘so we hadn’t dared arrange any. The day before yesterday we heard he wasn’t coming after all, and we’ve now been told to find a troupe of child actors and a shifan instrumental ensemble. They’re all waiting on the stage in the garden.’

Next came Lady Xing, Lady Wang, Xi-feng and Bao-yu. Jia Zhen and You-shi welcomed them inside. You-shi’s mother was already there. After they had exchanged greetings and politely urged one another to take the seats of honour, Jia Zhen and You-shi served the tea themselves.

‘The old lady is the senior member of our family, and my father is her nephew,’ Jia Zhen said. ‘We should never have presumed to invite her on such an occasion. But the weather is so pleasantly cool just now, and the garden is full of chrysanthemums. We thought she might come over for a little diversion and enjoy seeing all the children and grandchildren making merry. That was all we meant. Who would have thought she would refuse to honour us?’

Before Lady Wang could answer, Xi-feng spoke up.

‘Only yesterday Grandmother was still saying she would come. But in the evening she saw Cousin Bao and the others eating peaches, and got a fancy for some herself. She ate more than half a peach, and during the fifth watch she had to get up twice in quick succession. This morning she felt a little tired. She sent me to tell you she really couldn’t come today. But she wants a few of the good things to eat, and they must be cooked very soft.’

Jia Zhen laughed. ‘I said there must be some reason. She does love a lively gathering. If that’s all it is, there’s nothing to worry about.’

‘The other day Xi-feng told me Rong’s wife was not quite well,’ said Lady Wang. ‘What exactly is the matter with her?’

‘It’s a strange illness,’ You-shi replied. ‘At the Mid-Autumn Festival last month she stayed up half the night enjoying herself with the old lady and all of you. She was perfectly well when she came home. But after the twentieth she grew more listless every day, and didn’t feel like eating either. That’s been going on for over half a month now. And she hasn’t had her period for two months.’

‘Could she be expecting?’ Lady Xing put in.

Just then a servant announced from outside, ‘The Elder Master and the Second Master have arrived with the other gentlemen of the family. They’re in the hall.’

Jia Zhen hurried out. Only then did You-shi continue.

‘Some of the doctors did say that at first. Yesterday Feng Zi-ying recommended a gentleman he used to study under, who knows a great deal about medicine. He examined her and said she wasn’t expecting at all: it was quite a serious illness. He wrote a prescription yesterday, and she’s taken one dose. Her dizziness is a little better today, but there hasn’t been much improvement in anything else.’

‘I know she’d have struggled up on a day like this,’ said Xi-feng, ‘if she could possibly have kept going.’

‘You saw her here on the third,’ said You-shi. ‘She forced herself to stay for half the day. It was only because you two are so fond of each other that she couldn’t bear to go.’

Xi-feng’s eyes reddened. For a long while she could say nothing. At last she said, ‘It’s true enough: “The sky may change without warning; fortune or disaster may overtake us in a day.”’

‘At her age, if this illness should carry her off, what pleasure would there be in being left alive ourselves?’

As they were talking, Jia Rong came in. He paid his respects to Lady Xing, Lady Wang and Xi-feng before making his report to You-shi.

‘I took the food to Grandfather just now. I told him Father was at home attending to the elder gentlemen and entertaining the rest of the family, and had obeyed his instructions by not venturing to come himself. Grandfather was very pleased. “That’s as it should be,” he said. He told me to tell Father and Mother to look after the elder gentlemen and ladies properly, and said I must attend to my uncles and aunts and the older cousins. He also said the Tract on Hidden Virtue was to be cut on printing blocks at once, and ten thousand copies printed to give away. I’ve told Father all this already. I must go out now and see that the elder gentlemen and the rest of the family have their dinner.’

‘Wait a moment, Rong,’ said Xi-feng. ‘How is your wife today, really?’

Jia Rong frowned. ‘Not well at all!’

‘Come and see her later, Aunt, and you’ll know.’

With that he went out. You-shi turned to Lady Xing and Lady Wang.

‘Would you rather have dinner here, or go and eat in the garden? The child actors are ready there now.’

‘Why don’t we eat first and go over afterwards?’ Lady Wang suggested to Lady Xing. ‘It will save a good deal of trouble.’

‘Very well,’ Lady Xing agreed.

You-shi ordered the married women servants and old women to bring in dinner at once. A chorus of assent came from outside the door, and each went to fetch her share of the dishes. Before long the meal was set out. You-shi persuaded Lady Xing, Lady Wang and her own mother to take the seats of honour; she herself sat at the side with Xi-feng and Bao-yu.

‘We came to wish the Elder Master a happy birthday,’ Lady Xing and Lady Wang said. ‘Anyone would think we had come to celebrate our own!’

‘The Elder Master has always liked peace and quiet,’ said Xi-feng. ‘He’s perfected his spiritual training by now. You might almost call him an immortal.’

‘So when you put it like that, ladies, it’s a case of “A thought in the heart is known to the gods.”’

Everyone in the room laughed. When You-shi’s mother, Lady Xing, Lady Wang and Xi-feng had finished dinner, rinsed their mouths and washed their hands, they began talking of going to the garden. Jia Rong came in and spoke to You-shi.

‘The elder gentlemen and all the uncles and cousins, older and younger, have finished too. The Elder Master said he had business at home, and the Second Master doesn’t care for plays and finds the noise too much for him, so they’ve both just left. Second Uncle Lian and Cousin Qiang have persuaded all the other gentlemen to go over and watch the plays. The four princely households, the Princes of Nan’an, Dongping, Xining and Beijing, have all sent servants with visiting cards and birthday gifts. So have the six households that include the Niu family of the Duke of Zhen, and the eight that include the Shi family of the Marquis of Zhongjing. I’ve reported everything to Father. The gifts are in the counting-house for the moment, and the gift lists have all been entered in the register. Father’s cards acknowledging the gifts and conveying his thanks have been handed to the messengers. They’ve all had the customary tips, and we saw that every one of them had a meal before leaving.’

‘You should invite the two ladies, Grandmother and Aunt over to sit in the garden now, Mother.’

‘We’ve only just finished,’ said You-shi. ‘We were about to go.’

‘If you’ll excuse me, Aunt,’ Xi-feng said to Lady Wang, ‘I’ll go and see Rong’s wife first, and join you afterwards.’

‘Quite right,’ Lady Wang said. ‘We would all like to see her, but we’re afraid she might find a crowd too much. Give her our love.’

‘There’s a dear sister,’ You-shi said. ‘She listens to you. If you can talk her out of her low spirits, I shall feel easier too. But do come along to the garden soon.’

Bao-yu wanted to go with Xi-feng to see Qin-shi.

‘Just look in on her, then come straight over,’ Lady Wang told him. ‘Remember, she is your nephew’s wife.’

You-shi led Lady Xing, Lady Wang and her mother into the Garden of Gathered Fragrance.

Xi-feng and Bao-yu now went with Jia Rong to Qin-shi’s rooms. They entered quietly and approached the door of the inner room. At the sight of them Qin-shi tried to rise.

‘No, don’t get up,’ Xi-feng said. ‘You’ll make yourself dizzy if you get up too quickly.’

She hurried forward a couple of steps and took Qin-shi’s hand.

‘My dear girl! How can you have grown so thin in these few days?’

She sat down on the mattress beside her. Bao-yu asked how she was and took a chair opposite. Jia Rong called for tea.

‘Be quick! Aunt and Second Uncle haven’t had any tea in the main rooms yet.’

Holding Xi-feng’s hand, Qin-shi forced a smile.

‘It’s my own lack of good fortune. To have married into a family like this! My father-in-law and mother-in-law treat me as their own daughter. Your nephew may be young, Aunt, but he has always treated me with respect, and I him. We’ve never had a cross word. As for the other members of the family, older or of my own generation, there isn’t one who hasn’t been kind to me, or with whom I haven’t got on well. I needn’t speak of you, Aunt. Now this illness has taken away every bit of the spirit I had to do things well. I haven’t had a single day to show my devotion to my parents-in-law. And you’ve been so loving to me, Aunt. Even if I longed with all my heart to show you the same devotion, I couldn’t do it now. I don’t think I shall last out the year.’

Bao-yu had been gazing at Spring Sleep among the Crab-apple Blossoms and the couplet written by Qin Tai-xu:

A gentle chill confines the dream: spring is still cold; Fragrance enfolds the sleeper: wine perfumes the air.

He could not help remembering the afternoon nap he had taken here and his dream of the Land of Illusion. As he sat lost in thought, Qin-shi’s words struck him like ten thousand arrows in the heart. Before he knew it, tears were running down his face. Xi-feng was deeply distressed herself, but feared that the sight of them all looking so would only make the sick woman more miserable. That would defeat their purpose in coming to comfort and encourage her. Seeing Bao-yu’s tears, she spoke to him.

‘Cousin Bao, you’re being an old woman! She only talks like that because she’s ill. It hasn’t come to that yet. And how old is she, after all? A little illness, and she starts thinking this and imagining that. Isn’t that enough to make her worse?’

‘There’s nothing else needed for this illness,’ said Jia Rong. ‘If she could only eat a little, there’d be nothing to fear.’

‘Cousin Bao,’ Xi-feng said, ‘your mother wants you to go over at once.’

‘Don’t sit here going on like this. You’ll only upset Rong’s wife, and your mother will be wondering where you are.’

She turned to Jia Rong. ‘Go over with your Uncle Bao. I’ll stay a little longer.’

Jia Rong accordingly took Bao-yu to the Garden of Gathered Fragrance.

Left with Qin-shi, Xi-feng tried again to comfort her. They spoke for a long time in low voices, opening their hearts to one another. You-shi had sent two or three times to fetch her before Xi-feng finally took leave.

‘Look after yourself properly. I’ll come again. You must be meant to get well, or someone wouldn’t have recommended such a good doctor the other day. There’s nothing to be afraid of now.’

Qin-shi smiled. ‘An immortal himself might cure an illness, but he couldn’t cure one’s fate. Aunt, I know I’m only dragging out my days.’

‘How can you get well if you keep thinking like that?’ Xi-feng said. ‘You must try to put it out of your mind. Besides, I hear the doctor said if you weren’t treated, things might go badly in the spring.’

‘It’s only the middle of the ninth month now. There are still four or five months to go. What illness couldn’t be cured in all that time? If we were a family that couldn’t afford ginseng, that might be another matter. But if your father-in-law and mother-in-law heard it would cure you, never mind two qian of ginseng a day: they could afford two catties. Now take good care of yourself. I’m going over to the garden.’

‘You must forgive me for not going with you, Aunt,’ Qin-shi said. ‘When you have a little time, do please come and see me often. We can sit together, you and I, and have a few more talks.’

Xi-feng’s eyes reddened again in spite of herself.

‘Whenever I can get away, I’ll come.’

Taking the old women and maids who had accompanied her, together with the married women servants and old women of the Ning household, Xi-feng went round from the inner quarters and into the garden by a side gate. Here was a sight indeed: yellow flowers covered the ground, pale willows stretched across the slope. A little bridge might have led to the stream of Ruoye; a winding track might have joined the path to Tiantai. Clear water raced among the rocks; fragrance drifted from the hedges. Red leaves fluttered in the treetops, and the open woodland was a painting.

The west wind freshened; the orioles’ song had only just ceased. In the warm sunshine, the crickets added their voices. Far off to the south-east, several open pavilions nestled against the hillside; to the north-west stood a three-bayed pavilion beside the water. The sound of reed pipes filled the air, bringing a charm all its own; silk-clad figures threaded the trees, lending the scene still greater grace.

Xi-feng walked slowly on, admiring the garden. Suddenly a man appeared from behind the rockery and came up to her.

‘My respects to you, Sister-in-law.’

Taken by surprise, Xi-feng drew back a step.

‘Why, isn’t this Master Rui?’

‘Don’t you recognise me any more, Sister-in-law?’ Jia Rui asked.

‘Who else could it be?’

‘It’s not that I don’t recognise you,’ Xi-feng said. ‘You startled me. I never expected to see you here.’

‘It must be that you and I were meant to meet, Sister-in-law,’ Jia Rui said. ‘I slipped out of the feast just now for a little stroll somewhere quiet. And here you are, coming this way too! If that isn’t fate, what is?’

As he spoke, his eyes kept straying to Xi-feng.

Xi-feng was a clever woman. Seeing the way he behaved, how could she fail to guess most of what was in his mind? With a show of smiling friendliness she answered him.

‘No wonder your cousin Lian is always speaking of you and saying what a good fellow you are. Now I’ve met you and heard you talk, I can see at once how sensible and pleasant you are. I must go to the ladies just now, so I can’t stay and talk. We’ll have a chat another time, when we’re free.’

‘I’ve wanted to come to your house and pay my respects,’ Jia Rui said, ‘but I was afraid that, being so young, you might not care to receive visitors.’

Xi-feng put on another smile. ‘We’re all one family. What has being young to do with it?’

Jia Rui had never dreamed he would have such a stroke of luck that day. At these words, the look on his face and his whole manner became more unseemly than ever.

‘Do go back to your seat,’ Xi-feng said. ‘Take care they don’t catch you and make you drink a forfeit.’

By now Jia Rui felt half his body go numb. He walked slowly away, turning to look back as he went.

Xi-feng deliberately slowed her steps. When she saw that he was well out of the way, she thought, ‘You may know a man’s face and never know his heart. What a brute! If he means to go on like this, I’ll have him die at my hands one of these days. Then he’ll know what I’m capable of!’

Only then did she go on. She was about to round another slope when she saw two or three old women hurrying towards her in great agitation. At the sight of her they smiled.

‘Our mistress is beside herself because you still haven’t come, Mrs Lian. She’s sent us to ask you again.’

‘Your mistress is always in such a devil of a hurry,’ Xi-feng said.

Still walking slowly, she asked how many scenes had been performed.

‘Eight or nine,’ one of the women replied.

As they spoke, they reached the back entrance of the Tower of Heavenly Fragrance. Bao-yu was playing there with a group of maids.

‘Cousin Bao, don’t get up to too much mischief,’ Xi-feng told him.

‘The ladies are all sitting upstairs,’ one of the maids said. ‘Please come up this way, ma’am.’

Xi-feng gathered up her skirts and went leisurely upstairs. You-shi was waiting at the top.

‘You two are far too fond of each other,’ she said, laughing. ‘Once you’ve met, there’s no getting you to come away. You’d better move in with her tomorrow. Sit down. I must give you a cup of wine before anything else.’

Xi-feng asked Lady Xing and Lady Wang’s permission to be seated, then exchanged a few courtesies with You-shi’s mother before joining You-shi at her table to drink and watch the plays. You-shi called for the list and asked her to choose a scene.

‘With your mother and both our ladies here, how could I presume to choose?’ Xi-feng said.

‘We and our good kinswoman have chosen several already,’ Lady Xing and Lady Wang told her. ‘Choose a couple of good ones for us to hear.’

Xi-feng rose to acknowledge the invitation before taking the list. She read it from the beginning and chose Return to Life and The Ballad, then handed it back.

‘When they’ve finished The Double Investiture, which they’re performing now, let them do these two. By then it will be time.’

‘Indeed it will,’ Lady Wang said. ‘We ought to let your cousin and his wife have an early rest. They have enough to worry about.’

‘You don’t come over often,’ You-shi protested. ‘It’s much nicer when we can all sit together a little longer. There’s plenty of daylight yet.’

Xi-feng stood up and looked down from the tower.

‘Where have all the men gone?’

‘They’ve just taken the shifan ensemble over to the Pavilion of Gathered Morning Light to drink,’ an old woman beside her answered.

‘They couldn’t do as they pleased here, could they?’ Xi-feng said. ‘Who knows what they’ll get up to out of sight!’

‘Not everyone is as proper as you,’ You-shi said, laughing.

So they talked and laughed until the scenes they had chosen were over. Only then were the wine tables cleared and the meal brought in. When they had eaten, they left the garden and sat down in the main rooms for tea. At last they ordered the carriages and took leave of You-shi’s mother. You-shi, accompanied by the concubines and the household’s old women and married women servants, saw them out. Jia Zhen and the younger men of the family were already standing respectfully beside the carriages, waiting for them.

‘Aunts, do both come over again tomorrow,’ Jia Zhen said to Lady Xing and Lady Wang.

‘No, we’ve sat here the whole day and we’re tired,’ Lady Wang replied. ‘We shall rest tomorrow.’

They got into their carriages. Jia Rui was still stealing glances at Xi-feng from time to time. After Jia Zhen and the others had gone in, Li Gui brought up Bao-yu’s horse. Bao-yu mounted and followed Lady Wang home. Back at the Ning mansion, Jia Zhen and the other men, older and younger, had supper together before they finally dispersed.

The next day the members of the clan made merry for another whole day, of which we need give no detailed account. After this Xi-feng often went herself to see Qin-shi. Some days Qin-shi was a little better; on others she was just as before. Jia Zhen, You-shi and Jia Rong were desperately anxious. Jia Rui, meanwhile, called at the Rong mansion several times, but each time Xi-feng happened to be away at the Ning mansion.

That year the Winter Solstice fell on the thirtieth day of the eleventh month. During the days around the turn of the season, Grandmother Jia, Lady Wang and Xi-feng sent someone every day to ask after Qin-shi. The messengers all returned with the same report: ‘She hasn’t grown any worse these last few days, but she isn’t much better either.’

‘With an illness like this, if she can get through such an important turn of the season without growing worse, there’s a great deal to hope for,’ Lady Wang told Grandmother Jia.

‘Indeed there is,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘Such a dear child! If anything were to happen to her, it would break our hearts.’

The thought brought a pang to her heart. She called Xi-feng to her.

‘You two have been so close. Tomorrow is the first of the month; let tomorrow go by, then go and see her the day after. Look at her very carefully. If she’s a little better, come back and tell me, so that I can be glad too. And have them make the things she’s always liked to eat and send them over often.’

Xi-feng agreed to each of her requests.

On the second, after breakfast, she went to the Ning mansion. Qin-shi’s illness had not grown much worse, but the flesh had wasted away from her face and body. Xi-feng sat with her for half the day, making conversation and once again assuring her that there was no cause for alarm.

‘We shall know in the spring whether I’m going to get well,’ Qin-shi said.

‘Now the Winter Solstice is past and nothing has happened, perhaps I may recover after all. Do tell the old lady and Her Ladyship not to worry, Aunt. I ate two of the yam cakes filled with jujube paste that the old lady sent me yesterday. I really seemed able to digest them.’

‘I’ll send you some more tomorrow,’ Xi-feng said. ‘I’m going to look in on your mother-in-law now, then I must hurry back and report to Grandmother.’

‘Please give the old lady and Her Ladyship my respects, Aunt,’ Qin-shi said.

Xi-feng promised and went out. She took a seat in You-shi’s main room.

‘Now you’ve had a chance to judge for yourself, how do you think she is?’ You-shi asked.

Xi-feng kept her head lowered for a long time.

‘There really is nothing to be done. You ought to get the things ready that will be needed afterwards. It might even turn her luck.’

‘I’ve had people preparing them quietly,’ You-shi said. ‘Only we can’t find good wood for that one thing. We shall have to take our time over it.’

Xi-feng drank her tea and stayed talking for a while. Then she said, ‘I must hurry back now and tell Grandmother.’

‘Break it to her gently,’ You-shi said. ‘Don’t frighten her.’

‘I know.’

Xi-feng returned home. When she saw Grandmother Jia, she said, ‘Rong’s wife sends her respects and kowtows to you. She says she’s a little better, and begs you not to worry. As soon as she’s a little stronger, she wants to come and kowtow to you herself.’

‘What did you think of her?’ Grandmother Jia asked.

‘There’s no danger for the moment. She’s still quite bright.’

Grandmother Jia pondered this for a long while.

‘Go and change your clothes and rest,’ she told Xi-feng.

Xi-feng assented and went out. After seeing Lady Wang, she returned to her own rooms, where Patience helped her change into the everyday clothes she had been warming for her. As soon as she sat down, Xi-feng asked, ‘Has anything happened at home?’

Patience brought her tea and handed it to her.

‘Nothing much. Wang-er’s wife brought in the interest on those three hundred taels of silver. I took it from her. And Master Rui sent someone to ask whether you were at home. He wants to call and pay his respects, and have a word with you.’

Xi-feng gave a snort.

‘The brute is bent on getting himself killed. Let’s see what happens when he comes!’

‘Why does this Master Rui keep coming?’ Patience asked.

Xi-feng told her all about meeting him in the Ning garden in the ninth month, and what he had said.

‘A toad hankering after swan’s flesh!’ Patience said. ‘The shameless, good-for-nothing creature, with no respect for family decency! May he die a wretched death for thinking of such a thing!’

‘Let him come,’ said Xi-feng. ‘I know how to deal with him.’

What happened when Jia Rui came? To find out, listen to the next chapter.
