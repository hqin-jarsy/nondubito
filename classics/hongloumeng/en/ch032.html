Chapter 32 Living Bao-yu, lost in feeling, lays bare his heart; Golden, fierce in feeling, dies in shame

Chapter end

Now, Bao-yu was delighted to see the qilin. He reached for it.

‘Thank goodness you found it!’ he said, smiling. ‘Where was it?’

‘Luckily it was only this,’ Shi Xiang-yun said with a smile. ‘Suppose you lost your official seal one day. Would you let that pass too?’

‘Losing a seal would be nothing. If I lost this, I’d deserve to die.’

Aroma poured Xiang-yun some tea.

‘Miss,’ she said with a smile, ‘I hear congratulations were in order the other day.’

Xiang-yun blushed and drank her tea without answering.

‘Shy now, are we?’ Aroma went on. ‘Do you remember what you used to say to me at night ten years ago, when we were staying in the heated room on the west side?

‘You weren’t shy then. Why are you shy now?’

‘You can talk!’ Xiang-yun said, smiling. ‘We were such friends in those days. Then the lady of our household died, and I went home for a while. Why did they have to send you to wait on Second Cousin? When I came back, you didn’t treat me as you used to.’

‘You can talk too,’ Aroma said with a smile.

‘It used to be “sister” this and “sister” that, coaxing me to comb your hair and wash your face, make you this and do that for you. Now you’re grown up, you put on a young lady’s airs. If you act the young lady, how can I dare to be familiar?’

‘Amitabha! What an injustice! What a wicked accusation!’ Xiang-yun cried. ‘May I drop dead this minute if I’m like that. Just look: in all this heat, the moment I arrive I have to hurry over to see you first. Ask Kingfisher if you don’t believe me. When I’m at home, when does an hour pass without my talking of you?’

Before she had finished, Aroma and Bao-yu hastened to soothe her.

‘You’re taking a joke seriously again! Still as quick-tempered as ever.’

‘You don’t say how your words choke a person. You only say they’re quick-tempered.’

As she spoke she opened her handkerchief and gave Aroma the ring. Aroma thanked her warmly.

‘I’ve already had one of those you sent the young ladies the other day,’ she said, smiling. ‘Now you’ve brought me another yourself. That proves you haven’t forgotten me. This alone is enough to show it.

‘A ring isn’t worth much, but it shows your heart is true.’

‘Who gave you one?’ Xiang-yun asked.

‘Miss Bao.’

‘I thought it would have been Cousin Lin,’ Xiang-yun said with a smile. ‘So it was Cousin Bao-chai. At home I’m always thinking that none of the other cousins can compare with her. What a pity we weren’t born of the same mother. If only I had a sister like her, it wouldn’t matter even if I had no parents.’

Her eyes reddened as she spoke.

‘There, there, there!’ said Bao-yu. ‘Don’t talk about that.’

‘Why shouldn’t I? I know your sore point. You’re afraid your Cousin Lin will hear and be cross with me for praising Cousin Bao. Isn’t that it?’

Aroma gave a little snort of laughter.

‘Miss Yun, the older you grow, the more plainly you speak your mind.’

‘I’ve always said how hard it is to talk to you girls,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘And I was right.’

‘My dear cousin,’ said Xiang-yun, ‘do stop saying things to turn my stomach.

‘You can talk well enough in front of us. Once you see your Cousin Lin, goodness knows what happens to you.’

‘Enough joking,’ said Aroma. ‘There’s something I really must ask you to do for me.’

‘What is it?’

‘There’s a pair of shoes to make; the inner soles are cut out. I haven’t been well these last two days and can’t do them. If you have time, could you do them for me?’

‘Well, this is strange!’ Xiang-yun said, laughing. ‘Apart from all the clever people you have here, there are the sewing women and the people who do the cutting. Why ask me? Whoever you asked to do your work, would they have the nerve to refuse?’

‘You’re being silly again,’ Aroma said with a smile. ‘Don’t you know we never let those sewing women do the work for this room?’

Now Xiang-yun knew the shoes were Bao-yu’s.

‘In that case I’ll do them for you,’ she said, smiling. ‘Only on one condition: I’ll do yours, but I won’t do anyone else’s.’

‘There you go again,’ Aroma laughed. ‘Who am I, to trouble you to make my shoes?

‘To tell you the truth, they aren’t mine. Never mind whose they are. I’ll be grateful to you all the same.’

‘Well, goodness knows how many things of yours I’ve made already,’ said Xiang-yun. ‘You must know why I won’t do these today.’

‘I really don’t.’

Xiang-yun laughed coldly.

‘I heard the fan-case I made was taken out to compare with someone else’s work, and then cut up in a fit of temper. I heard about it long ago. You needn’t hide it from me. And now you want me to make something else. I’ve become your servant, have I?’

‘I didn’t know you’d made it,’ Bao-yu put in hastily, with a smile.

‘He really didn’t know,’ Aroma said, smiling too. ‘It was a story I told him. I said there was a girl outside who was wonderfully good at needlework, that she embroidered the most extraordinary flowers, and that I’d had her bring a fan-case for us to see how good it was. He believed me and took it out to show to everyone. Somehow he offended Miss Lin again, and she cut it in two. When he came back, he was still asking me to hurry and have another made. Only then did I tell him you had done it. You should have seen how sorry he was.’

‘Stranger still!’ said Xiang-yun. ‘Miss Lin had no reason to be angry. Since she knows how to cut, let her do the sewing.’

‘She won’t do it,’ said Aroma. ‘Even as things are, Her Old Ladyship worries about her overdoing it. The doctor says she needs quiet and rest. Who would trouble her to sew? Last year it took her a whole year to make one scent-bag. Half this year has gone, and we haven’t seen her pick up a needle yet.’

While they were talking, someone came to report, ‘The gentleman from Xinglong Street is here. The master wants Master Bao to come out and meet him.’

Bao-yu knew this meant Jia Yu-cun had come, and was thoroughly displeased. Aroma hurried to fetch his clothes. As he pulled on his boots, he grumbled, ‘Why isn’t it enough for Father to sit with him? He has to see me every time.’

‘Of course your father sends for you,’ Xiang-yun said, smiling as she fanned herself. ‘You’re so good at receiving guests.’

‘It isn’t Father. It’s the man himself who always asks to see me.’

‘An accomplished host never lacks for visitors,’ Xiang-yun said with a smile. ‘You must have some qualities that impress him, or he wouldn’t be so eager to see you.’

‘Enough, enough! I wouldn’t dare call myself accomplished. I’m the most common of common people, and I’ve no wish to associate with these men.’

‘Still the same temper,’ Xiang-yun said, smiling. ‘You’re growing up now. Even if you don’t want to study and take the examinations for juren and jinshi, you ought to see more of these men in office and talk about the business of an official career and public affairs. You’d learn how to deal with the world, and have some friends in years to come.

‘What do you mean by spending the whole year here, always mixing with us?’

‘Miss,’ Bao-yu replied, ‘please go and sit with one of the other girls. I’m afraid my room might sully someone so learned in public affairs.’

‘Miss Yun, please don’t talk like that,’ said Aroma. ‘Miss Bao once said the same thing. He didn’t care how humiliated she might feel: he gave a snort, got to his feet and walked away.

‘She hadn’t even finished speaking. When she saw him go, her face went scarlet. She didn’t know whether to go on or stop. Luckily it was Miss Bao. If it had been Miss Lin, heaven knows what a scene she would have made, or how much she would have cried. When I think of it, I really do respect Miss Bao. She looked embarrassed for a while, then went away.

‘I felt dreadful. I thought she must be angry. But afterwards she was just the same as ever. Such self-control, and such a generous heart! Yet this one here has grown distant towards her instead. If you sulked and wouldn’t speak to Miss Lin, think how many apologies you’d have to make!’

‘Has Miss Lin ever talked that damned nonsense?’ Bao-yu asked. ‘If she had, I’d have grown distant towards her long ago.’

Aroma and Xiang-yun both nodded, smiling.

‘Oh yes, damned nonsense, of course.’

Dai-yu, meanwhile, knew that Xiang-yun was there and that Bao-yu had hurried over too. They must be talking about the qilin. She reflected that in the romances and unofficial histories Bao-yu had lately acquired, clever young men and beautiful girls were usually brought together by little trinkets: mandarin ducks or phoenixes, jade rings or gold pendants, handkerchiefs of merfolk silk or luan-bird sashes. Some small thing always united them for life. Now that Bao-yu too had a qilin, she feared it might make an opening for some romantic affair between him and Shi Xiang-yun. So she slipped over quietly, to see what the two of them had in mind and act as the occasion required.

Unexpectedly, just as she arrived, she heard Xiang-yun speaking of public affairs, and Bao-yu saying, ‘Cousin Lin doesn’t talk that damned nonsense. If she did, I’d grow distant towards her too.’

At these words Dai-yu felt joy and alarm, and both grieved and sighed.

Her joy was that her judgment had proved sound. She had always thought he understood her, and he did. Her alarm was that he should speak so warmly of her in front of others, laying bare his private feelings with such intimacy, without the least concern for what people might suspect. Her sigh was for this: since you understand me, surely I may understand you too. If we understand each other, why should there be all this talk of gold and jade? And if gold and jade must be spoken of, it ought to be you and I. Why must a Bao-chai come into it?

Her grief was that her parents had died so early. However deeply words might be engraved upon her heart and bones, she had no one to speak for her. Besides, of late she had often felt dazed and absent-minded; her illness was gradually taking hold. The doctors even said her qi was weak and her blood deficient, and feared a wasting illness might develop. Though we understand each other, I fear I may not be able to stay long. Even if you understand me, what can you do about my ill-starred life?

At this thought tears rolled down her face. She considered going in to see them, but it seemed pointless now. Wiping her tears, she turned to go back.

Bao-yu hurried into his clothes and came out. Suddenly he saw Dai-yu walking slowly ahead of him, apparently wiping away tears. He hastened to catch up.

‘Where are you going, cousin?’ he asked with a smile. ‘Why are you crying again? Who’s offended you this time?’

Dai-yu turned and saw who it was. She forced a smile.

‘I’m perfectly well. When have I been crying?’

‘Look at you!’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘The tears aren’t dry on your eyes yet, and you’re still telling lies.’

As he spoke he could not help raising his hand to wipe them away. Dai-yu quickly stepped back.

‘Are you courting death again? What do you mean by pawing at me like that?’

Bao-yu smiled.

‘Talking to you, I forgot myself. My hand moved before I knew it. I couldn’t stop to think about life and death.’

‘Your dying wouldn’t matter much. But what would become of the gold you left behind, and that qilin of yours?’

This upset Bao-yu again. He stepped towards her.

‘Still saying that! Are you trying to curse me or drive me mad?’

Only then did Dai-yu recall what had happened the other day. Regretting her rash words, she hastily smiled.

‘Don’t get upset. I spoke wrongly.

‘It’s nothing to get so worked up about. The veins are standing out, and your face is covered in sweat.’

She could not help coming closer and raising her hand to wipe his face. Bao-yu looked at her for a long while before he spoke: only three Chinese characters, ‘Rest easy.’

Dai-yu stared for a long time.

‘What have I to be uneasy about? I don’t understand. Tell me what you mean by easy and uneasy.’

Bao-yu sighed.

‘Do you really not understand? Have I been wrong in all the thought I’ve given you? If I can’t even understand what you mean, it’s no wonder you’re angry with me every day.’

‘I really don’t understand this talk of resting easy or not resting easy.’

Bao-yu nodded and sighed.

‘My dear cousin, don’t deceive me. If you truly don’t understand, then not only has all my feeling for you been wasted, but I’ve failed all the feeling you’ve shown me too. It’s because you never rest easy that you’ve made yourself so ill.

‘If only you could set your mind at rest a little, your illness wouldn’t grow worse day after day.’

To Dai-yu the words were like a thunderclap and a flash of lightning. As she considered them, they seemed more heartfelt even than if they had been drawn from her own innermost being. Ten thousand things filled her heart that she longed to say, yet she could not utter so much as half a word. She only gazed at him. Bao-yu too had ten thousand things in his heart, with no idea which to begin with. He gazed at Dai-yu in turn.

They stood looking at each other for a long while. Then Dai-yu gave a single cough, tears rolled from both eyes, and she turned to leave. Bao-yu hurried forward and caught her.

‘My dear cousin, stay just a moment. Let me say one thing before you go.’

Wiping her tears, Dai-yu pushed his hand away.

‘What is there to say? I’ve known what you would say for a long time!’

She went straight on without even turning her head.

Bao-yu stood there in a daze. In his haste to leave he had forgotten his fan. Afraid he would be hot, Aroma had hurried after him with one, and on looking up had seen him standing with Dai-yu. Now Dai-yu had gone, but he had not moved. Aroma came up to him.

‘You went off without a fan. Luckily I noticed and hurried after you with it.’

Bao-yu was lost in thought. Though he saw Aroma speaking to him, he did not recognise who she was. He seized her hand.

‘My dear cousin, I’ve never dared speak of what’s in my heart. Today I’ll be bold and say it, and die content! I too have made myself ill over you, but I daren’t tell anyone. I can only hide it. I’m afraid my illness won’t get better until yours does. Even in sleep and dreams I can’t forget you!’

Aroma was frightened out of her wits.

‘Heaven and all the bodhisattvas! You’ll be the death of me!’ she cried.

She pushed him.

‘What are you talking about? Have you been possessed? Hurry up and go!’

Bao-yu came to himself and only then realised that it was Aroma who had brought his fan. His face flushed purple with shame. He snatched it from her and hurried away at a run.

After he had gone, Aroma thought over what he had said. It must have been on Dai-yu’s account. If things were like this, some disgraceful affair in the future could scarcely be avoided. The thought was frightening. She stood in a daze, tears falling, while she silently considered how to prevent such a shameful calamity.

While she was still pondering what to do, Bao-chai came towards her.

‘What are you dreaming about in this blazing sun?’ she asked with a smile.

Aroma quickly smiled back.

‘There were two sparrows fighting over there. They were rather amusing, and I got caught up in watching them.’

‘Where was Cousin Bao hurrying off to, all dressed?’ Bao-chai asked. ‘I saw him just now and was going to stop him and ask. But his talk gets more and more incoherent, so I didn’t call him. I let him go.’

‘The master sent for him.’

‘Oh dear!’ Bao-chai exclaimed. ‘Why send for him in this glaring heat? I hope he hasn’t remembered something and lost his temper, and called him out to give him a lecture.’

‘No, not that,’ Aroma said, smiling. ‘I think a guest wants to see him.’

‘What a tiresome guest!’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘Why go running about on such a hot day instead of staying at home in the cool?’

‘You should tell him so,’ Aroma said, smiling.

‘What’s Yun doing at your place?’ Bao-chai asked.

‘We were just chatting,’ Aroma said with a smile. ‘You know that pair of shoes I pasted up the other day? I’m going to have her sew them.’

At this Bao-chai looked in both directions. Seeing no one passing, she said with a smile, ‘You’re such a sensible person. How can you suddenly forget to consider someone else’s circumstances? From the way Yun has seemed lately, and things I’ve picked up here and there, she has no say in anything at home. Her family thinks it spends too much, so they don’t employ sewing women. Most ordinary things have to be made by the women and girls themselves. Why else, on these last few visits, would she tell me how tired she is at home as soon as we’re alone? When I ask another question or two about their daily life, her eyes redden. She mumbles and hesitates, wanting to speak and not quite daring.

‘When I think of her situation, it must be the misery of having lost her parents so young. I can’t help feeling sad when I look at her.’

Aroma clapped her hands.

‘Of course! Of course! No wonder, when I asked her last month to tie me ten butterfly knots, it took her so long to send them. She even said, “They’re roughly done. Use them somewhere they’ll do for now. If you want them neat and even, wait till I come to stay, and I’ll make them properly.” Now you’ve told me this, Miss Bao, I see she must have felt she couldn’t refuse. Who knows how late into the night she had to work at home to do them? How thoughtless I’ve been! If I’d known, I’d never have troubled her.’

‘Last time she told me she worked until the third watch at home,’ said Bao-chai. ‘And if she does even a little for someone else, the ladies of her family are displeased.’

‘And our stubborn, contrary young master won’t let the household sewing women do a single thing, large or small,’ said Aroma.

‘I can’t manage all of it.’

‘Why pay any attention to him?’ Bao-chai asked with a smile. ‘Have someone do it and say it was you.’

‘He wouldn’t believe it,’ Aroma said, smiling. ‘He can tell. There’s nothing for it: I shall just have to wear myself out a little at a time.’

‘Don’t worry. How would it be if I did some for you?’

‘If you really would, that would be a blessing for me. I’ll bring it over myself this evening.’

She had scarcely finished when an old woman came hurrying up.

‘Who could have thought it! Miss Golden’s thrown herself down a well and died, just like that!’

Aroma gave a start.

‘Which Golden?’ she asked quickly.

‘How many Goldens are there?’ the old woman said. ‘The one in Her Ladyship’s room. They sent her away the other day, I don’t know why. At home she cried fit to bring down heaven and earth, but no one took any notice. Then they couldn’t find her.

‘Just now someone was drawing water from the well in the south-eastern corner and saw a body. They hurried to call people to haul it out, and there she was. Her family are still rushing about trying to bring her back to life. What’s the use?’

‘How strange,’ Bao-chai said.

Aroma nodded and sighed at the news. Remembering their sisterly closeness, she began to cry. Bao-chai hurried to Lady Wang to offer comfort. Aroma went back to her own quarters; we leave her there.

When Bao-chai reached Lady Wang’s apartments, all was silent. Lady Wang sat alone in the inner room, weeping. Bao-chai did not like to mention the matter and merely sat down beside her.

‘Where have you come from?’ Lady Wang asked.

‘From the Garden.’

‘Did you see your Cousin Bao there?’

‘Yes, I saw him just now.

‘He had dressed and gone out. I don’t know where he was going.’

Lady Wang nodded.

‘Have you heard this extraordinary thing?’ she said through her tears. ‘Golden has suddenly thrown herself down a well and died!’

‘Why should she throw herself down a well, just like that?’ Bao-chai asked. ‘How strange.’

‘She broke something of mine the other day. I lost my temper, hit her a few times and sent her away. I only meant to leave her to fret for a couple of days and then have her back. Who could have known she was so proud-spirited that she would throw herself down a well? Isn’t it my sin?’

Bao-chai sighed.

‘You’re kind-hearted, Aunt, so naturally you think that. But I don’t believe she threw herself down in anger. Most likely, after she went home, she was playing about by the well and slipped and fell. She’d been so used to being kept in order here that, once she was out, naturally she’d want to wander about and amuse herself. Surely no one could take offence to that degree! Even if she did, she was only a foolish girl, and not worth regretting.’

Lady Wang nodded and sighed.

‘You may say that, but my mind is still uneasy.’

‘You needn’t keep dwelling on it, Aunt,’ Bao-chai said with a sigh. ‘If you feel so badly, give a few more taels of silver towards her funeral. That will fulfil your duty to her as her mistress.’

‘I’ve just given her mother fifty taels,’ said Lady Wang. ‘I also meant to give her two sets of your cousins’ new clothes to be buried in.

‘But Feng says there happen to be no newly made clothes, except the two sets for your Cousin Lin’s birthday. That child has always been so sensitive, and she has trouble enough with her health. After saying the clothes were for her birthday, how could we use them to dress someone for burial? It would be unlucky. So I’ve told the tailors to hurry and make two sets. With another maid, a few taels would have been enough. But although Golden was a maid, she had always been almost a daughter to me.’

As she spoke, the tears fell again.

‘Why make the tailors rush now, Aunt?’ Bao-chai said quickly. ‘I had two sets made the other day. Wouldn’t it save trouble if I brought those? Besides, she wore my old clothes when she was alive. We’re much the same size.’

‘Even so,’ said Lady Wang, ‘wouldn’t you mind the ill luck?’

‘Don’t worry, Aunt,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘I’ve never bothered about such things.’

She rose and went at once. Lady Wang quickly called two people to go with her.

Presently Bao-chai returned with the clothes. Bao-yu was sitting beside Lady Wang, weeping. She had just been reproaching him, but stopped short when Bao-chai appeared. Taking in their words and expressions, Bao-chai understood most of it. She saw that the clothes were properly handed over, and Lady Wang called Golden’s mother to take them away.
