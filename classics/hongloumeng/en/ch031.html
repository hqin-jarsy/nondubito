Chapter 31 Fans are torn for a smile worth a thousand gold pieces; A qilin foreshadows two stars in white-haired old age

Chapter end

Now, when Aroma saw the fresh blood she had spat on the floor, her heart sank. She remembered a saying she had often heard: ‘When the young spit blood, their days are numbered. Even if they live long, they will be invalids for life.’ At the thought of it, all her customary dreams of winning distinction and having something to be proud of in years to come turned to ashes. Tears fell from her eyes.

The sight of her crying wrung Bao-yu’s heart too.

‘How do you feel inside?’ he asked.

‘Perfectly well. How should I feel?’ Aroma forced a smile.

Bao-yu wanted to call someone at once to warm rice wine and fetch Shanyangxue Lidong Pills. Aroma caught his hand.

‘It’s easy enough for you to make a fuss,’ she said, smiling, ‘but think how many people you’ll wake. They’ll only blame me for making so much of myself. Nobody knows anything about it, and you’ll let everyone know. That won’t do either of us any good. The sensible thing is to send a page to ask Doctor Wang tomorrow and get me a little medicine. That will soon put me right. Wouldn’t it be better if not a soul knew?’

Bao-yu saw the sense of this and had to give way. He poured some tea from the table and brought it for her to rinse her mouth.

Aroma knew that Bao-yu was uneasy. If she tried to stop him waiting on her, he would never agree; besides, it would be sure to disturb someone else. Better let him have his way. So she stayed on her couch and let him attend to her.

As soon as the fifth watch began, Bao-yu hurried into his clothes and went out without stopping to wash or comb his hair. He sent for Wang Ji-ren and questioned him carefully in person. Having asked how it had happened and established that it was simply an injury, Wang Ji-ren named a pill and explained how to take it and how to apply it.

Bao-yu remembered the directions, returned to the Garden and treated her accordingly. Of that we need say no more.

This was the Dragon Boat Festival. Sweet flag and mugwort adorned the doors, and tiger charms were tied about people’s arms. At noon Lady Wang gave a feast, inviting Aunt Xue and her daughter and the others to celebrate. Bao-yu noticed that Bao-chai was cool towards him and did not speak to him; he knew it was because of what had happened the day before. Lady Wang, seeing his listlessness, assumed he was ashamed over yesterday’s affair with Golden, and took even less notice of him. Dai-yu thought his languor came of having offended Bao-chai. This made her unhappy, and her own manner grew languid too. Lady Wang had told Xi-feng about Bao-yu and Golden the previous evening. Knowing how displeased she was, Xi-feng did not dare to laugh and joke, but took her cue from Lady Wang’s face, which made things cooler still. Ying-chun and her sisters, seeing everyone else so dispirited, lost their spirits too. They all sat for a little while, then dispersed.

Dai-yu was by nature fonder of dispersals than of gatherings. There was a certain reason in her thinking. ‘Whenever people come together, they must part,’ she would say. ‘If they are happy together, won’t it be cold and lonely when they part? And loneliness brings sorrow. Better, then, not to come together at all. Flowers, for instance: we love them when they open, but when they fade they only add to our sadness. Better if they never opened.’

Thus, what others found joyful she found sad. Bao-yu, by contrast, wanted everyone always to be together, dreading the sorrow that parting might bring. He wanted flowers always in bloom, dreading the loss of pleasure when they faded. Only when the feast was over and the flowers had fallen did he yield to what could not be helped, for all his thousand griefs. So when today’s dispirited party broke up, Dai-yu thought nothing of it. It was Bao-yu who went back to his room in low spirits, sighing again and again.

Unfortunately, as Skybright came to help him change, she let a fan slip from her hand. It fell to the floor and its ribs broke.

‘Clumsy fool! Clumsy fool!’ Bao-yu sighed. ‘What will become of you? When you have a household of your own to manage, will you still be attending to one thing and forgetting the next?’

Skybright laughed coldly.

‘Master Bao’s temper has grown very grand lately. Nothing we do is right. First you hit Aroma, and now it’s our turn to be found fault with. Kick us or beat us, as you please. Dropping a fan is an everyday thing.

‘In the old days goodness knows how many glass jars and agate bowls like those got broken, and we never heard an angry word. Now all this over one fan! Why bother? If you don’t like us, send us away and choose better ones to wait on you. Wouldn’t it be nicer to part on good terms?’

Bao-yu was trembling all over with anger.

‘Don’t be in such a hurry. The day for parting will come!’

Aroma had already heard them from the other side of the room and hurried over.

‘What’s happened now?’ she asked Bao-yu. ‘There, just as I said: I’ve only to be away a moment and something happens.’

Skybright gave another cold laugh.

‘Since you know so well what to say, sister, you should have come sooner and saved the master from losing his temper. From the beginning of time you’re the only one who has ever waited on him. The rest of us never have. It was because you waited on him so well that you got a kick in the chest yesterday. Heaven knows what punishment is waiting tomorrow for those of us who don’t know how!’

Aroma was angry and ashamed. She was about to retort, but Bao-yu’s face had already turned yellow with rage. Swallowing her own anger, she gave Skybright a little push.

‘Be a good girl and go out for a walk. It’s our fault.’

‘Our’ could only mean herself and Bao-yu. This made Skybright more bitter still. She gave several cold little laughs.

‘I wonder who this “our” can be. Don’t make me blush for you!

‘Do you think I don’t know what you’ve been up to behind everyone’s backs? What right have you to talk of “our”? You haven’t even managed to become an acknowledged concubine. You’re no different from me. Where does “our” come in?’

Aroma’s face flushed purple with shame. Thinking it over, she saw that she had chosen the wrong words.

‘If you resent her,’ Bao-yu put in, ‘one day I’ll see that she is promoted, just to spite you.’

Aroma quickly caught his hand.

‘She’s a silly girl. Why argue with her? Besides, you’re usually so forbearing. You’ve let far worse things pass. What’s the matter today?’

‘Oh yes, I’m a silly girl,’ said Skybright with a cold laugh. ‘How could I be fit to speak to?’

‘Are you quarrelling with me, miss, or with Master Bao?’ Aroma asked. ‘If it’s me you’re angry with, tell me. There’s no need to shout about it in front of him. If it’s him you’re angry with, you shouldn’t shout till the whole world knows.

‘I only came in to settle things and persuade you both to take care of yourselves, and you’ve turned on me. You don’t seem to be angry with me, and you don’t seem to be angry with him. What are all these thrusts and jabs meant to achieve? I’ll say no more. Have it your own way.’

She started out.

‘There’s no need to be angry,’ Bao-yu told Skybright. ‘I’ve guessed what’s on your mind. You’re growing up now. Shall I tell Mother to send you away?’

This wounded Skybright afresh.

‘Why should I go?’ she said bitterly. ‘If you’ve taken a dislike to me, you needn’t think you can find some trick to get rid of me.’

‘When have I ever had to put up with such quarrelling?’ said Bao-yu.

‘You must want to leave. I’d better tell Mother and let her send you away.’

He stood up to go. Aroma hurried back and barred his way.

‘Where are you going?’ she asked with a smile.

‘To tell Mother.’

‘What nonsense! Won’t you be ashamed if you really go and tell her? Even if Skybright truly wants to leave, wait until everyone has cooled down. There’ll be time enough to mention it to Her Ladyship in an ordinary conversation. If you rush off now as though it were some urgent business, won’t she grow suspicious?’

‘She won’t suspect anything. I’ll tell her plainly that Skybright’s making a fuss because she wants to go.’

‘When did I ever make a fuss about going?’ Skybright cried through her tears. ‘First you lose your temper, then you try to pin things on me! Go and tell her, then. I’ll dash my brains out before I leave this door.’

‘How strange! You won’t go, and yet you make all this fuss. I can’t stand the noise. You’d better go and have done with it.’

He was determined to tell his mother.

Seeing that she could not stop him, Aroma sank to her knees. Emerald, Ripple, Musk and the other maids had been listening outside in complete silence throughout the quarrel. When they heard Aroma kneel and beg him, they all came in and knelt too. Bao-yu hastily raised Aroma, sighed, and sat down on the bed. He told the others to get up and go.

‘What am I to do?’ he said to Aroma. ‘I could wear my heart to pieces and nobody would know.’

Tears began to fall. Seeing him cry, Aroma cried too.

Skybright stood crying beside them and was about to speak when Dai-yu came in. She went out instead.

‘Why are you all crying on a festival day?’ Dai-yu asked with a smile. ‘Surely you haven’t been quarrelling over who gets the rice dumplings?’

Bao-yu and Aroma both gave a sudden little laugh.

‘Second Cousin won’t tell me,’ Dai-yu went on, ‘but I’ll find out if I ask you.’

She patted Aroma’s shoulder.

‘Do tell me, dear sister-in-law,’ she said, smiling. ‘You two must have had a quarrel. Tell your little sister, and I’ll make peace between you.’

Aroma pushed her away.

‘Miss Lin, what are you playing at? I’m only a maid, and you will keep talking nonsense.’

‘You may call yourself a maid. I treat you as my sister-in-law.’

‘Why must you give people something to say against her?’ Bao-yu asked. ‘They gossip quite enough already. She doesn’t need you to help them.’

‘Miss Lin, you don’t know what’s on my mind,’ Aroma said with a smile. ‘If only I could draw my last breath and have done with it.’

‘If you died, I don’t know about anyone else,’ said Dai-yu, smiling, ‘but I should be the first to cry myself to death.’

‘If you died, I’d become a monk,’ Bao-yu said with a smile.

‘Do behave yourself,’ Aroma said, laughing. ‘Why say such things again?’

Dai-yu held up two fingers and smiled with her lips pressed together.

‘That’s twice you’ve become a monk. From now on I shall keep count.’

Bao-yu knew she was referring to what he had said the other day. He merely smiled and let it pass. Presently, after Dai-yu had gone, someone came to say that Master Xue wished to see him, and he had to go. It turned out to be a drinking party. Unable to decline, he stayed until the company broke up.

By the time he returned that evening he was a little drunk. He made his unsteady way into his courtyard, where a couch and pillow had already been set out for enjoying the cool air. Someone lay asleep on the couch. Thinking it was Aroma, he sat on the edge and nudged her.

‘Is the pain any better?’

The girl rolled over and sat up.

‘Why must you start on me again?’

He looked: it was not Aroma but Skybright. He pulled her down to sit beside him.

‘You’re getting more and more spoilt,’ he said, smiling. ‘This morning you dropped a fan, and I said only a word or two, but you had all that to say to me. Never mind what you said to me: Aroma came to make peace out of kindness, and you dragged her into it too. Think it over. Was that right?’

‘It’s terribly hot,’ said Skybright. ‘Why all this pulling and tugging?

‘What would it look like if someone saw us? I’m not fit to sit here, anyway.’

‘If you know you’re not fit to sit here,’ Bao-yu said, smiling, ‘why were you lying here?’

Skybright had no answer. She gave a little snort of laughter.

‘It was all right until you came. Now you’re here, I’m not fit for it. Get up and let me go and bathe. Aroma and Musk have had their baths. I’ll call them out.’

‘I’ve been drinking quite a lot again,’ said Bao-yu, ‘and I need a wash too. Since you haven’t had yours, bring some water and let’s bathe together.’

Skybright waved her hand and laughed.

‘No, no! I wouldn’t dare get involved with you, sir. I remember when Emerald helped you bathe: it took two or three whole double-hours. Who knows what you were doing? We couldn’t very well go in. When you’d finished, we went to look. There was water right up the bed legs and pools of it even on the mat. Heaven knows how you managed it. We laughed for days. I haven’t the time to clear up after you. You needn’t bathe with me.

‘It’s cooler today, anyway. You had a bath earlier, and there’s no need for another. I’ll fetch a basin of water so you can wash your face and comb your hair. Faithful has just sent over a lot of fruit. It’s all cooling in the crystal jar. I’ll get them to serve you some.’

‘In that case you’re not to bathe either,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘Just wash your hands and bring out the fruit for us to eat.’

‘I’m so clumsy I even drop fans and break them,’ Skybright said, smiling. ‘How could I be fit to serve fruit? Suppose I broke a plate as well. That would be dreadful.’

‘Break it if you like,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘Things are only there for people to use. You like one thing and I like another; everyone has a different nature. A fan, for instance, is meant for fanning, but if you want to tear it up for fun, you can do that too. Only you mustn’t take your temper out on it. Cups and plates are meant to hold things, but if you enjoy the crash, you can even smash them on purpose. Only don’t take your temper out on them. That’s what it means to care for things.’

‘If that’s so,’ Skybright said, laughing, ‘give me a fan to tear. I like tearing them best.’

With a smile Bao-yu handed her one. She took it and, sure enough, ripped it in two. There came several more ripping sounds.

‘That sounds good!’ Bao-yu laughed beside her. ‘Tear it louder!’

Just then Musk came up.

‘Do spare yourselves a few sins,’ she said with a smile.

Bao-yu ran over, snatched the fan out of her hand and gave it to Skybright. Skybright tore that into several pieces too, and both of them laughed aloud.

‘What’s the meaning of this?’ said Musk. ‘Making sport with my things?’

‘Open the fan-box and choose another,’ Bao-yu said, laughing. ‘It’s nothing so precious.’

‘In that case, why not bring out the whole box and let her tear as many as she likes?’

‘Go and fetch it, then.’

‘I won’t commit that sin.

‘Her hands aren’t broken. Let her fetch it herself.’

Skybright leant against the couch, laughing.

‘I’m tired too. I’ll tear some more tomorrow.’

‘As the ancients said,’ Bao-yu laughed, ‘“A thousand gold pieces cannot buy a smile.”

‘How much could a few fans be worth?’

He called for Aroma as he spoke. She had just changed her clothes and came out. The little maid Melilot gathered up the torn fans, and they all enjoyed the evening air. We need not dwell on it.

At noon next day Lady Wang, Bao-chai, Dai-yu and the other girls were sitting in Grandmother Jia’s room when someone announced, ‘Miss Shi has arrived.’ Soon Shi Xiang-yun indeed appeared in the courtyard, followed by a crowd of maids and serving-women. Bao-chai, Dai-yu and the others hurried to the foot of the steps to meet her. There is no need to describe the affection of young cousins reunited after a month apart. They went inside and exchanged all the proper greetings.

‘It’s hot,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘Take off some of those outer clothes.’

Xiang-yun stood up at once to do so.

‘Why did you put all those things on?’ Lady Wang asked with a smile.

‘Second Aunt made me,’ Xiang-yun said, laughing. ‘Who wants to wear all this?’

‘You don’t know her, Aunt,’ Bao-chai put in with a smile. ‘She likes wearing other people’s clothes even better.

‘Do you remember when she was staying here in the third or fourth month last year? She put on Cousin Bao’s robe and boots and tied his brow-band on too. At first glance you’d have thought it was him, except for the two earrings. She stood behind that chair, and Grandmother kept calling, “Bao-yu, come here! The tassels on that lantern will shake dust into your eyes.” She only laughed and wouldn’t come. In the end none of us could keep from laughing, and Grandmother laughed too. “Well,” she said, “you look very handsome as a boy!”’

‘That’s nothing,’ Dai-yu said. ‘What about when we brought her here in the first month the year before last? She hadn’t been with us two days when it began to snow. I think Grandmother and Aunt had just come back from paying their respects to the ancestral portraits. Grandmother’s brand-new scarlet broadcloth cloak was lying there. The moment no one was looking, she put it on. It was far too big and far too long, so she tied a sash round the waist and went into the back courtyard to make a snowman with the maids. She fell headlong by the ditch and covered herself in mud and water.’

At these memories everyone laughed.

‘Nurse Zhou,’ Bao-chai asked the nurse with a smile, ‘is your young lady still as mischievous as ever?’

Nurse Zhou laughed too.

‘I don’t mind the mischief,’ Ying-chun said, smiling. ‘It’s the talking I object to. Even when she’s lying in bed, she goes on chattering, laughing for a bit and then talking again. Where does she find so much to say?’

‘I expect she’s better now,’ said Lady Wang. ‘A family came to look her over the other day. She’ll soon have a mother-in-law. Can she still behave like that?’

‘Are you staying today,’ Grandmother Jia asked, ‘or going home?’

‘Didn’t Your Old Ladyship see we’d brought her clothes?’ Nurse Zhou replied with a smile. ‘Of course she’s staying a couple of days.’

‘Is Cousin Bao-yu out?’ Xiang-yun asked.

‘She never thinks of anyone but Cousin Bao,’ Bao-chai said, laughing. ‘What a pair of innocents they are! You see, she hasn’t grown out of her mischief.’

‘You’re all growing up now,’ Grandmother Jia said. ‘You must stop using childhood names.’

She had scarcely spoken when Bao-yu appeared.

‘Cousin Yun’s here!’ he said, smiling. ‘Why didn’t you come when we sent for you the other day?’

‘Grandmother has just been telling this one off,’ said Lady Wang, ‘and here he comes calling her by name as well.’

‘Your cousin has something nice,’ Dai-yu told Xiang-yun. ‘He’s been waiting for you.’

‘What is it?’

‘Don’t believe her,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘It’s only a few days since I saw you, and you’ve grown taller still.’

‘Is Sister Aroma well?’ Xiang-yun asked with a smile.

‘Thank you for thinking of her,’ said Bao-yu.

‘I’ve brought her something nice.’

She took out a handkerchief tied in a knot.

‘What is it?’ Bao-yu asked. ‘You’d have done better to bring her a couple of those red-veined stone rings you sent the other day.’

‘And what do you call these?’ Xiang-yun said, laughing, and untied the bundle.

They looked. Sure enough, there were four of the same red-veined stone rings she had sent before.

‘Just look at the way her mind works,’ Dai-yu said, laughing. ‘When you sent ours the other day, wouldn’t it have saved trouble to send hers too? Now you’ve gone to all the bother of bringing them yourself. I thought it must be something new and wonderful, and here it’s only the same thing. You really are a silly girl.’

‘You’re the silly one!’ Xiang-yun replied with a smile. ‘Let me explain, and everyone can judge which of us is silly. When I send things to you, the messenger needn’t say a word. They’re brought in, and anyone can see they’re meant for the young ladies. But if I sent things for the maids as well, I’d first have to explain to him that this one was for one maid and that one for another. If he understood, well and good. But suppose he were a bit stupid and couldn’t remember the maids’ names? He’d mix everything up and talk such nonsense that even your presents would get muddled.

‘It would be all right if I sent a woman who knew them. But the other day I happened to send a page. How could I start telling him the maids’ names? Since I was coming anyway, wasn’t it simpler to bring their things myself?’

She laid out the four rings.

‘One for Sister Aroma, one for Sister Faithful, one for Sister Golden, and one for Sister Patience. Four different people. Could a page really remember all that so clearly?’

‘Quite clear, indeed!’ they all said, laughing.

‘Still as good at talking as ever,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘You’ll never give way to anyone.’

Dai-yu laughed coldly.

‘She needn’t be good at talking. Her gold qilin can talk for her.’

She rose and went out. Fortunately the others had not heard. Only Bao-chai pressed her lips together in a smile. Bao-yu, who had heard, was regretting his own ill-chosen words when he caught sight of Bao-chai’s smile and could not help smiling too. Seeing him smile, Bao-chai quickly got up and went to find Dai-yu and talk to her.

‘Have some tea and a rest,’ Grandmother Jia told Xiang-yun, ‘then go and see your cousins’ wives. It’s cool in the Garden too. Go and walk there with the girls.’

Xiang-yun agreed. She wrapped up three of the rings, rested a little, then rose to visit Xi-feng and the others. Her nurses and maids followed. She spent a while laughing and talking at Xi-feng’s, then went into Prospect Garden. After visiting Li Gong-cai and sitting with her briefly, she set off for the House of Green Delights to find Aroma.

She turned to her attendants.

‘You needn’t follow me. Go and see your friends and relations. Kingfisher can stay and look after me.’

Off they went to find their aunts and sisters-in-law, and soon only Xiang-yun and Kingfisher remained.

‘Why aren’t these lotuses in flower yet?’ Kingfisher asked.

‘It isn’t time.’

‘Are they the same as the ones in our pond, with petals in tiers?’

‘Theirs aren’t as good as ours.’

‘They have a pomegranate tree over there with four or five branches all covered in them, one tier rising out of another. It’s a wonder they can grow like that.’

‘Flowers and plants are just like people,’ Xiang-yun said. ‘If their vital energy flows strongly, they grow well.’

Kingfisher turned her face away.

‘I don’t believe that. If they’re like people, why have I never seen anyone with another head growing on top of their head?’

Xiang-yun could not help laughing.

‘I tell you not to talk, but you will.

‘How is anyone supposed to answer that? Everything between heaven and earth is born of the two vital energies, yin and yang. Whether it’s upright or perverse, strange or monstrous, all its countless transformations come from yin and yang working with or against each other. Many things only seem strange because people seldom see them. The principle is the same in the end.’

‘So ever since heaven and earth were made, right down to now, it’s all been yins and yangs?’ asked Kingfisher.

‘Silly creature!’ Xiang-yun laughed. ‘The more you talk, the more you talk through your backside. What do you mean, “all yins and yangs”? Do you think there’s a separate thing called a yin-and-yang? “Yin” and “yang” are two words for what is really one. When yang reaches its limit it becomes yin, and when yin reaches its limit it becomes yang. It isn’t that yin comes to an end and a new yang is born, or yang comes to an end and a new yin is born.’

‘You’ve muddled me completely! What is this yin-and-yang, without shape or shadow? Just tell me, miss: what does it look like?’

‘How could yin and yang have a shape? They’re simply vital energy. Things take form when they receive it.

‘Heaven, for example, is yang, and earth is yin. Water is yin and fire is yang. The sun is yang and the moon is yin.’

‘Yes, yes!’ Kingfisher said, smiling. ‘Now I understand. No wonder everyone calls the sun the “Great Yang”, and fortune-tellers call the moon the “Great Yin”. That’s the reason.’

‘Amitabha!’ Xiang-yun said, laughing. ‘You’ve understood at last.’

‘I can see how big things like that have yin and yang,’ said Kingfisher, ‘but surely mosquitoes and fleas and midges, flowers and grass, tiles and bricks don’t have them too?’

‘How could anything not have yin and yang?’ said Xiang-yun. ‘Even a leaf has them. The upper side, facing the sun, is yang; the lower side, turned away in the shade, is yin.’

Kingfisher nodded and smiled.

‘So that’s how it is. Now I understand.

‘But what about the fans in our hands? Which part is yang and which is yin?’

‘This front side is yang, and the back is yin.’

Kingfisher smiled and nodded again. She wanted to ask about a few more things, but could not think of any. Suddenly she looked down and saw the gold qilin hanging from Xiang-yun’s palace sash. She lifted it.

‘Miss, does this have yin and yang too?’

‘Among birds and beasts, the male is yang and the female is yin; the dam is yin, the sire is yang. Of course it does!’

‘Is this one male, then, or female?’

‘Even I don’t know that.’

‘Never mind that, then. But if everything has yin and yang, how is it that we people don’t?’

Xiang-yun spat towards her face.

‘Shameless creature! Walk on properly. Your questions get better and better!’

Kingfisher laughed.

‘Why shouldn’t you tell me that?

‘I know anyway. There’s no need to make it hard for me.’

‘What do you know?’ Xiang-yun asked, laughing.

‘You’re yang, miss, and I’m yin.’

Xiang-yun pressed her handkerchief to her mouth and laughed aloud.

‘There, I’ve got it right,’ said Kingfisher. ‘That’s why you’re laughing so much.’

‘Quite right. Quite right.’

‘The rule with people is that masters are yang and servants are yin. Do you think I don’t understand even that great principle?’

‘You understand very well,’ Xiang-yun said, laughing.

They walked on as they talked. Just as they reached the rose trellis, Xiang-yun said, ‘Look, someone’s dropped a piece of jewellery. It’s shining there, all gold.’

Kingfisher hurried over, picked it up and closed her hand round it.

‘Now we can tell yin from yang,’ she said, smiling.

First she examined Shi Xiang-yun’s qilin. Xiang-yun wanted to see what she had found, but Kingfisher would not open her hand.

‘It’s a treasure, miss. You mustn’t look. Where can it have come from? How strange! I’ve never seen anyone here with one of these.’

‘Give it to me and let me see,’ Xiang-yun said with a smile.

Kingfisher opened her hand.

‘There you are!’

Xiang-yun looked. It was a splendidly worked gold qilin, larger and more richly ornamented than her own. She took it in her palm and said nothing. While she stood lost in thought, Bao-yu came towards them.

‘What are you two doing out here in the sun?’ he asked with a smile.

‘Why haven’t you gone to find Aroma?’

Xiang-yun quickly hid the qilin.

‘We were just going. Let’s go together.’

They went into the House of Green Delights. Aroma was leaning on the railing by the steps to catch the breeze. Seeing Xiang-yun, she hurried down to meet her. Hand in hand, they smiled and exchanged news of all the time they had been apart.

When they had gone in and sat down, Bao-yu said with a smile, ‘You should have come sooner. I’ve got something nice, and I’ve been waiting especially for you.’

He began feeling about inside his clothes. After a long search he exclaimed, ‘Oh!’ and asked Aroma, ‘Did you put that thing away?’

‘What thing?’

‘The qilin I got the other day.’

‘You carry it about with you every day. Why ask me?’

Bao-yu clapped his hands.

‘Then it’s lost! Where am I to look for it?’

He was about to get up and search for it himself. Only now did Xiang-yun realise it was he who had dropped it.

‘When did you get a qilin?’ she asked with a smile.

‘It took some getting hold of the other day. I’ve no idea when I lost it. What a fool I am!’

‘Fortunately it’s only a plaything, and look at the state you’re in!’

She opened her hand.

‘Have a look. Is this it?’

At the sight of it Bao-yu was overjoyed, and began to say…
