Chapter 57 Clever Nightingale’s words test impetuous Bao-yu’s love; Kindly Aunt Xue comforts lovelorn Dai-yu with tender words

Chapter end

Now when Bao-yu heard that Lady Wang had sent for him, he hurried to the front of the house. She wanted to take him to call on Lady Zhen. Delighted, he changed his clothes at once and went with her. The appearance and circumstances of the Zhen household differed little from those of the Rong and Ning mansions; in one or two respects they were rather grander. He made careful inquiries, and there was indeed a Bao-yu. Lady Zhen kept them to a meal, and they did not return until the day was over. Only then was Bao-yu convinced. Back at home that evening, Lady Wang ordered a first-rate banquet and engaged a celebrated troupe to perform a full play, inviting Lady Zhen and her daughter. Two days later the mother and daughter left without a further farewell to return to the family’s official posting. Of this we need say no more.

One day, after seeing that Xiang-yun was gradually recovering, Bao-yu went to visit Dai-yu. She had just settled down for her afternoon nap, and he dared not disturb her. Nightingale was doing needlework in the covered walk, so he went over to ask her, ‘Was her cough any better last night?’

‘A little better.’

‘Buddha be praised!’ he said with a smile. ‘If only she would get well!’

‘So you’ve taken to invoking Buddha too!’ Nightingale laughed. ‘That is something new.’

‘As they say, “A desperate patient will try any doctor,”’ Bao-yu replied with a laugh.

As he spoke, he noticed that she had on a thinly padded jacket of ink-splashed silk damask, with only a lined blue satin waistcoat over it. He reached out and felt her clothes.

‘You’re wearing so little, and sitting right in a draught. The wind has a bite to it, and there’s sickness about. If you fall ill too, that will make things worse.’

‘From now on we may talk, but keep your hands to yourself,’ said Nightingale. ‘We’re growing older, not younger. It looks improper if people see us. Those wretched creatures say quite enough about you behind your back, but you never take any notice. You go on just as you did when you were little. How can that do? Miss Lin is always telling us not to laugh and joke with you. Haven’t you noticed how she keeps her distance from you lately? She’s afraid she can’t keep far enough away.’

She rose as she spoke and carried her sewing into another room.

Bao-yu felt as though a basin of cold water had been poured over his heart. He stared at the bamboos for a while, quite stupefied. Then Mamma Zhu came to dig up shoots and tend the bamboo stems, and he wandered out in a daze. His wits had deserted him; he scarcely knew anything. He sat down at random on a rock and gazed into space, tears falling before he knew it. He remained there long enough for five or six meals, turning a thousand thoughts over in his mind without knowing what he could do.

Snowgoose happened to pass that way on her return from fetching ginseng from Lady Wang’s rooms. Turning her head, she saw someone sitting on a rock beneath the peach blossom, his cheek propped on his hand as he stared before him. It was Bao-yu.

‘It’s so cold,’ she thought in bewilderment. ‘What is he doing here all alone? People with old complaints always have them come back in spring. Perhaps he’s having another fit of silliness.’

With this thought she went over, crouched beside him and asked with a smile, ‘What are you doing here?’

Bao-yu looked up to find Snowgoose before him.

‘And what have you come looking for me for? Aren’t you a girl too? If she wants to avoid suspicion and won’t let you have anything to do with me, why come after me? If someone sees you, won’t there be more gossip? Go home, quickly.’

Snowgoose supposed that Dai-yu had been upsetting him again, and could only return to the house.

Dai-yu was still asleep, so Snowgoose handed the ginseng to Nightingale.

‘What is Her Ladyship doing?’ Nightingale asked.

‘She was having her afternoon nap too. That’s why I had to wait so long. Listen, Nightingale, I have something funny to tell you. While I was waiting for Her Ladyship, I sat talking to Silver in the servants’ room. Then Aunt Zhao beckoned to me. I thought she had something to tell me, but it turned out she’d asked Her Ladyship’s leave to go out and sit up at her brother’s wake. The funeral procession is tomorrow. The little maid who goes with her, little Ji-xiang, had nothing to wear, and Aunt Zhao wanted to borrow my pale-blue satin jacket. I thought, they have a couple of things to wear just like anyone else. They’re going somewhere dirty and don’t want to soil their own clothes, so they borrow other people’s. I wouldn’t have minded so much if mine got dirty, but I thought, when has she ever done anything for us? So I said, “Miss Lin has Nightingale keep all my clothes and hair ornaments for me. I’ll have to tell her first, and then we’ll have to ask Miss Lin.

‘“Miss Lin is ill, too. It will be such a business, and keep you from going out. You’d better borrow from someone else.”’

‘You cunning little thing!’ Nightingale laughed. ‘You don’t want to lend it, so you put it on Miss Lin and me, and no one can blame you. Is she leaving now, or waiting until tomorrow morning?’

‘She’s going now. I expect she’s left already.’

Nightingale nodded.

‘Miss Lin hasn’t woken yet,’ Snowgoose went on. ‘Who can have upset Bao-yu? He’s sitting out there crying.’

Nightingale hurriedly asked where he was.

‘Under the peach blossom, behind Drenched Blossoms Pavilion.’

Nightingale put down her sewing at once and told Snowgoose to listen carefully in case Dai-yu called.

‘If she asks for me, say I’ll be back in a moment.’

She left Naiad’s House and went straight to find Bao-yu. Coming up to him, she said with a smile, ‘I only said those few words for everyone’s good, and you run out here into the wind to cry in a temper. You’ll make yourself ill and frighten me.’

‘Who’s in a temper?’ Bao-yu said quickly, smiling. ‘I thought what you said made sense. If that’s what you say, other people must be saying it too. Gradually none of you will have anything to do with me. I was thinking of that, and feeling sorry for myself.’

Nightingale sat down close beside him.

‘Just now you walked away when I was only talking to you,’ he said with a smile. ‘Why come and sit so close to me now?’

‘Have you forgotten? A few days ago, when you and Miss Lin were talking, Aunt Zhao walked straight in. I’ve just heard she’s out, so I’ve come to ask you something.

‘The other day you only got as far as saying “bird’s nests” to her and then stopped. You never mentioned it again. I’ve been meaning to ask you.’

‘It was nothing very important. I was only thinking that Cousin Bao-chai is a guest here too. Once Dai-yu has begun taking bird’s nests, she mustn’t stop, but it’s taking rather too much for granted to keep asking Bao-chai for them. It wasn’t convenient to ask Mother, but I dropped a hint to Grandmother. I expect she spoke to Cousin Feng.

‘I started to tell Dai-yu, but never finished. Now I hear you’re given a tael of bird’s nests every day, so that settles it.’

‘So you were the one who spoke!’ said Nightingale. ‘Thank you for taking the trouble. We were wondering what had suddenly made Her Old Ladyship think of sending a tael every day. Now we know.’

‘If she gets into the habit of taking them every day and keeps it up for two or three years, she’ll be well,’ said Bao-yu, smiling.

‘She may get used to them here, but where will she find the money for such things when she goes home next year?’

Bao-yu started.

‘Who? Goes home where?’

‘Your cousin is going home to Suzhou.’

‘Now you’re talking nonsense again,’ he said with a laugh. ‘Suzhou may be her native place, but she came here because Uncle and Aunt had died and there was no one to look after her. Who would she go back to next year? That shows you’re making it up.’

Nightingale gave a cold laugh.

‘You think very little of other people. Do you suppose yours is the only large clan with plenty of people in it? Does everyone outside the Jia family have just one father and mother, with no other relations at all?

‘When Miss Lin came here, Her Old Ladyship felt sorry for her because she was so young. She had uncles, but uncles aren’t one’s own parents, so she was brought here to stay a few years. When she’s old enough to marry, naturally she’ll be sent back to the Lins. A daughter of the Lin family can hardly live with you Jias all her life. Even if the Lins were too poor to eat, they’ve been scholars and officials for generations. They would never leave one of their own in a relation’s house for people to laugh at them. So it will be next spring at the earliest, or autumn at the latest. Even if you don’t send her back, someone from the Lin family is bound to come for her. The other night she told me to tell you to sort out all the playthings she gave you when you were children and return them. She’s packing up the things you gave her too.’

Bao-yu felt as though a thunderclap had burst directly above his head. Nightingale waited to see what answer he would make, but he said nothing.

Just then Skybright came looking for him.

‘Her Old Ladyship wants you. Who would have guessed you were here?’

‘He’s been asking about Miss Lin’s illness,’ Nightingale said with a smile. ‘I’ve been telling him about it all this time, but he won’t believe me. You’d better take him away.’

With that she went back to her room.

Skybright saw him staring blankly, his head covered in hot sweat and his whole face swollen and purple. She hastily took him by the hand and led him all the way back to the House of Green Delights. Aroma was alarmed at the sight of him. She thought he must have caught some seasonal sickness, with the wind striking him while he was sweating. But the fever was the least of it. His eyes had become fixed and staring, and saliva ran from the corners of his mouth without his knowing it. Given a pillow, he lay down; helped upright, he sat; given tea, he drank. Everyone grew frantic, but they dared not rush straight to Grandmother Jia with the news. First they sent out for Nurse Li.

Nurse Li soon arrived. She watched him for a while, but got no answer to her questions. She felt his wrist, then pinched the hollow above his upper lip hard, twice. Her fingers left deep marks, yet he seemed to feel no pain.

‘This is dreadful!’ was all Nurse Li said. Then, with a great cry, she threw her arms round him and began to wail.

Aroma anxiously pulled at her.

‘You’ve seen him now, nurse. Is it serious or not? Tell us, so we can report to Her Old Ladyship and Her Ladyship. Why are you the first to start crying?’

Nurse Li beat the bed and knocked the pillow over.

‘There’s no saving him! All the trouble I’ve taken all my life has been for nothing!’

Aroma and the others had summoned her because she was old and experienced. Hearing her speak like this, they believed every word and began to cry as well.

Skybright then told Aroma what had happened earlier. Aroma hurried to Naiad’s House, where Nightingale was helping Dai-yu take her medicine. Forgetting everything else, she went straight up to her.

‘What have you been saying to our Bao-yu? Go and look at him. You can report to Her Old Ladyship yourself. I’m having nothing more to do with it!’

She sat down on a chair. The sight of Aroma’s tear-stained face, so full of distress and anger, and her utterly altered manner alarmed Dai-yu. She hurriedly asked what was wrong. Aroma took a moment to recover herself, then answered through her tears.

‘I don’t know what Her Ladyship Nightingale has been telling him. That fool’s eyes are fixed, his hands and feet are cold, he won’t speak, and he can’t even feel Nurse Li pinching him. He’s more than half dead! Even Nurse Li says there’s no saving him, and she’s there crying at the top of her voice. For all I know, he’s dead by now!’

Hearing this, Dai-yu thought that Nurse Li was an experienced old woman. If she said there was no saving him, then there could be none.

With a sudden retch she choked up every drop of the medicine in her stomach. Then came several violent, agonising bouts of coughing that seemed to wrench her bowels and scour her lungs, set her stomach alight and fan her liver into flame. Her face was red, her hair disordered, her eyes swollen and her veins standing out. She gasped so hard she could not lift her head. Nightingale hurried to pat her back. After lying against her pillow, panting for some time, Dai-yu pushed her away.

‘Don’t pat me. You’d do better to bring a rope and strangle me!’

‘I didn’t say anything,’ Nightingale cried. ‘It was only a few words in fun, and he took them seriously.’

‘Don’t you know him yet?’ said Aroma. ‘That idiot is always taking jokes seriously.’

‘Whatever you said, go and explain it at once,’ Dai-yu told her. ‘Perhaps that will bring him round.’

Nightingale hastily got down from the bed and went with Aroma to the House of Green Delights.

They found Grandmother Jia, Lady Wang and the others already there. At the sight of Nightingale, Grandmother Jia’s eyes blazed.

‘You little hussy! What have you been saying to him?’

‘Nothing, ma’am. Only a few words in fun.’

But the moment Bao-yu saw Nightingale he gave a cry and burst into tears. Only then did everyone breathe more freely. Grandmother Jia caught hold of Nightingale. Supposing that she had offended him, she held her there and told Bao-yu to hit her. Instead, he seized Nightingale himself and would not let go for anything.

‘If you’re going, you must take me too.’

No one understood. Only after questioning them closely did they discover that it had all come from Nightingale’s joke about going back to Suzhou.

‘I thought something terrible had happened,’ Grandmother Jia said, weeping. ‘And it was only those few words!’ She turned to Nightingale. ‘You’ve always been such a clever, sensible girl. You know what a streak of foolishness he has. Why should you deceive him for no reason?’

‘Bao-yu takes things to heart,’ Aunt Xue said soothingly. ‘And Miss Lin came here when she was so little. The two of them have grown up together. Their closeness is quite different from his with the other girls.

‘To announce out of the blue that one of them is going away would hurt even a cold-hearted grown person, let alone a simple child who feels things so deeply. It’s nothing very serious. You and my sister must set your minds at rest, ma’am. A dose or two of medicine and he’ll be well.’

As she spoke, someone announced that Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife and Shan Da-liang’s wife had come to see the young master.

‘How thoughtful of them,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘Let them come and see him.’

At the sound of the name Lin, Bao-yu began thrashing about on the bed.

‘This is terrible! The Lins have come to fetch them. Drive them out at once!’

‘Drive them out!’ Grandmother Jia said hastily. Then she tried to comfort him. ‘They aren’t from the Lin family. The Lin family are all dead. There’s no one to come and fetch her. You needn’t worry.’

‘I don’t care who they are!’ Bao-yu cried. ‘No one is allowed to be called Lin except Cousin Lin!’

‘No one called Lin has come. I’ve driven everyone called Lin away.’

As she spoke, she ordered the others, ‘Don’t let Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife into the Garden again. And none of you is to say the word “Lin”. Be good children and do as I say!’

They all hastened to agree, not daring to laugh. Then Bao-yu caught sight of a Western gold mechanical boat on the curio shelves. He pointed to it and cried wildly, ‘There’s the boat that’s come to fetch them! It’s moored over there!’

Grandmother Jia told them to take it down at once. Aroma hurried to do so. Bao-yu held out his hand for it, and she passed it to him. He tucked it under the quilt.

‘Now they can’t go!’ he said with a smile.

All the while he kept his desperate grip on Nightingale. Presently the doctor was announced, and Grandmother Jia ordered him to be brought in at once. Lady Wang, Aunt Xue, Bao-chai and the others withdrew for the moment to the inner room, while Grandmother Jia sat upright beside Bao-yu.

Doctor Wang entered, saw the crowd, and hastened forward to pay his respects to Grandmother Jia. He took Bao-yu’s hand and felt his pulse for a while. Nightingale could only hang her head. The doctor did not understand what it meant either. He rose and said, ‘The young gentleman’s mind has been clouded by sudden grief. The ancients said, “There are different forms of phlegm-clouded consciousness.

‘“In some, qi and blood are depleted and weak, and food cannot be dissolved, so that phlegm clouds the mind. In others, anger causes phlegm to envelop the mind and obscure it. In others, sudden grief causes an obstruction.” This too is a case of phlegm-clouded consciousness, brought on by sudden grief. It is only a temporary obstruction, somewhat milder than the other forms of phlegm-clouding.’

‘Just tell me whether it’s dangerous,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘Who wants to recite medical books with you?’

Doctor Wang bent forward at once and smiled.

‘No danger, ma’am. No danger.’

‘Are you quite sure?’

‘Quite sure. I will answer for it myself.’

‘In that case, please go and sit outside and write a prescription. If he gets well after taking it, I’ll prepare a handsome extra fee and have him bring it to you himself and kowtow. If you let him come to harm, I’ll send men to pull down the hall of the Imperial Medical Academy.’

‘You are too kind, ma’am, too kind,’ said Doctor Wang, bowing and smiling. He had heard the promise of a handsome extra fee, to be delivered by Bao-yu with a kowtow, and was still protesting that he did not deserve such an honour. He had quite missed her subsequent joke about pulling down the Imperial Medical Academy, and was still saying, ‘Too kind.’ Grandmother Jia and the others began to laugh instead.

Soon the medicine was prepared according to his prescription and given to Bao-yu. He did indeed seem calmer than before. But nothing would make him release Nightingale. If she left, he said, it would mean she was going back to Suzhou.

Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang could do nothing but tell Nightingale to stay with him and send Amber to look after Dai-yu in her place. Dai-yu kept sending Snowgoose for news, and learnt everything that was happening there. She sighed to herself. Fortunately everyone knew that Bao-yu had always been a little foolish, and that he and Dai-yu had been close since childhood. Nightingale’s joke was an ordinary enough one, and Bao-yu’s illness was nothing unprecedented. No one suspected anything else.

By evening Bao-yu was a little easier, and only then did Grandmother Jia, Lady Wang and the others return to their rooms. Several times that night they still sent to ask after him. Nurse Li led Nurse Song and several other elderly attendants in keeping careful watch, while Nightingale, Aroma and Skybright stayed beside him night and day. Sometimes when he fell asleep he would wake in terror, crying that Dai-yu had gone, or that someone had come to fetch her. Each time Nightingale had to soothe him before he would settle. Grandmother Jia also ordered various secret preparations from the imperial pharmacy, including Pills to Expel Evil and Guard the Spirit and Powder to Open the Apertures and Free the Spirit, to be given as directed. The next day he took Doctor Wang’s medicine again and gradually improved.

Bao-yu now understood what had happened. Afraid that Nightingale would go back, he sometimes pretended to be distraught. Nightingale had deeply regretted her actions from that first day, and did not resent the hard work of tending him day and night. Aroma and the others were at ease again.

‘You caused it all,’ they told her with a laugh, ‘and you’ve had to cure it. Did you ever see anyone like our idiot? He hears the wind and thinks it’s raining. What’s to become of him?’

Let us leave them there for the moment.

Xiang-yun’s illness had now passed, and she came to see him every day. Once he had his wits about him, she acted out his wild behaviour for him, making him bury his face in the pillow and laugh. He had known nothing of what he was doing at first, and even now scarcely believed it when people told him.

When they were alone, with Nightingale beside him, Bao-yu took her hand again.

‘Why did you frighten me?’

‘I was only teasing you, and you took it seriously.’

‘But what you said was so reasonable and convincing. How could it have been a joke?’

‘I made it all up,’ she said, smiling. ‘There really is no one left in the Lin family, except perhaps some very distant relations. None of the clan lives in Suzhou now. They’re scattered about the provinces, with no settled home. Even if someone did come for her, Her Old Ladyship would never let her go.’

‘Even if Grandmother would, I wouldn’t.’

‘Would you really refuse?’ Nightingale asked with a smile. ‘Perhaps it’s only talk. You’re growing up now. You’re even engaged. In another two or three years, when you’ve married, who else will you have eyes for?’

Bao-yu was startled again.

‘Who’s engaged?

‘Engaged to whom?’

‘Before the New Year I heard Her Old Ladyship say she wanted to settle a match with Miss Bao-qin,’ Nightingale said with a smile. ‘Why else would she be so fond of her?’

‘Everyone calls me a fool, but you’re a bigger fool than I am,’ Bao-yu said with a laugh. ‘It was only a joke. She’s already promised to Academician Mei’s family. If I really were engaged to her, would you find me like this? When I swore those oaths and tried to smash this wretched thing before, didn’t you try to stop me and say I was mad? I’ve only just got better, and you start provoking me again.’

He ground his teeth as he spoke.

‘I wish I could die this very instant and burst my heart open for you all to see. Then let all of me, skin and bones, turn to ash. No, ash still leaves a trace. Better to turn into smoke. But smoke can gather and still be seen. It would have to be scattered in every direction at once by a great wild wind. Only then would it be right!’

Tears rolled down his face again.

Nightingale hastened to cover his mouth and wipe his tears away. Then she hurriedly smiled and explained.

‘Don’t upset yourself. I was worried, and that’s why I tested you.’

Bao-yu was more bewildered than ever.

‘What were you worried about?’

‘You know I don’t belong to the Lin family,’ she said, smiling. ‘I was one of the maids here, like Aroma and Faithful, only I happened to be given to Miss Lin.

‘And she happens to be so fond of me, ten times fonder than of the ones she brought from Suzhou. Neither of us can do without the other for a moment. What worries me now is that if she should go, I’d have to go with her. My whole family is here. If I stayed, I’d be betraying all our affection for each other; if I went, I’d be leaving my own family. I couldn’t see my way out, so I made up that story to ask you. How was I to know you’d take on like such a fool?’

‘So that was what worried you!’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘That’s why I call you a fool. Never worry about it again. I’ll give you one plain answer: while we live, we’ll live together; when we’re dead, we’ll turn to ash and smoke together. What do you say?’

Nightingale began quietly making her own plans. Just then someone came to announce that Master Huan and young Master Lan had called to ask after him.

‘Tell them I’m obliged to them,’ said Bao-yu, ‘but I’ve just gone to sleep, so they needn’t come in.’

The woman went off to give his message.

‘You’re better now,’ Nightingale said with a smile. ‘It’s time you let me go back and see how mine is doing.’

‘Of course.

‘I meant to tell you to go yesterday, but forgot. I’m quite well now. Off you go.’

Nightingale began packing her bedding, toilet box and other things.

‘I noticed two or three mirrors in your dressing case,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘Leave me the little flower-patterned mirror. I can keep it beside my pillow and look into it while I’m lying down. It will be light to carry when I go out, too.’

Nightingale could only leave it for him. She had her things sent ahead, then said goodbye to the others and returned to Naiad’s House.

The reports of Bao-yu’s condition over the past few days had made Dai-yu still more unwell and set her weeping several times. Now that Nightingale had returned, she asked what had happened. Learning that he was much better, she sent Amber back to attend Grandmother Jia.

That night, when everyone was quiet and Nightingale had undressed and lain down, she said softly to Dai-yu, with a smile, ‘Bao-yu does have a true heart. Only hearing we were going away was enough to put him in that state.’

Dai-yu said nothing. After a long pause, Nightingale went on, as though talking to herself.

‘Better to stay still than to make a move. This is a good enough family. Everything else is easily found, but to have grown up together from childhood, each knowing the other’s temper and ways, that’s the rare thing.’

‘Pah!’ said Dai-yu. ‘Haven’t these last few days worn you out? Why not rest while you can, instead of chewing over such filthy nonsense?’

‘It isn’t idle nonsense,’ Nightingale said with a smile. ‘I’m speaking out of real concern for you. I’ve worried about you for years. With no parents and no brothers, who is there to care whether you’re warm or cold? The important thing is to settle your marriage while Her Old Ladyship is still clear-headed and strong.

‘As the saying goes, “Vigour in old age is like a spring chill or autumn heat.” If anything should happen to Her Old Ladyship, your marriage would still be arranged, but precious time might have been lost, and you might not get what you wanted. There are plenty of noblemen’s sons and grandsons of princes, but which of them doesn’t have three wives and five concubines, looking east today and west tomorrow? Even a heavenly beauty would be forgotten after three or five nights. Some turn against their wives over a concubine or a maid until they’re enemies. It’s a little better if a girl has people with influence in her own family. But for someone in your position, each day Her Old Ladyship is here is a day of protection. Without her, you’d be at their mercy. That’s why it matters to make up your mind. You’re a sensible girl. Haven’t you heard the saying, “Ten thousand taels of gold are easily found; one understanding heart is hard to come by”?’

‘Has this girl gone mad today?’ said Dai-yu.

‘She’s only been away a few days, and suddenly she’s a different person. Tomorrow I shall tell Her Old Ladyship to take you back. I daren’t keep you any longer.’

‘What I’m saying is for your good,’ Nightingale replied with a smile. ‘I’m only asking you to bear it in mind, not to do anything wrong. Why tell Her Old Ladyship and get me into trouble? What good would that do?’

With that she went to sleep. For all Dai-yu’s words, what she had heard had grieved her deeply. Once Nightingale was asleep, she wept the whole night, only dozing off towards daybreak. The next day she forced herself to wash and rinse her mouth and took a little bird’s-nest congee. Grandmother Jia and the others then came in person to see her and gave her a great many instructions.

It was now Aunt Xue’s birthday. Everyone, from Grandmother Jia down, had congratulatory gifts for her. Dai-yu too had long since prepared needlework gifts of two kinds and sent them over. A short play had been engaged for the day, and Grandmother Jia, Lady Wang and the others were invited. Only Bao-yu and Dai-yu were unable to go. When the party broke up, Grandmother Jia and the others stopped to see them both again on their way back to their rooms. The following day the Xue household had Xue Ke entertain the business staff to a whole day’s drinking. The bustle lasted three or four days before everything was over.

Aunt Xue had noticed Xing Xiu-yan’s dignified looks and steady manner. She was a poor girl, accustomed to a simple wooden hairpin and a homespun skirt, and Aunt Xue thought of proposing her as a wife for Xue Pan. But Xue Pan’s habitual frivolity and extravagance made her afraid that he would ruin someone else’s daughter.

While she was hesitating, she suddenly remembered that Xue Ke was unmarried. The two of them seemed a couple made by Heaven and Earth, and she discussed it with Xi-feng.

‘You know how contrary my mother-in-law can be, Aunt,’ Xi-feng said with a sigh. ‘Let me work out a way to approach her.’

When Grandmother Jia came to see her, Xi-feng said, ‘Aunt Xue has a favour to ask of you, Grannie, but she’s rather shy of mentioning it.’

Grandmother Jia asked at once what it was, and Xi-feng explained the proposed marriage.

‘Why should she be shy about that?’ Grandmother Jia said with a laugh. ‘It’s an excellent idea. I’ll speak to your mother-in-law. She can hardly refuse me.’

On returning to her rooms, she immediately sent for Lady Xing and insisted on acting as matchmaker.

Lady Xing thought it over. The Xues came of a good family and were very rich at present; Xue Ke was a handsome young man, and Grandmother Jia was determined to arrange the match. She might as well turn the proposal to her own advantage, and agreed. Grandmother Jia was delighted and sent for Aunt Xue at once. When the two women met, there was naturally a great deal of polite self-deprecation on both sides. Lady Xing immediately sent word to Xing Zhong and his wife. They had come there to depend on her. How could they refuse? They were already declaring that nothing could be better.

‘I do love meddling in other people’s affairs,’ Grandmother Jia said with a laugh. ‘There, I've settled another matter. I wonder how much I'll get in matchmaker's fees.’

‘Of course you must have your fee,’ Aunt Xue answered, laughing. ‘Though if we carried in a hundred thousand taels of silver, I doubt you’d care for it. There’s only one thing: since you are the chief sponsor of the marriage, we ought to have someone else as well.’

‘We may be short of other things,’ Grandmother Jia said, laughing, ‘but our household can still produce a couple of good-for-nothings with useless hands and feet.’

She sent for You-shi and her daughter-in-law. When Grandmother Jia explained the matter, everyone hastened to offer congratulations.

‘You know all our family’s customs,’ Grandmother Jia told You-shi. ‘We never have two sets of in-laws quarrelling over gifts and precedence. You are to act for me in settling things between them. Be neither too stingy nor too extravagant. See that both families are properly provided for, and report back to me.’

You-shi promptly agreed. Aunt Xue was overjoyed. As soon as she returned home, she had a formal invitation written and sent after them to the Ning mansion. You-shi knew Lady Xing’s temper very well and had not wanted to take the business on, but with Grandmother Jia giving her the charge in person she could only accept. She would have to feel her way and act according to Lady Xing’s wishes. Aunt Xue, who would agree to almost anything, would be easier to deal with. Let us leave that for now.

Now that Aunt Xue had settled Xing Xiu-yan’s marriage into her family, the whole household knew about it. Lady Xing had intended to take Xiu-yan out of the Garden to live elsewhere, but Grandmother Jia objected.

‘What harm can there be? The young couple can’t see each other. There’s only Aunt Xue, and Xue Ke’s elder cousin and younger sister. What can be wrong with that? Besides, with the girls all together, it’s a good chance for them to grow close.’

Only then did Lady Xing give up the idea.

Xue Ke and Xiu-yan had seen each other once on their earlier journey, and in all probability both were pleased with the match. Xiu-yan was rather more constrained than before, however, and found it awkward to sit and chat with Bao-chai and the other girls. Xiang-yun’s fondness for teasing made her still more uncomfortable. Fortunately she was educated and knew how to conduct herself. She had a young woman’s proper reserve, but was not one of those frivolous creatures forever pretending to be shy and ashamed.

Ever since meeting her, Bao-chai had seen how poor her family was. Other people’s parents were venerable and worthy; hers were a pair steeped in drink, with little regard for their daughter. Lady Xing’s interest in her was a matter of appearances, not real affection. Xiu-yan herself was refined and dignified, while Ying-chun was a corpse that still breathed. She could scarcely look after herself, let alone anyone else. If Xiu-yan lacked any of the ordinary things a girl needed, no one saw to it, and she would not ask. Bao-chai often took pains to help her secretly. She did not dare let Lady Xing know, for fear of provoking suspicion and gossip.

Now, beyond anyone’s expectations, a strange turn of fate had brought about this match. In her heart Xiu-yan had chosen Bao-chai first, and Xue Ke only afterwards. Xiu-yan still talked with Bao-chai now and then, and Bao-chai continued to address her as a sister.

One day Bao-chai was going to visit Dai-yu when she met Xiu-yan on the same errand. She called her over with a smile, and the two went behind a rock face.

‘It’s still so cold,’ Bao-chai asked, smiling. ‘Why have you changed into lined clothes already?’

Xiu-yan lowered her head without answering. Bao-chai knew there must be some reason, and asked again, still smiling.

‘You can’t have had this month’s allowance. Is Feng becoming so careless now?’

‘No, she remembers and gives it on the right day. But Aunt sent word that I couldn’t use two taels of silver a month, and I was to save one and send it out to Father and Mother. Whatever I needed, she said, Cousin Ying-chun had things, and I could manage with some of hers. But you know what Cousin Ying-chun is like, so quiet and unobservant. She may not say anything if I use her things, but which of her nurses and maids is easy to deal with? Which of them hasn’t a sharp tongue?

‘Though I live there, I scarcely dare ask them to do anything. Every three or five days I have to give them money for wine and cakes to keep them in good humour. Even two taels a month weren’t enough, and now I’ve lost one of them. The other day I quietly had someone pawn my padded clothes for a few strings of cash to spend.’

Bao-chai frowned and sighed.

‘And the whole Mei family happens to be away at the Hanlin scholar’s post. They won’t come back until the year after next.

‘If they were here, Bao-qin could be married, and then we could discuss your wedding. Once you were out of this place, your troubles would be over. But until his sister is married, Xue Ke would never venture to marry first. It is a difficulty. If we wait another two years, I’m afraid you’ll worry yourself ill. I’ll discuss it with Mother. If people bully you, try to bear with them, and whatever you do, don’t make yourself ill with worry. You might as well send the other tael to your parents tomorrow too, and have done with it. Then everyone can be easy in their minds. And you needn’t keep giving those people things to eat for nothing. Let them make their sharp remarks. If it becomes too much to listen to, walk away. If you’re short of anything, don’t stand on the timid pride of a girl from a small family. Come to me.

‘It isn’t only because of the marriage. We’ve been friends since you first came. If you’re afraid of gossip, just send your little maid to tell me quietly.’

Xiu-yan bowed her head in assent. Bao-chai pointed to a green jade pendant hanging from her skirt.

‘Who gave you that?’

‘Cousin Tan-chun.’

Bao-chai nodded and smiled.

‘She saw that everyone had one but you, and was afraid you might be laughed at. That’s why she gave it to you.

‘It shows how clever and thoughtful she is. But there’s something else you ought to know. These ornaments belong to the young ladies of great official and wealthy families. Look at me from head to foot: do you see any of that costly, useless finery? Seven or eight years ago I wore such things too, but circumstances are no longer what they were, and I’ve cut out whatever I could.

‘When you come into our family, there may well be a whole chest of these useless things. But we can’t compare with the Jias now. We must make plainness and living within our station our rule in everything, and not compete with them.’

‘Since you say so, I’ll take it off when I get back,’ Xiu-yan said with a smile.

‘You take me much too literally!’ Bao-chai said quickly, laughing.

‘She gave it to you out of kindness. Won’t she wonder what’s wrong if you don’t wear it? I only happened to mention it. Just bear it in mind for the future.’

Xiu-yan agreed again, then asked, ‘Where are you going now?’

‘To Naiad’s House. You go back and have your maid bring me the pawn ticket. I’ll have the clothes quietly redeemed, and send them to you quietly this evening. Then you can wear them in the mornings and evenings. It would be serious if you caught a chill in this wind. But where did you pawn them?’

‘It’s called Hengshu Pawnshop, on West Drum Tower Street.’

‘So it’s all within the family!’ Bao-chai laughed. ‘If the clerks find out, they’ll be saying, “The clothes have arrived before their owner!”’

Xiu-yan understood that the shop was funded by the Xues. She blushed and smiled in spite of herself, and the two parted. Bao-chai went on to Naiad’s House, where she found her mother visiting Dai-yu and chatting with her.

‘When did you come, Mother?’ Bao-chai asked with a smile. ‘I never knew you were here.’

‘I’ve been busy day after day and haven’t come to see either Bao-yu or her. I’ve visited them both today. They’re both better now.’

Dai-yu quickly asked Bao-chai to sit down.

‘There really is no imagining how things will turn out,’ she said to her. ‘Who would have thought that Aunt and my elder uncle’s wife would become in-laws too?’

‘My child,’ said Aunt Xue, ‘what do you girls know of such things? As the old saying goes, “A marriage a thousand li apart is joined by a single thread.” There’s an Old Man under the Moon who governs marriages. He settles them beforehand, secretly tying the two people’s feet together with a red thread. Even if their families are separated by seas and countries, even if they have a hereditary feud, some chance will eventually make them husband and wife.

‘It’s always beyond anyone’s reckoning. The parents and the two people may all be willing; they may have been together year after year, so that everyone thinks the match is settled. But if the Old Man under the Moon hasn’t tied his red thread, they’ll never come together. Take the two of you: no one knows whether your future husbands are here before your eyes or beyond the mountains and seas.’

‘Only Mother would bring us into it the moment she opens her mouth,’ said Bao-chai. She nestled against her mother and laughed. ‘Let’s go.’

‘Look at her!’ Dai-yu said, laughing. ‘So grown up! Away from you, Aunt, she’s the most experienced person alive. The moment she sees you, she wants to be a little girl again.’

Aunt Xue stroked Bao-chai and sighed as she turned to Dai-yu.

‘She’s just like Feng with Her Old Ladyship. When there’s serious business, I consult her. When there isn’t, thank goodness she’s here to cheer me up. Whatever worries I have, seeing her like this sends them all away.’

Dai-yu sighed, tears coming into her eyes.

‘And she has to do it here, just to hurt someone who has no mother. She does it on purpose in front of me.’

‘Listen to her, Mother!’ Bao-chai laughed. ‘She’s the mischievous one, and she says I’m behaving like a child.’

‘You can hardly blame her for being sad,’ said Aunt Xue. ‘Poor child, with neither father nor mother. After all, she has no one of her own.’ She stroked Dai-yu too, and smiled. ‘Don’t cry, my dear.

‘You’re sad because you see me loving your cousin. You don’t know that in my heart I feel even more for you. Your cousin has lost her father, but she still has me and her own brother, and that makes her better off than you. I often tell her how much I care for you. Only I can’t show it openly.

‘There are so many people and so many tongues here, few to speak well of anyone and plenty to speak ill. They wouldn’t say you have no one to depend on, and deserve affection for the person you are. They’d say we’d seen how fond Her Old Ladyship was of you and were swimming with the current.’

‘If that’s what you say, Aunt,’ Dai-yu replied with a smile, ‘I’ll ask you to be my mother tomorrow. If you despise me and won’t acknowledge me, your affection can’t have been real.’

‘If you don’t despise me, I shall be glad to have you.’

‘You can’t do that,’ Bao-chai put in.

‘Why not?’

‘Let me ask you something,’ Bao-chai said, smiling. ‘My elder brother isn’t engaged yet. Why was Cousin Xing promised to my younger cousin first? What do you think the reason is?’

‘He’s away from home,’ said Dai-yu. ‘Or perhaps their animal signs or birth dates aren’t compatible, so she was promised to the younger one first.’

‘Wrong. My brother has already chosen someone. We’re only waiting for him to come home before we send the betrothal gifts. I needn’t name her. Think about why I just said you couldn’t call Mother your mother.’

As she spoke she winked at her mother and laughed.

Dai-yu leaned against Aunt Xue in her turn.

‘Unless you give her a smack, Aunt, I shan’t be satisfied.’

Aunt Xue quickly put an arm round her and laughed.

‘Don’t believe your cousin. She’s only teasing you.’

‘I mean it,’ said Bao-chai, laughing. ‘Mother, why don’t you ask Her Old Ladyship tomorrow if we may have her as your daughter-in-law? Wouldn’t that be better than looking outside?’

Dai-yu reached over to seize her.

‘You’re getting madder and madder!’ she said, laughing.

Aunt Xue laughed too and tried to soothe them, separating them with her hands before they would stop. Then she said to Bao-chai, ‘Even with Miss Xing I was afraid your brother would ruin her, which is why I arranged the match with your cousin. As for this child, I would never give her to him. The other day Her Old Ladyship wanted to arrange a match between Bao-qin and Bao-yu, only Bao-qin was already promised. Otherwise it would have been an excellent marriage. When I settled things for Miss Xing, Her Old Ladyship teased me: “I meant to ask for one of hers. Before I could get her, she’s talked one of ours away!” It was only a joke, but when you think it over, there’s something in it.

‘Bao-qin may be promised, and I may have no one to give, but surely I can still say a word. Her Old Ladyship is so fond of Bao-yu, and with a boy like him, she would never be satisfied with a match outside. Why not simply settle it between him and your Cousin Lin? Wouldn’t that make everything complete?’

Dai-yu had been listening blankly at first. When the talk turned to her, she gave a little ‘Pah!’ at Bao-chai, blushed, and caught hold of her, laughing.

‘You’re the one I’ll hit! Why did you set Aunt talking all this improper nonsense at her age?’

‘That’s odd!’ Bao-chai laughed. ‘Mother talks about you, and you hit me?’

Nightingale hurried over, smiling.

‘If that’s what you have in mind, ma’am, why don’t you speak to Her Ladyship?’

Aunt Xue laughed heartily.

‘What are you in such a hurry for, child? I suppose you want your young lady married off so you can find a little husband of your own.’

Nightingale blushed too.

‘Now you really are taking advantage of your age, ma’am!’ she said with a laugh, and turned away.

At first Dai-yu had scolded, ‘What has it to do with you, you little hussy?’ Now, seeing the outcome, she laughed too.

‘Buddha be praised! Serves you right, serves you right, serves you right! You’ve gone off with a faceful of shame as well!’

Aunt Xue, Bao-chai and all the women and maids in the room burst out laughing. The older women then said, still laughing, ‘You may be joking, ma’am, but it’s a very good idea. When you have a quiet moment, discuss it with Her Old Ladyship. Nothing could be more suitable than for you to act as matchmaker and bring it about.’

‘Once I suggest it, Her Old Ladyship is sure to be pleased,’ said Aunt Xue.

Before she had finished, Xiang-yun came in with a pawn ticket in her hand.

‘Is this a page from an account book?’ she asked, laughing.

Dai-yu looked at it, but did not recognise it either. The older women standing below them all laughed.

‘Now that’s a rare object! You can’t expect to learn about that for nothing.’

Bao-chai quickly took it from her. It was the pawn ticket Xiu-yan had just mentioned. She folded it up at once.

‘One of the nurses must have lost her pawn ticket,’ Aunt Xue said hurriedly. ‘They’ll be frantic when they start looking for it. Where did you find it?’

‘What is a pawn ticket?’ asked Xiang-yun.

‘What a goose!’ everyone laughed. ‘Not even to know what a pawn ticket is!’

‘You can hardly blame her,’ Aunt Xue said with a sigh. ‘She’s the daughter of a marquis’s house, and so young. How should she know about such things? Where would she find one?

‘Even if one of her servants had one, how would she ever see it? Don’t call her a goose. Show it to your young ladies and they’d all be geese too.’

‘Miss Lin didn’t know just now either,’ the older women said, laughing. ‘And never mind the young ladies: even Master Bao, who often goes out into the world, has probably never seen one.’

Aunt Xue explained what it was. Only then did Xiang-yun and Dai-yu laugh.

‘So that’s what it’s for! People are clever at thinking of ways to get money. Do your family’s pawnshops have these too, Aunt?’

‘There you go again!’ the others laughed. ‘“Crows are black the whole world over.” How could they be different?’

Aunt Xue asked once more where it had been found. Xiang-yun was about to answer when Bao-chai quickly said, ‘It’s an old ticket, no use any more. The account was settled goodness knows how many years ago. Caltrop had it to amuse them with.’

Aunt Xue took this for the truth and asked no more. Presently someone came to say, ‘The mistress from the other mansion has come to ask you over for a talk, ma’am.’ Aunt Xue rose and left.

When there was no one else in the room, Bao-chai asked Xiang-yun where she had found the ticket.

‘I saw your future cousin-in-law’s maid Zhuan-er quietly hand it to Oriole,’ Xiang-yun said with a smile. ‘Oriole slipped it into a book, thinking I hadn’t seen.

‘When they went out, I stole a look, but I couldn’t make it out at all. I knew you were all here, so I brought it for everyone to identify.’

‘What?’ Dai-yu asked at once. ‘Does she have to pawn her clothes too? And if she’s pawned them, why send the ticket to you?’

Bao-chai could hardly keep it from the two of them now they had asked, so she told them everything that had just happened.

‘When the rabbit dies, the fox grieves. Creatures mourn for their own kind,’ said Dai-yu, and could not help sighing over it.

Xiang-yun grew angry.

‘I’ll go and ask Cousin Ying-chun about this! How would it be if I gave those old women and maids a good scolding on your behalf?’

She was about to leave, but Bao-chai caught her by the arm.

‘Off on another mad fit!’ she said, laughing. ‘Sit down at once.’

‘If you were a man, you could go out and fight to right a wrong,’ Dai-yu said with a laugh. ‘But why set yourself up as Jing Ke or Nie Zheng? It’s too ridiculous.’

‘If you won’t let me go and question Cousin Ying-chun, why not bring Xiu-yan to live with us in our court tomorrow? Wouldn’t that be better?’

‘We’ll discuss it tomorrow,’ said Bao-chai, smiling.

As she spoke, someone announced, ‘Miss Tan-chun and Miss Xi-chun are here.’ All three immediately fell silent and said no more of the matter. To learn what followed, listen to the next chapter.
