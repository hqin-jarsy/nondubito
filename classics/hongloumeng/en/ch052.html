Chapter 52 Charming Patience kindly conceals the theft of a shrimp-whisker bracelet; Brave Skybright, though ill, mends a peacock-gold cloak

Chapter end

‘That’s just it,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘I wanted to say so the other day, but when I saw how many important things you had to attend to, and now all these new matters as well, I thought that, though you wouldn’t dare complain, you might feel I cared only for these young grandchildren of mine and had no consideration for those of you who run the household. Now that you’ve suggested it yourself, so much the better.’

Aunt Xue and Aunt Li were both there. Lady Xing and You-shi with her daughter-in-law had also come to pay their respects and had not yet left. Grandmother Jia turned to Lady Wang and the others.

‘I’ll say something today that I don’t usually say, partly for fear of making Feng too pleased with herself, and partly because the others might resent it. You’re all here now, and you’ve all had sisters-in-law of one sort or another. Have any of you ever known one so thoughtful?’

Aunt Xue, Aunt Li, You-shi and the others answered, laughing, ‘Very few indeed.

‘With most people it’s only politeness, something to keep up appearances. She really does care for her husband’s younger brothers and sisters. And she’s truly devoted to Your Old Ladyship too.’

Grandmother Jia nodded and sighed.

‘Fond as I am of her, I do sometimes fear that being so clever may not be a good thing.’

‘You’re wrong there, Grandmother,’ Xi-feng said quickly, laughing. ‘People say that those who are too clever and quick-witted may not live long. Other people can say it, and everyone can believe it, but you of all people ought neither to say it nor believe it. You’re ten times cleverer and quicker than I am. How is it that you enjoy such good fortune and such a long life? Perhaps one day I’ll do twice as well as you! When I’ve lived a thousand years, I’ll wait until you’ve gone to the Western Paradise before I die.’

‘What would be the fun of it,’ Grandmother Jia laughed, ‘with everyone else dead and only the two of us old monsters left?’

This made them all laugh.

Concerned about Skybright, Aroma and the rest, Bao-yu returned to the Garden ahead of the others. The scent of medicine filled his room, but no one was there except Skybright, lying alone on the kang, her face burning red. He touched it and found it hot against his hand. Hastily warming his hands at the stove, he put one under the quilt to feel her body. That too was burning.

‘It’s one thing for the others to go,’ he said, ‘but are Musk and Ripple so heartless that they’ve both gone off as well?’

‘I sent Ripple to have her meal. Patience came to fetch Musk just now, and she went out with her. The two of them were being very secretive. I don’t know what they’re talking about. It must be about my staying in here because I’m ill.’

‘Patience isn’t that sort of person. Besides, she didn’t know you were ill, so she can’t have come specially to see you. Most likely she came to talk to Musk, happened to find you ill, and said she’d come to ask after you. That’s an ordinary enough way of being tactful and pleasant. Even if you were wrong not to go out, what would it have to do with her?

‘You’ve always been good friends. She certainly wouldn’t spoil that over something that doesn’t concern her.’

‘That’s true,’ said Skybright. ‘But I still wonder why she should suddenly start keeping secrets from me.’

‘Let me slip out at the back,’ Bao-yu said with a smile, ‘and listen under the window. Then I can tell you what they’re saying.’

He did indeed go out by the back door and steal up beneath the window to listen.

He heard Musk whisper, ‘How did you manage to get it back?’

‘When it went missing while we were washing our hands that day,’ Patience said, ‘Mrs Lian wouldn’t allow any fuss. As soon as she left the Garden she sent word to the women in charge of all the different places to make discreet inquiries.

‘We only suspected Miss Xing’s maid. They’re poor, after all, and a child who’s never seen such things might well have picked it up. We never imagined it would turn out to be someone here. Luckily Mrs Lian wasn’t in the room when your Mother Song came with this bracelet. She said the little maid Zhui-er had stolen it; she’d seen her, and had come to tell Mrs Lian. I hurried to take the bracelet from her and thought it over. Bao-yu takes such particular care over you girls, and always wants to prove that you’re better than anyone else. There was that Liang-er who stole jade some years ago. It’s only been quiet for a year or two, and people still bring it up to gloat. Now here’s another one, stealing gold! And from a neighbour, too. The more he makes of you, the more his own people slap him in the face. So I told Mother Song very firmly not to let Bao-yu know. She must treat it as though it had never happened and not mention it to a soul. For another thing, Her Old Ladyship and Her Ladyship would be angry if they heard.

‘And then it would reflect badly on Aroma and all of you. So I told Mrs Lian, “I was on my way to Mrs Zhu’s when the bracelet must have come open and dropped among the grass roots. The snow was too deep for me to see it. Today the snow had all melted, and there it was, shining yellow in the sun. I picked it up.” She believed me, so I’ve come to let you know. Keep an eye on the girl from now on, and don’t send her on errands anywhere else.

‘When Aroma comes back, talk it over and find some way of sending her away. That will finish it.’

‘The little slut’s seen a few things in her time,’ said Musk. ‘How can she be so grasping?’

‘After all, how much can this bracelet weigh?’ said Patience. ‘Mrs Lian told me it’s called a “shrimp-whisker bracelet”. This bead is worth something, I grant you.

‘But that minx Skybright is a piece of charcoal that spits fire. If she hears about it, she won’t be able to hold herself in. She’ll get angry and start hitting or scolding, and the whole thing will come out after all. That would never do. So I’m only telling you, to put you on your guard.’

With that, she took her leave.

Bao-yu listened with mingled delight, anger and regret: delight that Patience could show him such consideration, anger at Zhui-er’s pilfering, and regret that a girl as bright as Zhui-er should have done something so shameful. He went back into the room and gave Skybright a full account of what Patience had said, adding:

‘She said you’re so determined not to give way that, ill as you are, hearing this would only make you worse. She meant to tell you when you were better.’

Sure enough, Skybright’s slender brows drew down in anger and her long eyes opened wide. She immediately called for Zhui-er.

‘If you start shouting now,’ Bao-yu hastened to plead, ‘won’t you be throwing away Patience’s kindness to both of us? Better to accept it and send the girl away later.’

‘That’s all very well. But how am I supposed to swallow this?’

‘What is there to be angry about?

‘Just concentrate on getting well.’

Skybright took her medicine, then another decoction of the same herbs that evening. She sweated a little during the night, but there was still no improvement: the fever persisted, her head ached, her nose was blocked and her voice thick. Next day Doctor Wang came again and adjusted the prescription. Her fever eased a little, but the headache remained.

‘Fetch the snuff,’ Bao-yu told Musk. ‘Let her take some and have a few good sneezes. That will open the passages.’

Musk brought him a flat box of gold-speckled glass, mounted in gold, with two clasps. Bao-yu flipped open the lid. Inside it was a Western enamel picture of a naked, fair-haired woman, with fleshy wings growing from her sides. The box contained genuine imported Wangqia snuff. Skybright was intent on the picture.

‘Take some,’ Bao-yu said. ‘It won’t be any good once the strength has gone out of it.’

Skybright scraped up a little with her fingernail and sniffed it into her nose. Nothing much happened, so she took a much larger pinch and sniffed again. Suddenly a sharp, stinging sensation shot up through her nose to the crown of her head. She sneezed five or six times in succession, and tears and mucus streamed out together.

She quickly shut the box and laughed.

‘Goodness! What a relief! Give me some paper.’

A little maid had already brought a sheaf of fine paper. Skybright took the sheets one by one to blow her nose.

‘Well?’ Bao-yu asked with a smile.

‘It certainly feels clearer,’ she said, laughing, ‘but my temples still ache.’

‘We might as well try foreign medicines for everything,’ Bao-yu laughed. ‘Perhaps that will cure you.’ He told Musk, ‘Go and ask Mrs Lian. Say it’s from me: “Cousin, you usually have some of that foreign plaster for headaches, the one called Yifuna. Could you find a little?”’

Musk agreed and went out. After some time she returned with half a piece of the plaster. She found a scrap of red satin and cut out two circles the size of a fingertip, softened the medicine over the fire, and spread it on them with the stem of a hairpin. Holding a hand-mirror, Skybright put them on her temples.

‘You looked like some dishevelled ghost with this illness,’ Musk laughed. ‘Now you’ve put those on, you look quite pretty.

‘Mrs Lian wears them so often that they don’t show up much on her.’

Then she turned to Bao-yu.

‘Mrs Lian says tomorrow’s your uncle’s birthday, and Her Ladyship says you’re to go. What will you wear? We’d better have everything ready tonight, instead of making work for ourselves first thing tomorrow.’

‘Whatever comes to hand,’ said Bao-yu. ‘There’s no end to all these birthdays, year in, year out.’

He rose and went out towards Xi-chun’s rooms to look at the painting. Just outside the courtyard gate he saw Bao-qin’s little maid Xiao-luo passing, and hurried to catch her up.

‘Where are you going?’

‘Both our young ladies are in Miss Lin’s room,’ Xiao-luo said with a smile. ‘I’m going there too.’

Bao-yu changed direction and went with her to the Naiad’s House. Not only Bao-chai and Bao-qin but Xing Xiu-yan was there. The four girls sat together on the warming frame, talking of family matters. Nightingale sat apart in the heated alcove, doing needlework by the window. At the sight of him they all laughed.

‘Here’s another one! There isn’t a place left for you.’

‘What a fine picture of “Beauties Gathered in a Winter Chamber”!’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘A pity I came a little late. Still, this room’s warmer than any of the others. I shan’t be cold sitting on this chair.’

He sat down in Dai-yu’s usual chair, with its grey-squirrel cover. In the heated alcove he noticed a long, narrow jade planter, filled with single-petalled narcissi in groups of three and five, with Xuan stones set among them. He was lavish in his praise.

‘What lovely flowers!

‘The warmer the room, the purer their scent. They weren’t here yesterday.’

‘Your head steward’s wife, Mrs Lai, sent them to Bao-qin,’ Dai-yu said. ‘Two pots of wintersweet and two of narcissi. Bao-qin gave me one pot of narcissi and our Plantain girl one of wintersweet. I didn’t want it at first, but I was afraid of slighting her kindness. If you’d like it, shall I pass it on to you?’

‘I’ve two pots in my room, but they’re not as good as this. How could you give away a present from Cousin Qin? That would never do.’

‘My medicine pot is never off the fire. I’m practically grown in medicine. How could I stand having flower scent poured over me as well? It would only make me weaker. Besides, the smell of medicine in this room spoils the scent of the flowers. Better for you to have them carried away. Then they can have some peace, with no other smells to trouble them.’

‘There’s someone ill in my room today too, and we’re boiling medicine,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘How did you know?’

‘What an odd thing to say!’ Dai-yu laughed. ‘It was only a chance remark. How should I know what goes on in your room? You should have come earlier to hear the stories. Instead you turn up now, finding mysteries and surprises of your own.’

‘We have subjects for our next poetry meeting now,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘Narcissi and wintersweet.’

‘No, no!’ Dai-yu laughed. ‘I daren’t write any more poems. Every time I write one, I’m made to pay a forfeit. It’s too humiliating.’

She covered her face with both hands.

‘What’s the need for that?’ Bao-yu laughed. ‘Why make fun of me again? I’m not ashamed, yet here you are hiding your face.’

‘I’ll host the next meeting,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘Four subjects for poems and four for lyrics. Each of us must write four poems and four lyrics. The first poem will be “On the Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate”: five-character regulated verse in the First xian rhyme group, and you must use every word in that group, without leaving a single one out.’

‘That shows you don’t really mean to hold a meeting, Cousin,’ Bao-qin laughed. ‘You’re plainly trying to make things difficult. I suppose one could force something out, turning phrases from the Book of Changes this way and that and stuffing them in. But where would be the pleasure in it?1

‘When I was eight, I went with my father to the shores of the Western Sea to buy foreign goods. There we came across a girl from the country of Zhenzhen, only fifteen years old. Her face was just like those beauties in Western pictures. She too had yellow hair hanging down, with braids linked together, and her head was covered in jewels: coral, cat’s-eyes and emeralds. Her jacket sleeves were foreign brocade, woven in gold thread with a pattern like mail. She wore a Japanese sword, also mounted in gold and set with jewels. Really, even the beauties in the pictures weren’t as lovely as she was. Someone said she knew Chinese poetry and books, could expound the Five Classics, and could write poems and lyrics. So my father asked an official interpreter to get her to write something for him. What she wrote was a poem of her own.’

They all marvelled at this.

‘Dear Cousin,’ Bao-yu said eagerly, smiling, ‘bring it out and let me see.’

‘It’s put away in Nanjing,’ Bao-qin laughed. ‘How could I fetch it now?’

Bao-yu was bitterly disappointed.

‘I’m not fortunate enough to see such wonders.’

Dai-yu took Bao-qin’s hand with a smile.

‘Don’t try to fool us.

‘I know you’d hardly have left all those things at home when you came here. Naturally you’d have brought them with you. Now you’re making up a story that you haven’t. The others may believe it, but I don’t.’

Bao-qin blushed, lowered her head and smiled without answering.

‘Little Pin-pin is always so good at talking nonsense,’ Bao-chai laughed. ‘What a clever creature you are!’

‘If she’s brought it,’ Dai-yu said, ‘she might as well give us the chance to see something new.’

‘There’s a whole pile of trunks and boxes we haven’t sorted yet,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘Who knows which one it’s in? When we’ve put things in order in a few days, we’ll find it and everyone can see it.’ Then she turned to Bao-qin. ‘If you remember it, why not recite it for us?’

Only then did Bao-qin answer.

‘I remember it was a five-character regulated poem. For a foreign girl, it was quite an achievement.’

‘Don’t start yet,’ said Bao-chai. ‘Let’s send for Yun so that she can hear it too.’

She called Xiao-luo and told her, ‘Go to my rooms and say that a foreign beauty has come here who writes wonderful poetry. Ask our “poetry madwoman” to come and see her, and bring our “poetry simpleton” along as well.’

Xiao-luo went away laughing.

After a while they heard Xiang-yun calling with a laugh, ‘Where’s this foreign beauty?’

Sure enough, she came in with Caltrop as she spoke.

‘We hear the voice before we see the person,’ they all laughed.

Bao-qin and the others hastily made room for them and repeated the story.

‘Do recite it for us,’ Xiang-yun said with a smile.

Bao-qin began:

Last night, a dream of crimson towers;

Tonight, a song in a land of waters.

Island clouds rise steaming from the sea;

Mountain mists reach to the clustered woods.

The moon knows neither now nor long ago;

The bonds of feeling have their depths and shallows.

Spring south of the Han lies clear before me;

How could it fail to touch my heart?

‘What an achievement!’ they all said when they had heard it. ‘She’s better than we Chinese are.’

They had scarcely finished when Musk came in.

‘Her Ladyship has sent someone to tell you, Master Bao, that you’re to go to your uncle’s first thing tomorrow. You’re to say she isn’t feeling well and can’t come herself.’

Bao-yu rose at once and answered, ‘Yes.’

He asked Bao-chai and Bao-qin whether they were going.

‘No,’ said Bao-chai. ‘We just sent our presents yesterday.’

They talked a little longer before parting.

Bao-yu let the girls go ahead and stayed behind. Dai-yu called him back.

‘When exactly will Aroma be coming home?’

‘Not until after the funeral, of course.’

Dai-yu had more to say, but did not say it. She was lost in thought for a moment, then said, ‘You’d better go.’

Bao-yu too felt that he had much to tell her, but could not find anything to say. After a moment’s thought he smiled.

‘We’ll talk tomorrow.’

He went down the steps and was about to walk on, his head lowered, when he turned back hastily.

‘The nights are getting longer. How many times do you cough in the night now? How often do you wake?’

‘Last night was better. I only coughed twice. But I only slept for the fourth watch, just that one watch, and couldn’t sleep any more.’

‘There’s something important I meant to say,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘I’ve only just remembered.’

As he spoke he drew closer and lowered his voice.

‘I was thinking about the bird’s nests Cousin Chai sent you…’

Before he could finish, Aunt Zhao came in to see Dai-yu.

‘Have you been well these last two days, Miss?’

Dai-yu knew that she must have come from Tan-chun’s and, passing the door, had stopped to pay a courtesy visit on her way.

Dai-yu hastily welcomed her with a smile and asked her to sit down.

‘How kind of you to think of me, Aunt, and come yourself in such cold weather.’

She called for tea, at the same time giving Bao-yu a glance. He understood and went out.

It was just supper-time. Bao-yu saw Lady Wang, who again told him to set out early. When he returned he watched Skybright take her medicine. That evening he did not ask her to move out of the heated alcove, but lay down on the outer side of her. He also had the warming frame brought up in front of the alcove, and Musk lay on it. Nothing more happened that night.

Next morning, before it was light, Skybright woke Musk.

‘It’s time you woke up. Haven’t you had enough sleep yet? Go out and tell them to get his tea and water ready. I’ll wake him.’

Musk hastily put on her clothes and got up.

‘Let’s wake him first and get his clothes on, then move this warming box away before we let them in. The old nurses have already told us not to let him stay in this room for fear of catching the illness. If they see us all crowded together, they’ll start grumbling again.’

‘That’s what I say too,’ said Skybright.

When they called him, Bao-yu was already awake. He hurriedly got up and put on his clothes. Musk first called in the little maids. Only when everything was in order did she let Ripple, Sandal and the others come in to help him wash and comb his hair.

‘The sky’s overcast again,’ said Musk. ‘It may snow. Wear that set of felt clothes.’

Bao-yu nodded and changed at once. A little maid brought a covered bowl of soup made with Jian lotus seeds and red dates on a small tea tray. He took two sips.

Musk brought him a little dish of prepared purple ginger, and he held a piece in his mouth. After giving Skybright more instructions, he went to Grandmother Jia’s. She was not yet up, but on hearing that he was going out she had the door opened and told him to come in. Behind her, Bao-qin was still asleep, her face turned inwards. Grandmother Jia saw that he was wearing a litchi-coloured, arrow-sleeved coat of duoluo cloth with sand-fox fur, and a scarlet broadcloth outer coat with couched gold and coloured embroidery, dark-blue figured-satin edging and rows of tassels.

‘Is it snowing?’ she asked.

‘It’s overcast, but it hasn’t started yet.’

She called Faithful.

‘Give him that Dark-cloud Leopard cloak from yesterday.’

Faithful answered and went to fetch it. When Bao-yu looked, it shone with gold and emerald green, its blue-green lustre flashing; yet it was unlike Bao-qin’s duck-feather cloak. He heard Grandmother Jia say with a smile:

‘This is called peacock-gold cloth. In Russia they twist peacock feathers into thread and weave it. I gave your little cousin the wild-duck one the other day. You may have this.’

Bao-yu made a kowtow and put it on.

‘Go and show it to your mother before you leave,’ Grandmother Jia said with a smile.

Bao-yu agreed and came out. Faithful was standing there rubbing her eyes. Ever since her vehement oath that day, she had refused to speak to him, and this had troubled him day and night. Seeing that she was about to avoid him again, he came up to her with a smile.

‘Dear Sister, have a look. Does this suit me?’

Faithful flung her hand aside and went into Grandmother Jia’s room. Bao-yu could only go on to Lady Wang’s and show it to her. Then he returned to the Garden to let Skybright and Musk see it, before going back to Grandmother Jia’s to report.

‘Mother has seen it. She said it would be such a pity to spoil it, and I must wear it carefully and not ruin it.’

‘This is the only one left,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘If you spoil it, there won’t be another. It’s not something we could have made specially for you now.’

She went on to warn him, ‘Don’t drink too much wine, and come back early.’

Bao-yu answered, ‘Yes,’ several times.

The old nurses followed him as far as the hall. There his foster-brother Li Gui and five other men, Wang Rong, Zhang Ruo-jin, Zhao Yi-hua, Qian Qi and Zhou Rui, had long been waiting with the four pages Tealeaf, Ban-he, Chu-yao and Sao-hong, carrying bundles of clothes and holding a sitting-cushion, with a white horse fitted with a carved saddle and coloured reins. The old nurses gave the six men further instructions. They hastily answered, ‘Yes, ma’am,’ several times and presented the whip and steadied the stirrup. Bao-yu mounted slowly. Li Gui and Wang Rong held the bit rings, Qian Qi and Zhou Rui led the way, and Zhang Ruo-jin and Zhao Yi-hua kept close behind him on either side.

‘Brother Zhou, Brother Qian,’ Bao-yu said from the saddle, smiling, ‘let’s go out by this side gate. Then I shan’t have to get down again when we pass Father’s study.’

Zhou Rui turned sideways with a smile.

‘The Master’s away, and the study’s kept locked every day. You needn’t dismount, sir.’

‘Even if it’s locked, I ought to get down.’

‘You’re quite right, sir,’ Qian Qi, Li Gui and the others said, laughing.

‘Even if you saved yourself the trouble and stayed on your horse, suppose we met Master Lai or Second Master Lin? They might not like to scold you, but they’d certainly say a word or two. And if anything were wrong, they’d put it all down to us, and say we hadn’t taught you your manners.’

Zhou Rui and Qian Qi led straight towards the side gate.

As they spoke, whom should they meet coming in but Lai Da himself. Bao-yu at once reined in, intending to dismount. Lai Da hurried up and held his leg to stop him. Bao-yu stood in the stirrups instead, took his hand with a smile and exchanged a few words with him. Next came a page leading twenty or thirty people with brooms and dustpans. At the sight of Bao-yu they stood against the wall with their hands lowered. Only the page at their head bent one knee and paid his respects. Bao-yu did not know his name and merely smiled and nodded. Not until the horse had passed did the page lead his people away.

Outside the side gate, the servants belonging to Li Gui and the other five men, with several grooms, had some ten horses ready and were waiting. Once through the gate, Li Gui and the others mounted. Leading the way and surrounding Bao-yu, they rode away in a rush. Of this we need say no more.

Meanwhile, Skybright had taken her medicine but still felt no better. In her impatience she began abusing the doctor.

‘All he can do is cheat people out of their money. He won’t give anyone a single dose of decent medicine.’

‘You’re too impatient,’ Musk said, trying to soothe her with a smile. ‘As the saying goes, “Illness comes like a mountain falling, and leaves like silk drawn thread by thread.” It’s not Lord Lao’s elixir. Where would you find a medicine that works like that? Just rest quietly for a few days and you’ll get well.

‘The more you fret, the worse it takes hold.’

Skybright began scolding the little maids.

‘Where have you all burrowed away to? Now I’m ill, you’ve all grown bold enough to go off. Just wait till I’m better. I’ll skin you one by one!’

Frightened, the little maid Zhuan-er hurried in.

‘What do you want, Miss?’

‘Are all the others dead, and only you left?’

As Skybright spoke, Zhui-er sidled in too.

‘Look at the little bitch! She wouldn’t have come if I hadn’t asked. If we were handing out the monthly pay or giving away fruit, you’d be first in the queue. Come closer. I’m not a tiger. I shan’t eat you!’

Zhui-er had to come forward. Without warning, Skybright raised herself and seized her hand. Taking a long hairpin from beside her pillow, she stabbed at the hand again and again, scolding as she did so.

‘What use are these claws? They can’t hold a needle or take up a thread. All they’re good for is sneaking food. Greedy eyes and thieving fingers! You shame us all and make us lose face. Better to stab them to pieces!’

Zhui-er screamed and cried with pain. Musk hastily pulled her away and pressed Skybright back onto the bed.

‘You’ve only just broken into a sweat, and now you’re killing yourself again,’ she said with a smile. ‘When you’re well, you can beat her as much as you like.

‘Why make this fuss now?’

Skybright had Mother Song called in.

‘Master Bao has just told me to let you know that Zhui-er is very lazy. When he gives her an order to her face, she pouts and won’t move. Even when Aroma tells her to do something, she curses her behind her back. She must be sent away today. Tomorrow Master Bao will explain to Her Ladyship himself.’

Mother Song knew at once that the bracelet affair had come out.

‘Even so,’ she said with a smile, ‘we should wait until Miss Hua comes back and knows about it before we send her away.’

‘Master Bao told me over and over again today,’ said Skybright. ‘Never mind your Miss Flower or Miss Grass. We know what we’re doing. Just do as I say and fetch her family to take her away at once.’

‘We might as well,’ said Musk. ‘She’ll have to go sooner or later. The sooner she’s taken away, the sooner we’ll have some peace.’

Mother Song had no choice but to go and fetch Zhui-er’s mother. The woman packed her daughter’s things, then came to see Skybright and the others.

‘What’s wrong, young ladies? If your little niece has misbehaved, you can teach her. Why send her away? You might at least leave us a little dignity.’

‘Wait till Bao-yu comes and ask him,’ said Skybright. ‘It has nothing to do with us.’

The woman gave a cold laugh.

‘As if I’d dare ask him! What does he ever do without you young ladies arranging it? Even if he agreed, it wouldn’t be much use if you didn’t. Take what you said just now. We may be speaking behind his back, but you called him straight out by his name. You young ladies are allowed to do that. If we did it, we’d be called savages.’

Skybright flushed still more with anger.

‘I called him by his name, did I? Go and tell Her Old Ladyship. Say I’m behaving outrageously and have me sent away too.’

‘Sister,’ Musk broke in, ‘just take your daughter out. If you have anything to say, say it later.

‘Is this a place for you to shout and lecture us on manners? Who have you ever seen teaching us manners? Never mind you: even Grannie Lai and Mrs Lin have to make allowances for us. As for calling him by his name, that’s what Her Old Ladyship has told us to do from when he was little right down to today. You know that. They were afraid he’d be hard to rear, so they actually wrote out his childhood name and pasted it up everywhere for all and sundry to call him by. It was to make it easier for him to survive. Even water-carriers, night-soil men and beggars could use it, so why shouldn’t we? Only yesterday Mrs Lin called him “sir”, and Her Old Ladyship spoke to her about it. That’s one thing. Then we’re always going to report things to Her Old Ladyship. If we don’t use his name, are we supposed to call him “sir”? We must say “Bao-yu” two hundred times a day, and now you’ve come to find fault with it!

‘When you have a free day, Sister, go and listen to us in front of Her Old Ladyship and Her Ladyship. Then you’ll hear what we call him to their faces. You’ve never had any responsible duties with either of them. You spend the whole year hanging about outside the third gate. No wonder you don’t know our rules in here. This isn’t a place for you to stand about. Stay a little longer and, without our having to say a word, someone will come and question you. If you have a case to argue, take her away first and report to Mrs Lin. Ask her to speak to Master Bao. There are over a thousand people in this household. If you all come running here, how are we even to learn your names and know who you are?’

She turned to a little maid.

‘Bring a cloth and wipe the floor!’

The woman could find nothing to say and dared not stay. In a temper, she took Zhui-er and was going, when Mother Song spoke up hastily.

‘Really, Sister, how little you know of manners! Your daughter has served in this room, after all. She should kowtow to the young ladies before she leaves.

‘She has no other gift to thank them with. Even if she had, they wouldn’t care for it. But a kowtow would at least show her gratitude. How can she just walk out?’

Zhui-er had to turn back and make two kowtows to Skybright and Musk. She then looked for Ripple and the others, but they ignored her. Her mother sighed and groaned, not daring to speak, and took her away full of resentment.

Skybright had caught another draught in the commotion and worked herself into a temper. She felt worse than before and tossed about until the lamps were lit. She had only just grown a little calmer when Bao-yu returned, sighing and stamping his foot as he came in. Musk hurried to ask what was wrong.

‘Grandmother was so pleased to give me this coat today. Who would have thought I’d burn a hole in the rear panel without noticing? Luckily it was late, so neither she nor Mother paid any attention.’

He took it off as he spoke. Musk looked and found a burn hole the size of a fingertip.

‘A spark must have jumped out of your hand-warmer. It’s nothing much. Have someone take it out quietly at once and find a skilled mender to reweave it.’

She wrapped it in a cloth and gave it to an older woman to take out.

‘We must have it back by daybreak. Whatever you do, don’t let Her Old Ladyship or Her Ladyship know.’

The woman was away a long time, but brought it back as it was.

‘We tried not only the skilled menders but tailors, embroiderers and women who do needlework. None of them recognised the stuff, and none dared take it on.’

‘What are we to do?’ said Musk. ‘You’ll have to leave it off tomorrow.’

‘But tomorrow’s the birthday itself. Grandmother and Mother have told me to wear it again. And I had to burn it on the very first day! It would spoil all their pleasure.’

Skybright had been listening for some time. At last she could contain herself no longer and turned over.

‘Bring it here and let me look. If you aren’t fortunate enough to wear it, then do without it.

‘What’s the use of fretting now?’

‘There’s something in that,’ Bao-yu said with a smile.

He handed it to her and moved the lamp closer. She examined it carefully for a while.

‘This is woven with peacock-gold thread. If we use peacock-gold thread too, and work it in closely as in darning, perhaps it will pass.’

‘We have peacock thread ready to hand,’ Musk said with a smile, ‘but who here can do that sort of darning except you?’

‘Then I’ll have to risk my life at it.’

‘How can you?’ Bao-yu protested at once. ‘You’ve only just begun to feel better. How could you do any work?’

‘Stop fussing and fretting. I know what I’m doing.’

As she spoke, she sat up, twisted her hair into place and put on some clothes. Her head felt heavy, her body insubstantial; gold stars darted before her eyes. She could hardly hold herself up. Yet if she did not do it, she feared Bao-yu would fret. There was nothing for it but to grit her teeth and force herself on. She told Musk simply to help by twisting the thread. First Skybright took a strand and compared it with the cloth.

‘It isn’t a very close match,’ she said with a smile, ‘but once it’s worked in, it shouldn’t show much.’

‘It’s perfectly good,’ said Bao-yu. ‘Where are we to find a tailor from Russia?’

Skybright first unpicked the lining and fastened a bamboo hoop the size of a teacup’s rim to the back. With a gold knife she scraped all four edges of the hole loose and fluffy. Then she threaded two strands into a needle and, separating warp from weft, worked as she would in darning: first making a ground, then weaving back and forth over it to follow the pattern of the garment.

She made two stitches and looked, made two more and examined them again. But her head swam, her sight darkened, she was short of breath and her strength was failing. She could hardly make three or five stitches before she had to sink onto her pillow and rest. Beside her, Bao-yu would ask if she wanted hot water, then tell her to rest; next he put a grey-squirrel cloak round her shoulders, then called for an armrest cushion for her to lean against. At last Skybright begged him in exasperation:

‘My little tyrant!

‘Do go to sleep. If you stay awake another half the night and your eyes are sunken tomorrow, what are we to do?’

Seeing her agitation, Bao-yu lay down as best he could, but still could not sleep. Presently the chiming clock sounded four strokes. The mending was just finished, and Skybright used a small toothbrush to tease out the nap carefully.

‘That’s very good,’ said Musk. ‘Unless someone looks closely, they’ll never see it.’

Bao-yu eagerly asked to look.

‘It really is exactly the same.’

Skybright had had several fits of coughing and had only finished with great difficulty.

‘It’s mended,’ she said, ‘but it still doesn’t look the same. I can’t do any more.’

With a groan, she collapsed helplessly.
