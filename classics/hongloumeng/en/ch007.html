Chapter 7 Palace flowers are delivered while Jia Lian dallies with Xi-feng; At a feast in the Ning mansion Bao-yu meets Qin Zhong

Chapter end

After seeing Grannie Liu off, Zhou Rui’s wife went to report to Lady Wang. But Lady Wang was not in her principal rooms. On asking the maids, she learnt that Her Ladyship had gone to chat with Aunt Xue. She accordingly went out through the eastern side gate, crossed into the eastern courtyard and made her way to Pear-tree Court. Just outside its gate she saw Lady Wang’s maid Golden playing on the sloping approach to the steps with a little girl whose hair had only lately been allowed to grow. Seeing Zhou Rui’s wife, they knew she had something to report, and gestured towards the room with their lips. She lifted the curtain softly and went in. Lady Wang and Aunt Xue were deep in a long conversation about household affairs and their dealings with relatives and friends.

Not daring to interrupt, Zhou Rui’s wife went into the inner room. There sat Xue Bao-chai in her everyday clothes, her hair loosely coiled in a simple bun. She was sitting towards the inner end of the kang, leaning over its little table to trace embroidery patterns with her maid Oriole. At the sight of her visitor Bao-chai put down her brush and turned, her face all smiles. ‘Do sit down, Sister Zhou.’

Zhou Rui’s wife hastened to smile back. ‘Are you well, miss?’

She sat down on the edge of the kang. ‘We haven’t seen you over our way for two or three days. Surely your cousin Bao hasn’t offended you?’

‘Of course not,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘It’s only that my old complaint has come back. I haven’t been out of my room these last two days.’

‘Ah, yes. But what is it that keeps troubling you, miss? You ought to have a doctor as soon as possible, get a proper prescription and take a few doses faithfully, to clear it up once and for all. It’s no laughing matter to have a chronic illness at your age.’

Bao-chai smiled. ‘Please don’t speak of medicine! I don’t know how much money we’ve thrown away on doctors and remedies for this complaint. The most famous doctors and the most miraculous medicines never did the slightest good. In the end it was a bald monk who helped. He said he specialised in nameless complaints, so we asked him to examine me. He said I had a heat-toxin that I’d brought with me from the womb. Luckily, my constitution was strong to begin with, so it wasn’t doing any harm.

‘Ordinary medicines would be no use. He told us a rare prescription and gave us a packet of medicinal powder to activate it, with the most extraordinary fragrance. I don’t know where he got it. He said one pill when the trouble came on would put it right. Strange to say, his medicine really has done me some good.’

‘What sort of rare prescription is it?’ asked Zhou Rui’s wife. ‘Tell us, miss, so that we can remember it and pass it on. If we ever meet anyone with the same illness, it would be a good deed.’

Bao-chai smiled at the question. ‘You’d be better off without it. If you tried to use it, the fuss and bother would be the death of you. The ingredients themselves are nothing very special. The difficulty is getting everything to happen just right. You need twelve taels of stamens from white peonies that flower in spring, twelve taels from white lotuses that flower in summer, twelve taels from white hibiscus in autumn and twelve taels from white plum blossom in winter. On the day of the Spring Equinox the following year you dry these four kinds of stamens in the sun, mix them with the medicinal powder and grind the whole lot together.

‘Then you need twelve qian of rainwater that falls on the day of Rain Water…’

‘Good gracious!’ Zhou Rui’s wife broke in. ‘By that reckoning it would take three years! And suppose it didn’t rain at all on Rain Water day? What would you do then?’

‘That’s just what I mean,’ Bao-chai said, smiling. ‘Where are you to find rain that comes so conveniently? If it doesn’t rain, you can only go on waiting. Then twelve qian of dew from the day of White Dew, twelve qian of frost from the day of Frost’s Descent and twelve qian of snow from the day of Lesser Snow. Blend those four waters thoroughly and mix in the medicine. Add twelve qian of honey and twelve qian of white sugar, make it into pills the size of longans and put them in an old porcelain jar, which you bury under the roots of a flowering plant. When the illness comes on, take out one pill and swallow it with a decoction of twelve fen of phellodendron bark.’

Zhou Rui’s wife laughed. ‘Merciful Buddha! What a torment! You might wait ten years and still not have everything happen just so.’

‘Luckily for us, everything did come just right within a year or two of his telling us. We managed at last to make up one batch.

‘We’ve brought it north from the south, and it’s buried under the pear tree at this very moment.’

‘Does the medicine have a name?’ Zhou Rui’s wife asked.

‘Yes. The scabby-headed monk gave us that too. They’re called “Cold Fragrance Pills”.’

Zhou Rui’s wife nodded. ‘And just how do you feel when the illness comes on?’

‘Nothing very much. I only get a little wheezy and cough. Once I’ve taken a pill, I feel rather better.’

Zhou Rui’s wife was about to speak again when she suddenly heard Lady Wang ask, ‘Who’s in there?’

She hurried out to answer, and took the opportunity to report about Grannie Liu. After waiting a little while and finding that Lady Wang had nothing more to say, she was just going to withdraw when Aunt Xue stopped her with a smile. ‘Wait a moment. I’ve something for you to take with you.’ She called for Caltrop.

The doorway curtain rattled, and the little maid who had been playing with Golden came in. ‘What did you want me for, ma’am?’

‘Bring the flowers in the box,’ said Aunt Xue.

Caltrop assented and brought over a little brocade-covered box.

‘These are twelve gauze flowers in the newest palace fashion,’ Aunt Xue said. ‘It occurred to me yesterday that it was a shame to leave them lying idle. Why not let the girls wear them? I meant to send them over yesterday, but then I forgot. You’ve come at just the right moment to take them. A pair for each of your three young ladies. Of the six left over, give two to Miss Lin, and the other four to our Feng.’

‘Keep them for Bao-chai to wear,’ Lady Wang said. ‘You’re always thinking of the others.’

‘You don’t know what a peculiar girl she is, Sister,’ said Aunt Xue. ‘She’s never cared for flowers and powder and such things.’

Zhou Rui’s wife took the box and went out. Golden was still there, basking in the sun.

‘That little Caltrop,’ she asked Golden, ‘is she that girl people keep talking about, the one they bought just before coming to the capital and got into a murder case over?’

‘The very one.’

As they were speaking, Caltrop came up smiling. Zhou Rui’s wife took her hand and studied her for a while, then turned to Golden with a smile. ‘She really is a pretty creature. There’s quite a look of Mrs Rong at the Ning mansion about her.’

‘That’s just what I say,’ Golden answered, smiling.

Zhou Rui’s wife asked Caltrop how old she had been when she entered service here, and then where her parents were now, how far into her teens she was and where she came from. To every question Caltrop shook her head and answered, ‘I can’t remember.’ Hearing this, Zhou Rui’s wife and Golden fell to sighing and grieving for her instead.

Presently Zhou Rui’s wife carried the flowers round to the back of Lady Wang’s principal rooms. Grandmother Jia had recently declared that there were too many granddaughters crowding together for comfort. She had kept only Bao-yu and Dai-yu with her to amuse her, and moved Ying-chun, Tan-chun and Xi-chun into three small rooms in the annex behind Lady Wang’s apartments, with Li Wan to keep them company and look after them.

As this lay on her way, Zhou Rui’s wife went there first. Several little maids were waiting in the annex to be called. Ying-chun’s maid Chess and Tan-chun’s maid Scribe were just coming out through the curtain, each carrying a teacup. From this she knew the sisters were sitting together. She went into the inner room and found Ying-chun and Tan-chun playing Go by the window. She presented the flowers and explained where they had come from. Both girls stopped their game at once, half rose to offer their thanks and told the maids to put the flowers away.

Zhou Rui’s wife assented. ‘Is the Fourth Miss not in her room? I suppose she’s over with the Old Lady.’

‘Isn’t she just in here?’ said the maids.

Hearing this, Zhou Rui’s wife went into the adjoining room. Xi-chun was there, laughing and playing with Sapientia, the little nun from Water-Moon Hermitage.

When she saw Zhou Rui’s wife, Xi-chun asked what had brought her. The woman opened the flower box and explained.

‘I was just telling Sapientia that one of these days I’ll shave my head and become a nun with her,’ Xi-chun said with a laugh. ‘And now you bring me flowers! If I shave my head, where am I to put them?’

They all laughed and joked about it for a while, and Xi-chun called her maid Picture

to come and put them away.

‘When did you get here?’ Zhou Rui’s wife asked Sapientia. ‘And where’s that bald old rascal, your teacher, gone?’

‘We came early this morning. My teacher went on to Mr Yu’s house after seeing Her Ladyship. She told me to wait for her here.’

‘Have you had your monthly silver for incense offerings, due on the fifteenth?’

Sapientia shook her head. ‘I don’t know.’

‘Who looks after the monthly allowances for the temples now?’ Xi-chun asked.

‘Yu Xin does.’

‘So that was it!’ Xi-chun said, laughing. ‘As soon as her teacher arrived, Yu Xin’s wife came hurrying over, and they whispered together for ages. I suppose that was what they were talking about.’

Zhou Rui’s wife chatted with Sapientia a little longer, then set off for Xi-feng’s rooms. She took the narrow passage past Li Wan’s back window,

passed the ornamental wall on the west and went out through the western side gate into Xi-feng’s courtyard. At the main room she found the little maid Felicity sitting on the threshold of Xi-feng’s room. At the sight of her, Felicity waved urgently for her to go into the room to the east. Taking the hint, Zhou Rui’s wife tiptoed in and found the nurse patting the little girl to sleep.

‘Is your mistress having her midday nap?’ she whispered to the nurse. ‘It must be time to wake her.’

The nurse shook her head. Just then a burst of laughter came from the other room, and Jia Lian’s voice could be heard among it. The door sounded, and Patience came out with a large copper basin. She told Felicity to ladle out some water and take it in.

Patience then came into the room where Zhou Rui’s wife was waiting. ‘What have you come all this way for now, Mrs Zhou?’

Zhou Rui’s wife rose at once, handed her the box and explained about the flowers. Patience opened it, took out four and went back. A little later she reappeared with two in her hand. First she called Sunshine. ‘Take these over to the other mansion for young Mrs Rong to wear.’ Only then did she tell Zhou Rui’s wife to go back with their thanks.

Zhou Rui’s wife now set off for Grandmother Jia’s quarters. As she passed through a passage hall, she looked up and saw her daughter, smartly dressed, just arrived from her husband’s home.

‘What have you come running over here for at this hour?’ she asked.

‘Have you been keeping well, Mother?’ her daughter said with a smile. ‘I’ve been waiting at home for ages, but you never came out. What’s keeping you so busy that you can’t get home? I got tired of waiting and came in myself to pay my respects to the Old Lady. Now I’m on my way to Her Ladyship. What errands have you still got to finish? What’s that you’re carrying?’

‘Oh!’ her mother said, smiling. ‘Of all days, this Grannie Liu had to turn up today. I took it upon myself to help her and spent half the day running about. Then Her Ladyship’s sister caught sight of me and sent me off with these flowers for the young ladies and young mistresses. I haven’t even finished delivering them yet.

‘There must be something the matter, for you to come hurrying here like this.’

‘How good you are at guessing!’ her daughter said with a smile. ‘To tell you the truth, your son-in-law had a glass or two too many the other day and quarrelled with someone. Somehow they’ve stirred up a nasty piece of mischief against him, saying he’s a man of dubious origins. They’ve reported him to the yamen and want him sent back under escort to his native place. So I’ve come to ask your advice. Who could we ask to do us a favour and settle it?’

‘I knew it! What’s so dreadful about that? Go home and wait for me. As soon as I’ve taken Miss Lin her flowers, I’ll come back. Her Ladyship and Mrs Lian are both busy just now. Go home and wait. It’s nothing to make such a fuss about.’

Her daughter turned to go. ‘Do come quickly, Mother, whatever you do.’

‘Yes, yes. You young things have never been through anything. Just look at the state you get into!’ With that,

she went to Dai-yu’s room. But Dai-yu was not there: she was in Bao-yu’s room, where they were all playing with a nine linked rings puzzle. Zhou Rui’s wife went in with a smile. ‘Miss Lin, Her Ladyship’s sister has sent me with some flowers for you to wear.’

‘What flowers?’ Bao-yu asked before anyone else could speak. ‘Give them to me.’ His hand was already out to take the box. When he opened it, they proved to be exquisitely fashioned artificial flowers of gathered gauze, made in the palace. Dai-yu merely glanced at them in Bao-yu’s hands.

‘Are these just for me, or have the other girls some too?’

‘All the young ladies have had theirs. These two are yours, miss.’

Dai-yu gave a cold laugh. ‘I knew it. Nothing comes to me till the others have had their pick.’

Zhou Rui’s wife said not a word.

‘What took you over there, Sister Zhou?’ Bao-yu asked.

‘Her Ladyship was there, and I went to report to her. Her sister took the opportunity to ask me to bring these over.’

‘What’s Cousin Bao doing at home?

‘Why hasn’t she come over for the last few days?’

‘She hasn’t been very well.’

Bao-yu turned to the maids. ‘Who’ll go and see her? Say Miss Lin and I have sent to ask after Aunt and Cousin Bao, and find out what’s wrong with her and what medicine she’s taking. I ought really to come myself. Say I’ve only just got back from school and have caught a bit of a chill, but I’ll come to see her another day.’

Snowpink answered that she would go, and set off.

Zhou Rui’s wife went away, and we need say no more of that. Her son-in-law was in fact Yu-cun’s friend Leng Zi-xing. He had lately become involved in a lawsuit over the sale of antiques, and had sent his wife to ask for help from her connections. With her masters’ power and influence behind her, Zhou Rui’s wife thought little of such matters. That evening she had only to ask Xi-feng, and it was settled.

At lamp-lighting time, Xi-feng, having taken off her make-up, came to report to Lady Wang.

‘I’ve received the things the Zhen family sent today. Their boat that brought the fresh provisions for the New Year will be going back. Shall we give them our return gifts to take with them?’

Lady Wang nodded.

‘And the birthday presents for the old lady of the Earl of Lin’an’s household are ready. Who shall we send with them?’

‘See who’s free and send four of the women. Why come asking me again as though it were some great matter?’

Xi-feng smiled. ‘Cousin Zhen’s wife came today and asked me to go over tomorrow for a little visit. There doesn’t seem to be anything to do tomorrow.’

‘Whether there is or not makes no difference,’ said Lady Wang. ‘Usually, when she asks us all, you can’t feel quite at ease with us there. Since this time she’s asked only you, it’s clear she really wants you to relax and enjoy yourself. Don’t disappoint her. You ought to go even if you do have things to attend to.’

Xi-feng assented. Li Wan, Ying-chun, Tan-chun and the other girls then came to pay their evening respects and returned to their own rooms. Of this we need say no more.

Next morning, after washing and dressing, Xi-feng first told Lady Wang she was going, then went to take leave of Grandmother Jia. When Bao-yu heard about the visit, he wanted to go too. Xi-feng had to agree, and stood waiting while he changed his clothes. The two cousins got into the carriage and soon arrived at the Ning mansion. Jia Zhen’s wife You-shi and her daughter-in-law Qin-shi, Jia Rong’s wife, had already come out through the ceremonial gate to welcome them, with a great many concubines, maids and married women servants. As always when she saw Xi-feng, You-shi began with a laughing bout of teasing. Then, taking Bao-yu by the hand, she led them into the main room and they sat down. Once Qin-shi had served the tea, Xi-feng spoke.

‘What have you invited me here for? If you have anything nice to offer me, bring it out quickly. I’ve things to do.’

Before You-shi or Qin-shi could reply, several of the concubines standing below them began to laugh. ‘If you hadn’t come today, ma’am, that would have been another matter. But now you’re here, you can’t have everything your own way.’

Just then Jia Rong came in to pay his respects.

‘Is Cousin Zhen not at home today?’ Bao-yu asked.

‘He’s gone out of town to pay his respects to his father,’ said You-shi. ‘But you must be finding this very dull. What are you doing sitting here? Why don’t you go and amuse yourself too?’

‘What a coincidence,’ Qin-shi said with a smile. ‘My brother’s here today, Uncle Bao, the one you wanted to see that very instant last time. I think he’s in the study. Why don’t you go and see him?’

At this Bao-yu climbed straight down from the kang and was off.

‘Careful! What’s the hurry?’ You-shi and Xi-feng both cried. Then they told his attendants, ‘Stay close and take good care of him. Don’t let him feel neglected here, as though it were not the same as coming with the Old Lady.’

‘If that’s so, why not ask young Master Qin in?’ Xi-feng said. ‘I should like to see him too. Is there some reason why I can’t?’

‘No, no, you needn’t see him!’ You-shi said, laughing. ‘He’s not like our children, used to all their rough-and-tumble ways. Other people’s children have been brought up to be quiet and well-mannered. If he suddenly came face to face with a rowdy like you, he’d laugh himself to death at us.’

‘Anyone in the whole wide world should be glad enough if I don’t laugh at them,’ Xi-feng retorted with a laugh. ‘And you think I’m going to let a mere child laugh at me?’

‘That’s not what we mean, Aunt,’ Jia Rong said, smiling. ‘He’s shy by nature and unused to grand occasions. If you saw him, you might only be annoyed.’

‘Pah!’ said Xi-feng. ‘I’d want to see him even if he were Nezha himself! Stop your damned nonsense. If you don’t bring him here, I’ll give you a good slap.’

‘I wouldn’t dare go against you,’ Jia Rong said with a grin. ‘I’ll bring him at once.’

He went out and returned with a youth a little slimmer than Bao-yu, with finely drawn brows, lovely eyes, a fair face and red lips. His figure was handsome, and there was a grace in his movements that seemed to surpass even Bao-yu’s. But he was bashful and timid, with something of a young girl’s air. Shy and faltering, he slowly made his bow to Xi-feng and greeted her.

Delighted, Xi-feng first gave Bao-yu a push. ‘You’ve been outdone!’ she said, laughing. Then she leant forward, took the boy’s hand and told him to sit beside her. She gently questioned him about his age and his studies, learning in the course of it that his school name was Qin Zhong. Meanwhile, her maids and married women servants, seeing that this was her first meeting with him and that she had brought no introductory gift, hurried back to tell Patience. Patience knew how close Xi-feng was to Qin-shi. Though the visitor was only a young boy, the gift must not be too meagre. On her own authority she therefore took a bolt of cloth and two small gold ingots bearing the auspicious words ‘First Place in the Palace Examination’, and gave them to the messenger to take over. Even so, Xi-feng laughingly protested that they were far too little, and Qin-shi and the others thanked her.

After they had eaten, You-shi, Xi-feng, Qin-shi and the others played dominoes, but we need not dwell on that. Bao-yu and Qin Zhong sat or stood as they pleased, talking together. The moment Bao-yu had seen Qin Zhong’s outstanding looks and bearing, he had felt a sense of loss, and had been spellbound for some time. Now his foolish fancies stirred again. ‘Can there really be someone like this in the world?’ he thought. ‘Beside him I’m nothing but a muddy pig, a mangy dog.

‘Why, oh why, was I born into this household of marquises and dukes? If only I’d been born into a poor family of minor officials, I could have made friends with him long ago, and my life would not have been wasted. For all my higher station, all this brocade, silk and gauze is only wrapping for a dead log. Fine wine and tender lamb merely fill a dung-pit, a muddy ditch. To think that “wealth and honour” should have suffered such abuse at my hands!’

Qin Zhong, for his part, had seen Bao-yu’s extraordinary beauty and his unaffected manner, together with his golden headpiece, embroidered clothes, haughty maids and extravagant pages. He too was thinking to himself. ‘This Bao-yu! No wonder everyone dotes on him. Why must I have been born into a family that is genteel but poor, so that I can’t be close beside him, day in, day out? That “rich” and “poor” should set bounds to people is one of the great vexations of this world.’

Both were lost in equally fanciful thoughts when Bao-yu suddenly asked what books Qin Zhong was reading. Qin Zhong answered him plainly. They exchanged a dozen or so remarks and found themselves growing closer all the time.

Presently tea and fruit were brought in.

‘Neither of us drinks wine,’ Bao-yu said. ‘Put the fruit on the little kang in the inner room. We’ll sit there, out of your way.’

The two boys went in to take their tea. While seeing that wine and fruit were set before Xi-feng, Qin-shi hurried in to speak to Bao-yu.

‘Uncle Bao, if your nephew should say anything thoughtless, please overlook it for my sake. Shy as he is, he can be stubborn and contrary, and is not always easy to get on with.’

‘Off you go,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘I understand.’

Qin-shi gave her brother a few admonitions as well, then returned to keep Xi-feng company.

A little later Xi-feng and You-shi sent someone to ask Bao-yu if he wanted anything to eat. ‘There’s plenty out here. Just ask for it.’

Bao-yu assented, but had no interest in food or drink. He only wanted to ask Qin Zhong about his present circumstances at home.

‘My tutor died of an illness last year,’ Qin Zhong told him. ‘My father is elderly and infirm, and has so much official business to attend to that there has been no discussion yet of engaging another. For the moment I’m simply revising my old lessons at home. Besides, if one is to make any progress in one’s studies, one really needs one or two congenial companions to discuss things with regularly.’

Before he had finished, Bao-yu answered. ‘Exactly! We have a family school. Anyone in the clan who can’t afford to engage a tutor can go there, and relations of the boys are allowed to attend too. My own tutor went home last year, so I’ve been neglecting my lessons as well. Father wants to send me there for the time being to revise what I’ve already learnt. When my tutor comes back next year, we can each study at home again. But Grandmother says there are so many boys at the school that she’s afraid they’ll get up to mischief and do me no good. Then, too, I was ill for a few days, so it’s been put off for the present.

‘From what you say, your father must be worrying about the same thing. Why not speak to him when you get home today and come to our humble school? I’d be there with you, and we could help one another. Wouldn’t that be splendid?’

Qin Zhong smiled. ‘When Father spoke the other day of engaging a tutor, he did mention that the charitable clan school here was a good one. He meant to ask my sister’s father-in-law to recommend me. But everyone here is so busy that he didn’t like to bother them with such a small matter. Uncle Bao, if you really think your nephew might be fit to grind your ink and wash your inkstone, why not arrange it as soon as possible? Then neither of us would neglect his lessons. We could meet and talk often, put our parents’ minds at rest and enjoy each other’s friendship. What could be better?’

‘Don’t worry, don’t worry,’ said Bao-yu. ‘We’ll tell your brother-in-law, your sister and Cousin Lian’s wife presently. When you get home today, you speak to your father, and I’ll speak to Grandmother when I get back. There’s no reason why it shouldn’t be settled at once.’

Having agreed on their plan, the two boys came out and watched the others play for a while longer. By now it was time to light the lamps.

When the accounts were settled, You-shi and Qin-shi had once again lost, and would have to give a party with wine and a theatrical performance. They agreed to hold it the day after next, and had dinner brought in. After dinner, since it was dark, You-shi said, ‘Have a couple of the lads take Master Qin home first.’

Some time after the married women servants had passed this order outside, Qin Zhong rose to take his leave.

‘Who’s been appointed to go with him?’ You-shi asked.

‘They’ve sent Jiao Da. But he’s drunk, and he’s swearing again.’

‘Why pick on him?’ You-shi and Qin-shi both exclaimed. ‘With all those lads standing about, couldn’t they send any one of them? They have to go and stir him up!’

‘I’m always telling you that you’re too soft,’ Xi-feng said. ‘How can you let the servants carry on like this?’

‘You know what Jiao Da is like,’ You-shi said with a sigh. ‘Even Father leaves him alone, and so does your cousin Zhen. As a boy he went on three or four campaigns with the old masters. He carried the old master out of a heap of dead bodies and saved his life. He went hungry himself, but stole food for his master to eat. Once, when they’d had no water for two days, he got hold of half a bowl and gave it to his master, while he drank horse piss. He’s traded on those services and old ties ever since. Our forebears used to make special allowances for him. Who’s going to make things difficult for him now?

‘Besides, he’s old, and cares nothing for his dignity. All he does is drink, and when he’s drunk there’s no one he won’t abuse. I keep telling the stewards not to give him any duties. They should simply treat him as though he were dead and leave it at that. But today they’ve sent him out again.’

‘Of course I know about Jiao Da,’ Xi-feng said. ‘It’s you who can’t make up your minds. With a man like that, why not send him off to some distant estate and have done with it?’ Then she asked, ‘Are our carriages ready?’

‘All ready and waiting,’ the attendants answered.

Xi-feng rose to take her leave and walked out hand in hand with Bao-yu. You-shi and the others accompanied them as far as the great hall, where lamps and candles blazed and the young male servants stood waiting in the red-paved forecourt. Jiao Da was emboldened by Jia Zhen’s absence. Even if his master had been at home, there was not much he could have done to him, so now he could let himself go all the more freely. With drink to spur him on, he began by abusing the chief steward Lai Er for being unfair, bullying the meek and fearing the strong.

‘Whenever there’s a nice job, you give it to somebody else. But taking someone home in the dead of night, that’s for me! You heartless son of a whore! Playing at being steward! You’d better remember that if old Master Jiao Da lifts his foot, it’s higher than your head.

‘Twenty years ago, who did old Master Jiao Da give a damn about? Never mind a pack of bastard sons of whores like you!’

He was still swearing with great relish when Jia Rong came out to see Xi-feng’s carriage off. The others shouted at him to stop, but he would not listen. Unable to bear it any longer, Jia Rong cursed him a couple of times and ordered the men to tie him up. ‘Tomorrow, when he’s sober, we’ll ask if he still wants to get himself killed!’

But Jiao Da thought nothing of Jia Rong. Instead he raised his voice and came after him, shouting, ‘Rong, my boy, don’t put on your master’s airs with Jiao Da. Never mind you: even your father and grandfather wouldn’t dare stand up to me! If it hadn’t been for Jiao Da alone, would you be holding office and enjoying all this glory and wealth?

‘Your ancestors came within a hair of death to win this family fortune. And now, instead of repaying what you owe me, you start playing the master! Keep your mouth shut and I’ll say no more. One more word, and it’ll be red blade in, white blade out!’

From the carriage Xi-feng said to Jia Rong, ‘See that you get rid of this lawless creature as soon as possible. Keeping him here is asking for trouble. If our relatives and friends heard of this, wouldn’t they laugh at a family like ours, with no order or discipline at all?’

‘Yes,’ Jia Rong answered.

Seeing that he was quite out of hand, several of the lads had no choice but to seize him, throw him down, tie him up and drag him towards the stables. Jiao Da grew more outrageous still, bringing Jia Zhen into his ravings.

‘I’m going to the ancestral hall to weep for the old master! Who could have thought his descendants would turn out to be such beasts? Fooling around on the sly every day, “stealing dogs and teasing hens”! Some raking the ashes, some keeping their husbands’ younger brothers as lovers! What don’t I know? We all “hide the broken arm inside the sleeve”!’

At these appalling words the lads were frightened out of their wits. They could think of nothing else: they bound him fast and stuffed his mouth full of earth and horse manure.

Xi-feng, Jia Rong and the others could hear him from a distance too, but pretended they had heard nothing. Bao-yu, watching the drunken uproar from the carriage, found it rather entertaining.

‘Sister,’ he asked Xi-feng, ‘did you hear him say “raking the ashes”? What does “raking the ashes” mean?’

Her eyebrows shot up and her eyes flashed. ‘Stop that nonsense!’ she snapped. ‘It’s a drunkard’s filthy babble. A boy in your position ought to pretend he hasn’t heard it, not start asking questions! I’ll tell Her Ladyship when I get home, and we’ll see if you don’t get a beating!’

Frightened, Bao-yu hurried to plead with her. ‘Dear sister, I won’t do it again!’

At once Xi-feng softened her expression and coaxed him. ‘That’s better.

‘When we get home, we’ll tell the Old Lady and arrange for you to go to school with your nephew Qin. That’s what matters.’

So they returned to the Rong mansion. As the couplet has it:

Without good looks, they could hardly be friends;

For love’s delights alone, they turn to books.
