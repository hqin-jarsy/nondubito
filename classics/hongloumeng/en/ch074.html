Chapter 74 Malicious slander brings a search of Prospect Garden; A vow of solitary purity shuts out the Ning mansion

Chapter end

Patience was still amused by Ying-chun’s answer when Bao-yu suddenly appeared. It happened that the sister of Mrs Liu, who managed the kitchen, had also been found guilty of running a gambling bank. Some people in the garden who had long been on bad terms with Mrs Liu now accused her too, saying that she and her sister were partners: though the sister’s name was used, the two of them divided the profits equally. Xi-feng therefore intended to punish Mrs Liu. When the woman heard this, she was at her wits’ end. Remembering how close she had always been to the people at Green Delights, she went there and privately begged Skybright and Gold-flecked Glass to speak to Bao-yu. Bao-yu reflected that Ying-chun’s wet-nurse was presently charged with the same offence. It would be better to ask Ying-chun to join him in pleading for mercy than to go alone on Mrs Liu’s behalf. That was why he had come. Finding so many people there, he was greeted on all sides with questions.

‘Are you better? What have you come running over here for?’

Bao-yu could hardly mention his appeal now.

‘I’ve come to see Second Sister,’ he said.

No one gave the matter any further thought, and they talked of other things. Patience went out to see about the filigree gold phoenix. Wang Zhu-er’s wife followed close behind, imploring her in every way she could.

‘Have mercy on me in what you say, miss. I’ll redeem it, whatever happens.’

‘Sooner or later you have to redeem it,’ Patience said with a smile. ‘If it was to come to this, why do it in the first place? You thought you could let it pass if nobody stopped you.

‘As things stand, I don’t like to tell on anyone either. Go and redeem it at once, and give it to me to take back. I shan’t say a word.’

Only then did Wang Zhu-er’s wife feel easy. She bowed her thanks.

‘Please go about your business, miss. I’ll bring it this evening and report to you before taking it over. Will that do?’

‘If it isn’t here this evening, don’t blame me,’ said Patience.

With that, they separated and went their different ways.

When Patience returned to her rooms, Xi-feng asked, ‘What did Third Sister want with you?’

‘The Third Miss was afraid you might be upset, ma’am,’ Patience answered with a smile. ‘She told me to try to soothe you, and asked what you’ve been able to eat these last two days.’

‘At least she still thinks of me,’ said Xi-feng, smiling. ‘Another thing has cropped up just now. Someone came to accuse Liu Er’s wife of running a gambling den with her sister. Everything the sister does is supposed to be her doing. You’re always telling me that the less I take on, the better: it would give my mind a little rest, and some chance to look after myself. I wouldn’t listen, and some of it has certainly come true. First I offended Her Ladyship, and then all I earned for myself was an illness. Well, I see through it now. Let them make all the trouble they please. There are plenty of other people here. I worry myself for nothing, and get everyone’s curses in return. Getting well must come first. Even when I’m better, I mean to be easy-going: enjoy myself when I can, laugh when I can, and leave them to settle all their rights and wrongs.

‘So I merely said I’d heard, and gave it no more thought.’

‘If you really mean that, ma’am, what a blessing for us!’ Patience said with a smile.

She had scarcely finished when Jia Lian came in, clapping his hands together and sighing.

‘Now there’s more trouble out of nothing! How did Mother get to hear of my asking Faithful for things to pawn the other day? She sent for me just now and told me to raise two hundred taels, wherever I could, for the festival on the fifteenth of the eighth month. I said there was nowhere I could get it. “When you have no money,” she said, “you can find somewhere to borrow it. But when I ask you, you put me off and say there’s nowhere.

‘“Where did that thousand taels from the pawnshop come from the other day? You have magic enough to get hold of Grandmother’s things, yet now, for two hundred taels, you behave like this! Fortunately I haven’t spoken to anyone else about it.” She plainly isn’t short of money. Why must she go out of her way to make things difficult?’

‘There wasn’t a single outsider here that day,’ said Xi-feng. ‘Who could have let it out?’

Patience too tried to recall who had been there. After thinking for some time, she smiled.

‘Of course!

‘There were no outsiders while we were talking. But in the evening, when the things were brought over, Simpleton’s mother happened to come from Her Old Ladyship’s with the washing. She sat in the servants’ room for a while. Naturally she would ask about such a big chest of things. Perhaps the little maids didn’t know any better and told her.’

They summoned several of the little maids and asked which of them had told Simpleton’s mother that day. The terrified girls all knelt down, swearing oath upon oath.

‘We never dare say a word out of turn. Whatever anyone asks, we always say we don’t know. How could we dare say anything about this?’

Xi-feng considered the circumstances.

‘They wouldn’t dare. We mustn’t wrong them. Put that aside for now. The first thing is to satisfy Her Ladyship. Better go short ourselves than give her cause to be displeased.’

She told Patience, ‘Fetch my gold neck-ring. Have it pawned for two hundred taels for the time being. We’ll send her the money and have done with it.’

‘You might as well raise another two hundred,’ said Jia Lian. ‘We need money too.’

‘There’s no need at all. I have nothing to spend it on. Once this has gone, I don’t even know what money we can count on to redeem it.’

Patience fetched the neck-ring and sent someone to call Wang-er’s wife to take it away. Before long the silver was brought back. Jia Lian delivered it himself, and we need say no more of that.

Xi-feng and Patience went on wondering who had let the news out, but could think of no one.

‘It’s a small matter that she knows,’ said Xi-feng. ‘What frightens me is that some mischief-maker may seize the chance to invent more lies and start something else.

‘Just now, when that side of the family has fallen out with Faithful, they hear that she’s privately lent things to Master Lian! Those spiteful creatures are never satisfied. They’d put maggots into an egg without a crack in it. With this to go on, who knows what wicked stories they may invent? It wouldn’t do your Master Lian much harm, but Faithful is a decent unmarried girl. If we let her be wronged through us, wouldn’t it be our fault?’

‘There’s no need to worry about that either,’ Patience said with a smile. ‘It was for your sake that Faithful lent the things, not the Second Master’s. And though it’s supposed to be a private favour of her own, she did in fact tell Her Old Ladyship. The old lady was afraid that, with all those grandchildren, one would want to borrow and another would ask for something else. Once they’d wheedled it out of her, whom could she ask to give it back? So she merely pretends not to know.

‘Even if it comes out, no harm will be done in the end.’

‘That’s true enough,’ said Xi-feng. ‘But you and I know that. How can you stop people who don’t know from suspecting something?’

She had not finished when someone announced, ‘Her Ladyship is here.’

Xi-feng was astonished. What could have brought her in person? She hurried out with Patience and the others to meet her. Lady Wang’s whole expression had changed. Accompanied by just one little maid whom she trusted, she came in without a word and sat down in the inner room. Xi-feng hastily offered her tea.

‘What a pleasant surprise to have you come over today, Aunt,’ she said with a smile.

‘Patience, go out!’ Lady Wang commanded.

Patience, alarmed and unable to understand what had happened, answered at once and took all the little maids outside. She stopped at the door, then shut it altogether and sat down on the steps, allowing no one to enter. Xi-feng too was frightened, with no idea what could be wrong. With tears in her eyes, Lady Wang drew a scent-bag from her sleeve and flung it down.

‘Look at that.’

Xi-feng picked it up at once. The sight of the many-coloured embroidery with its erotic design startled her too.

‘Where did you get it, Aunt?’

At the question Lady Wang’s tears fell faster than ever.

‘Where did I get it!’ she said in a trembling voice. ‘I sit here every day like someone at the bottom of a well. I thought you were a careful person, so I allowed myself a little peace. Who would have thought you were no better than I am? Something like that lying openly on a rock in the garden in broad daylight, for one of Grandmother’s maids to pick up! If your mother-in-law hadn’t happened to meet her, it would have been taken straight to Grandmother.

‘Tell me, how did this thing come to be left there?’

Xi-feng’s colour changed.

‘What makes you think it’s mine, Aunt?’

Lady Wang wept and sighed.

‘You ask me that? Think! Apart from you two, a young married couple, who is there in this household but old women? What use would they have for it? And where would the unmarried girls get such a thing? Naturally that worthless, low-minded Lian has picked it up somewhere. You two get on so well. You treated it as a plaything. Young people have their private pleasures in the bedchamber. Yet you still deny it to me! Luckily the people in the garden are too innocent to understand, and hadn’t picked it up yet. What if one of the maids had found it and your sisters had seen it? What then? Or if some little girl had picked it up and gone outside saying she’d found it in the garden, and outsiders had heard? Do we want to keep our lives and our good name or not?’

Distress and shame flooded Xi-feng’s face a deep purple. She sank to both knees beside the kang, and answered through her own tears.

‘There’s reason in what you say, Aunt. I daren’t argue that I possess no such things. But I beg you to consider this more closely. That scent-bag was embroidered by hired workers outside, copying palace work. The cords and tassels are all shop goods. Young and frivolous as I may be, I wouldn’t want that rubbish. Anything of mine would naturally be good. That is the first point. Second, this isn’t something one ordinarily carries about. Even if I had one, I could only keep it at home. Why would I wear it everywhere I went, and into the garden of all places? We sisters are always catching hold of one another. If it showed, what a position I should be in, not merely before the girls but even before the servants! I may be young and frivolous, but I cannot be as foolish as that. Third, among the family I may be the young wife, but among the servants there is more than one wife younger than I am.

‘They often go into the garden too, and each goes home at night. How do we know it wasn’t dropped by one of them? Fourth, I’m not the only one who goes there often. Her Ladyship from the other side is always bringing some of the younger concubines, Carmine and Cui-yun, for instance. They are young concubines, and even more likely to own such a thing. Then there’s Cousin Zhen’s wife. She’s hardly an innocent in such matters either, and she often brings Pei-feng and the others over. How do we know it isn’t theirs? Fifth, there are so many maids in the garden. Can we guarantee that every one of them is respectable? Some of the older ones know about these things. They might slip out when nobody happens to ask where they are, or find an excuse to flirt and banter with the young pages at the inner gate, and get it from outside. Who can say? As things stand, not only have I had nothing to do with it, but I can answer for Patience as well. Please think it over, Aunt.’

Lady Wang found this reasoning very persuasive.

‘Get up,’ she sighed. ‘I know you were brought up as a young lady in a good family. How could you be so shameless? I was simply beside myself, and said it to sting you.

‘But what are we to do now? Your mother-in-law has just sent someone over with this sealed up for me to see. She says she took it from Simpleton the day before yesterday. I was furious enough to die.’

‘Please don’t be angry any more,’ said Xi-feng. ‘If everyone notices, there’s no keeping it from Grandmother. We must stay calm and inquire quietly. Then we may find out the truth, and even if we don’t, outsiders won’t hear of it. As they say, “Keep a broken arm inside the sleeve.” Now that so many people have been dismissed over the gambling, our only course is to take advantage of it and put four or five trusted women in the garden who won’t let anything slip: Zhou Rui’s wife, Wang-er’s wife and the like. They can say they’re looking into gambling. Besides, the girls have too many maids now. As they grow older they grow more ambitious, and we can’t be sure they won’t make trouble. Once something happens, it will be too late for regrets. If we dismiss them without a reason, the girls will feel hurt and miserable, and you and I will find it difficult too. Better use this opportunity. In future, find some fault with the older ones, or any who are obstinate and troublesome, send them away and marry them off.

‘For one thing, it would prevent further trouble; for another, it would save something in expenses. What do you think, Aunt?’

‘You have a point,’ Lady Wang sighed. ‘But if we consider it fairly, these sisters of yours have a sorry enough time. We needn’t look far for a comparison. Think of your cousin Dai-yu’s mother before she married. How tenderly she was brought up, how precious she was! That was the life of a young lady of a great family. These girls are scarcely better off than other people’s maids. Each has only two or three maids fit to be seen. There may be four or five little ones besides, but they’re no better than the little demons in a temple. If we dismiss still more, I couldn’t bear it, and I doubt Grandmother would consent. Things may be difficult, but surely not as bad as that. I never enjoyed any extraordinary splendour or wealth myself, yet I was better off than you. I would rather go without than let them suffer. If there’s to be economy, begin with me.

‘For now, have someone call in Zhou Rui’s wife and the others. The important thing is to tell them to investigate this quietly and catch the culprit at once.’

Xi-feng called Patience in and sent her out with the order. Soon Zhou Rui’s wife, Wu Xing’s wife, Zheng Hua’s wife, Wang-er’s wife and Lai Xi’s wife arrived. These were the five dowry households presently there; the others had their several duties in the south. Lady Wang was regretting that there were too few people to investigate when Wang Shan-bao’s wife, one of Lady Xing’s dowry servants, appeared. She was the very woman who had brought the scent-bag over. Lady Wang had always treated Lady Xing’s trusted attendants and capable servants without distinction. Seeing how eagerly she had come to inquire about the matter, she said, ‘Go and tell Her Ladyship, and you can help keep an eye on the garden too. You’ll be more use than someone else.’

Wang Shan-bao’s wife had long resented the maids’ failure to court her when she visited the garden. She had wanted to find something against them, but had never managed it. Now, with this affair, she thought she had her chance.

Lady Wang’s commission was exactly what she had hoped for.

‘That’s easily done. Not that your servant wishes to talk too much, but there ought to have been stricter rules long ago. You don’t go into the garden very often, ma’am. Every one of those girls behaves as though she’d been granted a title. They’ve made themselves into fine young ladies. They could bring the sky down, and who would dare utter a word? Otherwise they stir up the young ladies’ maids to say their mistresses are being bullied. Who can afford to be blamed for that?’

‘It’s natural enough,’ said Lady Wang. ‘The maids attending the girls have always been more pampered than the rest. You should reason with them. Even the daughters of the family will turn out badly if no one teaches them, let alone their maids.’

‘Never mind the others. But there’s one you don’t know about, ma’am: Skybright in Bao-yu’s rooms. She trades on being prettier than the rest, and she’s got a ready tongue besides. Every day she decks herself out like a Xi Shi, and wherever she goes she must have her say and get the better of everyone.

‘One word she doesn’t like, and she turns those wanton eyes on you and starts abusing you. The way she flaunts herself is quite disgraceful.’

This suddenly stirred a memory in Lady Wang.

‘The last time we went into the garden with Grandmother,’ she asked Xi-feng, ‘there was a girl with a waist like a water-snake, sloping shoulders,

‘and something of your cousin Dai-yu about the eyes and brows. She was scolding one of the little maids. I thoroughly disliked her wild manner, but as I was walking with Grandmother I couldn’t say anything. Afterwards I meant to ask who she was, and then forgot. Now it all fits. That must be the girl.’

‘If you compare all the maids,’ said Xi-feng, ‘none of them is as pretty as Skybright. She is rather frivolous in her speech and manner. Your description sounds very like her, but I’ve forgotten what happened that day and wouldn’t care to say.’

‘There’s no need for this,’ said Wang Shan-bao’s wife. ‘We can easily call her here now for Your Ladyship to see.’

‘The only ones from Bao-yu’s rooms who regularly come to see me are Aroma and Musk,’ said Lady Wang. ‘They’re a dull pair, and all the better for it. A girl like this would naturally not dare come before me. I’ve always detested such people, and now there’s this affair as well. What if that little hussy were to seduce and corrupt my good Bao-yu? What then?’

She called her own maid and sent her into the garden.

‘Simply say I have some questions for them. Aroma and Musk are to stay and look after Bao-yu; they needn’t come. There’s a particularly clever one called Skybright. Tell her to come at once.

‘You’re not to tell her anything else.’

The little maid answered and went to the House of Green Delights. Skybright was unwell and had just risen from her afternoon sleep, feeling out of sorts. When she heard the message, she had no choice but to go. The maids all knew that Lady Wang particularly disliked showy dress and frivolous talk, so Skybright ordinarily took care not to come forward. Having felt ill for several days, she had scarcely dressed up at all, and thought there could be nothing objectionable about her. But when she reached Xi-feng’s rooms, Lady Wang saw her with hairpins loose and hair disordered, her gown hanging slack and her sash undone: she had all the air of a beauty waking from spring sleep, of Xi Shi pressing a hand to her breast. Her face and figure were exactly those of the girl Lady Wang had seen the month before. The sight rekindled her anger. Lady Wang was by nature open and unguarded, showing what she felt, unlike those who disguise their meaning in fine words. Now that she was truly angry, and the old recollection had returned, she gave a cold laugh.

‘What a beauty! A perfect ailing Xi Shi. Whom do you put on these shameless airs for every day? Do you imagine I don’t know what you’ve been doing?

‘I’ll leave you for now, but I shall skin you one of these days! Is Bao-yu any better today?’

Skybright was astonished and knew at once that someone had been plotting against her. Angry though she was, she dared not speak. She was extraordinarily quick-witted. When asked whether Bao-yu was better, she would not answer truthfully.

‘I don’t often go into Bao-yu’s room, and I’m seldom with him, so I couldn’t say how he is. You would have to ask Aroma and Musk.’

‘You deserve a slap in the mouth for that!’ said Lady Wang. ‘Are you dead, then? What do we keep you for?’

‘I originally belonged to Her Old Ladyship’s rooms. She said the garden was big and empty, with too few people, and Bao-yu was frightened. So she sent me to keep watch at night in the outer room. I was only to look after the rooms. I did tell her I was too clumsy to wait on anyone. She scolded me and said, “I’m not asking you to look after him. What do I want with anyone clever?” That was why I went.

‘Once in ten days or a fortnight, when Bao-yu is bored, we all amuse ourselves together for a while and then separate. As for his meals and getting up and going to bed, there are the senior nurses, and below them Aroma and Musk and Ripple and the others. When I’m free, I still have needlework to do for Her Old Ladyship’s rooms, so I really haven’t paid attention to Bao-yu’s affairs. But since Your Ladyship is displeased, I shall take care to do so from now on.’

Lady Wang believed her.

‘Buddha be praised! If you keep away from Bao-yu, it’s a blessing for me. Pray don’t trouble yourself. Since it was Grandmother who gave you to him, I’ll speak to her tomorrow before sending you packing.’

She turned to Wang Shan-bao’s wife.

‘Go in there and watch her closely for the next few days. She’s not to sleep in Bao-yu’s room. Once I’ve spoken to Grandmother, I’ll deal with her.’

‘Go!’ she cried. ‘I can’t bear to have you standing here looking so wanton. Who gave you leave to dress yourself up in all those gaudy colours?’

Skybright could only leave. She was furious. Once outside, she pressed her handkerchief to her face and walked on crying, all the way back through the garden gate.

Lady Wang reproached herself before Xi-feng and the others.

‘I’ve grown so weary these last few years, I can’t keep an eye on everything. To think I never even noticed a creature like that, a regular enchantress! I’m afraid there may be more. I shall have to look into it tomorrow.’

Xi-feng saw that Lady Wang was in a fury. Besides, Wang Shan-bao’s wife was Lady Xing’s eyes and ears, forever prompting her to make trouble. Though she might have had a thousand things to say, she dared say none of them now, and merely bowed her head and assented.

‘Your Ladyship must rest and look after your health,’ said Wang Shan-bao’s wife. ‘Leave these little matters to your servants. It will be quite easy to find out whose this is. Wait till tonight, when the garden gates are shut and no word can pass between inside and out. Then we’ll take them unawares and bring people to search all the maids’ rooms. Whoever owns this is hardly likely to have only this one thing. There must be other things as well. Once we turn those up, naturally this will be hers too.’

‘That makes sense,’ said Lady Wang.

‘Without that, we’ll never distinguish the innocent from the guilty.’

She asked Xi-feng what she thought. Xi-feng had no choice but to agree.

‘You’re quite right, Aunt. Let it be done.’

‘It’s a very good plan,’ said Lady Wang. ‘Otherwise we might spend a year and never find out.’

They settled the arrangements together.

After the evening meal, when Grandmother Jia had gone to bed and Bao-chai and the others were returning to the garden, Wang Shan-bao’s wife invited Xi-feng to accompany her in. She ordered all the side gates locked. They began by searching the quarters of the old women on night duty, but found only surplus candles, lamp oil and similar things that had been put by.

‘These are stolen goods too,’ said Wang Shan-bao’s wife. ‘Leave them alone. Nothing is to be touched until we’ve reported to Her Ladyship tomorrow.’

They went first to the House of Green Delights and ordered the gates shut. Bao-yu was already troubled about Skybright. Suddenly this party appeared and made straight for the maids’ rooms, without his knowing why. He came out to meet Xi-feng and asked what was the matter.

‘Something important has gone missing,’ she said. ‘Everyone denies knowing anything about it. We’re afraid one of the maids may have stolen it, so we’re searching everywhere to clear things up.’

She sat down to drink tea as she spoke. Wang Shan-bao’s wife and the others searched for a while, then asked carefully whose each chest was, calling its owner to open it herself. From Skybright’s condition Aroma knew something unusual must have happened. Now, seeing the search, she could only come forward first and open her chest and boxes for them to examine as they pleased. They contained nothing but ordinary articles for daily use. These were put down and the others searched in turn, one after another. When they reached Skybright’s chest, they asked, ‘Whose is this? Why hasn’t it been opened for us?’

Aroma and the others were about to open it for her when Skybright burst in, holding up her hair. She flung the lid back with a crash, gripped the bottom with both hands and tipped it upside down, pouring every last thing onto the floor. Wang Shan-bao’s wife felt rather foolish. She looked over the contents, but there was nothing improper or concealed there either. She reported to Xi-feng and prepared to go on elsewhere.

‘Make sure you search thoroughly,’ said Xi-feng. ‘If we find nothing after all this, it will be hard to explain.’

‘We’ve been through everything carefully,’ they all said. ‘There’s nothing out of place.

‘There are a few male things, but they’re all children’s things. We suppose they’re old belongings of Bao-yu’s. Nothing that matters.’

‘In that case, let’s go,’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘We’ll look somewhere else.’

As they came out, she spoke to Wang Shan-bao’s wife.

‘There’s one thing I’d like to say, though I don’t know whether you’ll agree. We should search only our own people. Miss Xue’s rooms are quite out of the question.’

‘Of course,’ the woman said, smiling. ‘We can hardly start searching our relations.’

‘That’s what I thought,’ said Xi-feng, nodding.

Still talking, they reached Naiad’s House. Dai-yu was already asleep. When their arrival was announced, she had no idea what they wanted and was just about to get up when Xi-feng came in. Xi-feng pressed her back down at once.

‘Go on sleeping. We’ll be off in a moment.’

She stayed to chat, while Wang Shan-bao’s wife took the others to the maids’ rooms. There too they opened the chests and emptied the baskets, searching everything in turn. In Nightingale’s room they found two sets of charms of the sort Bao-yu wore for his nominal religious affiliations and regularly changed, a sash attachment from a waist-belt, two purses, and a fan case with a fan inside. When these were opened and examined, all proved to be things Bao-yu had carried in former days.

Wang Shan-bao’s wife was delighted with herself. She hastily called Xi-feng over to inspect them.

‘Where did these things come from?’ she asked.

‘Bao-yu grew up with these girls,’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘They spent years together. Naturally these are old things of his. There’s nothing remarkable about that. Leave them and let’s go on somewhere else.’

‘Even now, we couldn’t sort out which things belong to which of us,’ Nightingale said, smiling. ‘As for these, I’ve forgotten myself when they came here.’

After Xi-feng’s words, Wang Shan-bao’s wife could only drop the matter.

They went on to Tan-chun’s courtyard, but someone had already brought her the news. She guessed that something must have happened to produce such a disgraceful spectacle, and ordered her maids to light candles and open the doors to await them. When they arrived, she asked what had brought them, as though she did not know.

‘Something has gone missing,’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘We’ve been making inquiries for days and can’t find out who took it. We’re afraid people may blame these girls, so we’ve decided to search everywhere and remove all suspicion. It’s a good way of clearing them.’

Tan-chun laughed coldly.

‘Naturally all our maids are thieves, and I am their chief receiver.

‘Then begin with my chests and cupboards. Everything they steal is handed over to me to hide.’

She told the maids to open all the chests and cupboards, the mirror cases and dressing boxes, the bedding wraps and clothes bundles, large and small alike, and invited Xi-feng to search them.

‘I’m only carrying out Aunt’s orders, dear,’ Xi-feng said with an ingratiating smile. ‘Don’t blame me unjustly. There’s no need to be angry.’

She told the maids to shut everything at once. Patience, Felicity and the others hurried to help Scribe and the other maids put things away and close them up.

‘You may search my things,’ said Tan-chun. ‘But if you mean to search my maids, I shall not allow it. I’ve always been more wicked than the others. I know everything my maids possess, and it’s all kept here in my inner room. They have neither a needle nor a thread put by themselves. So if you want to search, search me. If that doesn’t suit you, go and tell Her Ladyship that I have disobeyed her. Whatever punishment is called for, I’ll go and receive it myself. Don’t be in such a hurry. Your turn to be searched will come too!

‘Weren’t you talking about the Zhen family this morning, and about having a perfectly good household searched and confiscated? And now, sure enough, we’re being searched today. Our turn is coming little by little. A great family like ours cannot be destroyed all at once by an attack from outside. As the ancients said, “A hundred-footed creature does not fall stiff even when dead.” We must first start killing and destroying ourselves from within before we can be brought utterly to ruin!’

As she spoke, tears began to fall. Xi-feng merely looked at the married women servants.

‘Since all the girls’ things are here,’ said Zhou Rui’s wife, ‘perhaps we should go on elsewhere, ma’am, and let the young lady get to bed.’

Xi-feng rose to take her leave.

‘Have you searched thoroughly?’ Tan-chun asked. ‘If you come back tomorrow, I won’t stand for it.’

‘Since the maids’ things are all here,’ Xi-feng said with a smile, ‘there’s no need to search.’

Tan-chun laughed coldly.

‘Very clever of you! You’ve even had my bundles opened, yet you say you haven’t searched.

‘Tomorrow you’ll be saying I protected my maids and wouldn’t let you look. Say plainly now. If you still want to search, you’re welcome to do it again.’

Xi-feng knew how unlike the others Tan-chun was. She could only smile appeasingly.

‘I have searched everything thoroughly, including your things.’

‘And have all of you searched thoroughly?’ Tan-chun asked the others.

‘We’ve looked through everything,’ Zhou Rui’s wife and her companions said with conciliatory smiles.

Wang Shan-bao’s wife had never been a woman of much judgement. She had heard of Tan-chun’s reputation, but put it down to other people’s lack of sense and courage. How formidable could a young girl really be? And a concubine’s daughter at that: what could she dare to do? The woman relied on her own position as one of Lady Xing’s dowry servants. Even Lady Wang treated her with special consideration, let alone anyone else. Seeing Tan-chun now, she assumed that her anger was directed entirely at Xi-feng and had nothing to do with them. She thought she would take the opportunity to show her own importance and make herself agreeable. Stepping out in front of the others, she caught Tan-chun’s robe and deliberately lifted its front, laughing as she did so.

‘I’ve even searched the young lady herself. There really is nothing!’

‘Come along, Mamma,’ Xi-feng said hastily. ‘Don’t behave so foolishly.’

Before she had finished, there was a sharp crack. Tan-chun had slapped the woman across the face.

Tan-chun’s anger blazed up. She pointed at Wang Shan-bao’s wife.

‘What are you, to dare pull at my clothes? Out of respect for Her Ladyship, and because you’re old, I call you Mamma. You take that as leave to play the dog with a powerful master behind it, making mischief every day and doing nothing but stir up trouble. Now there’s no holding you! If you think I have your young lady’s good temper and will let you bully me as you bully her, you’ve made a mistake. I’m not angry because you search our things. You have no business making a joke of me.’

She began undoing her own clothes and loosening her skirt, pulling Xi-feng forward to search her properly.

‘That will save having a servant rummage over my body.’

Xi-feng, Patience and the others hurried to fasten her skirt and straighten her sleeves, scolding Wang Shan-bao’s wife as they did so.

‘Two mouthfuls of wine, Mamma, and you don’t know what you’re doing! You offended Her Ladyship the other day too. Go out at once. We’ll say no more about it.’

They urged Tan-chun not to be angry.

‘If I had any spirit at all,’ she said with a cold laugh, ‘I’d have dashed my brains out long ago!

‘How else could I allow a servant to search my body for stolen goods? Tomorrow morning I shall speak to Grandmother and Mother first, and then go over to apologise to Aunt Xing. Whatever is to be done to me, I’ll accept it.’

Wang Shan-bao’s wife, having made a fool of herself, stood outside the window muttering.

‘Very well, very well. This is the first time I’ve ever been struck. I’ll tell Her Ladyship tomorrow and go back to my own family. What use is this old life of mine?’

Tan-chun called to her maids, ‘Do you hear what she’s saying? Are you waiting for me to go and argue with her myself?’

Scribe and the others went out.

‘If you really go back to your own family, it will be a blessing for us,’ they said. ‘We’re only afraid you won’t bear to leave.’

‘What a girl!’ Xi-feng said with a laugh. ‘Like mistress, like maid, indeed.’

‘We thieves all have a word or two to say for ourselves,’ Tan-chun said coldly. ‘This one’s a stupid specimen. She hasn’t even learnt how to set her mistress against people behind their backs.’

Patience hurried to soothe her with a smile, and drew Scribe inside. Zhou Rui’s wife and the others added their persuasions. Xi-feng stayed until she had helped Tan-chun settle down to sleep. Only then did she take her party across to the Warm Fragrance Retreat.

Li Wan was still ill in bed. Her quarters adjoined Xi-chun’s and were near Tan-chun’s, so they visited these two next on their way. Li Wan had just taken her medicine and fallen asleep, and they did not like to disturb her. They merely searched all the maids’ rooms, finding nothing, and then went on to Xi-chun’s. Young and inexperienced as she was, Xi-chun was frightened and could not imagine what might have happened, so Xi-feng had to reassure her. But in Picture’s chest they actually found a large packet of small gold and silver ingots, some thirty or forty altogether, a set of jade belt plaques, and a bundle of men’s boots, stockings and other things. Picture’s face too turned pale.

Asked where they had come from, she could only kneel down and tearfully tell the truth.

‘Master Zhen gave them to my brother as rewards. Our father and mother are both in the south, and we have only our uncle to live with now. My uncle and aunt care for nothing but drinking and gambling. My brother was afraid that if he handed the things over to them, they would spend them. So whenever he received anything, he quietly asked one of the old women to bring it in for me to keep.’

Xi-chun was timid, and the discovery frightened her as well.

‘I knew nothing about it. This is dreadful!

‘Second Sister-in-law, if you’re going to beat her, please take her outside to do it. I can’t bear to listen.’

‘If that story is true, she can be forgiven,’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘But she shouldn’t have had the things passed in secretly. If these can be brought in, what can’t? The person who brought them is the one at fault. If the story isn’t true, though, and they turn out to be stolen, don’t expect to live.’

Picture knelt there crying.

‘I wouldn’t dare lie. Please ask our mistress and the master tomorrow, ma’am. If they say the things weren’t rewards, you can beat my brother and me to death together, and we’ll have no complaint.’

‘Of course we shall ask,’ said Xi-feng. ‘But even if they really were rewards, there’s still something wrong. Who gave you leave to have things brought in secretly? Tell me who helped you and I’ll spare you. It must never happen again.’

‘You mustn’t spare her this time, Sister-in-law,’ said Xi-chun. ‘There are so many people here. If you don’t make an example of someone, who knows what the older ones will do when they hear of it?

‘If you let her off, I won’t agree.’

‘I’ve always thought her a good girl,’ said Xi-feng. ‘Who never makes a mistake? Only this once. If she does it again, she can be punished for both offences together. But who brought the things in?’

‘There can only be one person,’ said Xi-chun. ‘It must be Mamma Zhang at the back gate. She’s always ready to be secretive with these maids, and they’re all ready to do things for her.’

Xi-feng had someone take a note of this. For the moment she gave the things to Zhou Rui’s wife to keep until the facts could be checked the next day and a decision made. Then they took leave of Xi-chun and went on to Ying-chun’s rooms.

Ying-chun was asleep, and her maids were just going to bed. The party knocked for a long time before the door opened.

‘Don’t disturb your mistress,’ Xi-feng told them, and went to the maids’ rooms.

Chess was Wang Shan-bao’s granddaughter on her mother’s side. Xi-feng wanted to see whether his wife would show favouritism, and watched her search closely. They began with the other girls’ chests and found nothing out of the way. After looking through Chess’s chest, Wang Shan-bao’s wife said, ‘There’s nothing here either.’

She was about to shut it when Zhou Rui’s wife stopped her.

‘Wait. What’s this?’

She reached in and pulled out a pair of men’s stockings with brocade bands, and a pair of satin shoes. There was also a little bundle. They opened it and found a love-knot ruyi and a letter. Everything was handed to Xi-feng. Managing the household had accustomed her to looking at lists and accounts, and she had come to recognise quite a number of characters. The letter was written on scarlet paper bearing the Double Happiness emblem. It read:

‘Since you came home last month, my parents have become aware of our feelings for one another.

‘But as your young lady is not yet married, we cannot yet fulfil our wish. If it is possible to meet in the garden, please send me word through Mamma Zhang. A meeting there would give us more chance to talk than your coming home. I beg you, I beg you. I have received the two scent-bags you were kind enough to give me, and send you a string of scented beads as a small token of my heart.

‘Please take good care of them. With a respectful bow, your younger cousin, Pan You-an.’

Xi-feng finished reading, not angry but amused. None of the others could read. Wang Shan-bao’s wife had known nothing of the love affair between her granddaughter and the girl’s cousin. The shoes and stockings had already made her uneasy. Now, seeing the red letter and Xi-feng smiling over it, she said, ‘It must be some accounts they’ve scribbled, with not a proper character among them. That’s why you’re laughing, ma’am.’

‘Exactly. It’s an account I can’t make add up,’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘You’re Chess’s grandmother. Shouldn’t her cousin be a Wang too? How does he come to be a Pan?’

Wang Shan-bao’s wife found the question strange, but had to explain as best she could.

‘Chess’s father’s sister married into the Pan family, so her aunt’s son is called Pan. Pan You-an, who ran away the other day, is her cousin.’

‘That explains it,’ said Xi-feng, smiling.

‘Let me read it to you.’

She read it aloud from the beginning, and everyone was startled. Wang Shan-bao’s wife had set her heart on catching other people out; she had never imagined she would catch her own granddaughter. She was furious and mortified. Zhou Rui’s wife and the other three all questioned her.

‘Did you hear that, Mamma? Plain as can be. There’s nothing left to say. Now what would you have us do?’

Wang Shan-bao’s wife wished the ground would open and swallow her. Xi-feng merely looked at her and chuckled, then turned smiling to Zhou Rui’s wife.

‘It’s a good thing in its way. Without giving her grandmother the least trouble, she’s quietly found her a fine grandson-in-law. That saves everyone some bother.’

Zhou Rui’s wife laughed and joined in. With nowhere to vent her anger, Wang Shan-bao’s wife turned her hand on her own face, slapping herself and cursing.

‘You old whore who won’t die! What sin have you committed to bring this on you? Boast with your mouth and get slapped in the mouth. Punishment before everyone’s eyes, here and now!’

At this the others laughed without stopping, half comforting and half mocking her.

Xi-feng thought it curious that Chess stood with her head lowered and said nothing, yet showed neither fear nor shame. It was late, and there was no need to question her now. Afraid that shame might drive her to take her life during the night, she called two old women to keep watch over her. Then she took the party back with the incriminating objects and settled down to rest, leaving matters to be dealt with the next day. But during the night she had to get up several times, the bleeding below continuing without relief.

By morning she was terribly weak and grew dizzy when she rose. She could struggle on no longer. An imperial physician was called. After taking her pulse, he wrote his case report:

‘The young mistress is deficient in heart qi. Deficiency-fire has invaded the spleen, all owing to injury from anxiety and overwork. Hence her inclination to lie down and sleep, the weakness of the stomach and of the earth element, and her lack of appetite. For the present, a preparation for raising yang and nourishing the blood is indicated.’

He then wrote down a few medicines, no more than ginseng, danggui, astragalus and others of that kind, and presently left. The old nurses took the prescription to Lady Wang. This could only add to her worries, and the cases of Chess and the others were left unsettled for the time being.

You-shi happened to visit Xi-feng that day. After sitting with her for a while, she went into the garden to see Li Wan as well. She was just about to call on the other girls when a message came from Xi-chun asking her over. You-shi went to her rooms, and Xi-chun told her every detail of the previous night’s affair. She also had all the things taken from Picture brought back for You-shi to inspect.

‘Your brother really did give them to her brother,’ said You-shi. ‘They shouldn’t have been passed in secretly, that’s all. Authorized salt has turned into contraband salt.’

She scolded Picture.

‘You fool, with grease clogging your wits!’

‘You fail to keep your people in order, and then blame the maid,’ said Xi-chun. ‘Of all us sisters, I’m the only one whose maid has disgraced her like this. How am I to face anyone? Last night I insisted that Cousin Feng take her away, but she wouldn’t. Thinking it over, I suppose she was right not to: the girl belongs to your side of the family. I was just going to send her over today, so you’ve come at the right moment, Sister-in-law. Take her away at once. Beat her, kill her, sell her, I shall have nothing to do with it.’

Picture knelt down again in tears, pleading.

‘I’ll never dare do it again.

‘Please remember how we’ve been together since we were little, miss, and let us stay together, in life or death.’

You-shi, the wet-nurse and the others all argued earnestly on her behalf.

‘She was foolish for a moment, that’s all. She won’t dare do it again. After serving you since she was a child, surely she ought to stay.’

But young as Xi-chun was, she had been born with an unbending, aloof and solitary streak, fiercely jealous of her own purity. Whatever anyone said, she saw only an affront to her dignity, and set her teeth in absolute refusal. She went further still.

‘It isn’t only Picture I don’t want. I’m older now, and it’s no longer proper for me to go over to your house either. Besides, lately I keep hearing that people are talking behind your backs, saying all sorts of dreadful things. If I go there again, they’ll make up stories about me too.’

‘Who is talking, and what are they saying?’ You-shi asked. ‘What is there to talk about? Who are you, and who are we? If you hear people talking about us, you ought to question them.’

Xi-chun laughed coldly.

‘That’s a fine thing to ask of me!

‘I’m an unmarried girl. My business is to keep out of scandal. What sort of person would I be if I went looking for it? And there’s something else. I’m not afraid of making you angry: right and wrong have their own public judgement. Why should I question anyone? The ancients put it well: “In good and evil, life and death, even father and son cannot help one another.” How much less can you and I? All I know is that I must keep myself safe. That’s enough. I won’t concern myself with you. From now on, don’t drag me into your affairs.’

You-shi was torn between anger and laughter. She turned to the people standing below them.

‘Everyone says this fourth girl is young and foolish, and I never would believe it. Just listen to that speech! No reason for it, no sense of kindness, no idea of what should and shouldn’t be said. They may be a child’s words, but they can still chill your heart.’

‘She’s young, ma’am,’ the nurses said, smiling. ‘Naturally you must put up with a little.’

‘Young I may be, but there’s nothing young about what I say,’ Xi-chun answered with a cold laugh. ‘You don’t read books and hardly know a character, so you’re all fools. When you meet someone who understands, you call her young and foolish.’

‘You’re the first, second and third scholar on the honours list,’ said You-shi. ‘The greatest genius of ancient or modern times. We’re stupid people, not as enlightened as you. Will that do?’

‘Do you suppose the first and second scholars can’t be fools? There are things they cannot fully understand either.’

‘Very good,’ You-shi said with a laugh. ‘A moment ago you were a scholar, and now you’re a great monk, talking of enlightenment.’

‘Without enlightenment, I couldn’t bear to part with Picture.’

‘That proves what you are: cold-hearted and cold-tongued, hard in heart and hard in purpose.’

‘The ancients said this too,’ Xi-chun replied.

‘“Unless you make your heart a hard one, You cannot win deliverance for yourself.”

‘I am perfectly innocent. Why should I let you drag me down?’

You-shi had her own secret uneasiness and dreaded such talk. Already the mention of gossip had stung her with shame and anger, but for Xi-chun’s sake she had held herself in and endured a great deal. Now, at these words, she could restrain herself no longer.

‘How have we dragged you down? Your maid is at fault, and you attack me for no reason. I’ve put up with it all this time, and you grow more pleased with yourself and keep saying these things. You’re a young lady worth a thousand, ten thousand in gold. We’ll keep away from you in future, lest we damage your precious reputation. I’ll have Picture taken over this instant!’

She rose in a huff and left.

‘If you really don’t come,’ said Xi-chun, ‘it will save a good deal of talk and trouble. We can all have some peace.’

You-shi made no answer, but went straight out towards the front.

What followed, we do not yet know.
