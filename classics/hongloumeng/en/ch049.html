Chapter 49 White snow and red plum blossom fill a world of glass; Fragrant, powdered beauties cut raw meat and feast on venison

Chapter end

Seeing them laughing and talking, Caltrop came up with a smile.

‘Look at this one. If it will do, I’ll go on learning. If it’s still no good, I’ll give up all hope of writing poetry.’

She handed the poem to Dai-yu and the others. It read:

Such pure radiance must be hard to hide;

Its form is lovely of itself, its spirit cold.

Fulling-stones sound across a thousand li of white;

Cocks crow beneath half a disk as the fifth watch wanes.

A green rain cape on the autumn river hears a flute;

Red sleeves lean on a tower’s rail by night.

It should move Chang-e herself to ask:

Why can we not stay whole, together, always?1

‘This is not merely good,’ they said with smiles after reading it. ‘It’s fresh and skilful, with real feeling and thought. As the saying goes, “Nothing under Heaven is too hard for someone who sets their heart on it.” We shall certainly invite you to join the society.’

Caltrop could not quite believe them. Suspecting that they were only humouring her, she kept questioning Dai-yu, Bao-chai and the others.

As they were talking, several little maids and older women hurried up, all smiles.

‘A whole lot of young ladies and married ladies have come. We don’t know any of them. Come quickly, all of you, and meet your relations!’

‘What sort of announcement is that?’ Li Wan laughed. ‘Tell us properly. Whose relations are they?’

‘Your two younger sisters have both come, ma’am. And there’s another young lady they say is Miss Xue’s younger sister, and a gentleman they say is Master Xue’s brother. I’m going to fetch Aunt Xue now. You and the young ladies had better go ahead.’

With that, they went straight on.

‘Can Xue Ke and his sister have arrived?’ Bao-chai said with a smile.

‘And can my aunt have come to the capital again?’ Li Wan smiled. ‘But how could they all have come together? This is extraordinary.’

Wondering, they went to Lady Wang’s principal rooms and found the place crowded with people.

Lady Xing’s elder brother and his wife had brought their daughter Xiu-yan to the capital to seek Lady Xing’s help. Xi-feng’s elder brother Wang Ren had happened to be travelling there too, so the two families, connected by marriage, had joined forces on the journey. When they moored halfway, they met the widow of Li Wan’s younger uncle, also bound for the capital with her two daughters, the elder named Li Wen and the younger Li Qi. In conversation they established that they too were related, and the three families continued together. Then Xue Pan’s younger paternal cousin Xue Ke followed with his sister. Years before, while their father was in the capital, he had betrothed his daughter Xue Bao-qin to the son of Academician Mei. Xue Ke was about to bring his own sister up for the marriage when he heard that Wang Ren was going, and hurried after him with her. So they had all arrived together that day to visit or seek shelter with their respective relations.

Greetings and introductions followed. Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang were delighted.

‘No wonder the lamp-wick kept crackling and forming flowers last night,’ Grandmother Jia said with a smile. ‘This is what it foretold!’

She asked after their families, inspected the presents they had brought and ordered food and wine so that they could stay to eat. Xi-feng, needless to say, was busier than ever. Li Wan and Bao-chai naturally had much to say to the aunt and cousins from whom they had been parted. Dai-yu was pleased at first. Then she thought how everyone had their own relations, while she alone was solitary, with no family of her own, and went away to shed tears. Bao-yu understood her feelings well and spent a long time comforting her before she stopped.

Then he hurried back to the House of Green Delights and addressed Aroma, Musk, Skybright and the others with a smile.

‘Why aren’t you going to look at them? Who would have thought it? Cousin Bao-chai’s own brother is what he is, but this cousin of theirs is quite different in looks and manners. He’s much more like her own brother. And there’s something more extraordinary. You’re always saying Bao-chai is a perfect beauty. Now look at her younger cousin, and Sister-in-law’s two cousins as well. I can’t even describe them! Heaven, Heaven, how much pure essence and lovely spirit you must have, to make such extraordinary people! I’ve been a frog at the bottom of a well. I was always saying the girls we had here were unique. Who knew we needn’t even look far afield? Here on our own doorstep, each new one surpasses the last. I’ve learnt something more today.

‘Can there possibly be still more, beyond these?’

As he spoke he laughed and sighed to himself. Seeing another of his infatuations coming on, Aroma refused to go and look. Skybright and the others had already been and come back, giggling.

‘Do go and see them! There’s Lady Xing’s niece, Miss Bao-chai’s cousin and Mrs Zhu’s two cousins. Like four fresh water-onions in a bunch!’

Before they had finished, Tan-chun came in smiling to find Bao-yu.

‘Our poetry society is going to flourish now.’

‘Exactly!’ Bao-yu smiled. ‘You had the happy thought of starting it, and the spirits have sent all these people here. There’s only one thing: have they ever learnt to write poetry?’

‘I’ve just asked them all. They’re modest about it, but from what I can see, every one of them can. Even if they couldn’t, it wouldn’t be difficult. Look at Caltrop.’

‘They say Miss Xue’s younger cousin is even more beautiful,’ Aroma said with a smile. ‘What do you think, Third Miss?’

‘It’s true. To my mind, neither her elder cousin nor any of the rest of us can compare with her.’

Aroma was astonished.

‘That is strange!’ she laughed. ‘How could anyone be more beautiful? I must go and see.’

‘Grandmother was so enchanted the moment she saw her that she’s already made Mother adopt her as a daughter.

‘Grandmother means to keep her here. It was all settled just now.’

‘Really?’ Bao-yu asked eagerly, delighted.

‘When have I ever lied?’ Tan-chun said, then laughed. ‘Now she’s found such a lovely granddaughter, she’s forgotten this grandson.’

‘That doesn’t matter,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘Girls ought to have more affection. It’s only right. Tomorrow’s the sixteenth. Isn’t it time our society met?’

‘Lin has only just got up again, and now Second Sister is ill. Someone always seems to be missing.’

‘Second Sister hardly ever writes poetry. What does it matter if she isn’t there?’

‘Why not wait a few days, let the newcomers get to know us and invite them too? Sister-in-law and Cousin Bao-chai naturally aren’t thinking about poetry just now. Besides, Xiang-yun isn’t here, and Pin-pin has only just recovered. It doesn’t suit anybody. Wait till Yun comes, the new girls feel at home, Pin-pin is quite well, Sister-in-law and Bao-chai have their minds free and Caltrop has improved. Then we could invite a full gathering. Wouldn’t that be better?

‘Let’s go to Grandmother’s now and hear what’s happening. We needn’t worry about Bao-chai’s cousin: she’s certainly staying with us. But if the other three aren’t, we can beg Grandmother to keep them here in the Garden. We shall have more company, and it will be much more fun.’

Bao-yu’s whole face lit up.

‘You’re the sensible one. I’m a muddle-head after all. I was simply delighted for a while, and never thought of that.’

The brother and sister went together to Grandmother Jia’s. Sure enough, Lady Wang had adopted Bao-qin as her daughter. Grandmother Jia was so fond of her that she would not even let her stay in the Garden: at night she slept in Grandmother’s quarters. Xue Ke settled into Xue Pan’s study.

‘There’s no need for your niece to go home either,’ Grandmother Jia told Lady Xing. ‘Let her stay in the Garden for a few days and enjoy herself before she goes.’

Lady Xing’s brother and sister-in-law were badly off. They had come to the capital relying on her to find them a house and help with their expenses. How could they be anything but pleased? Lady Xing entrusted Xiu-yan to Xi-feng. Xi-feng considered the many girls in the Garden and their different temperaments. It would be inconvenient to establish a separate household for the newcomer. The best plan was to put her with Ying-chun. If Xing Xiu-yan found anything unsatisfactory later, even if Lady Xing heard of it, Xi-feng could not be held responsible. Thereafter, leaving out the days Xiu-yan spent at her parents’ home, whenever she had stayed a full month in Prospect Garden, Xi-feng sent her an allowance equal to Ying-chun’s.

Watching Xiu-yan quietly and weighing her character, Xi-feng found her quite unlike Lady Xing or her own parents: she was gentle, generous and lovable. Xi-feng pitied her poverty and hard lot, and came to care for her rather more than for the other girls. Lady Xing, by contrast, paid her little attention.

Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang had always admired Li Wan’s goodness and the fidelity with which she had remained a widow from so young an age. Now that her widowed aunt had come, they would not hear of her lodging outside. Aunt Li protested most earnestly, but Grandmother Jia was immovable. She had no choice but to stay at Sweet-rice Village with Li Wen and Li Qi.

These arrangements had just been settled when Shi Nai, Marquis of Baoling, was appointed to another senior provincial post and was shortly to leave with his family. Grandmother Jia could not bear to part with Xiang-yun, so she kept her behind and brought her to the house. At first she meant to have Xi-feng prepare separate rooms for her, but Xiang-yun firmly refused. She would only stay with Bao-chai, and the matter was dropped.

Prospect Garden was now livelier than ever. There was Li Wan at their head, then Ying-chun, Tan-chun, Xi-chun, Bao-chai, Dai-yu, Xiang-yun, Li Wen, Li Qi, Bao-qin and Xing Xiu-yan; with Xi-feng and Bao-yu, thirteen in all. As for their ages, apart from Li Wan, the eldest, the other twelve were no more than fifteen, sixteen or seventeen. Three might have been born in the same year, or five might be of one age; two might share a month and day, another two the same hour and moment. For the most part only the month or hour separated them. Even they could not sort it all out precisely. They simply called one another younger brother, elder brother, elder sister or younger sister as it happened.

Caltrop’s whole heart was now set on poetry, but she dared not pester Bao-chai too much. Xiang-yun’s arrival was exactly what she needed. Xiang-yun loved talking; how could she resist when Caltrop asked her about poetry? Her enthusiasm grew, and they talked away at length, day and night.

‘I really can’t stand the noise any longer,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘Girls treating poetry as their serious business! If learned people heard you, they would laugh at you for neglecting your proper duties. I haven’t finished dealing with one Caltrop, and now I have a whole sack of chatter like you as well. What is it you keep saying? The brooding depth of Du of the Board of Works, the spare elegance of Wei of Suzhou, the gorgeous luxuriance of Wen Ba-cha, the hidden obscurities of Li Yi-shan.2 You don’t recognise two poets right under your noses. Why talk about all those dead men?’

‘Which two?’ Xiang-yun asked at once, laughing. ‘Dear cousin, do tell me.’

‘Simple Caltrop’s painful industry, and Mad Xiang-yun’s endless talk.’

Xiang-yun and Caltrop both laughed. Just then Bao-qin arrived wearing a cloak of some unknown substance, brilliant in gold and emerald green.

‘Where did that come from?’ Bao-chai asked at once.

‘Because it was sleeting, Grandmother found it for me,’ Bao-qin said with a smile.

Caltrop came closer to look.

‘No wonder it’s so beautiful. It’s woven from peacock feathers.’

‘Peacock feathers? Nothing of the kind,’ said Xiang-yun. ‘It’s made from feathers from the heads of wild ducks. That shows how fond Grandmother is of you. For all her love of Bao-yu, she’s never given him one to wear.’

‘It’s just as they say,’ said Bao-chai. ‘“Each has their own appointed fortune.”

‘She never dreamt she would come here now, and once here, find Grandmother so fond of her.’

‘When you aren’t with Grandmother, come into the Garden,’ Xiang-yun told Bao-qin. ‘In those two places you can play, laugh, eat and drink as much as you like. When you go to Lady Wang’s, if she’s in, stay and talk to her as long as you please. But if she isn’t there, don’t go in. The people in those rooms are full of nasty suspicions. They all want to do us harm.’

Bao-chai, Bao-qin, Caltrop, Oriole and the others laughed.

‘Call you thoughtless, and you turn out to have thought of everything,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘But for all your thoughtfulness, your tongue is much too plain. Our Qin is rather like you. You’re always saying you want me for your own elder sister. Today I’ll make you take her for your own younger one.’

Xiang-yun looked at Bao-qin for a long while, then smiled.

‘She’s the only one who ought to wear that cloak. It really wouldn’t suit anyone else.’

Just then Amber came in, smiling.

‘Grandmother says Miss Bao-chai mustn’t keep Miss Qin on too tight a rein.

‘She’s still young. Let her do as she likes. If she wants anything, she has only to ask. She isn’t to worry about it.’

Bao-chai rose promptly to acknowledge the message, then gave Bao-qin a laughing push.

‘Where did you find such good fortune? Off you go, before we ill-treat you.

‘I don’t believe I’m a bit worse than you.’

Bao-yu and Dai-yu came in while she was speaking, and Bao-chai continued her teasing.

‘Cousin Bao,’ Xiang-yun said with a smile, ‘you may be joking, but someone really does feel that way.’

‘There’s only one person who’s truly put out, and it’s him,’ Amber laughed, pointing to Bao-yu.

‘He isn’t that sort of person,’ Bao-chai and Xiang-yun both said, smiling.

‘If it isn’t him, it must be her.’

Amber pointed to Dai-yu. Xiang-yun said nothing. Bao-chai quickly spoke, still smiling.

‘Even less likely. My younger cousin is hers too. She likes her even more than I do. Why should she be put out? You’re talking at random.

‘Nothing that comes out of that mouth has any foundation.’

Bao-yu knew Dai-yu’s touchiness well. He had not yet heard what had recently passed between her and Bao-chai, and had been afraid Grandmother’s affection for Bao-qin would upset her. Now, after Xiang-yun’s remark and Bao-chai’s answer, he studied Dai-yu’s expression and manner. They were indeed different from before and bore out Bao-chai’s words. He felt troubled and unhappy.

‘Those two were never such good friends,’ he thought. ‘Now they seem ten times closer than anyone else.’

Presently Dai-yu went after Bao-qin, calling her ‘little sister’ without using her name at all, just as though they were sisters by birth. Bao-qin was young and warm-hearted, naturally clever, and had read and written since childhood. After two days in the Jia household she already had a general notion of its people. She saw that none of the girls was a frivolous, painted beauty, and that they all got on well with her elder cousin; she would not be less friendly in return. Dai-yu struck her as exceptional even among them, and she showed her particular affection and respect. Bao-yu could only watch in silent wonder.

After a while Bao-chai and her cousin went to Aunt Xue’s, Xiang-yun went to Grandmother Jia’s, and Dai-yu returned to her room to rest. Bao-yu came to find her.

‘I’ve read The Western Chamber,’ he said with a smile, ‘and there were a few lines I understood well enough to quote as a joke. You were angry with me for it. Now I think of it, there’s one line I don’t understand. I’ll recite it, and you can explain it to me.’

Dai-yu knew at once that there was something behind this.

‘Recite it, then. Let me hear.’

‘There’s a wonderful line in “The Quarrel over the Letter”,’ Bao-yu smiled. ‘“When was it that Meng Guang received Liang Hong’s tray?”

‘That’s the finest line. Those seven characters, “Meng Guang received Liang Hong’s tray”, are only a familiar allusion. But what a delightful question he makes with the three little grammatical characters, “When was it that”! When was it that she received it? Do tell me.3’

Dai-yu could not help laughing too.

‘A very good question. His question is good, and so is yours.’

‘You used to suspect me. Now you’ve nothing left to say, and I’m the one left out.’

‘Who knew she really was so good?’ Dai-yu said with a smile. ‘I always thought she was hiding some treachery.’

Beginning with her mistake in the drinking game, she told Bao-yu everything in detail, including the bird’s nests Bao-chai had sent and their conversation during her illness. Only then did he understand.

‘I thought so!’ he laughed. ‘I kept wondering, “When was it that Meng Guang received Liang Hong’s tray?” So she received it when “children don’t know how to guard their tongues”.’

Dai-yu began talking of Bao-qin again. Remembering that she had no sisters herself, she could not help crying once more.

‘You’re making yourself miserable again,’ Bao-yu said, hastening to comfort her. ‘Look how much thinner you are this year than last. Yet you still won’t take care of yourself. Every day, when everything is perfectly all right, you have to find something to grieve over and cry for a while before you consider the day’s work done.’

Dai-yu wiped her tears.

‘Lately my heart keeps aching, but I seem to have fewer tears than last year. The ache goes on and on, but there aren’t many tears.’

‘That’s only a fancy you have from crying so much. How could tears grow fewer?’

While they were talking, a little maid from Bao-yu’s rooms brought his broadcloth cloak.

‘Mrs Zhu has just sent word that, as it’s snowing, she wants to discuss inviting everyone to write poetry tomorrow.’

Before she had finished, Li Wan’s maid arrived to fetch Dai-yu. Bao-yu invited Dai-yu to go with him to Sweet-rice Village. Dai-yu changed into small boots of red xiang-yang sheepskin, with cloud-shaped work ornamented in gold thread. Over her clothes she put a crane cloak faced with scarlet yusha gauze and lined with white fox fur, fastened with a blue-and-gold sash shot with green, with double rings and a fourfold ruyi knot. She covered her head with a snow hood, and the two set out through the snow together. The other girls were all there, uniformly wrapped in scarlet broadcloth or feather-satin cloaks. Only Li Wan wore a dark-blue duoluo woollen jacket opening down the front, while Bao-chai had a lotus-blue crane cloak of fanba cloth made with imported yarn, its rich patterned ground bearing dou-patterning and flowers. Xing Xiu-yan was still in her ordinary, worn clothes, with nothing to protect her from the snow. Presently Xiang-yun arrived in a lustrous outer coat Grandmother Jia had given her, with sable-head fur outside and thick black-and-grey squirrel fur inside. On her head was a Zhaojun hood of scarlet broadcloth, lined with goose-yellow gold-patterned cloth with cloud-shaped work; she had also wrapped a large sable neck-wrap about her neck.

‘Look, here comes Monkey!’ Dai-yu laughed first. ‘She has a snow cloak like the rest of us, but she’s deliberately dressed herself up as a little Tartar imp.’

‘Look at what I’m wearing underneath,’ Xiang-yun said with a smile, taking off the coat as she spoke.

Under it was a half-worn short ermine jacket, autumn-fragrance in colour, fitted and narrow-sleeved, with an overlapping front and matching-colour triple bands at collar and cuffs. Dragons in five colours were embroidered on it with couched gold work. Beneath that was a very short zhezi garment of pink figured satin and fox-flank fur. A five-coloured palace sash with long tassels was drawn tightly round her waist in a butterfly bow. She too wore small doe-skin boots. The clothes showed off still more her wasp waist and ape’s back, her crane’s poise and mantis-like figure.

‘She will dress like a boy!’ they all laughed. ‘And she looks even prettier than when she dresses as a girl.’

‘Come on, let’s discuss the poems,’ said Xiang-yun. ‘Who’s the host this time?’

‘It was my idea,’ said Li Wan. ‘Yesterday was our regular day, and now we’ve missed it, the next one is too far away. Since it happens to be snowing, why not have a meeting? We can welcome the newcomers and write poems as well. What do you think?’

‘An excellent idea,’ Bao-yu said first. ‘Only it’s late today. If we wait until tomorrow, it may clear up and spoil the fun.’

The others looked at the sky.

‘It may not stop. And even if it does, one night’s fall will give us plenty to enjoy.’

‘My rooms are pleasant,’ said Li Wan, ‘but Reed Snow Lodge is better. I’ve already sent people to light the heated floor. We can all gather round the stove and write. Grandmother may not feel inclined to join us, and it’s only a little amusement of our own. We need only send word to Feng. One tael each will be enough. Send it to me.’

She pointed to Caltrop, Bao-qin, Li Wen, Li Qi and Xiu-yan.

‘These five are exempt. Of our own people, Second Sister is ill, so she’s exempt, and Fourth Sister has asked to be excused, so she is too. Send me your four shares. I’ll take charge of the whole thing; five or six taels altogether will be quite enough.’

Bao-chai and the others agreed. They then began to discuss subjects and rhymes.

‘I’ve already decided,’ Li Wan said with a smile. ‘You’ll find out when the time comes tomorrow.’

They chatted a little longer, then went to Grandmother Jia’s. Nothing more need be said of that day.

Bao-yu had been so full of the party that he slept badly all night, and next morning he scrambled up at daybreak. Drawing back the bed-curtain, he saw a dazzling brightness at the windows, though the doors and windows were still closed. He began to worry, grumbling that it must have cleared and the sun was already out. He hurried to lift the inner window-screen and looked through the glass. It was not sunlight at all. Heavy snow had fallen all night and lay more than a chi deep; above, it was still as though someone were rubbing cotton and tearing wadding from the sky. Bao-yu was delighted. He called the servants up at once, washed his face and rinsed his mouth, and put on only an aubergine-coloured duoluo woollen jacket lined with fox fur, with a little sea-dragon-fur over-jacket whose shoulders spread like an eagle’s wings. He belted himself, put on a jade-needle rain cape and a golden-vine hat, stepped into shatang-wood clogs and hurried towards Reed Snow Lodge.4 Outside his courtyard he looked about him. Everything was one colour; far away stood the green pines and emerald bamboo. He felt as if he had been put inside a glass box. He went down to the foot of the hill and had just turned along its base when a cold fragrance brushed his nose.

Looking back, he saw a dozen or more red plum trees in Green Bower Hermitage before Adamantina’s door, their blossoms like rouge. Against the snow they looked wonderfully vivid. What a delight! Bao-yu stopped and admired them closely for a while before going on. On the narrow-waisted plank bridge he saw someone approaching under an umbrella: the person Li Wan had sent to invite Xi-feng.

At Reed Snow Lodge the maids and older women were sweeping the snow away to open a path. The lodge stood on a strip of riverbank between the hill and the water: a row of several rooms, with thatched eaves, earthen walls, a hibiscus hedge and bamboo windows. One had only to push open a window to fish. Reeds surrounded it on every side. A winding path led away through them to the bamboo bridge at Lotus Pavilion. Seeing him arrive in cape and hat, the servants laughed.

‘We were just saying all we lacked was a fisherman. Now the picture’s complete! The young ladies aren’t coming until after breakfast. You’re in far too much of a hurry.’

Bao-yu had to turn back. At Drenched Blossoms Pavilion he saw Tan-chun coming from Autumn Studio, wrapped in a scarlet broadcloth cloak and wearing a Guanyin hood. She leaned on a little maid’s arm, while a woman behind held an oiled umbrella of blue silk over her. Knowing she was going to Grandmother Jia’s, Bao-yu waited at the pavilion until she reached him, and they left the Garden together. Bao-qin was in the inner room, washing and changing.

Presently all the girls arrived. Bao-yu kept saying he was hungry and calling for breakfast. At last it was served. The first dish was lamb steamed in cow’s milk.

‘This is medicine for old people like us,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘It never saw daylight. It’s a pity you children can’t eat it. There’s fresh venison today as well. Wait for that.’

They agreed, but Bao-yu could not wait. He poured tea over a bowl of rice and hurriedly swallowed it with some minced pheasant and pickled melon.

‘I know you have something on today,’ Grandmother Jia said. ‘You can’t even spare time to eat.’

She ordered them to keep the venison for his supper. Only when Xi-feng hastily assured her there was more did she leave the matter alone. Xiang-yun whispered a plan to Bao-yu.

‘Since there’s fresh venison, why don’t we ask for a piece and take it into the Garden to cook ourselves? Something to play with and something to eat.’

Bao-yu needed no second bidding. He did indeed ask Xi-feng for a piece and had a woman carry it into the Garden.

When the party broke up, everyone went into the Garden and on to Reed Snow Lodge to hear Li Wan announce the subject and rhymes. Only Xiang-yun and Bao-yu were missing.

‘Those two must never be allowed together,’ said Dai-yu. ‘Whenever they are, there’s no end to their schemes. I’m sure they’re plotting what to do with that venison.’

Just then Aunt Li came to see the fun.

‘That young gentleman with the jade and the young lady wearing a gold qilin,’ she said to Li Wan, ‘such clean, delicate-looking children, and neither of them short of food: why are they discussing eating raw meat? They’ve been talking it over quite seriously. I can’t believe meat can be eaten raw.’

‘How dreadful!’ they all laughed. ‘Bring them here at once!’

‘I knew it was Yun’s doing,’ Dai-yu said with a smile. ‘My divination never fails.’

Li Wan and the others hurried out to find them.

‘If you two want to eat it raw, I’ll send you to Grandmother’s to do it. You can eat a whole raw deer there for all I care. If you stuff yourselves sick, it won’t be my responsibility. Such heavy snow, such bitter cold, and you have to make trouble for me!’

‘Nothing of the sort,’ Bao-yu laughed. ‘We’re going to roast it.’

‘That’s better.’

The women brought an iron brazier, iron forks and a wire grid.

‘Mind you don’t cut your hands,’ Li Wan warned. ‘There’ll be no crying if you do!’

She went back inside with Tan-chun.

Xi-feng sent Patience to say that she could not come: she was busy distributing the New Year allowances. Once Xiang-yun saw Patience, she would not let her leave. Patience liked a bit of fun herself and went everywhere with Xi-feng. Seeing how enjoyable this was, she was happy to join them. She slipped off her bracelets, and the three gathered round the brazier to roast three pieces for themselves first.

Bao-chai and Dai-yu were used to such things and thought nothing of it. Bao-qin, the other newcomers and Aunt Li found it extraordinary. Tan-chun, Li Wan and the others had now settled the subject and rhymes.

‘Just smell that!’ Tan-chun laughed. ‘The scent reaches all the way in here. I’m going to have some too.’

She went out to join them. Li Wan followed.

‘All the guests have arrived. Haven’t you eaten enough yet?’

‘This is what makes me want wine,’ Xiang-yun said as she ate. ‘And once I’ve had wine, there’ll be poetry.

‘Without this venison, I certainly couldn’t write a poem today.’

Bao-qin stood nearby smiling in her duck-feather cloak.

‘Silly, come and try some,’ Xiang-yun called with a laugh.

‘It looks so dirty,’ Bao-qin smiled.

‘Try it,’ said Bao-chai. ‘It’s good. Your Cousin Lin is too delicate to digest it, or she would like some too.’

Bao-qin went over and tasted a piece. It was indeed delicious, and she began to eat with them. Presently Xi-feng sent a little maid to fetch Patience.

‘Miss Shi won’t let me go. You go back first,’ Patience said.

The maid left. Before long Xi-feng herself appeared, wrapped in a cloak.

‘Eating such good things and never telling me!’ she laughed, joining the group and helping herself.

‘Where could you find another gang of beggars like this?’ Dai-yu laughed. ‘Well, well! Reed Snow Lodge has met its doom today. Yun has utterly disgraced it. I shall weep great tears for Reed Snow Lodge!’

Xiang-yun gave a scoffing laugh.

‘What do you know? “A true person of distinction has a natural grace.” You’re all pretending to be above such things, and it’s utterly tiresome. We’re chewing and chomping our way through rank, gamey meat now, but presently our thoughts will be brocade and our words embroidery.’

‘If your poems aren’t good,’ Bao-chai laughed, ‘we’ll scoop out the meat and stuff you with some of these snow-flattened reeds instead. That will complete the disaster.’

When they had finished eating, they washed their hands and rinsed their mouths. As Patience put on her bracelets, she found one missing. They searched in a flurry, right and left, in front and behind, but found no trace of it. Everyone was astonished.

‘I know where that bracelet has gone,’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘You go and write your poems. We needn’t look any more. We’ll go back to the front. I guarantee it will turn up within three days.’

Then she asked, ‘What sort of poems are you writing today? Grandmother says New Year is getting close. You ought to make some lantern riddles for everyone to enjoy in the first month.’

‘Of course!’ they all laughed. ‘We’d forgotten.

‘We must hurry and make some good ones ready for the first month.’

They went together into the room with the heated floor. Cups, dishes, fruit and food were all laid out, and the subject, rhyme group and form had been posted on the wall. Bao-yu and Xiang-yun hurried to read:

‘Linked verses on the scene before us: one extended regulated poem in five-character lines, using the second rhyme group, xiao.’

No order of turns had yet been given below it.

‘I’m not very good at poetry,’ Li Wan said. ‘I’ll just begin with three lines. After that, whoever thinks of something first can add it.’

‘We really ought to have an order,’ said Bao-chai.
