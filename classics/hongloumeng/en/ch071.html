Chapter 71 An ill-disposed woman deliberately breeds ill feeling; Faithful unwittingly encounters a pair of lovers

Chapter end

Now, after Jia Zheng returned to the capital and had attended to all his business, he was granted a month’s leave to rest at home. He was growing older; his responsibilities weighed on him and his strength was failing. Besides, he had spent these last few years away from his family. To be peacefully reunited with them under his own roof seemed a happiness and a piece of good fortune beyond anything he could express. More than ever he put all affairs, great and small, out of his mind. He read, and when he tired of reading played go or drank with his literary companions, or spent the daylight hours in the inner apartments, enjoying the pleasures of family life with his mother and his wife.

The third of the eighth month that year was Grandmother Jia’s eightieth birthday. With all their friends and relations coming, Jia Zheng feared that there would not be room to entertain them. He therefore consulted Jia She, Jia Zhen and Jia Lian well in advance. They agreed to hold banquets at both mansions from the twenty-eighth of the seventh month until the fifth of the eighth. The Ning mansion would entertain only the gentlemen, the Rong mansion only the ladies. In Prospect Garden, the Brocade-adorned Pavilion, the Hall of Auspicious Shade and several other spacious buildings were to be prepared as places where the guests could withdraw between sittings. On the twenty-eighth the guests would be imperial kinsmen, princes consort, princes and dukes, together with imperial princesses, princesses of the blood, princely consorts, ladies of rank, titled dowagers and wives. The twenty-ninth would be for grand secretaries, governors-general, governors and military commanders, with the titled ladies; the thirtieth for the other senior officials and titled ladies, and friends and relations from near and far, with their womenfolk. On the first Jia She would give a family banquet, on the second Jia Zheng, and on the third Jia Zhen and Jia Lian. The fourth would be a family feast to which the whole Jia clan, young and old, would contribute. On the fifth Lai Da, Lin Zhi-xiao and the other household stewards would join in providing a day’s entertainment. From early in the seventh month the birthday presents began arriving in an unbroken stream.

By imperial command, the Board of Rites bestowed a gold-and-jade ruyi, four bolts of coloured satin, four gold-and-jade rings and five hundred taels of treasury silver. Yuan-chun also sent a eunuch with a gold figure of the God of Longevity, an aloeswood staff, a string of kanam beads, a box of incense for Blessing and Long Life, a pair of gold ingots, four pairs of silver ingots, twelve bolts of coloured satin and four jade cups. All the other families with whom they had dealings, from princes of the blood and princes consort down to civil and military officials of every rank, sent presents too, too many to record. Large tables were set out in the hall and covered with red felt, and all the finer objects were arranged on them for Grandmother Jia to inspect. For the first day or two she enjoyed going over to look, but then grew tired of it and would not trouble to inspect them.

‘Have Xi-feng put them away,’ she said. ‘I’ll look at them another day when I want something to do.’

By the twenty-eighth both mansions were hung with lanterns and festoons. Phoenix screens stood open, lotus-patterned seat cushions were laid out, and the sound of mouth-organs, flutes and drums travelled down the great streets and across the lanes. That day the guests at Ning were only the Prince of Beijing, the Prince of Nan’an, the Prince Consort of Yongchang, the Prince of Leshan and a few holders of hereditary dukedoms and marquisates whose families were old friends. At Rong were the Dowager Princess of Nan’an, the Princess of Beijing and several titled ladies from those old ducal and marquisate families. Grandmother Jia and the others received them in full ceremonial dress appropriate to their rank. After the greetings, they were first conducted into Prospect Garden to the Hall of Auspicious Shade. There they took tea and withdrew to refresh themselves before coming out to the Hall of Glorious Celebration to offer their birthday congratulations and sit down to the feast. There was a long exchange of polite refusals before anyone would take a seat. The two tables at the head were for the princesses of Nan’an and Beijing, with the other titled ladies below them in order of precedence. At the lower table on the left, the Marchioness of Jinxiang and the Countess of Linchang sat to keep the guests company. The lower table on the right was Grandmother Jia’s place as hostess. Lady Xing and Lady Wang, with You-shi, Xi-feng and several of the other young married women of the clan, stood behind her in two slanting ranks like the wings of wild geese. Mrs Lin and Lai Da’s wife headed the married servants waiting outside the bamboo blind to serve the food and wine. Zhou Rui’s wife and several maids waited behind the surrounding screens to answer any summons.

The guests’ attendants had already been taken elsewhere to be entertained. Presently the company of players made their appearance on the stage. Below it twelve pages, none of whom had yet grown a queue, stood ready to serve. In a little while one of them carried the playbill to the foot of the steps and handed it first to the married servant who received messages. She passed it to Mrs Lin, who placed it on a small tea tray and edged inside the blind to hand it to Pei-feng, the concubine attending You-shi. Pei-feng then presented it to You-shi. Carrying the tray, You-shi went up to the places of honour. After some courteous reluctance the Dowager Princess of Nan’an chose an auspicious play. There was another exchange of courtesies, and the Princess of Beijing chose one too. The rest, after further polite offers and refusals, said that any good pieces the players cared to choose would do.

Before long four courses of dishes had been served and the first soup course had begun. The attendants of the various visiting households gave out gratuities. The guests withdrew to refresh themselves, then returned to the garden, where fine tea was served afresh. The Dowager Princess of Nan’an asked after Bao-yu.

‘The Sutra for Peace and Long Life is being recited in several temples today,’ Grandmother Jia said with a smile. ‘He’s gone to kneel through the recitations.’

The princess asked after the young ladies as well.

‘Some are ill, some are delicate, and they’re shy of visitors,’ Grandmother Jia said. ‘So I’ve left them to look after my rooms for me. We have plenty of young players. I’ve had a troupe sent to the hall over there, and the girls are watching plays with their aunt’s daughters.’

‘Then have them invited over,’ the Dowager Princess said, smiling.

Grandmother Jia turned to Xi-feng and told her to fetch the Shi, Xue and Lin girls.

‘And just bring your Third Sister along with them.’

Xi-feng assented and went to Grandmother Jia’s rooms. The girls were eating fruit and watching the plays, and Bao-yu had only just returned from kneeling in the temple. Xi-feng delivered her message. Bao-chai and Bao-qin, Dai-yu, Tan-chun and Xiang-yun, five in all, went into the garden. Their arrival brought the usual greetings, inquiries and invitations to sit down.

Some of the company had seen them before; one or two families had not. All joined in praising them without stint. Xiang-yun was the one the Dowager Princess knew best.

‘So you’re here!’ she said, smiling. ‘You heard I’d come and still wouldn’t come out till you were sent for. I shall settle accounts with your uncle tomorrow.’

Taking Tan-chun by one hand and Bao-chai by the other, she asked their ages and praised them again and again. Then she let them go and drew Dai-yu and Bao-qin towards her, studying them closely and finding much to admire.

‘They’re all so good,’ she said with a smile. ‘How am I to choose which one to praise?’

Someone had already made up five portions from the presents kept ready for such occasions: five gold rings and five jade rings, and five bracelets of scented beads.

‘Don’t laugh at these, girls,’ said the Dowager Princess. ‘Keep them to give your maids.’

The five hastened to bow their thanks. The Princess of Beijing also gave five kinds of presents. We need not detail the rest.

After tea and a short stroll through the garden, Grandmother Jia and the others invited the guests to return to the feast. The Dowager Princess of Nan’an, however, took her leave, saying she was unwell.

‘It was quite impossible for me not to come today, but you must forgive me for being the first to say goodbye.’

At this Grandmother Jia and the others could hardly press her to stay. After a further exchange of courtesies they escorted her to the garden gate, where she entered her sedan chair and left. The Princess of Beijing stayed a little longer, then took her leave as well. Of the others, some remained until the feast was over and some left before the end.

Grandmother Jia was exhausted by the day and received no visitors on the next. Lady Xing and Lady Wang did all the entertaining. The young men of the old allied families who came to offer birthday congratulations merely paid their respects in the hall, where Jia She, Jia Zheng, Jia Zhen and the others returned their bows and received them before they went to dine at Ning. Of this we need say no more.

During these days You-shi did not return to her own mansion at night. She entertained guests by day and slept in Li Wan’s rooms in the garden. One evening, after she had attended Grandmother Jia at supper, the old lady said:

‘You’re all tired, and so am I. Find yourselves something to eat and get some rest early. We shall have to be up early for more bustle tomorrow.’

You-shi assented and withdrew, then went to Xi-feng’s rooms for her meal. Xi-feng was upstairs supervising the putting away of the new screens sent as presents. Only Patience was in the room, folding clothes for her.

‘Has your mistress eaten yet?’ You-shi asked.

‘Wouldn’t we have sent to ask you, ma’am, if we were having dinner?’ Patience said with a smile.

‘Then I’ll find something somewhere else,’ You-shi said, laughing. ‘I’m so hungry I can’t bear it.’

She turned to go.

‘Please come back, ma’am,’ Patience called hastily, smiling.

‘There are some cakes here. Have a little to keep you going, and then you can have dinner.’

‘You’re all so busy,’ You-shi said. ‘I’ll go and amuse myself with the girls in the garden.’

She went out as she spoke. Patience could not keep her and had to let her go.

You-shi went straight to the garden. Seeing that the main gate and all the side gates were still open and the coloured lanterns still hung alight, she turned and told a little maid to call the women on duty. The girl went into their room, found not a soul there and came back to report. You-shi then told her to summon one of the women in charge. The maid assented and went out to the flat-roofed lodge beyond the inner gate, where the women stewards met to consult and muster their people. There she found only two old women dividing up dishes of food and fruit.

‘Which of the mistresses is here?’ she asked.

‘The mistress from the east mansion wants one of the mistresses in charge. She has instructions to give and is waiting.’

The two women were intent on dividing the food. Hearing that it was the mistress from the east mansion, they paid very little heed.

‘The mistresses in charge have all just gone,’ they said.

‘If they’ve gone, go to their homes and fetch them.’

‘We only look after the rooms. Fetching people isn’t our work. If you want someone fetched, miss, send someone whose business it is.’

‘Well! Well! This is a fine state of things!’ cried the little maid. ‘Why won’t you fetch them? You may fool the new ones, but don’t try it on me. Who goes to fetch them every other day if you don’t? If there were some private news to pass on, or a present for one of the mistresses, you’d be tumbling over each other like little dogs to get there first. Nobody would know which was which! Would you give Mrs Lian that answer if she sent for someone?’

The two old women had been drinking, and the girl’s exposure of their failings made them so angry and ashamed that they retorted:

‘Shut your filthy mouth!

‘Whether we take a message is our business, not yours. Don’t you start picking holes in us. Think of your own father and mother, toadying to the stewards over there. They’re better at it than we are! “Mixed-grain noodles in clear water: you eat them, and I can see.” We all know what goes on. Separate doors, separate households. If you’re so clever, throw your weight about among your own people. You’ve a long way to go before you can order ours about!’

The maid’s face went white with rage.

‘Very good! Very good! A fine thing to say!’

She turned and went in to report.

You-shi had already gone into the garden. There she had met Aroma, Bao-qin and Xiang-yun chatting and laughing with two nuns from the Kshitigarbha Convent. Saying she was hungry, she had gone first to the House of Green Delights, where Aroma brought out several kinds of snacks, some with meat and some without. The nuns, Bao-qin and Xiang-yun took tea and went on with their stories. The little maid found her way to them and angrily repeated everything that had been said.

You-shi gave a cold laugh.

‘Who are these two women?’

The nuns and the two girls, afraid she would lose her temper, hastened to soothe her.

‘They couldn’t have said that. The child must have misheard.’

The nuns gave the maid a smiling push.

‘What a temper you have, child! You shouldn’t have repeated what those muddle-headed old mammies said. Our mistress is far too precious to be troubled like this. She’s worn out after these last few days, and hasn’t had so much as a drop of hot soup. We can scarcely manage to amuse her for a little while. Why bring up such things?’

Aroma too hastened to take her out, smiling as she did so.

‘There’s a good girl. Go out and rest a little. I’ll send someone to fetch them.’

‘Don’t send anyone,’ You-shi said. ‘You go and fetch those two old women yourself, and go over there and fetch their Xi-feng too.’

‘I’ll go and invite her, ma’am,’ Aroma said with a smile.

‘No, you shan’t go,’ said You-shi.

The two nuns hastily rose, still smiling.

‘You’re always so generous and forbearing, ma’am. It’s Her Old Ladyship’s birthday. If you lose your temper today, won’t people talk?’

Bao-qin and Xiang-yun also smiled and pleaded with her.

‘If it weren’t for Her Old Ladyship’s birthday, I wouldn’t let it pass,’ said You-shi. ‘Leave it for now, then.’

While they were talking, Aroma had already sent another maid out beyond the garden gate to find someone. By chance the girl met Zhou Rui’s wife and told her what had happened. Zhou Rui’s wife held no position of responsibility, but as one of Lady Wang’s dowry servants she had always enjoyed a certain standing. Shrewd and slippery, she made it her particular business to offer her services and curry favour everywhere, so that the masters and mistresses of all the apartments liked her. On hearing the story she hurried straight into Green Delights, talking as she flew along.

‘To think of upsetting you like this, ma’am! It’s dreadful! Our people are so spoilt now, there’s no bearing them. Just my luck not to be there. If I had been, I’d have given them a few slaps first and settled accounts with them properly once these few days were over.’

At the sight of her You-shi smiled too.

‘Come here, Sister Zhou. Tell me what you think of this. At this hour the gates are still wide open, all the lamps and candles alight, and all sorts of people coming and going. Suppose something happened that we weren’t prepared for. What should we do?

‘That’s why I wanted the women on duty to put out the lights and shut the gates. And there wasn’t so much as a shadow of one of them.’

‘That’s outrageous!’ said Zhou Rui’s wife. ‘Only the other day Mrs Lian told them that with so much to do and so many strangers about, they must shut the gates and put out the lights as soon as it was evening, and let no one in who didn’t belong in the garden. And today there’s nobody there! Once these few days are over, some of them must certainly be beaten.’

You-shi repeated what the little maid had reported.

‘Don’t be angry, ma’am. When it’s all over, I’ll tell the people in charge to beat them within an inch of their lives. I’ll ask them who told them they could talk about “separate doors, separate households”! I’ve already had the lights put out and the main gate and side gates shut.’

In the midst of this commotion someone arrived from Xi-feng to invite You-shi to dinner.

‘I’m not hungry now,’ she said. ‘I’ve just eaten a few buns. Ask your mistress to have her dinner without me.’

Presently Zhou Rui’s wife found an opportunity to slip out and report the whole affair to Xi-feng.

‘Those two old women are mistresses in charge themselves,’ she added. ‘Whenever we speak to them, they’re like a pair of wild beasts. If you don’t reprimand them, ma’am, the senior mistress will feel slighted.’

‘If that’s how it is, put down their names,’ said Xi-feng. ‘When these few days are over, have them tied up and sent to the other mansion for Cousin Zhen’s wife to deal with. She can have them given a few strokes or be merciful and let them off, just as she pleases. It’s nothing to make a fuss about.’

This was just what Zhou Rui’s wife had been waiting to hear. She had long been on bad terms with these women. Once outside, she sent a page to Lin Zhi-xiao’s house with instructions in Xi-feng’s name that Mrs Lin was to come at once and see the senior mistress. At the same time she sent orders for the two old women to be bound immediately and put under guard in the stables.

Mrs Lin had no idea what was the matter. The lamps were already lit. She hurried into her carriage and came in, going first to Xi-feng. Her arrival was announced at the inner gate, but the maids came out to say:

‘Our mistress has just gone to rest. The senior mistress is in the garden. You’re to go and see her, Mamma.’

Mrs Lin could only go into the garden to Sweet-rice Village. When the maids announced her, You-shi felt quite embarrassed and had her brought in at once.

‘I only asked for you because I couldn’t find anyone,’ she told her with a smile. ‘If you’d gone home, it wasn’t anything important. Who’s fetched you back and made you come all this way for nothing? It was a trifling matter, and I’ve already let it drop.’

‘Mrs Lian sent someone for me,’ Mrs Lin said, smiling too. ‘She said you had instructions for me, ma’am.’

‘Nothing of the sort. I asked for you because I thought you hadn’t gone. Who’s been meddling and telling Xi-feng now? Sister Zhou, I suppose. Go home and rest. There’s nothing of any importance.’

Li Wan was about to explain what had happened, but You-shi stopped her.

Seeing how matters stood, Mrs Lin turned to leave the garden. She happened to meet Aunt Zhao, who greeted her with a smile.

‘Well, well, my good sister! Why aren’t you home resting at this hour? What are you still running about for?’

Mrs Lin smiled and said that she had been home, then explained how she had been called back, giving the story from the beginning. Aunt Zhao was always eager to ferret out such things. She also habitually cultivated the women in charge, keeping in touch with them so that they could back one another up. She had already heard most of what had happened. After listening to Mrs Lin, she gave her own account of the affair.

‘So that was it!’ Mrs Lin said with a laugh. ‘It isn’t worth a fart! If they feel merciful, they needn’t take any notice. If they’re feeling small-minded, a few strokes would settle it.’

‘My good sister,’ said Aunt Zhao, ‘the business may be small, but it shows how high-handed they’ve grown. Sending all that way to fetch you in! They were plainly making a fool of you, having their fun at your expense.

‘Go and get some rest. You’ve plenty to do tomorrow. I won’t keep you for tea.’

Mrs Lin went out. At the side gate the daughters of the two old women came up to her, crying and begging her to intercede.

‘What foolish children you are!’ Mrs Lin said with a laugh. ‘Who told your mothers to drink and talk nonsense and get themselves into trouble? I knew nothing about it. Mrs Lian had them tied up, and even I’m in the wrong.

‘Who am I supposed to plead for?’

The two little maids were only seven or eight and understood nothing of these matters. They went on crying and begging until Mrs Lin was at her wits’ end.

‘Little fool! You’ve a way out in front of you, and instead you pester me. Your elder sister is married to the son of Mamma Fei, Her Ladyship’s dowry servant over at the other house. Go and tell your sister. Get her mother-in-law to speak to Her Ladyship, and what is there that can’t be settled?’

This opened one child’s eyes, but the other went on pleading. Mrs Lin spat in exasperation.

‘You stupid little wretch! Once she’s gone and spoken, it’ll all be settled. They’re not going to let her mother go and beat yours alone.’

With that she got into her carriage and left.

The first little maid duly went over and told her elder sister, and spoke to Mrs Fei. Mrs Fei was one of Lady Xing’s dowry servants. She had enjoyed her day of influence, but lately, since Grandmother Jia had shown Lady Xing little favour, the people on that side had lost much of their importance too. Every person of any standing on Jia Zheng’s side was watched with hostile eyes by those on the other. Mrs Fei was always trading on her age and Lady Xing’s backing. She often drank and gave vent to her feelings in abuse and wild complaints. At an occasion as grand as Grandmother Jia’s birthday she could only stand by and watch the others display their talents and their skill at managing affairs, shouting orders and bustling about. Already deeply disgruntled, she had been making oblique attacks, grumbling and chattering spitefully, but the people on this side would not quarrel with her. Now the news that Zhou Rui’s wife had bound her son’s mother-in-law poured oil on the fire. Emboldened by drink, she pointed at the dividing wall and let loose a torrent of abuse. Then she went to petition Lady Xing, saying that her relation had done nothing wrong.

‘She only had a little exchange of words with the senior mistress’s little maid from the other mansion. Zhou Rui’s wife egged our Mrs Lian on to have her tied up in the stables, and they mean to beat her when these two days are over.

‘I beg Your Ladyship to say a word to Mrs Lian and have her spared this once. My son’s mother-in-law is an old woman of seventy or eighty.’

Ever since her unsuccessful attempt to obtain Faithful, Lady Xing had found Grandmother Jia increasingly cold towards her. Xi-feng, by contrast, enjoyed more consideration than she did herself. Moreover, when the Dowager Princess of Nan’an had come the other day and asked to see the girls, Grandmother Jia had summoned only Tan-chun of the sisters, as though Ying-chun did not exist. Lady Xing was already full of resentment and discontent, with no way to express it. Around her were these petty creatures, unable to act openly on their own jealousy and grievances, who made up stories and stirred up trouble behind people’s backs to set their mistress against the others. At first they merely complained of the servants on that side. Gradually they began to accuse Xi-feng herself.

‘She only coaxes the old lady into liking her so that she can rule the roost. She keeps Master Lian under her thumb and puts ideas into the Second Lady’s head. As for the real mistress on this side, she pays her no heed at all.’

Later they began accusing Lady Wang too.

‘It’s the Second Lady and Mrs Lian who turn the old lady against Your Ladyship.’

Even had Lady Xing possessed a heart of iron and a breast of bronze, being a woman she could hardly have avoided some feelings of ill will. As it was, these things had lately made her detest Xi-feng.

She listened to this latest tale without saying whether it was right or wrong. Early next morning she went to see Grandmother Jia. All the members of the clan assembled, the feast began and the players took the stage. Grandmother Jia was in excellent spirits. There were no distant connections present today, only younger members of her own clan, so she came out in ordinary clothes and everyday ornaments to receive their respects in the hall. A couch had been set by itself in the centre, complete with bolster, backrest and footstool, and she reclined on it. Low stools of the same kind stood all round it, with Bao-chai, Bao-qin, Dai-yu, Xiang-yun, Ying-chun, Tan-chun and Xi-chun gathered about her. Jia Bin’s mother had brought her daughter Xi-luan, and Jia Qiong’s mother her daughter Si-jie. With the granddaughters from several other branches, there were about twenty girls, older and younger. Grandmother Jia was particularly taken with Xi-luan and Si-jie, who were not only pretty but spoke and behaved quite differently from the others. She called them over to sit by her couch as well.

Bao-yu sat at her feet on the couch, gently pounding her legs for her. Aunt Xue occupied the place of honour. Below her the two rows of guests were arranged by branch of the family and seniority of generation. Outside the blind, the male members of the clan sat in the two galleries, also in their proper order. First the women came forward in groups to pay their respects, and then the men. Grandmother Jia reclined on her couch, telling someone to say, ‘That will do, you needn’t,’ but by then they had already performed their bows. Next Lai Da led the menservants in, kneeling in ranks that stretched from the ceremonial gate to the great hall. When they had finished their kowtows, the married women servants followed, and then the maids from the different apartments. The whole bustle lasted long enough for two or three meals. Many cages of birds were then brought in and the birds released in the courtyard. Only after Jia She and the others had burnt the paper offerings to Heaven and Earth and the God of Longevity did the plays and the drinking begin. Grandmother Jia remained until the middle interval of the performance, then went inside to rest, telling the others to do as they pleased. She asked Xi-feng to keep Xi-luan and Si-jie there to play for a couple of days before going home. Xi-feng came out and spoke to their mothers, both of whom had often received help from her and were only too glad to agree.

The two girls were eager to play in the garden too, and did not go home that evening. Not until the party was breaking up did Lady Xing, smiling ingratiatingly before a crowd of people, plead with Xi-feng.

‘I hear you were angry last night, Mrs Lian, and sent Steward Zhou’s wife to tie up two old women. I don’t know what crime they committed. Strictly speaking, I ought not to ask for mercy. But I thought, on the old lady’s happy day even hard-hearted people give money and rice to help the poor and the old, whereas our family starts by tormenting people. If not for my sake, then for the old lady’s, let them go.’

With that she got into her carriage and left. Hearing such words in front of so many people, Xi-feng was both ashamed and angry. For a moment she could not collect her thoughts. Her face flushed purple with pent-up feeling, but she turned to Lai Da’s wife and the others with a smile.

‘What can she mean? Some of our people offended my cousin’s wife from the other mansion yesterday. I was afraid she might feel slighted, so I left it entirely to her to deal with them. They hadn’t offended me.

‘Who has been so quick to whisper in Her Ladyship’s ear?’

Lady Wang asked what had happened. Still smiling, Xi-feng told her about the previous day.

‘Even I knew nothing about it,’ You-shi said with a smile. ‘You really took too much trouble.’

‘I thought you might feel slighted,’ said Xi-feng. ‘That’s why I left them for you to deal with. It was only a matter of courtesy. If I were at your house and someone offended me, you’d naturally send them over for me to deal with. However good a servant they might be, you couldn’t disregard that courtesy. Now someone has gone over there to curry favour, with nothing better to do than make a story of it.’

‘Your mother-in-law is right,’ said Lady Wang. ‘Zhen’s wife is hardly a stranger. There’s no need for these empty courtesies. The old lady’s birthday is what matters. It’s best to let them go.’

She turned and ordered the two old women released. The more Xi-feng thought of it, the angrier and more ashamed she became. Her spirits sank, her hurt gave way to grief, and tears began to roll down her cheeks. She went back to her room to cry in vexation, determined that no one should know.

Just then Grandmother Jia sent Amber to fetch her, saying she wanted to speak to her at once. Amber was astonished at what she found.

‘What’s happened? You were quite all right. Her Old Ladyship is waiting for you.’

Xi-feng quickly dried her tears, washed her face and put on fresh rouge and powder before going with her.

‘How many of the families who sent presents the other day sent screens?’ Grandmother Jia asked.

‘Sixteen altogether,’ Xi-feng replied. ‘Twelve large screens and four little kang screens. The finest is the large twelve-panel screen from the Zhen family in the south. It has A Bedful of Official Tablets in tapestry-work on scarlet satin on one side, and Hundred Longevities painted in gold on the other. The glass screen from General Wu of Yuehai’s family is quite good too.’

‘Then don’t touch those two. Put them away carefully. I want to give them to someone.’

Xi-feng assented. Faithful came over and kept looking at her face until Grandmother Jia asked:

‘Don’t you know her? What are you staring at?’

‘Her eyes look rather swollen,’ Faithful said with a smile. ‘I was surprised. That’s why I kept looking.’

Grandmother Jia called Xi-feng closer and peered at her too.

‘They were itching just now,’ Xi-feng said, smiling. ‘I rubbed them and made them a little swollen.’

‘Surely no one’s been upsetting you again?’ Faithful said with a smile.

‘Who would dare upset me? And even if someone did, I wouldn’t dare cry on Grandmother’s happy day.’

‘Quite right,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘I’m just going to have supper. Stay and wait on me, then you and Zhen’s wife can eat what’s left. Afterwards you can help the two good sisters pick over Buddha beans for me, and earn yourselves a little longer life as well. The girls and Bao-yu did theirs the other day. Now it’s your turn, so don’t say I’m partial.’

While she was speaking, a table of vegetarian food was brought in first for the two nuns. Only after they had eaten was the meat served. When Grandmother Jia had finished, her table was carried to the outer room. While You-shi and Xi-feng were eating, she had Xi-luan and Si-jie brought to join them. When they had all finished, they washed their hands and lit incense, and a sheng of beans was carried in. The two nuns first recited a Buddhist verse. Together they picked the beans one by one into a shallow basket, invoking the Buddha for every bean.

The beans were to be cooked next day and distributed at a crossroads to form bonds of merit for long life. Grandmother Jia reclined and listened while the nuns told her more stories of Buddhist retribution and good deeds. Faithful had already heard from Amber that Xi-feng had been crying, and had learnt the reason from Patience. That evening, when the others had gone, she reported:

‘Mrs Lian really had been crying. The Elder Lady from the other house humiliated her in front of everyone.’

Grandmother Jia asked why, and Faithful explained.

‘It only shows that Xi-feng understands propriety,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘Are we to let the servants offend every master and mistress in the clan without doing anything about it, merely because it’s my birthday?

‘Her Ladyship has been out of humour for a long time without daring to show it. Today she found a way to vent it. She was plainly trying to humiliate Xi-feng in front of everyone.’

Just then Bao-qin and the others came in, and they dropped the subject.

‘Where have you come from?’ Grandmother Jia asked.

‘We were all talking in Cousin Lin’s rooms in the garden,’ Bao-qin replied.

Something suddenly occurred to Grandmother Jia. She hastily called an old woman over and gave her instructions.

‘Go round to the women in the different parts of the garden and impress this on them. Xi-luan and Si-jie, who are staying here, may be poor, but they are to be treated just like our own girls. Everyone is to look after them properly. I know our people, men and women alike: one heart set on wealth and two eyes for appearances. They may well think those two beneath their notice. If I hear that anyone has slighted them, I shan’t let it pass.’

The old woman assented and was about to leave when Faithful said:

‘I’ll go and tell them. They won’t listen to her.’

She went straight to the garden, calling first at Sweet-rice Village. Neither Li Wan nor You-shi was there. When she asked the maids, they said both were at the Third Miss’s. Faithful turned back and went to the Hall of Dawn Green, where she did indeed find the garden company talking and laughing together.

‘What have you come running here for at this hour?’ they asked, laughing, and invited her to sit down.

‘Mayn’t I come out for a stroll too?’ Faithful said with a smile.

She then repeated Grandmother Jia’s instructions. Li Wan rose at once to hear them and sent for one of the women in charge from each part of the garden, telling them to pass the message on to everyone. Of this we need say no more.

‘The old lady really thinks of everything,’ You-shi said, smiling. ‘Put ten of us young, strong ones together and we still couldn’t keep up with her.’

‘Xi-feng’s devilish cleverness keeps her somewhere within sight,’ said Li Wan.

‘The rest of us haven’t a hope.’

‘Oh, never mind Xi-feng or tiger Feng!’ said Faithful. ‘She’s to be pitied too. She’s managed all these years without putting a foot wrong with Her Old Ladyship and Her Ladyship, but who knows how many people she’s offended out of their sight? In short, it’s hard to please anyone. If you’re too honest and have no resource, your parents-in-law complain you’re too simple, and the household won’t stand in awe of you. If you do have resource, you’re bound to mend one thing at the expense of another. Our household is better than ever now. These new “mistresses” of ours, with servant written all over them, are all so pleased with themselves they don’t know what they want next. Let the least thing displease them, and they’re either backbiting or setting people against one another.

‘I don’t breathe a word of it, for fear of upsetting Her Old Ladyship. If I told all I know, none of us would have a peaceful day. I’m not saying this just because the Third Miss is here. When people grumble behind Her Old Ladyship’s back because she favours Bao-yu, that’s one thing. You might call it partiality. But now I even hear complaints because she’s fond of you, miss. Isn’t it ridiculous?’

‘There are too many fools to argue with them all,’ Tan-chun said with a smile. ‘I think it would be better to belong to a small household with fewer people. They might be poor, but at least they could be cheerful and happy together. In a great household like ours, with so many people, those outside see us as young ladies worth thousands upon thousands of gold pieces and imagine how happy we must be. Little do they know how much worse our troubles are, troubles we can’t even speak of.’

‘Not everyone worries as much as you, Third Sister,’ said Bao-yu. ‘I’m always telling you, whatever it is, not to listen to vulgar talk or think about vulgar affairs. Just enjoy your wealth and honour. You’re not like us, with no claim to such serene good fortune, born to grub about in the dirt.’

‘As though everyone were like you!’ said You-shi. ‘Nothing to trouble your heart, nothing to tie you down. All you know is playing and laughing with the girls, eating when you’re hungry and sleeping when you’re tired. A few more years will go by and you’ll still be the same, never giving a thought to what comes afterwards.’

‘If I can spend another day with the girls, that’s another day gained,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘When I’m dead, it’s over. What do I care about afterwards?’

‘Now you’re talking nonsense again,’ Li Wan and the others said, laughing. ‘Even if you’re such a good-for-nothing that you stay here till you’re old, do you suppose the girls will never marry?’

‘No wonder everyone says he only looks like a grown boy,’ You-shi said with a laugh. ‘Underneath he’s just as silly and foolish as ever.’

‘Nothing in this world is certain,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘Who knows who’ll live and who’ll die? Suppose I died today or tomorrow, this year or next. I should still have had a life to my liking.’

They would not let him finish.

‘There he goes again! We’d better not talk to him. If you do, it’s either foolishness or madness.’

Xi-luan smiled.

‘Don’t say that, Cousin Bao. When all the girls here really have married, Grandmother and Her Ladyship will be lonely too. I’ll come and keep you company.’

‘Don’t you talk nonsense as well, miss,’ Li Wan and You-shi said, laughing. ‘Do you mean you’ll never marry?

‘Who do you expect to believe that?’

Xi-luan lowered her head. By now it was the beginning of the first watch, and everyone went back to their rooms to rest. We may leave the others for the moment and return to Faithful, who was making her way home. As she reached the garden gate she saw that the side gate was ajar and had not yet been barred. No one was passing through the garden now. Only the light from the duty room showed faintly, and a dim moon hung in the sky. Faithful had no companion and carried no lantern. Alone and light-footed as she was, she had escaped the notice of the women on duty. Just then she felt the need to relieve herself. She stepped off the paved path to look for a patch of low grass, and came into the shade of a great cassia tree behind an ornamental rock. As she turned the corner of the rock, a sudden rustle of clothing gave her a tremendous fright. Looking closely, she saw two people there. At her approach they tried to hide among the bushes behind the rock. But Faithful had sharp eyes. By the moonlight she made out one tall, strongly built figure in a red skirt, her hair dressed high: Chess, from Ying-chun’s rooms.

Faithful supposed that Chess and another girl had come there for the same purpose as herself, and were hiding deliberately to give her a fright for fun. She called out, laughing:

‘Chess, come out at once! If you frighten me, I’ll shout and have you caught as a thief. A great girl like you, and still you can’t have enough of your games, day or night!’

It was only a joke to bring her out. But Chess had a guilty conscience and thought Faithful had seen what she was about. If she cried out and brought the others, things would be worse still. Besides, Faithful had always been close to her, unlike the others. She ran from behind the tree, seized Faithful and dropped to her knees.

‘Dear sister, please, please don’t shout!’

Faithful, quite at a loss, hastily pulled her up and asked with a smile:

‘What is all this?’

Chess’s face was scarlet and swollen with distress, and tears began to fall. Thinking back, Faithful recalled that the other dim figure had looked rather like a page. She guessed most of the truth and found herself blushing to the roots of her hair, frightened as well as ashamed. After a moment she hurriedly whispered:

‘Who is he?’

Chess knelt again.

‘My cousin.’

Faithful spat.

‘You’ll be the death of yourselves! The death of yourselves!’

Chess turned her head and whispered:

‘There’s no use hiding. Sister’s seen you. Come out and kowtow to her.’

The page could only crawl out from behind the tree and knock his head to the ground over and over, like a pestle pounding garlic. Faithful made haste to turn away, but Chess clung to her and begged her through her tears.

‘Both our lives are in your hands, sister. Please, please spare us!’

‘Don’t worry,’ said Faithful. ‘I won’t tell a soul.’

Before she had finished, someone at the side gate called:

‘Miss Jin’s gone out. Lock the side gate.’

Chess was still clinging to Faithful and she could not get free. Hearing this, she called back:

‘I’m here. I have something to do. Wait a moment. I’m coming out.’

Chess had to let go of her and allow her to leave—
