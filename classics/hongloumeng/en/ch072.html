Chapter 72 Wang Xi-feng is too proud of her strength to admit illness; Lai Wang’s wife borrows power to force a marriage

Chapter end

Faithful came out through the corner gate with her cheeks still burning and her heart thumping. Nothing could have been more unexpected. This was no ordinary matter, she reflected. If it got out, illicit love and theft went hand in hand: lives might be at stake, and there was no telling who else might be implicated. It had nothing to do with her, after all. She would keep it to herself and tell no one. Back in her room, she reported to Grandmother Jia that her errand was done, and they all settled down for the night. From then on she seldom entered the garden after dark. If such strange things could happen even there, she thought, what might not happen elsewhere? She grew reluctant to go about elsewhere too.

Now Chess and her cousin, her paternal aunt’s son, had grown up playing and living together. In their childish games they had promised that neither would ever marry anyone else. In recent years they had grown into an attractive young pair, and whenever Chess went home they exchanged tender glances, neither having forgotten their early attachment. But they had never found an opportunity to act on it. Both were afraid their parents would refuse consent. Between them, working from inside and outside, they contrived to bribe the old women in the garden to leave a gate open and keep watch. Amid the confusion that day they had at last found their first chance of a tryst.

Though they had not yet become one, they had sworn vows as lasting as the seas and mountains and secretly exchanged keepsakes; there had already been tenderness enough between them. Faithful’s sudden appearance broke up their meeting. The young servant had slipped through flowers and willows and escaped by the corner gate. Chess did not sleep all night, but it was too late for regrets.

When she saw Faithful next day, she flushed and turned pale by turns, scarcely knowing where to put herself. With this guilty fear in her heart, she had no appetite for food or tea and was bewildered whether she sat or stood. Two days dragged by without a word being said, and she began to feel a little easier. That evening an old woman came and told her in a whisper, ‘Your cousin has run away. He hasn’t been home for three or four days.

‘They’re sending people out to look for him everywhere.’ Chess nearly fell backwards with rage. Even if it had all come out, she thought, he ought to have stayed so that they could die together. He called himself a man, yet he had been the first to run! That showed how little feeling he had for her.

This gave her a fresh cause for anger. Next day she felt so wretched that, struggle as she might, she could no longer keep going. She took to her bed and fell seriously ill. Faithful heard that a young servant had disappeared from the other side for no apparent reason, and that Chess was so ill in the garden that they meant to move her out. She concluded that both were afraid of punishment. ‘They’re frightened I’ll tell. That must be what has brought them to this.’ Feeling guilty herself now, she found an excuse to visit Chess, sent everyone out of the room and stood before her to swear an oath. ‘If I tell a single person, may I drop dead on the spot! Set your mind at rest and get well. Don’t throw your little life away for nothing.’

Chess caught hold of her and wept. ‘My dear sister, we’ve been close ever since we were children. You’ve never treated me as an outsider, and I’ve never dared be careless of you. Now I’ve taken this one false step. If you really won’t tell a soul, you’ll be as good as my own mother. Every day I live from now on will be a day you’ve given me. Once I’m well, I’ll put up a tablet praying for your long life and burn incense and bow before it every day, so that you may have happiness and long life together. If I die, I’ll come back as a donkey or a dog to repay you. Besides, as they say, “Build your banquet shelter a thousand li long: there is no feast that does not end.” In another two or three years we shall all have to leave this place. And there’s another saying: “Even drifting duckweed meets again one day; Must people never meet again?” If we should meet somewhere one day, how shall I ever repay your goodness?’ She spoke through her tears. Her words made Faithful’s heart ache, and she began to cry too. She nodded. ‘That’s just it.

‘I’m not in charge of things here. Why should I ruin your good name simply to make a show of being useful? Besides, it’s not the sort of thing I could easily bring myself to tell anyone. Don’t worry. But when you’re well again, you must behave properly. No more carrying on like this.’ Chess kept nodding on her pillow.

Faithful comforted her a little longer before leaving. She knew Jia Lian was out, and had noticed that for the last two days Xi-feng had looked and sounded rather listless, quite unlike her usual self. As she was passing, she went to see her too. At the entrance to Xi-feng’s courtyard, the servants at the inner gate rose when they saw her and waited for her to pass. She had just entered the main room when Patience came out of the bedroom. Seeing her, Patience hurried forward with a quiet smile. ‘She’s only just had a little to eat and lain down for her afternoon sleep. Come and sit in here for a while.’ Faithful followed her into the room on the east. A little maid brought tea. ‘What’s the matter with your mistress these last two days?’ Faithful asked softly. ‘She looks so tired.’ There was no one else in the room. Patience sighed. ‘It isn’t just these last two days. She’s been like this for over a month. Then all the rushing about lately, and the vexations she’s had to put up with, have brought it on again.

‘These last two days she’s been worse than before. She can’t hold out any longer, and now it’s beginning to show.’ ‘If that’s so, why didn’t you send for a doctor sooner?’ Faithful asked at once. Patience sighed. ‘My dear, don’t you know her temper? Never mind getting a doctor and taking medicine. I couldn’t bear to see her like that and merely asked her how she felt, and she flew into a rage and said I was wishing an illness on her. And still she spends every day prying into this and inquiring into that. She won’t let things go and look after herself.’ ‘Even so, you ought to have a doctor find out what it is. Then everyone could stop worrying.’ ‘My dear, as to what it is, I don’t think it’s anything slight.’ ‘What is it?’ Faithful asked anxiously. Patience moved closer and whispered in her ear. ‘Ever since her courses came last month, there’s been a constant trickle of blood for a whole month.

‘Isn’t that serious?’ ‘Oh dear!’ Faithful exclaimed. ‘From what you say, doesn’t that make it flooding?’ Patience hastily spat, then smiled and whispered, ‘You an unmarried girl, and talking like that! You really do know how to wish misfortune on people.’ Faithful blushed. With a little answering smile, she whispered, ‘Well, I don’t really know what is flooding and what isn’t. Have you forgotten? My elder sister died of this illness.

‘I didn’t know what it was either. I happened to hear Mother talking about it to my sister’s mother-in-law and couldn’t understand. Afterwards I heard Mother explain how it had happened, and only then did I understand a little.’ ‘Of course you would know,’ said Patience with a smile. ‘I’d quite forgotten.’

While they were talking, a little maid came in and told Patience, ‘Mrs Zhu was here again just now. We told her the mistress had only just gone to sleep, so she went over to Her Ladyship’s.’ Patience nodded. ‘Which Mrs Zhu?’ Faithful asked. ‘Mrs Zhu the official matchmaker. Some Mr Sun’s family wants a marriage with ours, so these last two days she’s been coming every day with a card and refusing to take no for an answer.’ Before she had finished, a little maid ran in to say that the Second Master had come home. Even as she spoke, Jia Lian reached the door of the main room, calling for Patience. Patience answered and had scarcely started out to meet him when he found his way to this room himself. At the doorway he suddenly caught sight of Faithful sitting on the kang and stopped short. ‘Sister Faithful!’ he said, smiling. ‘What brings your honoured feet to our humble door today?’ Faithful remained seated and smiled. ‘I came to pay my respects to master and mistress, only to find one out and the other asleep.’ ‘You work hard all year round looking after Grandmother. I haven’t even been to see you. How could I presume to trouble you to visit us?

‘This is a happy chance. I was just going to look for you. This robe was too hot, so I came back first to change into a lined one before going over. And now Heaven has been kind enough to spare me the walk: here you are, waiting for me already!’ He sat down on a chair as he spoke. ‘What is it this time?’ Faithful asked. Jia Lian smiled before answering. ‘There’s something I’ve quite forgotten. I thought you might still remember.

‘On Grandmother’s birthday last year, a monk from another part of the country presented her with an ornament, a Buddha’s-hand citron carved in that oily-translucent stone. Grandmother liked it and had it brought in and put on display at once. On her birthday the other day I saw it was still entered in the antiques register, but no one knows where it is now. The people in the antiques room have asked me about it twice. They’re waiting for me to find out so that they can put a note in the book. Is Grandmother still displaying it, or has it been handed over to somebody?’ ‘She had it out for a few days, got tired of it and gave it to your wife,’ Faithful said. ‘And here you are asking me! I even remember the day. I sent old Wang’s wife over with it myself. If you’ve forgotten, ask your wife or Patience.’ Patience was fetching his clothes. Hearing this, she hurried out. ‘Yes, it was handed over. It’s upstairs now. The mistress has already sent word outside that it was given to these rooms. They’ve muddled it and forgotten to enter it, and now they’re bothering us again with all this nonsense.’

‘If it was given to your mistress, how is it I knew nothing about it?’ Jia Lian said with a smile. ‘You must have kept it for yourselves.’ ‘She told you, sir, and you wanted to give it away. She wouldn’t let you. It took some doing to keep it.

‘Now you’ve forgotten, you say we kept it behind your back! As if it were some marvellous treasure, something no one had ever seen before! We’ve never kept anything for ourselves, even when it was worth ten times as much. Why should we fall in love with that worthless thing?’ Jia Lian lowered his head, smiling, and thought for a moment. Then he clapped his hands. ‘I’m getting quite muddle-headed! Forgetting this and losing track of that, and making everyone complain. I’m not what I used to be.’ ‘You can hardly be blamed,’ Faithful said with a smile. ‘So much to do, so many people talking at you, and then a cup or two of wine on top of it. How could you keep it all straight?’ As she spoke she rose to leave.

Jia Lian quickly rose too. ‘My good sister, sit a little longer. Your younger brother still has a favour to ask.’ Then he scolded the little maids. ‘Why haven’t you made some proper tea? Get a clean covered cup and make some of that fresh tea presented yesterday. Hurry!’ Turning to Faithful, he went on, ‘Grandmother’s birthday has used up all the several thousand taels we had. The rents from the houses and the income from the land won’t come in till the ninth month, and just now we can’t make ends meet. Tomorrow there’s a present to send to the Nan’an household; then we must prepare Her Highness’s Double Ninth gift, and there are weddings and funerals in several other families that call for substantial presents. We shall need at least another two or three thousand taels, and there’s nowhere I can readily borrow it. As they say, “Better rely on yourself than ask others.” I’m afraid I must ask you to risk the blame, sister. Quietly get out a chest of Grandmother’s gold and silver plate, things she won’t miss, and let us pawn it for a thousand taels or so to tide us over. In less than half a year, when the money comes in, I’ll redeem it and put it back. I certainly won’t let you get into trouble.’ ‘You do know how to contrive things,’ Faithful said with a smile. ‘Fancy thinking of that!’ ‘I’m not lying to you,’ Jia Lian said, smiling. ‘There are others besides you with a thousand taels or so under their hands. Only none of them has your good sense and courage.

‘If I mentioned it to them, I should only frighten them. That’s why I’d rather strike a golden bell once than beat a broken drum three thousand times.’ Before he had finished, one of Grandmother Jia’s little maids came hurrying in to find Faithful. ‘Her Old Ladyship’s been asking for you for ages, sister. We’ve looked everywhere, and here you are!’ Faithful hurried off to Grandmother Jia.

When she had gone, Jia Lian could only go in to see Xi-feng. She was already awake. She had heard him asking Faithful to help pawn the things, but had not thought it a suitable moment to join in and had remained lying on the couch. Now that Faithful had left and he had come in, she asked him, ‘Did she agree?’ ‘She hasn’t actually said yes, but there’s a fair chance,’ Jia Lian said with a smile. ‘You only need to speak to her again this evening and it will be certain.’ ‘I’ll have nothing to do with it,’ Xi-feng said, smiling. ‘Suppose I do persuade her. It sounds very fine now, but once you have money again you’ll put it clean out of your head. Who’s going to chase you for it then? And if Grandmother finds out, there goes all the credit I’ve built up these last few years.’ ‘My dear, if you settle it, how shall I thank you?’ ‘You tell me. What will you give me?’ ‘Anything you ask for.’ Patience smiled beside them. ‘The mistress doesn’t want a reward.

‘Only yesterday she was saying there was something she wanted to do, and she was just a hundred or two short. Why not borrow the money and let her have a hundred or two of it? That would suit everyone.’ ‘A good thing you reminded me,’ Xi-feng said, smiling. ‘Very well, that will do.’ ‘You two are too hard on me,’ said Jia Lian. ‘Never mind finding a thousand taels’ worth of pledges. If it were three or five thousand in ready silver, I doubt you’d find it difficult. It ought to be enough that I haven’t asked to borrow from you. Now I want you to say one word for me, and you have to charge interest on it! Really, this is too much.’ Xi-feng turned over and sat up. ‘Whether I have three thousand or fifty thousand, I didn’t make it out of you. There are enough people indoors and out, high and low, wagging their tongues about me behind my back. You were the only one missing. It just shows: without a traitor in the family, the demons outside can’t get in. Where could we Wangs ever have got any money? Naturally the Jias earned it all! Don’t make me sick. You think your family are a lot of Shi Chongs and Deng Tongs. Sweep out the cracks in the floors of the Wang house and you’d have enough to live on for the rest of your lives.

‘Aren’t you ashamed to say such things? There’s proof before your eyes. Take a good look at your aunt’s dowry and mine, and compare them with what your family brought. Which part of ours wasn’t good enough for you?’ ‘One little joke and you fly into a temper,’ Jia Lian said with a smile. ‘What is there to make such a fuss about? If you need a hundred or two hundred taels, what’s that? I may not have more, but I can still find that much. I’ll have it brought in first. You can use it and we’ll talk afterwards. How’s that?’ ‘It’s not as if I needed it this minute for my own burial. What’s the hurry?’ ‘Oh, why say that? It isn’t worth working yourself into such a rage.’ Xi-feng began to smile again. ‘It isn’t that I want to lose my temper. Your words go straight to the heart. I was thinking that the day after tomorrow is the first anniversary of You Er-jie’s death. We were fond of each other, after all. Even if I can do nothing else, I ought to visit her grave and burn some paper money. We were sisters for a time. She may not have left a son or daughter, but we must remember that “The earth scattered by those who go before gets in the eyes of those who follow.”’ This left Jia Lian with nothing to say. He bowed his head and thought for a good while before answering, ‘It’s kind of you to think of everything. I’d quite forgotten.

‘If you don’t need it till the day after tomorrow, and I get this money tomorrow, you can use as much of it as you like.’ He had not finished when Wang-er’s wife came in. ‘Well, is it settled?’ Xi-feng asked. ‘It was no use at all. I said from the first that it would take you to settle it, ma’am.’ ‘What is it now?’ Jia Lian asked. ‘Nothing of any consequence,’ Xi-feng told him. ‘Wang-er has a boy of seventeen who hasn’t got a wife yet. They wanted Cai-xia from your aunt’s rooms, but they didn’t know how your aunt felt about it, so nothing was arranged. The other day your aunt, seeing that Cai-xia was grown up and was always ill besides, was kind enough to send her home and let her parents choose a husband for her themselves. So Wang-er’s wife came to ask my help. I thought the two families were well enough matched and it would be settled as soon as it was proposed. But now she comes and says it’s no use.’ ‘What is there to worry about?’ said Jia Lian. ‘There are plenty better than Cai-xia.’

‘You may say so, sir,’ Wang-er’s wife replied with an ingratiating smile, ‘but if even her family looks down on us, others will look down on us all the more. It’s taken us so long to find a girl who would do. All I ask is that you and the mistress be kind enough to make the match for us.

‘The mistress said they were sure to agree, so I troubled someone to go over and try them. Who would have thought we’d get nothing but a rebuff? The girl herself is good enough. From the little hints I’ve dropped to her privately, she has nothing against it. It’s just those two old creatures, her father and mother, who think too highly of themselves.’ This touched both Xi-feng and Jia Lian on a tender point. Since Jia Lian was there, Xi-feng said nothing for the moment, but watched to see how he would take it. He had things on his mind and little room for a trifle like this. He would have liked to let it alone, but she belonged to one of Xi-feng’s dowry households and had done useful service for them; he could not in decency refuse her. ‘What a lot of whispering and grumbling over nothing!’ he said. ‘Go along and don’t worry. Tomorrow I’ll act as matchmaker and send two people of standing to speak for you, taking the betrothal gifts with them. They can say it’s my wish. If the parents absolutely refuse, tell them to come and see me.’ Wang-er’s wife looked at Xi-feng, who made a little movement with her lips.

Taking the hint, Wang-er’s wife dropped to her knees and kowtowed to Jia Lian in thanks. ‘Kowtow to your young lady, not to me,’ he said quickly. ‘What I’ve said will do, but it would be better still if your young lady sent for the girl’s mother and spoke nicely to her. Of course they’ll agree, but we oughtn’t to use force over it.’ ‘If even you are being so kind and taking such trouble, am I to stand by with my hands in my sleeves?’ Xi-feng said at once. ‘You heard him, Wang-er’s wife. Now that’s settled, hurry up and finish my business too. Tell your husband to collect every debt outside by the end of this year. I won’t have a single cash missing. My name’s bad enough. If I let the loans run another year, they’ll eat me alive.’ ‘You’re too timid, ma’am,’ Wang-er’s wife said with a smile. ‘Who would dare talk about you? Though, to be fair, if we call the money in, it will save us some trouble and we shan’t offend so many people.’ Xi-feng gave a cold laugh. ‘All my pains for nothing.

‘As if I were really waiting for the money for myself! It’s only because we spend more than we take in. Take everything in these rooms: my monthly allowance and your young master’s, and the wages of all four maids as well, amount to no more than ten or twenty taels. That isn’t enough for three or five days’ expenses. If I hadn’t scraped money together and shifted it from one thing to another, Heaven knows what ruined kiln we’d have ended up living in. And all I get for it is the name of a disreputable moneylender.

‘Very well, I’ll call it in. I know how to spend money as well as anyone. We’ll just sit here and spend it, and let it last as long as it lasts. Here’s an example. Before Grandmother’s birthday Her Ladyship worried for two months and couldn’t think what to do. I was the one who suggested those four or five chests of big copper and pewter things lying upstairs at the back, things no one needed. They took them away and raised three hundred in silver, and that got her through with a present that saved her face. As for me, you know: that gold striking clock sold for five hundred and sixty taels. In less than a fortnight there were a dozen things, great and small, to swallow it up. Now they’ve run short outside too, and somebody has had the bright idea of rummaging among Grandmother’s things. Give it another year and we shall all be hunting through our jewellery and clothes. That will be a fine state of affairs!’ ‘Any one of the ladies could sell her jewellery and clothes and have enough to live on for the rest of her life,’ Wang-er’s wife said, smiling. ‘They simply won’t.’ ‘I don’t like to say I’m beaten,’ said Xi-feng, ‘but if this is how it’s going to be, I can’t go on.

‘I suddenly had a dream last night. It’s laughable, really. Someone came looking for me, a person whose face I knew, though I couldn’t put a name to it. I asked what he wanted, and he said Her Highness had sent him for a hundred bolts of brocade. I asked which Highness he meant, and the name he gave wasn’t our own. So I refused to let him have it, and he came up and tried to snatch it away. I woke in the middle of the struggle.’ ‘That comes of all the worrying you do by day, ma’am,’ said Wang-er’s wife with a smile, ‘always having to see to things for the palace.’ Before she had finished, someone announced, ‘Eunuch Xia has sent a young eunuch with a message.’ Jia Lian frowned. ‘What is it now? Surely they’ve carried off enough this year.’ ‘Go and hide,’ said Xi-feng. ‘I’ll see him. If it’s something small, let it pass. If it’s anything large, I’ll find an answer for him.’ Jia Lian withdrew into the inner room. Xi-feng had the young eunuch brought in, invited him to sit on a chair and drink tea, and asked what had brought him.

‘Master Xia happened to see a house today,’ the young eunuch said, ‘and he’s just two hundred taels short. He sent me to ask whether you have any ready silver at home, ma’am, and could lend him a hundred or two for the moment. He’ll send it over in a day or two.’

‘What do you mean, send it over?’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘There’s plenty of silver. Have it weighed out and take it away. When we run short another day, we can borrow from him. It comes to the same thing.’ ‘Master Xia also said there are twelve hundred taels from the last two occasions that he hasn’t sent back yet. By the end of this year he’ll certainly send it all together.’ ‘How petty your Master Xia is! As if that were worth keeping in mind. I’ll say something, and I hope he won’t take it amiss: if they all remembered so carefully and paid us back, who knows how much we’d have had back by now? The only question is whether we have it. If we do, he’s welcome to it.’ She called Wang-er’s wife. ‘Go out and draw two hundred taels from somewhere, wherever you can.’ Understanding her, Wang-er’s wife smiled. ‘It was because I couldn’t draw any elsewhere that I came to ask you, ma’am.’ ‘You only know how to come in here for money. Ask you to find it outside, and you can’t do it.’ She called Patience. ‘Take out those two gold neck-rings of mine and pawn them for four hundred taels for the time being.’ Patience answered and went away. After some time she came back with a brocade box containing two brocade-wrapped bundles.

When they were opened, one neck-ring proved to be of gold filigree set with pearls, each pearl as large as a lotus seed; the other was decorated with kingfisher-feather work and set with gems. Both could well have stood beside anything in the palace. They were taken away, and before long four hundred taels were indeed brought back. Xi-feng had half packed up for the young eunuch and the other half given to Wang-er’s wife, telling her to use it for the Mid-Autumn festival in the eighth month. The young eunuch took his leave. Xi-feng had someone carry the silver for him and escort him out through the main gate. Jia Lian emerged with a smile. ‘When will we ever be rid of these outside demons?’

‘We’d only just spoken of them, and in came another,’ Xi-feng said, smiling. ‘Eunuch Zhou came yesterday and opened his mouth for a thousand taels. I was a little slow to answer and he was put out at once. There’ll be no end of people to offend before we’re done. If only we could come into another two or three million now!’ While they talked, Patience helped Xi-feng wash her face afresh and change her clothes to go and attend Grandmother Jia at the evening meal.

Jia Lian went out. He had just reached the outer study when Lin Zhi-xiao came up. Jia Lian asked what he wanted. ‘I’ve just heard that Yu-cun has been demoted, though I don’t know what for. It may not be true.’ ‘True or not, he may not keep his post for long,’ Jia Lian said. ‘If he gets into trouble, there’s a chance he’ll drag us into it. Better keep our distance.’ ‘Quite so, sir. Only it won’t be easy to do that at once.

‘The master at the Ning mansion is closer to him than ever, and His Lordship likes him too. They’re always visiting each other. Everyone knows it.’ ‘Well, as long as we don’t join him in any schemes, it needn’t concern us. Go and find out whether it’s true and what it’s about.’ Lin Zhi-xiao agreed, but did not leave. Sitting in a chair lower down the room, he chatted for a while. The conversation turned again to the household’s financial difficulties, and he took the opportunity to say, ‘There are too many mouths to feed. Why not choose a quiet day and explain to Her Old Ladyship and His Lordship? Some of the old servants have done their share and are no longer needed. A few households could be allowed to leave. They could earn their own living, and we should save on food and wages every year. There are too many girls inside as well. As the saying goes, “One time is not another.” We can’t talk about the old scale now. Everyone will have to put up with a little less. Those who ought to have eight can make do with six, and those who ought to have four with two. Added up over all the rooms, it would save a good deal in rice and wages every year.

‘Besides, half the girls inside are too old as it is. Those who ought to marry should be married off. Once they have households of their own, won’t they produce more people?’ ‘I’ve been thinking the same thing,’ said Jia Lian. ‘Only His Lordship has just come home, and there are any number of important matters we haven’t reported yet. We’ve hardly come to this. The other day the official matchmaker brought a card with birth particulars to propose a marriage. Her Ladyship said His Lordship had only just returned and was talking happily every day about having the whole family together again. If we suddenly brought this up, it might upset him, so for the moment we were not to mention it.’ ‘That’s reasonable enough. Her Ladyship has thought of everything.’ ‘Yes. Speaking of marriages reminds me of something. Wang-er’s boy wants Cai-xia, from Her Ladyship’s rooms. Wang-er asked me about it yesterday. I thought it was a simple enough matter: anyone could go and say a word. Who’s free just now? I’ll send someone over to say it’s my wish.’ Lin Zhi-xiao could only murmur assent. After a pause he smiled. ‘If you take my advice, sir, you’ll leave this one alone.

‘Young as Wang-er’s boy is, he drinks and gambles outside, and there’s nothing he won’t get up to. They may all be servants, but marriage is still for a lifetime. I haven’t seen Cai-xia these last few years, though I hear she’s grown into a fine girl. Why spoil someone’s life for nothing?’ ‘His boy drinks, does he? He’s no good?’ Lin Zhi-xiao gave a cold smile. ‘Drinking and gambling are only the beginning. There’s nothing he won’t do outside. Knowing he belongs to the mistress, we see half of it and turn a blind eye to the rest.’ ‘I knew nothing of all this,’ said Jia Lian. ‘If that’s the way it is, why should we find him a wife? Give him a beating first, lock him up, then send for his father and mother to answer for him.’ ‘There’s no need to do it this very moment,’ Lin Zhi-xiao said with a smile. ‘Whatever he’s done, let us wait till he makes trouble again. We shall naturally report it for you to punish him. Let him off for now.’ Jia Lian said nothing, and presently Lin Zhi-xiao went out.

That evening Xi-feng had already sent for Cai-xia’s mother and spoken to her about the marriage. Unwilling as the woman was in her heart, it was such an honour for Xi-feng to speak to her in person that she went away full of promises, agreeing against her own wishes. Xi-feng now asked Jia Lian whether he had spoken. ‘I meant to,’ he said, ‘but I heard the boy was thoroughly worthless, so I haven’t said anything yet. If he really is no good, they ought to discipline him for a while. There’ll be time enough to get him a wife afterwards.’ ‘Who told you he was worthless?’ ‘People in the household. Who else?’ Xi-feng smiled. ‘You don’t even like me, let alone any servants from our Wang family. I’ve just spoken to her mother, and she agreed with the greatest delight. Are we to call her in again and say we don’t want her daughter after all?’ ‘Since you’ve spoken, there’s no need to go back on it,’ said Jia Lian. ‘Just tell his father tomorrow to keep him properly in hand.’ We may leave their conversation there.

Now Cai-xia, sent home the other day to wait while her parents chose a husband, had an old attachment to Jia Huan, but nothing had been settled. Seeing Wang-er’s repeated attempts to secure the marriage, and having long heard that his son drank and gambled, was ugly and had not a single useful skill, she grew more and more distressed. She was afraid Wang-er would use Xi-feng’s influence to settle it all in a moment and condemn her to lifelong misery. Her anxiety became unbearable. That evening she secretly sent her younger sister Xiao-xia through the inner gate to find Aunt Zhao and ask where matters stood.

Aunt Zhao had always been on excellent terms with Cai-xia and had been eager for Jia Huan to have her, so that she herself would gain a helper. She had never expected Lady Wang to send her away. Again and again she urged Jia Huan to ask for her. But he was too embarrassed to speak, and did not particularly care besides. She was only a maid; if she went, there would naturally be another later. He put it off, said nothing and let the idea drop. Aunt Zhao could not bear to give her up. Now that Cai-xia’s younger sister had come to ask, she found an opportunity that evening and first approached Jia Zheng herself. ‘What is the hurry?’ Jia Zheng said. ‘Let them study for another year or two. It won’t be too late to give them girls then. I’ve already picked out two maids, one for Bao-yu and one for Huan. Only the boys are still young, and I’m afraid it would interfere with their studies, so we shall wait another year or two.’ ‘Bao-yu’s had one for two years already. Didn’t you know, sir?’ ‘Who gave her to him?’ Jia Zheng asked at once. Aunt Zhao was just about to answer when a crash came from outside. No one knew what it was, and they were all badly startled.
