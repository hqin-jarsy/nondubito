Chapter 62 Guileless Xiang-yun sleeps drunk on a bed of peonies; Innocent Caltrop unfastens a pomegranate-red skirt in a tender exchange

Chapter end

Patience came out and gave Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife her instructions.

‘A flourishing household makes large troubles small and small troubles disappear. It’s no way to carry on, making a great to-do with bells and drums over every little thing you can’t put up with. Take mother and daughter back now and let them go on with their duties as before. Send Qin Xian’s wife back to her old place. We needn’t hear any more about this. What does matter is that you keep a careful watch every day.’

With that she got up and left. Mrs Liu and her daughter hastily kowtowed towards her. Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife took them back into the Garden and reported to Li Wan and Tan-chun.

‘We understand,’ they both said. ‘The fewer troubles the better. That’s very good.’

Chess and the others had had all their excitement for nothing. Qin Xian’s wife, who had waited so long for an opening to squeeze into, had enjoyed her triumph for no more than half a day. She was still in a bustle in the kitchen, taking over the utensils, rice, charcoal and other stores, when she discovered all sorts of shortages.

‘There are two shi of polished rice missing, and a whole extra month’s ordinary rice has been issued. The charcoal’s short of its quota too.’

Meanwhile she had been preparing a present for Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife. Quietly assembling a basket of charcoal, five hundred catties of firewood and a load of polished rice, she had sent sons and nephews from outside to deliver them to the Lin household. She was preparing another gift for the accounts office, and arranging several dishes with which to entertain some of her fellow servants.

‘Now I’m here, I shall depend on all of you. We’re one family from now on. If there’s anything I overlook, do please see to it for me.’

In the midst of these preparations someone came to tell her:

‘You can leave once you’ve seen to breakfast. There was nothing against Mrs Liu after all, and she’s to have charge again.’

The news knocked the spirit out of Qin Xian’s wife. Crestfallen, she struck her colours at once, silenced her drums, rolled up her bundle and left. Much of what she had given away was simply lost; worse, she would have to sell her own belongings to make good the shortages. Even Chess was nearly bowled over with rage. But there was no recovering the position, and they had to let it go.

Ever since Silver had made an outcry over the many things Cai-yun had secretly given away, Aunt Zhao had been terrified that an investigation would bring the truth to light. Every day she listened anxiously for news. Then Cai-yun came to tell her:

‘Bao-yu has taken it all on himself. There’ll be no more trouble.’

Only then could Aunt Zhao breathe again. Jia Huan, however, became suspicious as soon as he heard this. He brought out everything Cai-yun had ever given him in secret and flung it at her face.

‘You two-faced creature! I don’t want your things. If you weren’t carrying on with Bao-yu, why would he take the blame for you? If you had the courage to give them to me, you ought not to have let a single other person know. Now you’ve told him, what pleasure is there in my keeping them?’

In her distress Cai-yun called down curses on herself to prove her innocence, and at last burst into tears. She tried every explanation, but Jia Huan obstinately refused to believe her.

‘If it weren’t for what there’s been between us, I’d go and tell Cousin Feng you stole them for me and I was afraid to take them. Think that over.’

He flung out his arm and left. Aunt Zhao cursed him in exasperation.

‘You wretched, ill-fated brat! You maggot-hearted little sinner!’

Cai-yun wept until her tears were spent and her heart seemed torn apart. Aunt Zhao did everything she could to comfort her.

‘My good girl, he’s repaid your love badly. I can see that plainly enough. Let me put these away. He’ll come round of his own accord in a day or two.’

She began to gather the things up, but Cai-yun angrily bundled them together herself. When no one was looking she went into the Garden and threw the whole lot into the stream. Some sank; some floated away with the current. That night, bitterly hurt, she cried in secret under her quilt.

Now Bao-yu’s birthday had come round. Bao-qin’s, as it happened, fell on the same day. Since Lady Wang was away, there was none of the usual bustle of celebration. Daoist Zhang sent gifts of four kinds and a replacement talisman for the name under which Bao-yu was registered with him. Monks and nuns from several other temples sent choice portions from their offerings, a paper image of the God of Longevity, written prayers, and the annually renewed protective locks for his natal star and the Tai Sui presiding over the year. The women storytellers who regularly visited the house came to offer their birthday wishes. From Wang Zi-teng’s household came the customary set of clothes, a pair of shoes and stockings, a hundred birthday peaches and a hundred bundles of the finest silver-thread noodles, of the grade supplied to the court. Aunt Xue’s gift was on a slightly smaller scale. Of the others in the family, You-shi sent her usual pair of shoes and stockings. Xi-feng gave him a palace-made ‘Fourfold Harmony’ purse, containing a gold figure of the God of Longevity and a toy made in Persia. People were sent to the various temples to distribute alms to the assembled clergy. There were separate gifts for Bao-qin too, too many to enumerate. The girls gave whatever they pleased: a fan, a piece of calligraphy, a painting or a poem, simply to mark the occasion.

That morning Bao-yu rose early, washed, dressed his hair and came out in formal cap and sash. In the courtyard before the front hall, Li Gui and the other men, four or five in all, had already set out incense and candles for Heaven and Earth. Bao-yu lit the incense, performed his obeisances, poured the tea libation and burned the paper offerings. He then went over to the Ning mansion and paid his respects in both the clan shrine and the ancestral hall. Coming out onto the terrace, he bowed from afar towards Grandmother Jia, Jia Zheng, Lady Wang and the others. On his way back he called at You-shi’s principal room, saluted her and sat for a while before returning to the Rong mansion. He went first to Aunt Xue, who repeatedly detained him; then he met Xue Ke, and after another exchange of invitations and courtesies he finally entered the Garden.

Skybright and Musk accompanied him, with a little maid carrying a felt kneeling-mat. Beginning with Li Wan, he went in turn to the rooms of everyone senior to him. Then he went out through the inner gate again to pay his respects and extend his invitations at the homes of his four nurses, the Li, Zhao, Zhang and Wang women, before returning inside. People tried to make their obeisances to him, but he accepted none. Back in his own rooms, Aroma and the others merely spoke their good wishes. Lady Wang had forbidden the young people to receive formal obeisances, for fear of diminishing their store of good fortune and long life, so no one kowtowed.

After he had rested a little, Jia Huan and Jia Lan came with the others. Aroma hastily stopped them from kneeling. They sat a short while and went away. Bao-yu said with a smile that all the walking had tired him, and stretched out on the bed. He had scarcely drunk half a cup of tea when a clatter of voices outside announced a whole laughing troop of maids: Ebony, Xiao-luo, Kingfisher, Picture, Xing Xiu-yan’s maid Zhuan-er, a nurse carrying Qiao-jie, Cai-luan and Xiu-luan, eight or nine of them, coming in with red felt mats in their arms.

‘The birthday visitors are crowding your door off its hinges! Hurry up and give us some noodles!’

They had barely entered when Tan-chun, Xiang-yun, Bao-qin, Xiu-yan and Xi-chun arrived too. Bao-yu hurried out to meet them.

‘You shouldn’t have troubled to come! Quickly, have some good tea ready!’

Once inside there was the inevitable exchange of courtesies before everyone sat down. Aroma and the others brought in tea. They had taken only a sip when Patience arrived, dressed as brightly as a flowering branch. Bao-yu hurried out again.

‘I called at Cousin Feng’s door just now and sent in word, but couldn’t see her. Then I sent someone in with an invitation for you too.’

‘I was helping your cousin with her hair,’ Patience said, smiling, ‘and couldn’t come out to answer you. Afterwards, when I heard you’d sent specially to invite me as well, I felt quite unworthy of the honour. So I hurried over to kowtow.’

‘I’m not worthy of that either,’ said Bao-yu.

Aroma had already placed a seat for her in the outer room and invited her to sit. Patience sank into a curtsey, and Bao-yu repeatedly bowed with his hands clasped. She then knelt; he hastily knelt in return, and Aroma hurried to help them up. Patience curtseyed once more, and Bao-yu answered with another bow.

‘Bow again,’ Aroma said, laughing and giving him a push.

‘We’ve finished. Why should I bow again?’

‘Those were her birthday wishes to you.

‘It’s her birthday today too. You ought to wish her a happy birthday.’

Delighted, Bao-yu bowed at once.

‘So this is your birthday too, dear sister!’

Patience kept returning his salutations. Xiang-yun caught Bao-qin and Xiu-yan by the hand.

‘The four of you ought to bow birthday wishes to one another all day long!’

‘Is it your birthday too, Cousin Xing?’ Tan-chun asked at once. ‘How could I have forgotten?’

She immediately told a maid:

‘Go and tell Mrs Lian to make up another birthday present at once, the same as Miss Qin’s, and send it to Miss Ying-chun’s rooms.’

The maid went off as instructed. Since Xiang-yun had blurted it out, Xiu-yan could hardly avoid going round the various rooms to extend her own invitations.

‘It is rather amusing,’ Tan-chun said with a smile. ‘Twelve months in a year, and several birthdays every month. With so many of us, these coincidences happen: three people on one day, two on another. Even New Year’s Day isn’t left empty. Our eldest sister has claimed that. No wonder she has such good fortune. Even her birthday comes before everyone else’s. And it was our honoured ancestor’s birthday too.

‘After the Lantern Festival come Grandmother’s and Cousin Bao’s: a happy coincidence for the two of them, old and young. Mother’s is the first of the third month, and Cousin Lian’s the ninth. There’s no one in the second month.’

‘Miss Lin’s is the twelfth of the second month,’ said Aroma. ‘How can you say there’s no one? Though she isn’t one of this family.’

‘What is happening to my memory?’ Tan-chun said, laughing.

‘She and Cousin Lin share the same day,’ Bao-yu said, pointing at Aroma with a smile. ‘That’s why she remembers.’

‘So you two share a birthday too!’ said Tan-chun. ‘And never so much as a birthday kowtow to us from you. We didn’t know Patience’s birthday either. We’ve only just found that out.’

‘People on our sort of register have no right to receive presents or be honoured with birthday greetings,’ Patience said with a smile. ‘What would we have to make a fuss about? We let the day go by quietly. Now she’s gone and made it public. When you young ladies are back in your rooms, I’ll come and pay my respects.’

‘We couldn’t trouble you to do that,’ Tan-chun said. ‘But I shan’t feel easy unless we celebrate your birthday for you today.’

‘Quite right!’ Bao-yu, Xiang-yun and the others said together.

Tan-chun gave a maid her instructions.

‘Go and tell her mistress that we’ve all decided not to let Patience out of here all day. We’re clubbing together to celebrate her birthday.’

The maid went off laughing. After a while she returned.

‘Mrs Lian says to thank the young ladies for doing Patience such an honour.

‘She wonders what you’ll give her to eat for her birthday. As long as you don’t forget Mrs Lian, she won’t come and bother her.’

They all laughed.

‘It happens the inner kitchen isn’t preparing any meals today,’ said Tan-chun. ‘All the noodles and dishes are being done outside. Why don’t we put some money together and have Mrs Liu take it on? She can do it here in our own kitchen.’

Everyone thought this an excellent plan. Tan-chun sent to ask Li Wan, Bao-chai and Dai-yu, and also sent for Mrs Liu, whom she told to prepare two tables of food and wine immediately in the inner kitchen. Mrs Liu did not understand.

‘But it’s all being prepared in the outer kitchen.’

‘So you don’t know,’ Tan-chun said, smiling. ‘Today is Miss Patience’s birthday. The feast they’re preparing outside is for the family. We’ve privately subscribed a little more for two tables expressly to entertain Miss Patience. Choose some fresh, ingenious dishes and bring them here. Make out your bill and collect the money from me.’

‘So today is Miss Patience’s birthday too!’ Mrs Liu said with a smile. ‘I had no idea.’

She dropped to her knees to kowtow to Patience, who hurriedly pulled her up. Mrs Liu then hastened away to prepare the feast.

Tan-chun invited Bao-yu to go with her to the hall for noodles. Once Li Wan and Bao-chai had both arrived, someone was sent to invite Aunt Xue and Dai-yu. The warm weather had helped Dai-yu’s illness abate, so she came too. The hall was crowded with people, bright as clusters of flowers and rich brocade.

Then Xue Ke sent Bao-yu four kinds of birthday gifts: kerchiefs, fans, incense and silk. Bao-yu accordingly went over to keep him company over the noodles. Both households had prepared birthday feasts, and each sent the other a share, which was duly accepted. At noon Bao-yu drank a couple of cups with Xue Ke as well. Bao-chai brought Bao-qin over to pay her respects to her brother. When the ceremonial cups had been offered, Bao-chai told him:

‘There’s no need to send any of our feast over there. We can put an end to these empty formalities. Just invite the shop assistants to eat it. Bao-yu and I have people to entertain inside, so we can’t stay with you.’

‘Please go, both of you,’ Xue Ke said at once. ‘The assistants will probably be here soon anyway.’

Bao-yu offered another apology for leaving, then went back with the two girls.

As soon as they were through the side gate, Bao-chai ordered an older woman to lock it and give her the key, which she kept herself.

‘Why lock this gate?’ Bao-yu protested. ‘Hardly anyone comes this way. Besides, Aunt and all you girls are inside. If you want to fetch anything from home, won’t it be a nuisance?’

‘You can’t be too careful,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘Look at all the things that have been happening on your side these last few days. Not one of our people was involved. That shows what good keeping this gate locked has done. If it were open, some of those people would be sure to take a short cut through here for their own convenience. Who could you stop?

‘Better to lock it and restrict Mother and me as well. Nobody comes through. Then even if something does happen, no one can blame the people on our side.’

‘So you know about things disappearing from our side lately?’ Bao-yu asked with a smile.

‘All you know about is the rose essence and the fuling frost. You came to hear of the things because you were concerned about the people. Otherwise you wouldn’t even know about those two. There are several other matters far more serious. If they don’t get unearthed later, it will be everyone’s good fortune. If they do, there’s no knowing how many people will be dragged into them. I’m only telling you because you have nothing to do with running things. Patience is a sensible girl. I told her the other day too. Her mistress hasn’t been out and about, so I thought Patience should understand. If nothing comes out, everyone can be glad to leave it alone. If it does, she’ll already have some idea of how to proceed, and innocent people won’t be wronged. Just listen to me and be careful from now on. You mustn’t repeat this to anyone.’

As she spoke they came to Drenched Blossoms Pavilion. Aroma, Caltrop, Scribe, Su-yun, Skybright, Musk, Parfumée, Rui-guan, Ou-guan and several others were there, amusing themselves by watching the fish.

‘Everything’s ready by the peonies,’ they said on seeing them. ‘Hurry along to the feast!’

Bao-chai and the others took them along to the little three-bay open hall in Red Fragrance Court, among the peony beds. Even You-shi had been invited over. Everyone was there except Patience.

Patience had gone outside to deal with birthday gifts from the Lai and Lin households and others. They kept arriving one after another. A good many servants from all three ranks, upper, middle and lower, came with gifts and congratulations, keeping her busy with tips and thanks. At the same time she reported each kind of gift to Xi-feng. Only a few were kept; some were declined, and some accepted and immediately given away. When all this business was done, she waited for Xi-feng to finish her noodles before changing her clothes and setting off for the Garden.

She had scarcely entered when several maids came to look for her and accompanied her to Red Fragrance Court. There the feast was spread in tortoiseshell splendour, the cushions flowered with lotus.

‘Now all our birthday stars are here!’ everyone cried, laughing.

They insisted that the four celebrants should take the four places of honour, but all four refused.

‘I’m getting on in years,’ said Aunt Xue, ‘and I don’t belong in your young company. It makes me feel constrained. I’d be much happier lying down comfortably in the hall. I can’t eat much, and I scarcely drink. They’ll be better off with my place here.’

You-shi and the others would not hear of it.

‘Why not let Mother be comfortable in the hall?’ Bao-chai said. ‘We can send her anything she likes to eat, and she’ll be more at her ease. Besides, there’s nobody in front just now. She can keep an eye on things there.’

‘In that case,’ Tan-chun and the others said, smiling, ‘we shall show our respect by doing as you wish.’

They all escorted her to the council hall and stayed while the maids spread a brocade mattress and arranged backrests and cushions. They also gave them strict instructions.

‘Massage Aunt’s legs properly. If she wants tea or water, don’t keep passing the job from one to another. When the food comes, she can give you what she doesn’t want after she’s eaten. Only don’t leave her and go wandering off.’

The little maids all promised to obey.

Only then did Tan-chun and the others return. In the end Bao-qin and Xiu-yan were persuaded to take the places at the head, while Patience faced west and Bao-yu east. Tan-chun had sent for Faithful too, and the two of them sat side by side opposite the pair at the head to keep them company. At the table to the west were Bao-chai, Dai-yu, Xiang-yun, Ying-chun and Xi-chun; they drew Caltrop and Silver in to sit across the end. At the third table You-shi and Li Wan had Aroma and Cai-yun join them. Around the fourth sat Nightingale, Oriole, Skybright, Xiao-luo, Chess and the others.

Tan-chun and the others were still for formally offering birthday cups, but Bao-qin and her three fellow celebrants protested.

‘At this rate we shan’t get a chance to sit down all day!’

Only then did they desist. The two women storytellers offered to perform a sung tale in honour of the birthdays.

‘None of us wants to hear those fanciful stories,’ everyone said. ‘Go and tell them to Aunt in the hall to amuse her.’

They also chose an assortment of food and sent it over to Aunt Xue.

‘Sitting here looking elegant is dull,’ Bao-yu said. ‘We must have a drinking game.’

One person suggested this game, another that.

‘Why not get a brush and inkstone,’ Dai-yu said, ‘write them all down and make them into lots? Whichever we draw is the one we play.’

Everyone thought it an excellent idea. A brush, inkstone and flower-patterned paper were brought. Caltrop, who had lately been learning poetry and now practised her writing every day, could not resist the sight of writing materials. She jumped up.

‘Let me write!’

Between them they thought of ten or so games. As they called them out, Caltrop wrote them down one by one, rolled the slips into lots and dropped them into a vase. Tan-chun told Patience to choose. Patience stirred them round, lifted one out with her chopsticks and opened it. It said ‘The Concealed-Word Game’.

‘You’ve drawn the ancestor of all drinking games,’ Bao-chai said, laughing. ‘The game goes back to antiquity, but the old way of playing it has been lost. This version was made up by later generations, and it’s harder than all the others.

‘Half the people here don’t know how to play it. Why not put it aside and draw another that everyone can enjoy, learned or otherwise?’

‘We’ve drawn it now,’ Tan-chun said with a smile. ‘How can we throw it away? Let’s draw one more. If it’s something everyone can enjoy, they can play that while we play this.’

She had Aroma draw another slip. This time it was ‘Finger-Guessing’.

‘That’s brisk and straightforward,’ Xiang-yun said, laughing. ‘Just my sort of thing! I’m not playing your concealed-word game, hanging my head over some depressing puzzle. I’m going to guess fingers.’

‘She’s the only one who ever disrupts a game,’ said Tan-chun. ‘Cousin Bao, fine her a cup at once.’

Bao-chai would hear no excuses, and made Xiang-yun drink.

‘I’ll drink a cup myself,’ said Tan-chun. ‘I’m mistress of the games. There’s no need for a proclamation. Just do as I tell you.’

She had the gaming dice and dish brought in.

‘Bao-qin throws first, then everyone in turn. Two people with matching throws play the concealed-word game.’

Bao-qin threw a three. Neither Xiu-yan nor Bao-yu nor the others matched it, until Caltrop threw a three as well.

‘We must keep our springtime within these four walls,’ Bao-qin said, smiling. ‘If we start going outside, there’ll be far too much to choose from.’

‘Of course,’ said Tan-chun. ‘Three wrong guesses cost one cup. You give the clue and she guesses.’

Bao-qin thought for a moment, then said, ‘Old.’

Caltrop was new to the game and could think of nothing. All round the room and on the tables she could see nothing that would combine with ‘old’ in a familiar phrase. Xiang-yun had heard the clue too and was looking everywhere. Suddenly she noticed the words ‘Red Fragrance Court’ above the doorway. She knew then that Bao-qin was concealing the inscription’s last word, pu, a garden or gardener, from ‘I am no match for an old gardener.’ Caltrop still could not guess, and the others were drumming to hurry her. Xiang-yun quietly tugged her and whispered that she should say, ‘Medicinal herb.’

Dai-yu happened to see.

‘Fine her! She’s passing answers again!’

Her outcry let everyone into the secret, and Xiang-yun was promptly fined another cup. She was so vexed that she tapped Dai-yu’s hand with her chopsticks. Caltrop was then fined a cup too.

Next Bao-chai and Tan-chun threw matching numbers. Tan-chun offered ‘Man’ as her clue.

‘“Man” covers rather a lot,’ Bao-chai said with a smile.

‘I’ll add a word,’ said Tan-chun. ‘Two clues for one answer won’t be so vague.’

She added, ‘Window.’

Bao-chai considered. Seeing chicken on the table, she guessed that Tan-chun was alluding to the ‘cock window’ and the ‘cock man’, and gave ‘Wall-roost’ as her answer. Tan-chun knew that she had solved it by alluding to ‘The fowls roost in their wall-roosts.’ They smiled at each other and each took the customary sip.

Xiang-yun had not been able to wait. She and Bao-yu were already playing finger-guessing, shouting ‘Three!’ and ‘Five!’ in a jumble. Across the tables You-shi and Faithful were calling ‘Seven!’ and ‘Eight!’ as they played. Patience and Aroma had paired off too, their bracelets clinking and jingling on their wrists. Presently Xiang-yun beat Bao-yu, Aroma beat Patience, and You-shi beat Faithful. The three winners set their conditions for a drinking prelude and a drinking coda. Xiang-yun declared:

‘The prelude must have one phrase of classical prose, one line of old poetry, the name of a domino combination, the title of a tune, and a phrase from the official almanac, all joined into one connected utterance. The coda must name a fruit or vegetable and relate it to human affairs.’

‘Even her drinking rules are wordier than anyone else’s,’ they all said, laughing. ‘But it is a good idea.’

They urged Bao-yu to begin.

‘Who’s ever heard of such a game?’ he said with a smile. ‘At least give me time to think.’

‘Drink one extra cup and I’ll do it for you,’ Dai-yu said.

Bao-yu really did drink, then listened as Dai-yu began:

‘“Sunset clouds and a lone duck fly together”; “In the river sky’s fierce wind, wild geese pass crying mournfully.” Yet it is “A Goose with a Broken Leg”, Whose cries set “The Heart Turning Nine Times”: Here are “Wild Geese Arriving as Guests”.’

Everyone laughed.

‘That’s a clever string of things!’

Dai-yu picked up a hazelnut kernel and gave her coda:

‘This zhen, a hazelnut, is no fulling-block zhen across the court;

Why, then, the sound of beating clothes in ten thousand homes?’

When she had finished, Faithful, Aroma and the others all used common sayings containing the word ‘longevity’. We need not give them all here.

After another round of indiscriminate finger-guessing, Xiang-yun found herself paired with Bao-qin, while Li Wan and Xiu-yan threw matching numbers. Li Wan gave ‘Gourd-ladle’ as her clue; Xiu-yan answered ‘Green’. Each understood the other, and both took a sip. Xiang-yun, meanwhile, lost her bout of finger-guessing and was called upon for her prelude and coda.

‘Pray step into your own jar,’ Bao-qin said with a smile.

The others laughed.

‘That’s an apt allusion!’

Xiang-yun began:

‘“Surging and crashing”, “The waves amid the river mount to heaven”: We need “An Iron Chain to Moor a Lonely Boat”. Since we have met “A Riverful of Wind”, This is “No Day for Travelling”.’

Everyone laughed again.

‘What a side-splitting concoction! No wonder she thought up this game. She meant to make us laugh.’

They waited for her coda. Xiang-yun drank her wine and took a piece of duck to follow it. Then she noticed half a duck’s head in the bowl, picked it out and began eating the brains.

‘Don’t just go on eating,’ they urged her. ‘Come on, give us the rest!’

Holding it up in her chopsticks, Xiang-yun said:

‘This ya-tou, a duck’s head, is no ya-tou, a maid; Where on its head would you find osmanthus oil?’

Their laughter grew louder still, bringing Skybright, Xiao-luo, Oriole and a whole group of maids over.

‘Miss Yun certainly knows how to amuse herself, making fun of us! She must drink a penalty cup. Why should we be the ones to wear osmanthus hair oil? You ought to give each of us a bottle to use.’

‘He would like to give you a bottle of oil,’ Dai-yu said with a smile, ‘only he’s afraid of being dragged into a theft case.’

The others paid no attention, but Bao-yu understood and quickly lowered his head. Cai-yun’s guilty conscience made her blush despite herself. Bao-chai hastily shot Dai-yu a private glance.

Dai-yu regretted her slip. She had meant to tease Bao-yu and had forgotten that her teasing would strike Cai-yun too. Too late to take it back, she hurriedly diverted everyone with a bustle of drinking games and finger-guessing.

Next Bao-yu happened to throw the same number as Bao-chai. Bao-chai gave ‘Precious’ as her clue. Bao-yu thought for a moment and realised that she was making a playful reference to the jade of spiritual understanding which he wore.

‘You’re making me the subject of a literary joke,’ he said, smiling, ‘but I’ve guessed it. Don’t be angry when I say it. The answer is the chai, “hairpin”, in your own name.’

‘How does that work?’ everyone asked.

‘She says “precious”, bao, and the word to follow it must be “jade”, yu,’ Bao-yu explained. ‘My answer is “hairpin”, chai, because an old poem says, “The jade hairpin is snapped; the red candle is cold.” Doesn’t that give the answer?’

‘You can’t use present-day matters,’ Xiang-yun objected. ‘They should both be fined.’

‘It isn’t only a present-day matter,’ Caltrop put in eagerly. ‘There’s a literary source as well.’

‘There’s no source for “precious jade”,’ said Xiang-yun. ‘You might find it in a New Year’s couplet, but it isn’t in poetry or the classics. That doesn’t count.’

‘The other day I was reading a five-character regulated poem by Cen Jia-zhou,’ Caltrop said, ‘and there was the line, “This land abounds in precious jade.” How have you forgotten it? Afterwards I read a seven-character quatrain by Li Yi-shan with the line, “No day but dust collects upon the jewelled hairpin.” I even laughed about discovering that both their names were in Tang poetry.’

‘That’s stopped you!’ everyone laughed. ‘Drink up!’

Xiang-yun had no answer and had to drink. The dice-throwers went back to matching their throws and the finger-guessers to their game. With Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang away, there was no one to hold them in check. They enjoyed themselves as freely as they pleased, shouting threes and fours, sevens and eights. Throughout the hall red and green fluttered, jade swung and pearls quivered. It was a thoroughly lively scene.

After playing a while they rose from the tables and scattered a little. Suddenly Xiang-yun was nowhere to be seen. At first they assumed she had gone outside to relieve herself and would soon return. But they waited and waited without a sign of her. People were sent to search everywhere, and still she could not be found.

Then Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife arrived with several older women. They were afraid there might be some business needing attention. They were also afraid that the young maids, with Lady Wang away, might disregard the authority of Tan-chun and the others, drink to excess and abandon all decorum. They had come to ask whether anything was needed. Tan-chun understood their purpose as soon as she saw them.

‘You can’t trust us, can you?’ she said with a smile. ‘You’ve come to inspect us. We haven’t had much wine. We’re only amusing ourselves, using it as an excuse for games. Don’t worry, mammas.’

‘Go and rest,’ Li Wan and You-shi added, smiling. ‘We wouldn’t dare let them drink too much either.’

‘We know,’ Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife and the others replied with smiles. ‘Even when Her Old Ladyship tells the young ladies to drink, they won’t. With the mistresses away, of course it’s only a game. We thought there might be something to do and came to ask. Besides, the days are long now. When you’ve played a while, you ought to have a little something to eat. You don’t generally eat much between meals. If you have a cup or two of wine and don’t eat more to go with it, we’re afraid it might upset you.’

‘You’re quite right, mammas,’ Tan-chun said. ‘We were just wanting something.’

She turned and ordered refreshments.

The maids on either side answered and hurried to have the refreshments brought. Tan-chun smilingly urged the women to leave.

‘Go and rest, or go and talk to Aunt. We’ll send someone with wine for you in a moment.’

‘We wouldn’t presume to accept,’ Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife and the others replied with smiles.

They stood a little longer, then withdrew. Patience felt her face.

‘My face is quite hot,’ she said, laughing. ‘I’m ashamed to let them see me. I think we ought to clear it all away. It would be no fun having them come round again.’

‘Never mind,’ Tan-chun said with a smile. ‘So long as we don’t settle down to serious drinking.’

Just then a little maid came up, grinning.

‘Misses, come and look at Miss Yun! She’s drunk. She went to cool off and fell asleep on a blue-grey stone bench behind the rockery.’

‘Keep your voice down!’ they said, laughing, and all went to look.

Sure enough, Xiang-yun was lying on a stone seat in a secluded place among the rocks, deep in fragrant dreams. Peony petals had blown over her from every side, scattering red and scent across her hair, her face and the front of her clothes. Her fan lay on the ground, half buried in fallen flowers. A crowd of bees and butterflies bustled round her. She had also wrapped a bundle of peony petals in a fine silk kerchief and made it her pillow. The others watched her with mingled affection and amusement, then hurried forward to shake her gently, call her and help her up. But Xiang-yun was still murmuring the drinking game in her sleep:

‘“The spring is fragrant and the wine is clear”; “In a jade cup it shines with amber light.” Drink on till “The Moon Rises over the Plum-tips”, Be “Helped Home Drunk”: A day “Favourable for Meeting Friends and Kin”.’

They laughed and shook her.

‘Wake up and come to eat! You’ll make yourself ill sleeping on that damp bench.’

Xiang-yun slowly opened her limpid eyes. Seeing the others, she lowered her gaze to herself and only then realised that she was drunk. She had meant merely to cool off somewhere quiet, but the two extra penalty cups had been more than she could bear, and without knowing it she had fallen asleep. Now she felt ashamed. Hastily getting up, she struggled along with the others to Red Fragrance Court. There she washed and drank two cups of strong tea. Tan-chun promptly had the sobering stone brought for her to hold in her mouth, and after a while made her drink some sour soup as well. Only then did she feel a little better.

They chose several dishes of fruit and vegetables to send to Xi-feng, who sent some things back in return. After the refreshments, Bao-chai and the others made themselves comfortable as they pleased. Some sat, some stood; some went outside to look at the flowers, others leaned on the balustrade watching the fish. Everywhere there was talk and laughter. Tan-chun began a game of go with Bao-qin, while Bao-chai and Xiu-yan watched. Under a cluster of flowers, Dai-yu and Bao-yu were whispering together about no one knew what.

Presently Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife came in with a group of women, bringing a married servant with them. The woman looked thoroughly miserable. She did not dare enter the hall but stopped below the steps and knelt towards it, knocking her head audibly on the ground. A group of Tan-chun’s stones was under attack. She had calculated and recalculated until the group had its two eyes, then turned to a standard endgame move. Her own two eyes remained on the board, while one hand rummaged among the stones in their box as she considered. Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife had been standing there a long while before Tan-chun turned to ask for tea, noticed her and asked:

‘What is it?’

Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife pointed at the servant.

‘This is the mother of Cai-er, the little maid in Miss Xi-chun’s rooms. She works here in the Garden.

‘She has a very foul mouth. I heard her just now and questioned her. I wouldn’t dare repeat to you what she said. She really ought to be sent away.’

‘Why haven’t you reported it to the elder mistress?’ Tan-chun asked.

‘The elder mistress and the others have just gone over to Aunt’s in the hall. I met her on the way and told her everything. She told me to report to you.’

‘Why haven’t you reported it to Mrs Lian?’

‘There’s no need to go back just for that,’ Patience said. ‘I’ll tell her when I go.’

Tan-chun nodded.

‘In that case, send her out. When Her Ladyship returns, we’ll report it and let her decide.’

She went back to her game. Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife took the woman away, and we shall leave them there.

Standing under the flowers, Dai-yu and Bao-yu understood what was happening from a distance.

‘That third girl of yours is sensible,’ Dai-yu said. ‘They’ve given her some authority, but she won’t overstep it by so much as a pace. Most people would have been dispensing favours and punishments long ago.’

‘You don’t know the half of it,’ Bao-yu said. ‘She did several things while you were ill.

‘The Garden’s been divided up and put in people’s charge. Now you can’t even pick an extra blade of grass. She’s abolished several things too, always making examples of Cousin Feng and me to bring everyone else into line. She’s a great one for working things out. “Sensible” is hardly the word for it.’

‘That’s how it ought to be,’ said Dai-yu. ‘We spend far too much in this house. I don’t manage anything, but whenever I have a quiet moment I do a little reckoning for you. More goes out than comes in. Unless you start saving now, you won’t be able to keep it up.’

‘However short things get,’ Bao-yu said with a smile, ‘there’ll still be enough for the two of us.’

At this Dai-yu turned away and went into the hall to talk and laugh with Bao-chai.

Bao-yu was about to leave when Aroma came over carrying a small Western-lacquer tray in an interlocking-ring shape. Two cups of fresh tea fitted neatly inside it.

‘Where has she gone?’ she asked. ‘I noticed neither of you had had any tea for ages, so I specially poured two cups, and now she’s gone.’

‘There she is,’ Bao-yu said. ‘Take hers over to her.’

He took one cup himself. Aroma carried the other over, but Dai-yu was with Bao-chai, and there was only one cup.

‘Whichever of you is thirsty, please take this first. I’ll pour another.’

‘I’m not thirsty,’ Bao-chai said with a smile. ‘I only want a mouthful to rinse my mouth.’

She took it first, drank a mouthful and passed the remaining half-cup to Dai-yu.

‘I’ll pour another,’ Aroma said, smiling.

‘You know about my illness,’ said Dai-yu with a smile. ‘The doctor won’t let me drink much tea. Half a cup is quite enough. It was kind of you to remember us.’

She finished it and set the cup down.

Aroma went back to collect Bao-yu’s cup.

‘I haven’t seen Parfumée for ages,’ he said. ‘Where is she?’

Aroma looked around.

‘She was here just now playing at plant names with some of the others. I can’t see her now.’

Bao-yu hurried back to his rooms and found Parfumée asleep on the bed, facing the wall. He shook her gently.

‘Don’t sleep now. Come outside and play. It’ll soon be time to eat.’

‘You were all drinking and taking no notice of me,’ Parfumée said. ‘I was bored for ages. What else could I do but come back and sleep?’

Bao-yu pulled her up, smiling.

‘We’ll have another party here tonight. For now I’ll ask Sister Aroma to take you to eat at her table. How would that be?’

‘Ou-guan and Rui-guan aren’t going to sit there,’ Parfumée said. ‘It wouldn’t be nice for me to be the only one. I’m not used to those noodles either, and I hardly ate anything this morning. I was hungry just now, so I told Mrs Liu to make me a bowl of soup and send it over with half a bowl of polished rice. I’ll eat here and that will do. But if we’re drinking tonight, you mustn’t let anyone stop me. I mean to drink as much as I can. At home I used to drink two or three catties of good Huiquan wine. Since I started learning this wretched acting, they’ve said it would spoil my voice, and I haven’t even smelt it for years. Today I mean to break my fast.’

‘That’s easily arranged,’ said Bao-yu.

As he spoke, someone arrived from Mrs Liu with a food-box. Swallow took it and lifted the lid. Inside were a bowl of shrimp-ball and chicken-skin soup, a bowl of duck gently steamed with fermented rice, a dish of cured rouge-coloured goose breast, a plate with four cream-and-pine-kernel flaky rolls, and a large bowl of steaming fragrant rice from the green plots, its grains shining green.

Swallow set the food on the table, fetched small side dishes, bowls and chopsticks, and served a bowl of rice.

‘All that grease!’ Parfumée said. ‘Who could eat those things?’

She merely poured soup over her rice, ate the bowlful and picked out two pieces of cured goose, then stopped. To Bao-yu, however, the smell seemed even better than usual. He ate one of the flaky rolls, then had Swallow serve him half a bowl of rice too. With soup poured over it, it tasted wonderfully sweet and fragrant. Swallow and Parfumée both laughed.

When he had finished, Swallow was going to send the rest back.

‘You eat it,’ Bao-yu said. ‘If there isn’t enough, ask for some more.’

‘There’s no need. This is plenty. Sister Musk gave us two plates of cakes just now. Once I’ve eaten this, I shan’t need any more at all.’

Standing by the table, she ate the food, but saved two of the flaky rolls.

‘I’ll keep these for my mother. If there’s wine tonight, just give me two bowls of that.’

‘Do you like wine too?’ Bao-yu asked with a smile.

‘Wait till tonight and we’ll all have a good drink. Sister Aroma and Sister Skybright can hold their wine too, and they like it, only they’re usually too shy to let on. Today we’ll all break our fast. There’s something else I meant to tell you. I’d forgotten it completely, and only just remembered. From now on I want you to look after Parfumée in everything. If she goes wrong anywhere, remind her. Aroma can’t attend to so many people.’

‘I know,’ said Swallow. ‘You needn’t worry about any of that. Only what about Wu-er?’

‘Go and tell Mrs Liu to let her come straight in tomorrow. I’ll tell the others, and that will settle it.’

‘Now that’s something worth doing,’ Parfumée said, smiling.

Swallow called in two little maids to attend to the hand-washing and pour tea. She cleared away the dishes and gave them to an older woman, washed her own hands and went to find Mrs Liu. Of her errand we need say no more.

Bao-yu went out again to find the girls at Red Fragrance Court, with Parfumée behind him carrying his kerchief and fan. They had just left the courtyard when Aroma and Skybright came back hand in hand.

‘What are you doing?’ Bao-yu asked.

‘The meal’s laid out,’ Aroma said. ‘They’re waiting for you to eat.’

Bao-yu laughingly told them about the meal he had just had.

‘You eat like a cat,’ Aroma said, smiling. ‘Whatever smells good takes your fancy. The food in someone else’s pot is always sweeter. Even so, you ought to go and eat a little with them for politeness’ sake.’

Skybright poked Parfumée’s forehead with her finger.

‘You little fox-spirit! When did you slip away to eat? How did you two arrange it without telling us?’

‘They simply happened to run into each other,’ Aroma said, laughing. ‘There was no arrangement at all.’

‘Well, we’re no use any more, then,’ said Skybright. ‘Tomorrow we’ll all go away. Parfumée alone will be quite enough to wait on him.’

‘The rest of us could go,’ Aroma said with a smile, ‘but not you.’

‘I should be the first to go. Lazy, stupid, bad-tempered and no use at all.’

‘And if he burns another hole in that peacock coat, who will mend it when you’ve gone?

‘Don’t try those airs on me. If I ask you to do something, you’re so lazy you won’t lift a needle or stir a thread. And it isn’t my own private work I bother you with. It’s always his, but you still refuse. Then I go away for a few days, and you, ill and half dead, risk your life doing it for him in a single night. What’s the reason for that? Come on, tell me. Don’t just play the innocent and smile at me. That won’t do you any good.’

Talking like this, they reached the hall. Aunt Xue had come over too. Everyone sat down in order for the meal. Bao-yu ate only half a bowl of rice soaked in tea, just to observe the forms. When the meal was over they drank tea, chatted and amused themselves as they pleased.

Outside, Xiao-luo, Caltrop, Parfumée, Rui-guan, Ou-guan and Dou-guan, a little party of four or five, had been playing all over the Garden. They gathered flowers and plants in the folds of their clothes, then sat among the heaps they had collected for a plant-name contest.

‘I have Guanyin willow,’ said one.

‘I have arhat pine,’ said another.

‘I have gentleman’s bamboo,’ another said.

‘I have beauty’s canna.’

‘I have starry green.’

‘I have monthly red.’

‘I have the peony from The Peony Pavilion.’

‘I have pipa, the loquat, from The Lute, the pipa play.’

‘I have sisters’ flowers,’ said Dou-guan.

No one had an answer until Caltrop said:

‘I have married-couple orchids.’

‘I’ve never heard of married-couple orchids,’ Dou-guan said.

‘One flower on a stem makes a lan orchid; several flowers on a stem make a hui,’ Caltrop explained. ‘When a hui has two branches with flowers one above the other, it’s a brothers’ orchid. When the flowers are side by side, it’s a married-couple orchid. These are side by side. Why shouldn’t it be one?’

Dou-guan had no answer. She got up, laughing.

‘By your reasoning, if one branch were big and the other little, it would be a father-and-son orchid. If they flowered with their backs to each other, it would be an enemies’ orchid. Your man’s been away for more than half a year. Are you missing married life? You even drag the orchids into marriage. Shame on you!’

Caltrop blushed and tried to get up to pinch her.

‘You little hussy, may your mouth rot!’ she scolded, laughing. ‘A mouthful of made-up nonsense! Wait till I get up. I’ll beat the life out of you!’

Dou-guan saw her reaching over and would not let her rise. She quickly flung her whole weight on her and pushed her flat, then looked back and appealed laughingly to Rui-guan and the others.

‘Come and help me pinch that lying mouth of hers!’

The two rolled about on the grass.

The others clapped and laughed.

‘Oh, look! There’s a puddle there! What a shame, you’ve spoiled her new skirt!’

Dou-guan looked back. There was indeed a pool of rainwater beside them, and half of Caltrop’s skirt was wet and dirty. Embarrassed, she pulled her hands free and ran away. The others could not stop laughing, but for fear that Caltrop would take her anger out on them, they all scattered in a laughing rush.

Caltrop got up and looked down. Green water was still dripping from her skirt. She was angrily cursing them when Bao-yu appeared. Having seen them at their plant-name contest, he had gathered some flowers and leaves to join in, only to find that everyone had run away and Caltrop was standing alone, looking down and fussing over her skirt.

‘Why has everyone gone?’ he asked.

‘I had a married-couple orchid. They didn’t know what it was and said I’d made it up. That started the scuffle, and they’ve dirtied my new skirt.’

‘You have a married-couple orchid,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘Well, I have a twin-flowered water-caltrop.’

He really was holding a sprig of twin-flowered water-caltrop. As he spoke, he took the married-couple orchid into his hand too.

‘Never mind married couples and twin flowers,’ Caltrop said. ‘Look at this skirt.’

Only then did Bao-yu lower his eyes.

‘Oh dear! How did it get dragged in the mud? What a pity! This pomegranate-red silk damask stains so easily.’

‘It’s some material Miss Qin brought the other day.

‘Miss Bao had a skirt made, and so did I. I put mine on for the first time today.’

Bao-yu stamped his foot and sighed.

‘Your family could spoil a hundred of these a day and think nothing of it. But first of all, Miss Qin brought it, and you and Cousin Bao have only one each. Hers is still good and yours is dirty already. Won’t that seem a poor return for her kindness? Then there’s Aunt, with her sharp tongue. Even as things are, I often hear her saying you don’t know how to manage, that you only waste things and never appreciate your good fortune. If she sees this, there’ll be no end to it.’

He had touched the very heart of Caltrop’s concern. Far from making her more miserable, his words cheered her.

‘That’s exactly it,’ she said, smiling. ‘I have several new skirts, but none the same as this. If I had one like it, I could change at once and all would be well. I could explain afterwards.’

‘Don’t move,’ Bao-yu said. ‘Just stand there, or you’ll drag the mud over your underclothes, your knee-trousers and your shoe uppers as well.

‘I have an idea. Aroma had a skirt made last month that’s exactly like this one. She’s in mourning, so she isn’t wearing it. What if she gave it to you to change into?’

Caltrop shook her head, smiling.

‘No. It wouldn’t be good if they heard about it.’

‘What’s there to be afraid of? When she’s out of mourning, surely you can give her something else she likes? If you insist on refusing, you’re hardly being your usual self. Besides, there’s nothing to conceal. You could tell Cousin Bao. It’s only Aunt’s temper we’re afraid of.’

Caltrop thought it over and saw the sense of it. She nodded and smiled.

‘Then let’s do that. I won’t reject your kindness. I’ll wait here. Only do make sure she brings it herself.’

Delighted, Bao-yu promised and hurried back. As he went, he looked down and thought:

‘What a pity for a girl like her. No parents, not even a memory of her own surname, abducted and sold of all people to that tyrant.’

He remembered how unexpected the incident with Patience the other day had been. Today’s was more unexpected still. Turning all these thoughts over, he reached his rooms, took Aroma aside and told her exactly what had happened. No one could help feeling affection for Caltrop. Aroma was open-handed by nature, and she and Caltrop had always been friends. As soon as she heard, she opened her chest, took out the skirt and folded it. She followed Bao-yu to find Caltrop, who was still standing there waiting.

‘You really are too mischievous,’ Aroma told her with a smile. ‘You won’t stop till you’ve made a story worth telling.’

Caltrop blushed and smiled.

‘Thank you, dear sister. Who could have known those little wretches would be so wicked?’

She took the skirt and spread it out. It was indeed identical to her own.

She told Bao-yu to turn his back, reached both hands inside to undo her skirt, took it off and tied on the other one.

‘Give me the dirty one to take back,’ Aroma said. ‘I’ll clean it and send it to you. If you carry it home and they see it, they’ll still ask questions.’

‘Dear sister, take it and give it to any of the girls you please. Now I have this one, I don’t want it.’

‘You certainly are generous,’ said Aroma.

Caltrop hastily curtseyed her thanks again, and Aroma went off with the dirty skirt.

Caltrop saw Bao-yu crouching on the ground. With a twig he had scraped a hole for the married-couple orchid and the twin-flowered water-caltrop. He first gathered fallen blossoms to line it, laid the two sprigs carefully inside and covered them with more blossoms. Only then did he draw the earth over them and smooth it level. Caltrop caught his hand, smiling.

‘And what do you call this? No wonder everyone says you’re always up to these furtive little things that make people squirm. Look at your hands, all black mud and slippery moss! Go and wash them.’

Bao-yu smiled, got up and went to wash. Caltrop walked away too. They had each gone several paces when she turned back and called him. Not knowing what she wanted, Bao-yu came back smiling, his two muddy hands held out clear of him.

‘What is it?’

Caltrop only laughed. Then her little maid Zhen-er came over from the other side.

‘Second Miss is waiting to speak to you.’

Only then did Caltrop say to Bao-yu:

‘You mustn’t tell your cousin about the skirt.’

She turned and went away at once.

‘I’d have to be mad,’ Bao-yu said, laughing. ‘Putting my head into the tiger’s mouth!’

With that he too went back to wash his hands. To learn what followed, listen to the next chapter.
