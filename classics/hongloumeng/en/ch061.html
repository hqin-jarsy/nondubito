Chapter 61 Bao-yu conceals a theft rather than break the vessel in striking the rat; Patience uses her discretion to right a wrongful charge

Chapter end

‘You little monkey!’ Mrs Liu said, laughing. ‘Your own dear auntie’s been looking for a man on the side. That gives you another uncle, doesn’t it? What’s there to wonder about? Don’t make me pull those few cunt-hairs off that chamber-pot lid you call a head! Open the gate and let me in.’

The boy would not open it yet. Still holding her back, he said with a grin:

‘Be a good auntie and steal me some apricots when you get inside. I’ll be here waiting. If you forget, next time you’re out in the middle of the night buying wine or oil, I shan’t open the gate for your ladyship. I shan’t even answer. You can shout till you’re tired.’

Mrs Liu spat.

‘Have you lost your wits? This year isn’t like last year. They’ve shared all those things out among the good ladies. They’ve scratched every scrap of shame off their faces: let anyone so much as pass under a tree, and their eyes are on him like a fighting cock’s. Touch their fruit, indeed! Yesterday I was walking under a plum tree when a bee flew past my face. I waved it away, and your precious aunt happened to see me.

‘She was too far off to see properly and thought I was picking plums. Up went her whore’s screech: “They haven’t been offered to the Buddha yet!” And then, “Her Old Ladyship and Her Ladyship are away. We haven’t presented the first fruits. Once they’ve had theirs, there’ll be a share for all you sisters.” As if I were sick with craving and waiting for those plums to bring me out in a sweat! Well, I had no kind words for her either. I gave her a proper dressing-down. With two or three of your own relations, your aunts on both sides, in charge of the fruit, why don’t you ask them? Why come to me? It’s the granary rat borrowing grain from the crow: the one sitting over it has none, but the one flying about must have some!’

‘Oh, dear, oh, dear!’ the boy said, laughing. ‘If there aren’t any, say so. What a lot of talk! I suppose you’ll never need me again? Even if our sister’s got herself a good place, she’ll have all the more need to call on us. She’ll have plenty for us to do, answering her calls.’

‘You little monkey-spirit!’ Mrs Liu said with a laugh. ‘More of your sly tricks and nonsense. What good place has your sister got?’

‘Don’t try to fool me,’ the boy said, grinning. ‘I’ve known for ages. Are you the only ones with connections inside? Haven’t we any? I may stand out here waiting on orders, but I’ve two sisters in there with some standing of their own. What can you keep from us?’

As they were talking, an old woman called from inside the gate:

‘You little monkeys! Hurry up and fetch your Aunt Liu. If she doesn’t come now, everything will be late.’

Mrs Liu broke off her conversation with the boy, pushed the gate open and hurried in.

‘No need to fuss. Here I am!’ she called, laughing.

She went to the kitchen. There were several others working there, but none dared act on her own authority: they were all waiting for her to settle things and allot the work.

‘Where’s Wu-er?’ she asked.

‘She’s just gone to the tea-room to find the other girls,’ they told her.

Mrs Liu put the fuling frost away and began assigning the dishes to the different sets of rooms. Just then Lotus, a little maid from Ying-chun’s rooms, came in.

‘Sister Chess says she wants a bowl of steamed eggs, done nice and soft.’

‘Such grand tastes!’ said Mrs Liu. ‘I don’t know why, but eggs are terribly scarce this year. You can’t get one even for ten cash. Yesterday the masters sent rice and provisions to some relations for a lying-in. Four or five buyers went out, and it was all they could do to scrape together two thousand eggs. Where am I to find any?

‘Tell her to have them another day.’

‘The other day she wanted tofu, and you gave her some that had gone sour,’ said Lotus. ‘I got a scolding for it. Today she wants eggs, and you haven’t got those either! What a precious delicacy! I don’t believe you haven’t even got eggs. You’d better not let me find any.’

As she spoke, she actually went over and lifted the lid of the food chest. Sure enough, there were ten or so eggs inside.

‘What are these, then? You do like to throw your weight about! The food belongs to our masters, and we’ve our regular allowance. Why should you grudge it us? It’s not as if you laid the eggs yourself and can’t bear to have them eaten.’

Mrs Liu dropped what she was doing and hurried over.

‘Stop that filthy nonsense! It’s your mother who lays eggs! These few are all I’ve kept back for garnishing the dishes. I won’t even use them for that unless the young ladies ask. They’re here in case of need. If you eat them and someone suddenly wants something, there’ll be nothing good to give her, not even an egg.

‘Here you sit in your great house behind its high walls: put out your hands and the water’s brought, open your mouths and there’s your food. You think eggs are everyday things. What do you know of market prices outside? Never mind eggs. There was one year when there were days you couldn’t even get grass roots.

‘I’d advise them to make do with their fine white rice and the fat chickens and ducks they get every day. But no, they’ve eaten rich food till it sticks in their gullets, and now there’s a new demand every day. Eggs, tofu, wheat gluten, bits of pickled radish: a change of taste, if you please! Only I’m not here to wait on you. If every set of rooms wants a different thing, that makes ten or more dishes. I might as well stop serving our first set of mistresses and spend my time on you second set.’

Lotus flushed.

‘Who’s asking you for things every day?’ she cried. ‘Listen to you, with your two cartloads of talk! What were you brought here for, if not to make things easier for us? When Swallow came the other day and said Sister Skybright wanted mugwort shoots, how quick you were to ask whether she’d like them fried with pork or chicken!

‘Swallow said, “She doesn’t fancy anything with meat. That’s why she’s asked you to do a separate dish with wheat gluten. And go easy on the oil.” You were so quick to say you must have lost your wits, and off you rushed to wash your hands and cook it. Then you carried it over yourself, frisking along like a dog. But today you pick on me to make an example of, telling me off for everyone to hear!’

‘Blessed Buddha!’ Mrs Liu exclaimed. ‘Everyone here has eyes. Never mind that one time the other day. Ever since this kitchen was set up last year, whenever a young lady or one of the girls in any of the rooms has happened to want an extra dish or two, haven’t they always brought the money first, to buy the extra things? People talk whether they know anything or not. It sounds very nice: I’ve only the young ladies’ kitchen to look after, an easy job with something left over. But the accounts would turn your stomach. There are forty or fifty mouths, young ladies and maids together, and all I can draw each day is two chickens, two ducks, ten-odd catties of meat and a string of cash for vegetables. Work it out for yourselves. What can you do with that? It won’t stretch to the two regular meals, let alone this one ordering one thing and that one another. And when it’s bought, they won’t eat it, and you have to go and buy something else.

‘If that’s how it’s to be, we’d better tell Her Ladyship and have the regular allowance increased. We could do as the main kitchen does for Her Old Ladyship’s meals: write every vegetable under heaven on a wipe-clean menu board, work through them day by day, and settle up at the end of the month. That would be better. Why, the other day the Third Miss and Miss Bao happened to agree that they’d like some wolfberry shoots stir-fried with nothing but oil and salt. They actually sent a maid with five hundred cash for me. I laughed. “Even if the two young ladies had bellies like Maitreya the Buddha, they couldn’t eat five hundred cash’s worth,” I said. “It only costs twenty or thirty cash. I can still afford that.” I hurried over to give the money back, but they wouldn’t take it. They said I was to buy myself some wine. And they said, “Now the kitchen’s inside, we can’t be sure the people in our rooms won’t come bothering you. Salt or sauce, it all costs money. You can hardly refuse them, but if you give it to them, where are you to make up the loss? Keep this money and count it as payment for all the things they’ve been pestering you for.” Those are young ladies who understand and have some feeling for the people below them. We can only pray to the Buddha for them in our hearts.

‘But then Aunt Zhao had to hear of it and take offence. She said I’d come off far too well. Not ten days would go by without her sending a little maid too, looking for this and that. It made me want to laugh. You’ll all be making a rule of it, one thing after another. Where am I to find the money to make good all that?’

In the midst of this uproar, another messenger came from Chess to hurry Lotus back.

‘Has she died in there? Why doesn’t she come back?’

Lotus went off in a temper and told Chess what had happened, adding a whole chapter of her own. Chess’s anger was duly roused. Once she had finished waiting on Ying-chun at her meal, she came over with the little maids. A number of people were eating when she arrived. Seeing that she meant trouble, they all jumped up, smiling, and offered her a seat.

Chess shouted to the little maids to get to work.

‘Every bit of food in those chests and cupboards, throw it out for the dogs! Then nobody will make anything out of it!’

The little maids needed no second telling. They rushed forward, all hands and feet, rummaging and flinging things about. The others tried to pull them back and calm them while pleading with Chess:

‘Miss, don’t be misled by what a child says. Mrs Liu wouldn’t dare offend you if she had eight heads to lose. Eggs really are hard to buy. We’ve just told her she ought to have known better: whatever you wanted, she should have found some way to get it.

‘She’s seen her mistake now and put them on to steam straight away. If you don’t believe us, miss, look at the stove.’

After all their soothing words, Chess’s anger gradually subsided. The little maids were pulled away before they could finish smashing everything. Chess carried on for a while, scolding and cursing, before the others persuaded her to go. Mrs Liu could only bang down bowls and fling down plates, muttering to herself for a time. She steamed a bowl of eggs and sent someone over with it. Chess tipped the whole bowlful onto the floor. The messenger dared not tell them on returning, for fear of making more trouble.

Mrs Liu saw that her daughter had some soup and half a bowl of rice gruel, then told her about the fuling frost. Wu-er wanted to give some to Parfumée, so she wrapped half of it separately in paper. At dusk, when few people were about, she went to find Parfumée, keeping behind flowers and willows as she went. To her delight, no one stopped to question her. She made straight for the gate of the House of Green Delights, but did not like to go in. Instead she stood by a cluster of rose bushes, watching from a distance.

After about the time it takes to drink a cup of tea, Swallow happened to come out. Wu-er hurried forward and called to her. Swallow did not recognise her until she came close enough to see her face, then asked what she wanted.

‘Call Parfumée out,’ Wu-er said with a smile. ‘I want to speak to her.’

‘You’re too impatient, sister,’ Swallow said, laughing quietly. ‘You’ll be coming here in ten days or so anyway. Why keep looking for her? They’ve just sent her out to the front. You’ll have to wait a little. Or tell me what you want to say, and I’ll tell her. You may not be able to wait. I’m afraid they’ll be shutting the garden gate.’

Wu-er handed Swallow the packet, explaining that it was fuling frost, how it was taken and what nourishment it gave.

‘I’ve had some given me, and I’ve brought this for her. Do be so kind as to pass it on.’

Then she said goodbye and went back.

She was walking near the knotweed shallows when she suddenly saw Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife coming towards her with several older women. There was no time to hide. She could only go forward and greet her.

‘I heard you were ill,’ said Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife. ‘What are you doing running about here?’

‘I’ve been a little better these last two days,’ Wu-er said with a deferential smile. ‘I came in with Mother for a little diversion. Just now she sent me to the House of Green Delights with some dishes.’

‘That story doesn’t fit. I only shut the gate after I’d seen your mother go out. If she sent you, why didn’t she tell me you were still here? What did she mean by going off and letting me lock up? It’s plain you’re lying.’

Wu-er could find no answer.

‘Mother told me early this morning to take them,’ was all she could say. ‘I forgot, and only just remembered.

‘She must have thought I’d already gone out. That’s why she didn’t tell you, ma’am.’

Her faltering words and uneasy expression made Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife suspicious. Silver had lately reported things missing from the principal rooms over there. The maids were blaming one another, and no culprit had been found. Just then Chan-jie, Lotus and several married servants came along. Seeing what was happening, they said:

‘You ought to question her properly, Mrs Lin. She’s been running in and out of here these last two days in a most suspicious way, all whispering and sneaking about. Who knows what she’s up to?’

‘That’s right,’ Chan-jie put in.

‘Yesterday Sister Silver said the cupboard in Her Ladyship’s side room had been opened and all sorts of little things were missing. Mrs Lian sent Miss Patience to ask Sister Silver for some rose essence, and there was a jar of that missing too. If they hadn’t wanted the essence, they wouldn’t even have known.’

‘I hadn’t heard that,’ Lotus said with a smile. ‘But I did see a bottle of essence today.’

The unsolved thefts were a sore point with Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife. Xi-feng sent Patience to press her about them every day. At this she hastily asked where the bottle was.

‘In their kitchen,’ Lotus replied.

Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife immediately ordered a lantern to be lit and took them all to look.

‘It was Parfumée from Master Bao’s rooms who gave it to me!’ Wu-er cried in distress.

‘Parfumée, Square, Round: I don’t care what she’s called,’ said Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife. ‘We have the stolen goods as evidence. I’ll report it, and you can make your excuses to your masters.’

Still talking, she went into the kitchen, where Lotus led her to the bottle of essence and took it out.

Afraid that other things might have been stolen too, they made a thorough search. They found a packet of fuling frost as well, seized both, and took Wu-er with them to report to Li Wan and Tan-chun. Jia Lan was ill, however, and Li Wan was attending to no business. She merely told them to go to Tan-chun, who had already retired to her rooms.

Their arrival was announced. The maids were all taking the cool air in the courtyard, while Tan-chun was washing inside. Only Scribe went in with the message. After some time she came out.

‘Miss Tan-chun has been told. She says you’re to find Patience and have her report to Mrs Lian.’

Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife had no choice but to take them away again. At Xi-feng’s quarters she first found Patience, who went in to report. Xi-feng had just lain down. On hearing what had happened, she gave her orders:

‘Give the mother forty strokes of the board and turn her out. She’s never to come inside the second gate again. Give Wu-er forty strokes too, then hand her straight over to the country estate, to be sold or married off.’

Patience came out and passed these instructions on to Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife. Terrified, Wu-er knelt before Patience, sobbing, and told her all about Parfumée.

‘That’s easy enough to settle,’ said Patience. ‘We’ll ask Parfumée tomorrow and see whether it’s true. But this fuling frost only came as a gift the other day. No one would dare touch it before Her Old Ladyship and Her Ladyship came back and saw it. You ought not to have stolen it.’

At this Wu-er hastily told her how her uncle had sent it.

‘So by your account,’ Patience said with a smile, ‘you’re entirely innocent, and they’ve brought you here to take the blame for someone else. It’s late now. My mistress has just taken her medicine and lain down. I can’t pester her with such a little matter at this hour. For the present, hand her over to the night-watch women. They can keep her till morning. I’ll speak to my mistress then, and we’ll decide what to do.’

Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife dared not disobey. She took Wu-er away, handed her over to the married women on night duty and went about her own affairs.

Wu-er was kept under guard and dared not move a step beyond where she was allowed. Some of the women lectured her on the disgraceful things she ought not to have done. Others complained:

‘We can hardly get through our proper night watch as it is, and now they’ve given us a thief to look after. If she kills herself or runs away when our backs are turned, it’ll all be our fault.’

Then a number of people who had long been on bad terms with the Liu family, delighted at this turn of events, came to jeer and make fun of her. Wu-er was angry and full of a sense of injustice, but had nowhere to take her grievance. She was already frail and ill. That night, when she wanted tea there was no tea, when she wanted water there was no water, and when she wanted sleep there was neither quilt nor pillow. She sobbed and wept the whole night through.

Those who were at odds with mother and daughter could not wait to see them driven out. Afraid that things might change next day, they all rose early and went quietly to win Patience over, bringing little gifts, praising the decisiveness with which she had handled the matter and recounting all sorts of faults in Mrs Liu’s everyday conduct. Patience acknowledged each of them and sent them away. Then she went privately to Aroma and asked whether Parfumée really had given Wu-er the essence.

‘The essence was given to Parfumée,’ Aroma said. ‘I don’t know who she passed it on to.’

She asked Parfumée, who, when she heard what had happened, sprang up in alarm and hastily said that she herself had given it to Wu-er. Parfumée then told Bao-yu, who grew alarmed too.

‘We’ve accounted for the essence, but if they bring up the fuling frost, she’ll naturally tell them the truth about that as well. Once they hear her uncle got it at the gate, he’ll be in trouble too. They meant to be kind, and we’ll have brought trouble on them in return!’

He hurriedly took counsel with Patience.

‘The essence is settled, but there’s still trouble over the frost.

‘Dear sister, tell her to say Parfumée gave her that too. Then it’s all settled.’

‘That may be,’ Patience said with a smile, ‘but she told everyone last night that her uncle gave it to her. How can she now say it came from you? Besides, we still haven’t found who took the essence over there. If we let someone with the stolen goods go free, who are we to look for next? Who’s going to admit it? And people won’t necessarily be satisfied.’

Skybright came over, smiling.

‘No one else took Her Ladyship’s essence. It was plainly Cai-yun, who stole it for Master Huan. You’re all talking at random.’

‘Who doesn’t know that?’ Patience said with a smile. ‘Only now Silver’s so upset she’s crying. If Cai-yun had admitted it when she was asked quietly, Silver would have let it go, and we could all have muddled along without asking any more. Do you think we went looking for this affair? What’s infuriating is that Cai-yun won’t admit it. Worse, she’s putting the blame on Silver and saying she stole it. The two of them started firing at each other inside their own nest till the whole household knew. How could we pretend nothing had happened?

‘We had to investigate. Little do they know that the very person crying theft is the thief. But without the stolen goods to prove it, how are we to accuse her?’

‘All right,’ Bao-yu said. ‘I’ll take the blame for this too. Say I secretly stole Mother’s things to give them a fright for a joke. That will settle both matters.’

‘It would earn you some hidden merit,’ said Aroma, ‘saving someone from the name of thief. Only when Her Ladyship hears, she’ll say you’re childish again, with no sense of right and wrong.’

‘That’s a small matter,’ Patience said, smiling. ‘It would be easy enough to recover the stolen things from Aunt Zhao’s rooms now. I’m only afraid of hurting one good person’s reputation as well. Never mind the others: wouldn’t that one person be upset again? She’s the one I feel for. I won’t smash a jade vase just to hit a rat.’

She held up three fingers. Aroma and the others knew at once that she meant Tan-chun.

‘Of course!’ they all hastened to say. ‘It’s best to let our rooms take the blame.’

‘Still,’ Patience went on, smiling, ‘we must send for those two troublemakers, Cai-yun and Silver, and make certain where we stand. Otherwise they’ll get all the benefit without knowing why. They’ll think I was too useless to find anything out and had to come here to have it settled for me. After that, the thief will steal more than ever, and the one who doesn’t keep watch won’t bother at all.’

‘Quite right,’ Aroma and the others said, laughing. ‘You must leave yourself some ground to stand on too.’

Patience sent for the two girls.

‘Don’t be frightened,’ she told them. ‘The thief’s been found.’

Silver at once asked where the thief was.

‘At Mrs Lian’s quarters now,’ said Patience. ‘Ask her anything, and she admits it. I know perfectly well she didn’t steal the things. The poor girl’s so frightened she’ll confess to anything. Master Bao here can’t bear it and wants to take half the blame for her. I was going to speak out, only the girl who really stole them is an old friend of mine, a sister. The receiver of the stolen things wouldn’t matter, but there’s a good person’s reputation involved too. That’s what makes it difficult. I’ve had to beg Master Bao to admit it, so that everyone can be left in peace.

‘Now I want to ask you two what you mean to do. If from now on everyone will take care and preserve some decency, we’ll ask Master Bao to take the blame. If not, I’ll tell Mrs Lian, rather than let an innocent person suffer.’

Cai-yun blushed. Shame and a sudden revulsion from her own wrongdoing moved her to speak.

‘Don’t worry, sister. We mustn’t wrong a good person or drag someone innocent into it and damage her reputation. As for the theft, Aunt Zhao begged me over and over again. It’s true I took some things for Master Huan. We’ve taken things even when Her Ladyship was at home, all of us, to give to other people. It’s quite usual. I thought they’d make a fuss for a couple of days and then let it drop. But now an innocent person’s suffering for it, I can’t bear it. Take me to our mistress, sister. I’ll admit everything and have done with it.’

Everyone was astonished that she should show such courage. Bao-yu hastily smiled.

‘Sister Cai-yun really is a decent person. But there’s no need for you to admit anything. I’ll just say I stole the things to frighten you for a joke. Now that it’s caused trouble, of course I ought to own up.

‘Only do try to make less trouble in future, sisters, and all will be well.’

‘Why should you admit to something I did?’ said Cai-yun. ‘Live or die, I ought to bear it myself.’

‘That’s not the way to look at it,’ Patience and Aroma said quickly. ‘Once you confess, Aunt Zhao is bound to be dragged in again. Won’t the Third Miss be upset when she hears? It’s much better to let Master Bao admit it and leave everyone in peace. No one beyond the few of us here need know anything about it. How neatly that would settle things! Only everyone must be very, very careful from now on. Whatever you want to take, at least have the patience to wait till Her Ladyship comes home. Then even if the whole house is given away, it’s nothing to do with us.’

Cai-yun lowered her head and thought for a while before agreeing.

Once everything had been arranged, Patience took the two girls and Parfumée out towards the front. At the night-watch room she called Wu-er out and quietly instructed her to say that Parfumée had given her the fuling frost as well. Wu-er could not thank her enough.

Patience brought them back to her own quarters, where Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife and several married servants had already been waiting a long time with Mrs Liu under guard.

‘I had her brought here first thing this morning,’ Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife told Patience. ‘I was afraid there’d be no one to see to the young ladies’ meals in the garden, so I sent Qin Xian’s wife to do it for the time being. You might tell our mistress that as well. She’s clean and careful. Why not give her the work permanently?’

‘Who is Qin Xian’s wife?’ Patience asked. ‘I don’t know her very well.’

‘She keeps the night watch at the south corner of the garden. There’s not much for her to do by day, so you wouldn’t know her. High cheekbones, big eyes, a very clean, capable woman.’

‘Of course,’ said Silver. ‘How could you forget, sister? She’s Chess’s aunt, the wife of her father’s younger brother. Chess serves the Second Miss. Her parents belong to the Elder Master’s establishment, but this uncle belongs to ours.’

Now Patience remembered.

‘Oh! If you’d said that to begin with, I’d have known who you meant.’

Then she smiled again.

‘You’ve been rather hasty with that appointment. It’s all clear now, from every side. Even the things that went missing from Her Ladyship’s rooms the other day have been accounted for. Bao-yu went over and asked these two wretches for something. They wanted to tease him and said they daren’t give it to him while Her Ladyship was away. So he waited till they weren’t looking, went in himself and took some things out. They knew nothing about it and were thrown into a panic. Only when Bao-yu heard that other people had been dragged into it did he tell me the whole story and bring the things out to show me. There wasn’t a single thing missing. As for the fuling frost, Bao-yu had that from outside and gave it to all sorts of people. It’s not only the people in the garden who’ve had some. Even the older women have asked him for it to take to their relations, and they’ve passed it on to others. Aroma, too, has given some to Parfumée and girls like her.

‘They’re friends and exchange little presents. There’s nothing unusual about that. Those two baskets that came the other day are still standing in the council hall, quite safe, with their seals untouched. How could you go accusing people at random? Wait till I’ve spoken to my mistress.’

She left them and went into the bedroom, where she gave Xi-feng the same account.

‘That may be what he says,’ Xi-feng replied, ‘but Bao-yu will take anything on himself, whether it’s right or wrong. Let someone plead with him and say a couple of nice things: put a charcoal basket on his head as if it were a fine hat, and there’s nothing he won’t agree to. If we believe him, what happens when it’s something serious and he does the same? How are we to keep people in order? We must make a thorough inquiry. If I had my way, we’d bring all the maids from Her Ladyship’s rooms here. We can hardly have them beaten on our own authority, but we could make them kneel on broken porcelain in the sun and give them neither food nor tea. A day’s silence, a day on their knees. Even if they were made of iron, they’d confess within a day. Besides, “a fly won’t cling to an egg without a crack.” Mrs Liu may not have stolen anything, but there must have been something to make people accuse her. Even if we don’t punish her as a thief, she should lose her place and never be employed again. The court itself punishes people implicated in someone else’s offence. She couldn’t call it an injustice.’

‘Why give yourself all this worry?’ Patience said. ‘“When you can let go, let go.” It’s nothing so very serious. Why not take the easy chance to do someone a kindness?

‘If you ask me, however much we wear ourselves out over this household, in the end we shall go back to the other branch. Why make enemies of petty people and leave them nursing grudges? Besides, you’ve had one illness and misfortune after another. After all the trouble of conceiving a boy, you lost him at six or seven months. How do we know it wasn’t all the overwork and vexation that harmed you? It’s time you learnt to see half of what goes on and turn a blind eye to the rest. That would be better.’

This speech brought a smile to Xi-feng’s face.

‘Go and settle it as you like, you little hussy. I’ve only just begun to feel more myself. There’s no sense in wearing myself out.’

‘Now that’s sensible!’ Patience said, smiling.

She turned and went out to deal with each matter in turn.
