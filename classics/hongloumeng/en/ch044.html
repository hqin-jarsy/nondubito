Chapter 44 An unforeseen turn sends Xi-feng into a jealous rage; Unexpected delight attends Patience’s toilette

Chapter end

While everyone watched The Thorn Hairpin, Bao-yu sat with the girls. When they reached the scene called ‘The Husband’s Offering’, Dai-yu said to Bao-chai:

‘This Wang Shi-peng is quite unreasonable. He could make his offering anywhere. Why must he run all the way to the riverbank? As the saying goes, “The sight of a thing brings the person to mind.” All the water in the world comes from one source. He could scoop a bowlful up anywhere, look at it and cry. That would give his feelings all the expression they needed.’

Bao-chai did not answer.

Bao-yu turned round and asked for wine to be warmed so that he could offer Xi-feng a cup. Grandmother Jia had said that this day was to be different from other days: Xi-feng must be allowed a whole day of unrestrained enjoyment. She herself had not felt inclined to sit at a formal feast, but reclined on a couch in the inner room, watching the play with Aunt Xue. A few dishes she happened to fancy had been set on a little table, so that she could eat and talk as she pleased. She had given the food from her own two banquet tables to the senior and junior maids who had no places, and to the women on duty or waiting to be called. They were to sit beneath the eaves outside the window and eat and drink as they liked, without standing on ceremony.

Lady Wang and Lady Xing sat at a high table in the main part of the room. The girls occupied several tables outside. Every so often Grandmother Jia would tell You-shi and the others:

‘Put Feng at the head of the table. You must be good hostesses on my behalf. She works so hard all year round.’

You-shi assented, then replied with a smile:

‘She isn’t used to the place of honour. She can’t get comfortable however she sits, and she won’t drink either.’

‘You don’t know how to manage her,’ Grandmother Jia said, laughing. ‘I’ll go and offer her some myself.’

Xi-feng hurried in, smiling.

‘Don’t believe them, Grannie. I’ve had several cups already.’

Grandmother Jia laughed and told You-shi:

‘Quick, take her out and hold her down in the chair. All of you must take turns offering her wine. If she still won’t drink, I really shall come myself.’

Laughing, You-shi drew Xi-feng outside again and sat her down. She called for a cup and stand, filled the cup with wine and said:

‘All year long you take such pains over the old lady, Her Ladyship and me.

‘I’ve nothing to show my affection for you today but a cup I’ve poured myself. Be a good girl and take a sip from my hand.’

‘If you really mean to honour me,’ Xi-feng said with a laugh, ‘kneel down, and I’ll drink.’

‘Listen to her! She doesn’t know who she is any more! Let me tell you, a chance like today doesn’t come easily. Once it’s over, who knows whether you’ll have another like it? While you can, just swallow a couple of damned cups.’

Xi-feng saw there was no escaping and drank two cups. Next the girls came to offer theirs, and she had to take a sip from each. Seeing Grandmother Jia still in such high spirits, Lai Da’s mother could hardly fail to join in. She brought some of the other old nurses to offer their cups. Xi-feng could not easily refuse them either and had to drink another couple of mouthfuls. When Faithful and the others came in their turn, she truly could take no more.

‘Dear sisters, do let me off!’ she begged. ‘I’ll drink again tomorrow.’

‘So we really count for nothing?’ Faithful said, smiling. ‘Even Her Ladyship shows us some consideration when we’re with her.

‘Usually we have some standing, but today, in front of all these people, you’ve started putting on your airs as mistress. I should never have come. If you won’t drink, we’ll go.’

She actually turned and went away. Xi-feng hurried after her and caught hold of her.

‘Dear sister, I’ll drink. There!’

She took the wine, poured herself a brimming cup and drained it. Only then did Faithful smile and go off with the others. Xi-feng returned to her place at the table.

Xi-feng could feel the wine weighing on her. Her heart was thumping, as though it were trying to force its way upwards. She wanted to go home and rest. Just then the acrobats came on.

‘Have the reward money ready,’ she told You-shi. ‘I’m going to wash my face.’

You-shi nodded.

While no one was watching, Xi-feng left the feast and went round beneath the eaves at the back of the rooms. Patience had been keeping an eye on her and hurried after her. Xi-feng leaned on her arm. They had only just reached the covered passage when they saw a little maid from their own rooms standing there. At the sight of the two of them she turned and ran. Suspicious, Xi-feng called after her. At first the maid pretended not to hear, but when Patience too began calling from behind, she had to come back. Now thoroughly suspicious, Xi-feng hurried into the passage-hall with Patience, called the little maid in after them and shut the lattice doors. She sat on the steps of the little courtyard and ordered the girl to kneel.

‘Fetch two boys from the inner gate,’ she snapped at Patience. ‘Bring ropes and whips, and flog that little bitch to pieces. She has no eyes for her mistress!’

The child was already terrified out of her wits. She wept and kept knocking her forehead on the ground, begging for mercy.

‘I’m not a ghost,’ Xi-feng demanded. ‘When you saw me, why didn’t you stand still and behave properly? What were you running away for?’

‘I didn’t see you coming, ma’am,’ the maid sobbed.

‘I was worried about there being no one in the rooms. That’s why I ran.’

‘If there was no one in the rooms, who told you to come here? Even if you didn’t see me, Patience and I were behind you, calling ourselves hoarse a dozen times. The more we called, the faster you ran. We weren’t far away. Have you gone deaf? And now you’re answering me back!’

She raised her hand and slapped the girl’s face, making her stagger, then struck the other cheek. Both cheeks immediately swelled and turned purple.

‘Mind you don’t hurt your hand, ma’am,’ Patience hastened to say.

‘Then you hit her and ask her why she ran. If she still won’t say, tear her mouth open!’

The little maid at first stuck to her story. Only when Xi-feng threatened to have an iron heated red-hot to burn her mouth did she weep out:

‘The Second Master is at home. He sent me here to watch for you, ma’am. When I saw you leaving the feast I was to run ahead and tell him. I didn’t expect you to come just now.’

Xi-feng saw there was something behind this.

‘What did he want you to watch me for?

‘Was he afraid I might go home? There must be some other reason. Tell me quickly, and I’ll be kind to you from now on. If you don’t tell me every bit of it, I’ll have a knife brought this instant and cut your flesh.’

Reaching back, she pulled a hairpin out of her hair and jabbed wildly at the girl’s mouth. The terrified child dodged and wept, pleading:

‘I’ll tell you, ma’am, but don’t say I told you.’

Patience tried to reason with Xi-feng while urging the child to speak quickly.

‘The Second Master had only just come back to the rooms,’ the maid began. ‘He slept a little, and when he woke up he sent someone to see how you were getting on. They said you’d only just sat down to the feast and wouldn’t be back for a good while.

‘Then he opened the chest and took out two pieces of silver, two hairpins and two bolts of satin. He told me to take them secretly to Bao Er’s wife and ask her to come in. She took the things and came to our rooms. The Second Master sent me to keep watch for you. I don’t know what happened after that.’

Xi-feng was so furious that her whole body went weak. She rose hastily and went straight home. As she reached the courtyard gate, she saw another little maid peeping out. This one too ducked her head and ran at the sight of her. Xi-feng shouted her name and ordered her to stop. The girl was quick-witted. Seeing she could not escape, she ran out towards Xi-feng instead, smiling.

‘I was just going to tell you, ma’am. How lucky you’ve come.’

‘Tell me what?’

The maid told her what the Second Master was doing at home, repeating what the other girl had said.

‘Pah! What were you doing all this time?’ Xi-feng spat. ‘Now I’ve caught sight of you, you come and wash your hands of it!’

She raised her hand and gave this girl too a slap that sent her stumbling. Then she crept to the window and listened. Inside there was talk and laughter.

‘When will that King Yama of a wife of yours die?’ the woman said with a laugh. ‘What a good thing that would be.’

‘If she died, and I married another, she’d be just the same,’ said Jia Lian. ‘What good would that do?’

‘If she died, you could make Patience your lawful wife. She might be better.’

‘Nowadays she won’t even let me touch Patience,’ Jia Lian said. ‘Patience has a bellyful of grievances too, but she daren’t speak.

‘Why was I fated to have a yaksha for my evil star?’

Xi-feng was shaking all over with rage. Hearing them both praise Patience, she suspected that Patience must have been voicing resentment behind her back as well. The wine surged up more fiercely than ever. Without stopping to think, she turned and struck Patience twice, kicked the door open and rushed in. Allowing no explanations, she seized Bao Er’s wife and began tearing at her and beating her. Then, afraid Jia Lian might get out, she planted herself in the doorway and cursed:

‘You whore! You steal your master for a lover, and you want to kill your mistress too! Patience, come here! You whores and bastards are all tangled on the same vine. All of you can’t bear the sight of me, and to my face you coax and flatter!’

She struck Patience several more times. With nowhere to appeal against this injustice, Patience could only choke and cry with rage.

‘You do these shameless things, and what do you drag me into them for?’ she cried, and began tearing at Bao Er’s wife and beating her too.

Jia Lian had also drunk too much. Coming in high spirits, he had not taken proper precautions. The sight of Xi-feng had already left him at his wits’ end; now that Patience was making trouble too, his anger sent the wine to his head. When Xi-feng was beating Bao Er’s wife he had been angry and ashamed, but had hardly been in a position to speak. Seeing Patience join in, he came forward, kicking and cursing her.

‘You little whore! So you’re laying hands on people too!’

Patience lost her nerve and stopped at once.

‘You were talking behind my back,’ she cried. ‘Why did you have to drag me in?’

Seeing her afraid of Jia Lian made Xi-feng angrier still. She rushed up and hit Patience again, insisting that she strike Bao Er’s wife. Driven beyond endurance, Patience ran outside to look for a knife and kill herself. The older women and maids hurried to stop her and tried to reason with her.

When Xi-feng saw that Patience had gone to take her own life, she threw herself head first against Jia Lian’s chest.

‘You’re all in league to harm me! I caught you at it, and now you all turn round and threaten me. Strangle me too, then!’

In a rage, Jia Lian snatched a sword from the wall.

‘No need to kill yourselves. I’ve had enough too. I’ll kill the lot of you and pay with my own life. That’ll finish everything!’

As the uproar grew impossible to settle, You-shi arrived with a crowd of people.

‘What’s all this? You were perfectly all right just now. How did this begin?’

The sight of an audience made Jia Lian use his drinking as an excuse to act still drunker. He swaggered all the more, deliberately making a show of wanting to kill Xi-feng.

Once people had come, Xi-feng was no longer so wildly defiant. She left them all and ran weeping towards Grandmother Jia’s. By now the play was over. She rushed up to Grandmother Jia and flung herself into her arms.

‘Save me, Grannie! Master Lian’s going to kill me!’

Grandmother Jia, Lady Xing and Lady Wang hastily asked what had happened.

‘I’d just gone home to change,’ Xi-feng sobbed. ‘I didn’t expect to find him there talking to someone. I thought he must have a visitor, and I was too frightened to go in. I listened outside the window, and it was Bao Er’s wife he was talking to. They were saying how fierce I was, and planning to poison me and make Patience his lawful wife. Of course I was angry, but I didn’t dare quarrel with him. I only gave Patience a couple of blows and asked her why she wanted to harm me. He was ashamed, and then he wanted to kill me.’

Grandmother Jia and the others believed every word.

‘This is beyond everything! Bring that low-bred wretch here at once!’

Before they had finished, Jia Lian came rushing after her, sword in hand, with a crowd of people behind him.

Jia Lian was well aware of Grandmother Jia’s habitual indulgence towards them. With that to rely on, even his mother and aunt were no obstacle, and he had come blustering after Xi-feng. Lady Xing and Lady Wang hastily barred his way, furious at the sight of him.

‘You low-bred wretch! You’re getting quite beyond yourself. The old lady is here!’

Jia Lian looked sideways at them through half-closed eyes.

‘It’s the old lady who’s spoilt her. That’s why she behaves like this. She’s even started swearing at me!’

Lady Xing angrily wrested the sword from him, ordering him over and over to get out. Jia Lian went on with his wilful, wheedling, drunken nonsense.

‘I know you have no regard for any of us,’ Grandmother Jia said in anger. ‘Send someone for his father!’

Only then did Jia Lian stagger out. Sulking, he did not go back to his rooms but made for the outer study.

Lady Xing and Lady Wang now turned to scolding Xi-feng as well.

‘What is there to make such a fuss about?’ Grandmother Jia said with a laugh. ‘They’re young things, like greedy cats. How can you keep them from doing this sort of thing? People have always gone through it when they’re young. It’s all my fault. I let her have a little too much wine, and then she started on the vinegar of jealousy as well.’

Everyone laughed.

‘Don’t worry,’ Grandmother Jia continued. ‘Tomorrow I’ll have him come and apologise to you. Don’t go back and put him to shame today.’

Then she began scolding Patience.

‘That little hussy! I always thought well of her. Who would have guessed she was so wicked in secret?’

‘Patience has done nothing wrong,’ You-shi and the others said, smiling. ‘Feng took her anger out on her. Husband and wife couldn’t very well beat each other, so they both vented their temper on Patience. The poor girl has been wronged dreadfully, and now you’re scolding her too.’

‘Oh, so that’s it! I thought she didn’t seem the sort to play those sly tricks. In that case, poor child, she’s borne their anger for nothing.’

She called Amber.

‘Go and tell Patience this from me: I know she’s been wronged. Tomorrow I’ll make Feng apologise to her.

‘Today is her mistress’s birthday. She mustn’t make a scene.’

Li Wan had already taken Patience into Prospect Garden. Patience was sobbing so hard that she could scarcely recover herself.

‘You’re a sensible girl,’ Bao-chai told her. ‘Think how Feng usually treats you. Today she’s only had a little too much wine. If she couldn’t take her temper out on you, who could she take it out on? Someone else would only laugh at her for being drunk. If you dwell on your grievance now, won’t you make all the good in you at other times seem a sham?’

As she was speaking, Amber arrived with Grandmother Jia’s message. Patience felt that her standing had been restored. Only then did she gradually recover, though she did not go back to the front of the mansion. After resting a while, Bao-chai and the others went to see Grandmother Jia and Xi-feng.

Bao-yu invited Patience to the House of Green Delights. Aroma hurried forward to welcome her.

‘I wanted to ask you before,’ she said, smiling. ‘But with the elder mistress and all the young ladies inviting you, it wouldn’t have done for me to join in.’

Patience managed a smile in return.

‘Thank you.’ Then she added, ‘Everything was perfectly all right. Who could have expected this? All that anger poured on me for no reason whatever.’

‘Your mistress is usually so good to you,’ Aroma said with a smile. ‘She only lost her temper for a moment.’

‘I’ve nothing to say against her,’ said Patience. ‘It was that whore who brought this on me, and then she had to drag me in to make it more amusing. And as if that weren’t enough, our muddle-headed master turned round and hit me too.’

The injustice of it overcame her again, and the tears fell.

‘Dear sister, don’t be unhappy,’ Bao-yu hastened to say. ‘Let me apologise for the two of them.’

‘What has it to do with you?’ Patience said, smiling.

‘We brothers and sisters are all one,’ Bao-yu replied with a smile. ‘If they offend someone, it’s only right for me to apologise for them.’

He went on:

‘What a pity! Your new clothes are stained too. There are some of your Sister Hua’s clothes here. Why don’t you change, and we’ll spray these with a little distilled spirit and iron them? Do your hair again, too, and wash your face.’

As he spoke, he told the little maids to bring washing water and heat an iron. Patience had often heard it said that Bao-yu had a particular gift for getting on with girls. For his part, knowing her to be Jia Lian’s favourite concubine and Xi-feng’s trusted confidante, Bao-yu had not felt free to become intimate with her. The impossibility of showing her all the attentiveness he wished had often been a regret to him.

Now, watching him, Patience quietly considered that what people said was true: he thought of everything. Aroma made a point of opening her chest and taking out two seldom-worn garments for her to change into. Patience hastily took off her own clothes and went to wash her face.

‘You ought to put on a little rouge and powder too,’ Bao-yu urged with a smile. ‘Otherwise it will look as though you’re sulking with Cousin Feng. Besides, it’s her birthday, and Grandmother has sent someone to comfort you.’

Patience thought this reasonable and looked for powder, but could see none. Bao-yu hurried to the dressing-table and lifted the lid of a Xuande-ware porcelain box. Inside was a row of ten powder-filled hosta buds. He picked one up and handed it to Patience.

‘This isn’t lead powder,’ he told her with a smile. ‘It’s made from zi-moli seeds, ground up and mixed with fragrant ingredients.’

Patience tipped some into her palm. It was indeed fine in all four respects: light, white, delicately rosy and fragrant. It spread easily and evenly over her face and left the skin soft and lustrous, unlike other powders, which looked bluish and heavy and felt dry and clogging. She then noticed that the rouge was not in sheets. It filled a little white-jade box and looked like rose paste.

‘The rouge they sell in the shops is never clean, and the colour is weak,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘This is the best rouge, with the juice squeezed out, washed and settled clear of the dregs, then blended with flower distillate and steamed down. Just pick up a little with a fine hairpin, put it on your palm, mix it with a drop of water and apply it to your lips. What’s left on your hand will be enough for your cheeks.’

Patience followed his directions. The result was remarkably fresh and bright, and a sweet fragrance lay over both cheeks. Bao-yu then used bamboo scissors to cut a twin-flowered autumn orchid from its pot and tucked it into her hair at the temple. Just then a maid arrived from Li Wan to fetch her, and she hurried away.

Bao-yu had always deeply regretted that he had never been able to do anything for Patience, especially as she was a girl of the first order, exceptionally clever, handsome and refined, quite unlike those vulgar, stupid, clumsy creatures. Today was Golden’s birthday, and he had been unhappy all day. He had never expected this later disturbance to give him a chance to show Patience even a little of his care. It was a pleasure he had never imagined could fall to him in this life. He lay back on the bed, contented at heart.

Then he thought how Jia Lian knew only how to gratify himself with sexual pleasure, not how to cherish the women about him. He thought too of Patience, with no parents, brothers or sisters, all alone in attendance on Jia Lian and Xi-feng. With Jia Lian so coarse and Xi-feng so overbearing, she had somehow managed to accommodate them both, yet today she had suffered this brutality. Surely, he thought, her lot was even harder than Dai-yu’s. At this he grew sad again, and tears sprang to his eyes. Seeing that Aroma and the others were out of the room, he let himself shed a few bitter tears without restraint.

He got up again. The spirit sprayed on Patience’s clothes was now half dry, so he took the iron, pressed the garments and folded them neatly. Noticing that she had forgotten her handkerchief, which still bore the marks of her tears, he took it to the basin, washed it and hung it up to dry.

By turns happy and sad, he sat listlessly for a while, then went to Sweet-rice Village for some conversation. They did not part until after the lamps were lit. Patience stayed the night with Li Wan, while Xi-feng remained with Grandmother Jia. When Jia Lian returned to his rooms that evening, he found them desolate. He could hardly go and fetch the women, so he slept through the night as best he could. On waking next morning, he thought of the previous day’s events, found them thoroughly mortifying, and regretted them bitterly.

Lady Xing, still concerned about his drunkenness the day before, came over early and told him to come with her to Grandmother Jia’s. He had to swallow his shame and go. He knelt before Grandmother Jia.

‘Well? What is it?’ she asked.

‘Yesterday I had too much wine and disturbed Your Old Ladyship,’ Jia Lian said quickly, with an ingratiating smile. ‘I’ve come to receive my punishment.’

‘Pah, you low creature! After pouring that yellow swill down your throat, why couldn’t you lie down like a corpse and keep quiet? Instead, you had to start beating your wife. Feng is always boasting, and she’s as fierce as a tyrant, but yesterday you frightened her pitifully. If I hadn’t been here, and you’d killed her, where would you be now?’

Full of grievances of his own, Jia Lian dared not argue. He could only admit his fault.

‘And aren’t Feng and Patience both beauties?’ Grandmother Jia went on. ‘Still you’re not satisfied! Always sneaking about after something, dragging every filthy, stinking creature into your room. Beating your wife over one of these whores, and beating your own concubine too! A fine son of a great family you are. You’ve slapped your own face. If you have any regard for me, get up. I’ll forgive you. Apologise nicely to your wife and take her home. That will please me. Otherwise you can go straight out. I wouldn’t presume to have you kneeling before me.’

At these words Jia Lian looked at Xi-feng standing nearby. She was not dressed in her finery. Her eyes were swollen from crying; she wore neither rouge nor powder, and her face was sallow. She seemed more appealing and pitiable than usual.

‘Better to apologise,’ he thought. ‘Then we can both be all right again, and it will please the old lady as well.’

Having decided, he smiled.

‘I daren’t disobey you, Grandmother. But it will only spoil her more.’

‘Nonsense!’ Grandmother Jia said with a laugh.

‘I know how well she understands proper behaviour. She would never offend anyone. If she gives you offence in future, I shall certainly see justice done and have her submit to you.’

Jia Lian got up, made Xi-feng a bow and smiled.

‘It was all my fault, after all. Pray forgive me, Mrs Lian.’

The whole room laughed.

‘Feng, no more anger,’ Grandmother Jia said, laughing. ‘If you’re angry again, I shall be angry too.’

She sent for Patience and told Xi-feng and Jia Lian to comfort her. At the sight of Patience, Jia Lian cast restraint still further aside. As the saying goes, ‘A wife cannot match a concubine, nor a concubine a stolen affair.’ No sooner had Grandmother Jia spoken than he hurried forward.

‘You were badly treated yesterday, my dear. It was all my fault. Your mistress offended you because of me as well. My own apology isn’t enough: I must apologise on her behalf too.’

He made another bow, which set Grandmother Jia laughing. Xi-feng laughed too.

Grandmother Jia then told Xi-feng to comfort Patience. Patience quickly came forward and kowtowed to her.

‘To have made you angry on your birthday, ma’am! I deserve to die.’

Xi-feng had already been ashamed and sorry for drinking too much the day before. Forgetting their accustomed affection, she had flown into a temper and, merely on hearing what others said, had humiliated Patience for no reason. Now that the girl behaved like this instead, shame and sorrow overcame her. She hastily pulled her up, and her tears fell.

‘I’ve waited on you all these years,’ Patience said, ‘and you’ve never so much as flicked me with a fingernail. Even when you hit me yesterday, I didn’t blame you. It was all that whore’s doing. No wonder you were angry.’

Her own tears fell too. Grandmother Jia ordered them all three to be taken back to their rooms.

‘If one of them mentions this again, come and tell me at once. I don’t care who it is. I’ll give them a beating with my stick.’

The three of them kowtowed once more to Grandmother Jia, Lady Xing and Lady Wang. An old nurse assented to the order and escorted them home. Back in their room, when Xi-feng saw that no one else was there, she spoke.

‘How am I like King Yama? How am I a yaksha?

‘That whore wished me dead, and you helped her curse me. Even if I’d been no good for a thousand days, surely there’d been one good day. After wearing myself out, I’m worth less than a whore. What face have I left to go on living like this?’

She began crying again.

‘Still not satisfied?’ said Jia Lian.

‘Think carefully. Who was more in the wrong yesterday? Today, in front of everyone, it was I who went down on my knees, and I who apologised. You’ve had all the credit you could want. Yet here you are, nagging again. Must I kneel down to you before you’ll stop? Insisting on having your own way in everything isn’t such a good thing.’

Xi-feng could find nothing to say. Patience gave a sudden little snort of laughter.

‘So we’re all right again!’ Jia Lian said, laughing too. ‘Upon my word, I don’t know what to do with you.’

They were still talking when a married servant came to report:

‘Bao Er’s wife has hanged herself.’

Jia Lian and Xi-feng were both startled. Xi-feng quickly suppressed her look of alarm and snapped instead:

‘If she’s dead, she’s dead. What is there to make a fuss about?’

Presently Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife came in and told Xi-feng quietly:

‘Bao Er’s wife has hanged herself. Her own family want to bring a complaint.’

‘Splendid!’ Xi-feng said with a laugh. ‘I’ve been longing for a lawsuit.’

‘I’ve just been talking to them with some of the others,’ said Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife. ‘We reasoned with them, threatened them a little and promised them some money. They’ve agreed to leave it.’

‘I haven’t a single cash. And if I had, I wouldn’t give it to them. Let them bring their complaint. You’re not to persuade them, and there’s no need to frighten them either. Just let them go and complain. When they fail, we’ll have them charged with extorting money over a corpse!’

Lin Zhi-xiao’s wife was at a loss. Then she saw Jia Lian signalling to her with his eyes. Understanding him, she went outside to wait.

‘I’ll go out and see what’s happening,’ said Jia Lian.

‘You’re not to give them any money,’ Xi-feng said.

Jia Lian went straight out to consult Lin Zhi-xiao. He sent people to cajole and threaten by turns, and only after two hundred taels had been promised for the funeral was the matter settled. Afraid there might still be trouble, Jia Lian sent a message to Wang Zi-teng and had several constables and coroners’ assistants called in to help with the funeral arrangements.

At the sight of this, her relatives would not have dared to argue further even if they had wanted to. They could only swallow their anger and say no more. Jia Lian also told Lin Zhi-xiao to put the two hundred taels into the year’s accounts, spreading it among different expense entries to make the sum up. He privately gave Bao Er some silver as well and consoled him.

‘Another day I’ll find you a good wife.’

With both his standing and his purse improved, what had Bao Er to object to? He went on currying favour with Jia Lian as before. Of that we need say no more.

Inside, though Xi-feng was uneasy at heart, she went on pretending to take no notice. When there was no one else in the room, she drew Patience towards her with a smile.

‘I poured too much damned wine down my throat yesterday. Don’t hold it against me. Where did I hit you? Let me see.’

‘You didn’t hit me hard,’ Patience replied.

Just then someone announced that the ladies and girls had all come in. To learn what followed, read the next chapter.
