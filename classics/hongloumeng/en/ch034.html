Chapter 34 Feeling within feeling moves a younger cousin’s heart; Error upon error brings a brother mistaken counsel

Chapter end

Now that Grandmother Jia, Lady Wang and the others had gone, Aroma came and sat beside Bao-yu. With tears in her eyes she asked, ‘How did they come to beat you like this?’

‘It was only about those things,’ Bao-yu sighed. ‘What’s the use of asking? It hurts terribly below the waist. Have a look and see where I’m injured.’

Aroma gently slipped her hands under him and began pulling down his undergarment. At the slightest movement Bao-yu clenched his teeth and cried out, and she hastily stopped. Only after three or four such attempts did she get it down. The upper part of his legs was black and blue, with hard, swollen welts four fingers wide. Aroma clenched her own teeth.

‘Dear heaven! How could he bring himself to hit so hard? If you’d listened to a single word I said, it would never have come to this. Thank goodness your bones and sinews aren’t damaged. What should we have done if you’d been crippled?’

As she spoke, the maids announced, ‘Miss Bao is here.’ There was no time to put his undergarment back on, so Aroma covered him with a lined gauze coverlet. Bao-chai came in carrying a single medicinal pill in her hand.

‘Grind this up with wine tonight and put it on him,’ she told Aroma. ‘Once it disperses the heat-toxin in the stagnant blood, he should soon be better.’

She handed it to Aroma and asked, ‘Are you feeling any better now?’

Bao-yu thanked her, said he was, and asked her to sit down. Seeing that he could open his eyes and speak, quite unlike before, Bao-chai felt much reassured. She nodded and sighed.

‘If only you’d listened to advice sooner, this need never have happened. It isn’t just Grandmother and Aunt who are distressed. Even we feel the pain when we see you.’

She hastily swallowed the rest of what she had begun to say. Regretting her haste, she blushed and lowered her head. To Bao-yu those words were full of tenderness and intimacy, with a world of meaning behind them. Seeing her suddenly break off, blush and hang her head, doing nothing but finger her sash, in a confusion of shy embarrassment beyond description, he felt his heart fill with delight. His pain had long since flown beyond the clouds, and he thought:

‘I’ve only had a few blows, and already each of them shows such pity and grief for me. How lovely to see, how touching, how worthy of affection and respect!

‘If some calamity were really to kill me, who knows how deeply they would mourn? If this is how they feel, I could die this very moment. To have such feelings from them would leave me nothing to regret, even if everything I might have achieved in life went pouring down the river. If I couldn’t be content in the world of shadows then, I should be a very foolish ghost indeed.’

As he thought this, he heard Bao-chai ask Aroma, ‘What suddenly made him so angry that he began to beat him?’

Aroma repeated what Tealeaf had told her. Bao-yu had known nothing of Jia Huan’s words until she spoke of them now. When she brought Xue Pan into it as well, he feared Bao-chai would take it to heart and hastily stopped her.

‘Cousin Xue has never been like that. You mustn’t go guessing at random.’

Bao-chai knew he had interrupted Aroma for fear of hurting her feelings. She thought to herself:

‘Beaten to this state, with more pain than he can bear, and still he is so considerate, so afraid of giving offence. He really does take thought for us.

‘With all this thoughtfulness, why can’t he put some effort into the important things in the world outside? His father would be pleased, and he wouldn’t have to suffer like this. Of course he stopped Aroma because he was afraid I’d take it to heart. But does he suppose I don’t know how my brother indulges his every whim and desire, without a thought for the consequences?

‘The uproar he made over Qin Zhong that time! He is bound to be worse now than he was then.’

She smiled and said, ‘There’s no need to blame this person or that. As I see it, Cousin Bao’s own conduct is at fault. His father is angry because he chooses to associate with such people. Even if my brother spoke carelessly and happened to mention him, he can’t have meant to stir up trouble. For one thing, it was only the truth; for another, he never considers these little precautions against giving people the wrong idea. Miss Aroma, you’ve grown up knowing someone as considerate as Cousin Bao. You’ve never met a man who fears neither heaven nor earth and says whatever comes into his head.’

As soon as Bao-yu had cut her short, Aroma had realised how rash she had been to mention Xue Pan. Already afraid of having embarrassed Bao-chai, she now felt so ashamed that she could say nothing. Bao-yu, meanwhile, found in Bao-chai’s words both high-minded good sense and a release from his own misgivings, and felt even happier than before.

He was just about to speak when Bao-chai rose.

‘I’ll come and see you again tomorrow. Take good care of yourself. I brought some medicine and gave it to Aroma just now. Put it on tonight and you’re sure to be better.’

She went out, and Aroma hurried after her to see her beyond the courtyard.

‘You’ve been so thoughtful, miss. When Master Bao is better, he’ll come himself to thank you.’

Bao-chai turned back with a smile. ‘There’s nothing to thank me for. Just persuade him to rest quietly and keep from filling his head with foolish fancies. Don’t disturb Grandmother, Aunt or any of the others about it. If word should reach his father, he might do nothing at the time, but when something brings it to mind later, there’s sure to be trouble.’

With that she went away.

Aroma turned back, deeply grateful to Bao-chai. She found Bao-yu silent and thoughtful, looking neither quite awake nor quite asleep, so she withdrew to wash and dress her hair. Bao-yu lay silently on the bed, but the pain in his buttocks was like needles probing and knives gouging, with a burning heat like fire. At the slightest turn he could not help crying out.

It was nearly evening. Aroma had gone, but two or three maids remained to wait on him. Since he needed nothing just then, he said, ‘Go and wash and do your hair. Come back when I call.’ They all withdrew.

Bao-yu lay in a drowsy stupor. He saw Jiang Yu-han come in and tell him how the Prince of Zhongshun’s household had seized him. Then Golden appeared, weeping as she told him how she had thrown herself into the well for his sake. Half dreaming, half awake, Bao-yu paid little heed. Suddenly he felt someone shaking him and dimly heard a sound of grief. He started out of his dream and opened his eyes. It was none other than Dai-yu.

Still afraid that he was dreaming, he hastily raised himself to examine her face. Her eyes were swollen like peaches, her whole face shining with tears. If it was not Dai-yu, who else could it be?

He wanted to look longer, but the pain below his waist was more than he could bear. Unable to hold himself up, he cried out and fell back. Then he sighed.

‘What did you come running here for? The sun may have gone down, but the heat hasn’t left the ground. Walking back and forth, you’ll make yourself ill with it again. I’ve been beaten, but it doesn’t hurt. I’m only putting this on to fool them, so they’ll spread the story outside and Father will hear. It’s all a pretence. You mustn’t take it seriously.’

Dai-yu was not weeping aloud. Yet this silent weeping, which stifled her breath and choked her throat, was all the more terrible. At his words a thousand things rose in her heart, but she could not utter them. Only after a long while did she manage to say between sobs, ‘Do change your ways from now on!’

Bao-yu gave a long sigh. ‘Rest easy. Don’t say such things. Even if I died for these people, I should do it willingly!’

He had scarcely finished when someone outside the courtyard announced, ‘Mrs Lian is here.’ Knowing that Xi-feng had come, Dai-yu hurriedly stood up.

‘I’ll go out through the back courtyard. I’ll come again later.’

Bao-yu caught her by the hand. ‘How strange! Why have you suddenly become afraid of her?’

Dai-yu stamped her foot in agitation and whispered, ‘Look at my eyes! She’ll only make fun of me for her own amusement.’

He let go at once. In two or three quick steps Dai-yu was round the bed and out through the back courtyard. Xi-feng had already entered from the front.

‘Are you any better?’ she asked Bao-yu. ‘If there’s anything you’d like to eat, send someone to my rooms for it.’

Aunt Xue came next, and presently Grandmother Jia sent someone as well.

By lamplighting time Bao-yu had taken only two mouthfuls of soup before sinking into a heavy sleep. Then Zhou Rui’s wife, Wu Xin-deng’s wife and Zheng Hao-shi’s wife, older women who often came to the house, arrived, having heard of his beating. Aroma hurried out to meet them.

‘You’re a moment too late, aunties,’ she said softly, smiling. ‘Master Bao has only just fallen asleep.’

She took them to sit in the other room and poured tea for them. They sat quietly for a little while, then told her, ‘When Master Bao wakes, tell him we came.’

Aroma promised and saw them out. She was just turning back when an old woman arrived from Lady Wang, saying that Her Ladyship wanted one of Master Bao’s attendants. Aroma thought for a moment, then went back and quietly told Skybright, Musk, Sandal, Ripple and the others:

‘Her Ladyship has sent for someone. Stay in the room and take good care of things. I’ll be back as soon as I can.’

She went straight out of the garden with the old woman to the main apartments. Lady Wang was sitting on a couch for keeping cool, fanning herself with a palm-leaf fan.

‘Any of them would have done,’ she said when Aroma appeared. ‘Now you’ve left him too. Who’s waiting on him?’

Aroma hastily answered with a respectful smile. ‘Master Bao has just settled into a sound sleep. Those four or five girls have improved now and know how to look after him. Please don’t worry, ma’am. I was afraid you might have some instructions, and if I sent one of them she might not understand properly and cause a delay.’

‘There’s nothing in particular. I only wanted to ask how bad his pain is now.’

‘I’ve put on the medicine Miss Bao brought. He’s better than he was.

‘Before, he was in too much pain to lie still. Now he’s sleeping soundly. That shows he must be better.’

‘Has he had anything to eat?’ Lady Wang asked.

‘He took two mouthfuls of the bowl of soup Her Old Ladyship sent. He kept saying he was thirsty and wanted some sour-plum drink. I thought sour plums were astringent. He’d only just been beaten and hadn’t been allowed to cry out, so all that distress was bound to leave heat-toxin and heated blood pent up in his heart. If he drank that and it trapped them there, what should we do if he developed a serious illness?

‘So I talked to him for ages and finally persuaded him not to have it. I only mixed him a drink with the rose syrup preserved in sugar. He had half a bowl, then said he was tired of it, that it didn’t taste sweet and fragrant any more.’

‘Oh dear, you should have come and told me sooner,’ said Lady Wang. ‘Someone sent me two bottles of essence the other day. I meant to give him some, but I was afraid he’d waste it, so I didn’t. If he’s tired of that rose syrup, take him two bottles of this.

‘You need only a teaspoonful in a bowl of water, and the fragrance is quite wonderful.’

She called Cai-yun. ‘Bring those bottles of essence that came the other day.’

‘Just bring two,’ said Aroma. ‘Any more would only be wasted.

‘When he needs more, I can come and get it. That will do just as well.’

Cai-yun went away and, after some time, brought back two bottles and handed them to Aroma. They were little glass bottles, about three cun high, with screw-on silver caps. One pale-yellow paper label read ‘Osmanthus Essence’, the other ‘Rose Essence’.

‘What precious stuff!’ Aroma said, smiling. ‘How much can a tiny bottle like this hold?’

‘It’s made for presentation to the throne,’ Lady Wang said. ‘Didn’t you see the pale-yellow labels? Keep it safe for him, and don’t let him waste it.’

Aroma promised and was about to go when Lady Wang called her back.

‘Wait. I’ve remembered something I wanted to ask you.’

Aroma hurried back. Seeing that there was no one else in the room, Lady Wang asked, ‘I seem to have heard that Bao-yu was beaten today because Huan said something to his father. Have you heard anything about that? If you have, tell me. I shan’t make a fuss and let people know it came from you.’

‘I haven’t heard that,’ said Aroma. ‘It was because Master Bao had been keeping an actor to himself, and the people came to His Lordship to demand him back. That’s why he was beaten.’

Lady Wang shook her head. ‘That was part of it. There was another reason too.’

‘I really don’t know of any other reason. But let me be bold enough to say something foolish to you today, ma’am. To be fair…’

She broke off and swallowed the rest.

‘Go on,’ said Lady Wang.

Aroma smiled. ‘If you won’t be angry, ma’am, I’ll say it.’

‘Why should I be angry? Say what you have to say.’

‘To be fair, Master Bao does need a lesson or two from His Lordship.

‘If His Lordship doesn’t keep him in hand, there’s no knowing what he may do in future.’

Lady Wang clasped her hands and murmured, ‘Amitabha!’ She could not help calling out to Aroma at once:

‘My child! Thank heaven you understand too. That is just what I feel.

‘When have I not known how to discipline a son? When Master Zhu was alive, how did I bring him up? Can I have forgotten how to bring up a son now? There is a reason for it. I think to myself that I’m nearly fifty, with only this one left. He’s frail, and his grandmother treasures him so. If I were too strict, and anything happened to him, or his grandmother made herself ill with anger, the whole household would be in an uproar. Wouldn’t that make things worse? That’s how I’ve let him become so spoilt. I keep talking till my mouth is worn out: advising him, reasoning with him, scolding in anger, crying. He’s good at the time, but afterwards it’s as though nothing had been said. He won’t stop until he’s suffered for it. But if they beat him beyond recovery, who will I have to depend on?’

As she spoke, her tears began to fall.

Lady Wang’s grief touched Aroma’s heart, and she too began to weep.

‘You bore him, ma’am. How could you not feel for him? Even for us servants, it would be a blessing if we could serve him and all come through safely. If things go on like this, we can’t even hope for that. What day, what hour passes without my trying to advise him? But nothing I say will bring him to his senses. Then those people are so ready to seek his company, so it isn’t entirely his fault. In the end, it’s always we who are in the wrong for trying to reason with him. Now you’ve brought it up, ma’am, there’s something else that’s been troubling me. I’ve often wanted to come and ask what you think should be done. Only I was afraid you might suspect me. Then it wouldn’t just be that I’d wasted my breath. There’d be nowhere left to bury me.’

Hearing that there was something behind this, Lady Wang hastily asked, ‘My child, tell me whatever is on your mind. Lately I’ve heard everyone praising you, both openly and in private. I thought it was simply that you took good care of Bao-yu and were pleasant to the others. Those little virtues were why I began treating you on the same footing as the senior concubines.

‘But what you’ve just said is sound good sense, exactly what I’ve been thinking. Tell me whatever you have to say. Only don’t let anyone else know.’

‘I have nothing else to say, ma’am. I only wanted your instructions: couldn’t some way be found to have Master Bao move out of the garden again?’

Lady Wang was startled. She caught Aroma’s hand.

‘Has Bao-yu been up to something with someone?’

‘Please don’t suspect anything, ma’am!’ Aroma answered hastily. ‘There’s been nothing of the kind. It’s only my humble opinion. Master Bao is growing up now, and so are the young ladies in the garden. Miss Lin and Miss Bao are his cousins, one his father’s sister’s daughter and the other his mother’s sister’s daughter. Cousins they may be, but there is still the distinction between a boy and girls. Living together day and night isn’t fitting. One can’t help worrying, and it doesn’t look right to outsiders either. These are family matters. As the saying goes, “In quiet times, think of what may go wrong.” So much of the senseless trouble in the world starts with something done quite innocently. Someone with a suspicious mind sees it, takes it as deliberate, and talks it into something bad.

‘We simply must guard against it beforehand. You know Master Bao’s usual ways, ma’am. He does so love playing about among us girls. If we aren’t careful, and something goes a little wrong sooner or later, whether it’s true or not, there are so many people and so many tongues. What restraint do those malicious mouths ever show? When they’re pleased, they make him out better than a bodhisattva; when they aren’t, they make him worse than a beast. If people speak well of Master Bao in future, it means no more than that we all go on our way and nothing happens. But if anyone says one bad word, never mind us: even being ground to pieces for our countless offences would be a small thing. Wouldn’t his reputation and character be ruined for the rest of his life? And how could you face His Lordship? There’s another saying: “A gentleman guards against trouble before it comes.” Better to take precautions now.

‘You have so much to attend to, ma’am. Naturally you can’t think of everything at once. It would be one thing if we hadn’t thought of it; but having thought of it, we’d be all the more to blame if we didn’t tell you plainly. Lately it’s kept me anxious day and night, and I haven’t been able to speak of it to anyone. Only the lamp knows.’

These words struck Lady Wang like thunder and lightning, bringing Golden’s affair directly to mind. Her gratitude and affection for Aroma knew no bounds. She hastily smiled.

‘My child, to think you have such judgement, and can consider everything so thoroughly! Of course I’ve thought of it too, but things kept happening and I forgot. What you’ve said today has reminded me. You’ve taken such pains to protect the name and standing of both my son and me. Truly, I never knew how good you were.

‘Well, go back now. I shall decide what to do. But there’s one thing more. Now that you’ve spoken like this, I’m entrusting him to you. Whatever happens, watch over him. In protecting him, you’ll be protecting me. I certainly shan’t let you go unrewarded.’

Aroma promised over and over, and left. When she got back Bao-yu had just woken, and she told him about the essences. Delighted, he immediately had some mixed for him to try. It was indeed wonderfully fragrant. But he was thinking of Dai-yu and longing to send someone to her. Afraid of Aroma, he devised a way round her and first sent her to borrow a book from Bao-chai.

Once Aroma had gone, Bao-yu called Skybright.

‘Go to Miss Lin’s and see what she’s doing. If she asks about me, tell her I’m better.’

‘What am I to go there for, out of the blue?’ said Skybright. ‘You must at least give me a message, so it looks like an errand.’

‘There’s nothing to say.’

‘Then give me something to take, or something to fetch. Otherwise how am I to strike up a conversation when I get there?’

Bao-yu thought for a moment, then reached for two handkerchiefs and tossed them to her with a smile.

‘Very well. Say I sent you to give her these.’

‘That’s stranger still! What would she want with these two handkerchiefs, neither new nor worn out? She’ll only be cross again and say you’re making fun of her.’

‘Don’t worry,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘She’ll understand.’

Skybright had no choice but to take the handkerchiefs to Naiad’s House. Chun-xian was hanging handkerchiefs over the railing to dry. Seeing her come in, she hastily waved a hand.

‘She’s gone to bed.’

Skybright went inside. The room was quite dark, with no lamp lit. Dai-yu was already in bed and asked who was there.

‘Skybright,’ she answered quickly.

‘What is it?’

‘Master Bao has sent you some handkerchiefs, miss.’

Dai-yu was puzzled. Why should he send her handkerchiefs?

‘Who gave them to him?’ she asked. ‘They must be very fine ones. Tell him to keep them for someone else. I’ve no use for them just now.’

Skybright smiled. ‘They aren’t new. Just old ones he uses every day.’

This puzzled Dai-yu still more. She searched her mind carefully, and after a little thought suddenly understood.

‘Put them down and go,’ she said at once.

Skybright could only set them down and leave. All the way back she turned the matter over, unable to make out what it meant.

Now that Dai-yu had divined the meaning of the handkerchiefs, her whole being was swept away. Bao-yu had taken such pains because he understood the pains of her heart: that made her glad. Yet she did not know what would come of those feelings: that made her grieve. To send two old handkerchiefs out of the blue! If it were not for his understanding of her deepest feelings, the mere sight of them would make her laugh. Then she thought of his having someone bring them to her in secret, and was afraid. And when she considered her own constant weeping, it seemed so pointless that she felt ashamed. Turning these thoughts this way and that, she suddenly felt all her inward parts blaze with heat. Her lingering feelings would not release her. She called for a lamp, quite forgetting questions of propriety and suspicion, ground ink at the desk, dipped her brush, and wrote swiftly on the two old handkerchiefs:

In vain my eyes store tears; in vain the tears fall down. For whom are they shed in secret, scattered to no end?

You understood, and took such care to send this square of merfolk silk: How could it leave my heart untouched by grief?

Second

Pearls spill and jade beads roll: I can only weep in secret, All day without a heart for anything, all day idle.

From pillow and sleeve they cannot easily be wiped away; Let them lie there, drop on drop and stain on stain.

Third

Coloured threads cannot gather the pearls upon my face; The ancient traces by the Xiang River have grown faint.

A thousand stems of bamboo stand outside my window too: I wonder, have these fragrant tear-marks stained them yet?

Dai-yu was about to write more when she felt her whole body grow hot and her face begin to burn. She went to the dressing table, lifted the brocade cover from the mirror and looked. Her cheeks were crimson. Admiring herself, she thought her colour put peach blossom to shame, not knowing that illness was taking root in that very moment. Presently she went to bed, still holding the handkerchiefs and pondering them. Of this we need say no more.

When Aroma went to see Bao-chai, she found that Bao-chai was not in the garden: she had gone to her mother’s. Aroma returned empty-handed. Bao-chai did not come back until the second watch. Knowing Xue Pan’s nature as she did, she had already half suspected that he had prompted someone to inform against Bao-yu. Hearing Aroma say it too had made her all the more convinced.

But Aroma’s information came from Tealeaf, and Tealeaf had merely guessed in private. With no facts to go on, he had settled it in his own mind that Xue Pan was responsible. Xue Pan owed it all to his usual reputation. This time he had actually done nothing of the kind, yet people fastened the charge on him so firmly that he could scarcely open his mouth to defend himself.

That day he came home after drinking outside and went to see his mother. Bao-chai was there. After a little idle talk, he asked, ‘I hear Cousin Bao has had a hard time of it. What was it about?’

Aunt Xue was already upset over this. At his question she clenched her teeth.

‘You good-for-nothing! It’s all your doing, and you still have the face to ask!’

Xue Pan was taken aback. ‘What have I done?’ he asked hastily.

‘Still playing the fool! Everyone knows you told on him, and you’re still denying it.’

‘If everyone said I’d killed someone, would you believe that too?’

‘Even your sister knows you told. Surely you don’t think she’d falsely accuse you?’

Bao-chai hastened to intervene. ‘Mother, Brother, please stop shouting. If we’re calm about it, we can get to the rights and wrongs.’

Then she turned to Xue Pan. ‘Whether you told or not, it’s over now. There’s no need to argue it out and turn a little matter into a big one.

‘All I ask is that from now on you spend less time making trouble outside and interfering in other people’s affairs. You all go roaming about together every day, and you’re so heedless. If nothing happens afterwards, well and good. But if something does happen, everyone will suspect you, even if you had nothing to do with it. Never mind anyone else: I should be the first to suspect you.’

Xue Pan was a blunt man who spoke his mind, and he had never been able to bear this sort of veiled insinuation. Now Bao-chai was telling him to stop roaming about, while his mother accused him of wagging his tongue and causing Bao-yu’s beating. He was already jumping with agitation, protesting his innocence with oaths staked on his own life.

‘Who’s fastening this filth on me?’ he cursed. ‘I’ll knock the teeth out of the bastard! It’s plain enough: Bao-yu’s been beaten, and they’re trying to curry favour by using me as a screen. Is Bao-yu the King of Heaven? His father gives him a beating, and the whole family has to make a commotion for days! That time he’d misbehaved and Uncle gave him a couple of blows, Grandmother somehow found out afterwards, said it was Cousin Zhen’s doing, and sent for him and scolded him for nothing. Now they’re dragging me in as well! Let them. I’m not afraid. I might as well go in and beat Bao-yu to death, then pay for his life with mine. That will settle it for everyone.’

Shouting as he went, he seized a door-bar and started off. Aunt Xue caught hold of him in alarm.

‘You wicked wretch! Are you trying to get yourself killed? Who are you going to hit? Hit me first!’

Xue Pan’s eyes bulged like bronze bells. ‘What do you want of me? You won’t let me go, and you won’t stop accusing me for nothing! As long as Bao-yu lives, I shall have to bear the blame. Better if we all died and had some peace.’

Bao-chai hurried forward to reason with him too. ‘Do try to be patient.

‘Mother is so distressed, and instead of trying to comfort her you make all this fuss. Even if it weren’t Mother, even if it were someone else trying to reason with you, it would be for your own good. But it only seems to make you lose your temper.’

‘Now you talk like that!’ said Xue Pan. ‘You started it all!’

‘You blame me for speaking, but never blame your own heedless behaviour.’

‘You’re always blaming me for being heedless. Why don’t you blame Bao-yu for the way he goes about inviting trouble? Never mind anything else. Take that business with Bijou the other day. We’d met him ten times or so, and I’d never said a single affectionate word to him. How was it that Bao-yu met him, didn’t even know his name yet, and gave him his sash? Are you going to blame that on my tongue as well?’

‘Why bring that up again?’ Aunt Xue and Bao-chai cried in agitation. ‘That’s exactly what he was beaten for! It must have been you who told.’

‘You’ll be the death of me with this!

‘I’m not angry at being accused of telling. What angers me is the whole world being turned upside down over one Bao-yu.’

‘Who’s made a commotion?’ said Bao-chai. ‘You’re the one who started brandishing weapons, and now you blame everyone else.’

Everything Bao-chai said was reasonable, and Xue Pan could find no answer. She was even harder to answer than his mother. He wanted to find something that would silence her, so that no one would dare contradict him again. Still in the heat of his anger, he gave no thought to the weight of his words.

‘My dear sister, there’s no need to quarrel with me. I’ve known what you were thinking for a long time. Mother told me once that this gold of yours could only be properly matched with someone who had jade. You took it to heart, and when you saw Bao-yu had that wretched thing, naturally you began taking his side at every turn.’

Before he had finished, Bao-chai was stunned with anger. She caught hold of Aunt Xue and cried, ‘Mother, listen to what Brother’s saying!’

Seeing his sister weep, Xue Pan knew he had been rash. But in a huff he went off to his own room and to bed, and we shall leave him there.

Aunt Xue was trembling all over with anger, but tried to comfort Bao-chai.

‘You know that wretch has never talked sense. Tomorrow I’ll make him apologise to you.’

Bao-chai’s heart was full of hurt and indignation. She wanted to give way to it, yet feared distressing her mother. There was nothing for it but to take leave of her in tears and return to her own rooms, where she wept the whole night. Next morning she had no heart to wash or dress her hair. She made herself ready anyhow and came out to see her mother. By chance she met Dai-yu standing alone in the shade of some flowers. Dai-yu asked where she was going.

‘Home,’ Bao-chai said, and walked on as she spoke. Dai-yu saw how listless she looked as she went, and the traces of tears round her eyes, so unlike her usual self. Smiling, she called after her:

‘Take care of yourself too, cousin. Even two vats full of tears won’t cure the wounds from a beating!’
