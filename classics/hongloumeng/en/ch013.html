Chapter 13 Qin Ke-qing is honoured in death as a Dragon Guard’s wife; Wang Xi-feng helps to manage the Ning mansion

Chapter end

Ever since Jia Lian had taken Dai-yu to Yangzhou, Xi-feng had felt thoroughly out of sorts. Each evening she did little more than talk and laugh with Patience for a while before going wearily to bed.

One night the two of them sat by the brazier, tired of their embroidery beneath the lamp. Xi-feng had already ordered the embroidered quilts to be well warmed and scented. They lay down and counted off the days of the journey on their fingers, reckoning how far the travellers ought to have gone. Before she knew it, the third watch had begun. Patience was fast asleep. Xi-feng’s bright eyes were only just growing heavy when, dimly, she saw Qin-shi come in from outside. Smiling, she said, ‘How soundly you sleep, Aunt! I am going back today, and you have not even come to see me on my way. We have always been so close that I could not bear to leave you. That is why I have come to say goodbye. There is also one wish I have not fulfilled. I must tell you about it, Aunt. Someone else might be of no use.’

Still in a haze, Xi-feng asked, ‘What is troubling you? Whatever it is, leave it to me.’

‘Aunt,’ said Qin-shi, ‘you are a hero among women. Even the men in their caps and girdles cannot outdo you. How can you not know a couple of common sayings? “When the moon is full it begins to wane; when water is full it spills over.” And again, “The higher the climb, the harder the fall.” Our family has now enjoyed almost a hundred years of splendour. Suppose one day joy turned to sorrow, and the saying came true: “When the tree falls, the monkeys scatter.” Would all our claims to be an old family of learning not have been empty words?’

These words greatly heartened Xi-feng and filled her with awed respect. ‘You have thought this out very well,’ she said quickly. ‘But what can we do to keep ourselves safe for ever?’

Qin-shi gave a cold laugh. ‘How foolish you are, Aunt.

‘At the furthest point of adversity, prosperity returns. Honour and disgrace have followed one another in a circle since ancient times. How could human effort keep things always as they are? Still, if we use our prosperity to provide an estate for the lean times ahead, that might indeed be called lasting security. Everything is in order now except for two things. If we dealt with them in this way, we could ensure our future.’

Xi-feng asked what they were.

‘At present,’ Qin-shi replied, ‘sacrifices are offered at the ancestral graves in all four seasons, but there is no fixed fund of money and grain for them. Secondly, the clan school has been established, but it has no assured provision either. While we are prosperous, of course, there is no shortage of money for sacrifices or for the school. But where will these two expenses come from when we have fallen on hard times? My settled view is that, while we are rich and honoured, we should buy more farms, houses and land near the ancestral graves, so that their income will meet both expenses. Move the clan school there too. Bring the elders and younger members of the clan together to draw up rules. Each branch should take its turn for a year, managing the land, the money and grain, the sacrifices and the school’s provision. With this rotation there would be no rivalry, nor such abuses as mortgaging or selling the property. Even if we were convicted of a crime and everything else confiscated, the government would not seize an estate devoted to ancestral sacrifices. Should the family decline, our descendants could go home to study and farm. They would have somewhere to fall back on, and the sacrifices could continue for ever. To suppose our present glory will never end, and give no thought to the future, is no lasting plan.

‘Very soon, you will see, there is to be another extraordinary piece of good fortune. Our splendour will be like oil seething over a fierce fire, like flowers heaped on brocade. But remember: it is only a moment’s glory, a brief time of happiness. Never forget the saying, “Every great feast must break up.” Unless you make provision now, when the time comes regret may be of no use.’

‘What good fortune?’ Xi-feng asked eagerly.

‘Heaven’s secrets must not be revealed. But for the sake of the affection we have shared, Aunt, I will leave you two lines before I go. Be sure to remember them.’ And she recited:

When the three springs are gone, all flowers end;

Each must seek a doorway of their own.

Xi-feng was about to question her further when four rapid strokes rang out from the cloud-shaped message gong at the second gate and startled her awake. Someone reported, ‘Mrs Rong of the Ning mansion has died.’ A cold sweat broke out all over Xi-feng. She sat stunned for a time, then had to hurry into her clothes and go to Lady Wang.

By then the whole household knew. Everyone was astonished, and all felt some misgiving. Those of the older generation remembered her filial devotion; those of her own, her warmth and affection; the younger generation, her kindness. Old and young among the servants remembered how she had pitied the poor, cared for the lowly, cherished the old and loved the young. There was not one who did not weep and wail.

But let us leave these matters. Since Lin Dai-yu had gone away, Bao-yu too had felt lonely and desolate. He no longer played with anyone, but went listlessly to bed each night. Now, hearing in his sleep that Qin-shi was dead, he rolled over and scrambled up. It was as though a knife had pierced his heart. Unable to bear it, he gave a cry and brought up a mouthful of blood. Aroma and the others hurried to support him, asking what was wrong. They were going to tell Grandmother Jia and send for a doctor.

‘Do not fuss,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘It is nothing. A sudden rush of fire has attacked the heart, and the blood has strayed from its channels.’

As he spoke he got up, called for clothes to change into and went to see Grandmother Jia, determined to go over at once. Aroma was still uneasy, but dared not stop him and could only let him have his way.

‘Someone has only just breathed her last there. The place is unclean,’ Grandmother Jia said when he asked to go. ‘Besides, the wind is strong at night. Tomorrow morning will be soon enough.’

Bao-yu would not listen. Grandmother Jia ordered a carriage and sent extra servants to escort and protect him.

They went straight to the Ning mansion. Its gates stood wide open, with lanterns on either side making the entrance as bright as day. People hurried noisily in and out, and the wailing within seemed enough to shake the mountains. Bao-yu got out of the carriage and rushed to the room where the body lay. After weeping bitterly there, he went to see You-shi, only to find her in bed with a recurrence of her old stomach pains.

He then came out to see Jia Zhen. Jia Dai-ru had arrived, bringing Jia Chi, Jia Xiao, Jia Dun, Jia She, Jia Zheng, Jia Cong, Jia Bin, Jia Heng, Jia Guang, Jia Chen, Jia Qiong, Jia Lin, Jia Qiang, Jia Chang, Jia Ling, Jia Yun, Jia Qin, Jia Zhen, Jia Ping, Jia Zao, Jia Heng, Jia Fen, Jia Fang, Jia Lan, Jia Jun and Jia Zhi. Jia Zhen was dissolved in tears. He was saying to Jia Dai-ru and the others, ‘Who among our household, old or young, our distant relations or our closest friends, did not know that this daughter-in-law of mine was ten times better than my son? Now she has stretched out her legs and gone. This senior branch of the family is finished. There is no one left.’ He began to weep again.

They hastened to comfort him. ‘She has passed away. Tears will do no good now. What matters is to decide how the arrangements are to be made.’

Jia Zhen struck his hands together. ‘How are they to be made? I shall simply give everything I have!’

As he spoke, Qin Ye and Qin Zhong arrived, together with several of You-shi’s relations, including her sisters. Jia Zhen told Jia Qiong, Jia Chen, Jia Lin and Jia Qiang to attend to the guests, and sent for an official from the Yin-yang Office of the Imperial Astronomical Bureau to select the dates. It was duly determined that the coffin should remain for seven times seven, forty-nine days, with formal mourning and the dispatch of notices to begin three days later. For those forty-nine days, a hundred and eight Chan monks alone were engaged to perform the Great Compassion repentance rite in the main hall, delivering the souls of those who had died before and those who would die hereafter, and absolving the dead of their sins. A separate altar was set up in the Tower of Heavenly Fragrance, where ninety-nine Quanzhen Taoist priests were to conduct, for forty-nine days, a Taoist offering rite to reconcile grievances and cleanse karmic burdens. The coffin was then placed in the Garden of Gathered Fragrance. Before it, another fifty eminent monks and fifty eminent Taoist priests performed rites at facing altars at each seven-day observance.

When Jia Jing heard that his eldest grandson’s wife had died, he was so certain that he himself would soon ascend to immortality that he could not possibly return home to be tainted by worldly dust and throw away all his previous efforts. He therefore paid little attention, leaving everything to Jia Zhen.

With his father taking no part, Jia Zhen indulged all the more freely in extravagant display. Several sets of fir boards were inspected for the coffin, but none was good enough. Xue Pan happened to come to pay his condolences and, seeing Jia Zhen in search of fine timber, said, ‘There is a set of boards in our timber shop. The wood is called qiang, or something of the sort. It comes from Iron-net Mountain in the Huang Sea region. Make a coffin of it and it will last ten thousand years without rotting. My late father brought it back years ago. His Highness Prince Yi-zhong originally wanted it, but when things went wrong for him, he never took delivery. It is still sealed up in the shop. Nobody has dared offer a price for it. If you want it, have it brought over.’

Jia Zhen was overjoyed and immediately ordered it to be fetched. When it arrived, they saw that the boards for the sides and bottom were all eight cun thick. Their grain resembled areca nut, their scent sandalwood and musk. Tapped with the hand, they rang like metal or jade. Everyone marvelled and praised them.

‘What is the price?’ Jia Zhen asked, smiling.

Xue Pan smiled too. ‘Even with a thousand taels of silver, you might not find anything like it. Why talk of a price? Just give the men a few taels for their trouble.’

Jia Zhen thanked him repeatedly and at once ordered the sawing, pasting and lacquering to begin.

Jia Zheng tried to dissuade him. ‘I fear this is not something an ordinary person is entitled to use. A coffin of the best fir would be quite sufficient.’ But Jia Zhen at that moment wished he could die in Qin-shi’s place. How could he listen? Then news came that a maid of Qin-shi’s named Gem, on seeing that her mistress was dead, had dashed herself against a pillar and died too.

The occurrence was extraordinary, and the whole clan praised her. Jia Zhen had her laid out and coffined with the rites due to a granddaughter, and her coffin too was placed in the Garden of Gathered Fragrance, in the Ascension Pavilion. A young maid named Jewel, seeing that Qin-shi had left no child, freely offered to become her adopted daughter and pledged herself to break the funeral earthenware basin and go before the coffin as chief mourner. Jia Zhen was delighted. He ordered at once that everyone was henceforth to address Jewel as Miss.

Jewel mourned as an unmarried daughter, weeping before the coffin as though her heart would break. Every member of the clan and everyone in the household now followed the established observances, none daring to depart from them. But Jia Zhen reflected that Jia Rong was only an Imperial Academy student. That would look poor on the funeral streamers and notices of the rites, and it allowed little ceremonial equipment either. The thought made him very unhappy.

By chance, on the fourth day of the first seven-day mourning period, Dai Quan, chief palace eunuch of Daming Palace, first sent someone ahead with funeral offerings, then came in person to present them. He arrived in a grand sedan-chair, with a ceremonial umbrella and gongs sounded before him. Jia Zhen hurried out to receive him and invited him into the Bee-enticing Pavilion for tea. Having made up his mind, he took the opportunity to mention buying an official appointment for Jia Rong.

Dai Quan understood. ‘To make a finer show at the funeral, I suppose?’ he said with a smile.

‘You see it exactly, sir,’ Jia Zhen replied, smiling eagerly.

‘Well, you are in luck. There is a very good vacancy. Two places are unfilled among the three hundred Dragon Guards. Yesterday the Marquis of Xiangyang’s younger brother, the third of the brothers, came to see me. He has already sent fifteen hundred taels of silver to my house. You know what old friends we are. For his grandfather’s sake, I had to promise him something, one way or another. That left one vacancy. Then who should come asking but Fat Feng, the military commissioner of Yongxing, wanting to buy it for his boy. I had no time to give him an answer.

‘But since our own boy wants the appointment, have his particulars written out at once.’

Jia Zhen immediately gave orders: ‘Tell the men in the study to prepare the young master’s particulars with all due care. Quickly!’

The pages dared not delay. A little later one returned with a sheet of red paper. Jia Zhen read it and hastened to hand it to Dai Quan. It ran:

Jia Rong, Imperial Academy student, aged twenty, of Jiangning county, Jiangning prefecture, Jiangnan. Great-grandfather: Jia Dai-hua, formerly Commander of the Metropolitan Garrison, hereditary General of Divine Might of the First Rank. Grandfather: Jia Jing, jinshi of the yimao examination. Father: Jia Zhen, hereditary holder of the third-rank title General of Martial Valour.

After reading it, Dai Quan passed it back to a personal attendant to put away. ‘Take this later to old Zhao, the senior official at the Board of Revenue. Give him my respects and tell him to issue a warrant for a fifth-rank Dragon Guard, together with a certificate. He can enter these particulars on them. Tomorrow I will have the silver weighed out and sent over.’

The attendant assented. Dai Quan then took his leave. Jia Zhen urged him to stay, but could not prevail, and had to see him to the gate. Just before he entered his chair, Jia Zhen asked, ‘Should I take the silver to the Board to be weighed, or send the whole sum to your residence, sir?’

‘If you go to the Board, you will only be out of pocket.

‘Better send a full twelve hundred in silver to my house, and that will settle it.’

Jia Zhen could not thank him enough. ‘When the mourning is over, I shall bring my unworthy son to your residence to offer our thanks in person,’ was all he could say.

They parted. Soon there were more cries clearing the way: the wife of Shi Ding, Marquis of Zhongjing, had arrived. Lady Wang, Lady Xing and Xi-feng had scarcely welcomed her into the principal room when offerings from the households of the Marquis of Jinxiang, the Marquis of Chuanning and the Earl of Shoushan were set before the coffin. A little later the three men stepped out of their chairs, and Jia Zheng and the others hurried to receive them in the main hall. Friends and relations came and went in numbers beyond counting. Throughout those forty-nine days, the street before the Ning mansion was a flowing stretch of white-clad mourners, brightened by the many-coloured ranks of officials coming and going.

Jia Zhen told Jia Rong to put on his ceremonial robes the next day and collect his credentials. All the equipment and insignia displayed before the coffin were arranged according to the rules for a fifth-rank official. The spirit tablet and ritual petitions bore the words: ‘Spirit tablet of Qin-shi of the Jia family, invested by the Celestial Dynasty with the honorific title gongren.’ The street gate of the Garden of Gathered Fragrance stood wide open. Music pavilions were hastily erected on either side, where two bands of musicians in blue robes played at the appointed times. The paired ceremonial insignia stood in ranks as straight and even as if cut into line by axe and blade.

Two more scarlet boards, bearing large gilt characters, stood facing one another outside the gate. On them was written: ‘Dragon Guard, Imperial Bodyguard, Guardian of the Inner Court’s Forbidden Ways.’ Opposite, a high platform had been erected for proclamations. The monks and Taoists displayed their notices at the facing altars. In large characters the notices announced: ‘Funeral of Qin-shi of the Jia family, bearer of the title gongren, wife of the Dragon Guard and Imperial Bodyguard charged with protecting the Inner Court, and wife of the eldest grandson in the hereditary line of the Duke of Ning.

‘Here in the central land of the Four Great Continents, in this realm of peace which reverently receives Heaven’s mandate, Wan-xu, Chief of the Buddhist Registry, presiding over the teaching of emptiness and stillness, and Ye Sheng, Chief of the Taoist Registry, presiding over the teaching of the Primordial Origin and the Three-in-One, and their fellows, reverently perform the fasting rites, bowing to Heaven and prostrating themselves before the Buddha.’ There were also such passages as ‘Respectfully inviting the monastery guardians, the jiedi and the divine officers of merit’, and ‘May sacred grace be spread over all, and divine might hold distant evils in check: a Water-and-Land rite of forty-nine days to dispel calamity, cleanse karmic burdens and bring peace.’ We need not record it all.

Although Jia Zhen was now satisfied on these counts, You-shi’s old illness had returned and she could not manage affairs inside. He feared that titled ladies coming and going would not receive the proper courtesies, and people would laugh at him. This made him uneasy. As he was fretting, Bao-yu, who stood beside him, asked, ‘Everything seems to be settled, Cousin. What is still worrying you?’

Jia Zhen told him there was no one to take charge inside.

‘That is easily put right,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘I can recommend someone to manage things for you this month. I guarantee she will do it properly.’

‘Who?’ Jia Zhen asked at once.

There were still many friends and relations sitting about, so Bao-yu could not say openly. He went to Jia Zhen and whispered a few words in his ear. Delighted, Jia Zhen rose at once. ‘Just the person! Let us go now.’ Taking Bao-yu with him, he excused himself to the company and made for the principal room.

It happened not to be one of the principal days of observance, and there were few visitors. Only a handful of close female relations were inside, with Lady Xing, Lady Wang, Xi-feng and the women of the clan to keep them company. At the announcement, ‘The master is coming in,’ the married women of the household scattered in alarm, scarcely able to conceal themselves fast enough. Xi-feng alone rose unhurriedly.

Jia Zhen was somewhat ill himself, besides being worn down by excessive grief, and came slowly in on a stick.

‘You are unwell, and you have had so much to do these past few days,’ Lady Xing and the others said. ‘You ought to be resting. What have you come in for?’

Supporting himself on his stick, he struggled to bend his knees, meaning to kneel in greeting and acknowledge their trouble. Lady Xing and the others quickly told Bao-yu to support him, and ordered a chair to be brought. Jia Zhen firmly refused to sit. With a forced smile he said, ‘Your nephew has come to ask a favour of you two aunts, and of my sister here.’

‘What is it?’ they asked.

‘As you know, Aunt, your grandson’s wife is dead, and your nephew’s wife has fallen ill as well. Affairs inside here are in a very poor state.

‘If my sister would condescend to spend a month managing them for me, I could be at ease.’

‘So that is it!’ Lady Xing said, smiling. ‘Your sister is working in your second aunt’s household now. You need only speak to her.’

Lady Wang hastened to say, ‘She is only a child. When has she ever dealt with anything of this sort? If she makes a muddle of it, you will only be laughed at. Better trouble someone else.’

‘Aunt, I can guess what you mean,’ Jia Zhen replied with a smile. ‘You are afraid of tiring her. If you mean she cannot manage it, I guarantee she can. Even if she makes some small mistake, people will still think she has done well.

‘Even as a child, playing games, she knew how to take charge and make decisions. Now she is married and managing things over there, she is far more practised and capable. I have thought about it for days. There is no one but her. If you will not do it for my sake or my wife’s, Aunt, do it for the one who has died!’ Tears rolled down his face.

Lady Wang’s real concern was that Xi-feng had never managed a funeral. She feared the girl would fail to put things in order and make herself a laughing-stock. Now that Jia Zhen had pleaded so earnestly, she was beginning to relent, but still sat looking intently at Xi-feng.

Xi-feng had always loved taking work upon herself and displaying her abilities. Though she managed the household well, she had never been responsible for a wedding or a funeral, and feared that people were not yet convinced of her powers. She had longed for just such an opportunity. When Jia Zhen made his appeal, she was already delighted. At first Lady Wang had refused, but now, seeing how earnestly he spoke and how Lady Wang was beginning to give way, Xi-feng said, ‘My cousin asks so sincerely, Your Ladyship. Do let me do it.’

‘Can you manage it?’ Lady Wang asked her quietly.

‘Why should I not? My cousin has already settled all the important business outside. It is only a matter of looking after things inside. If there is anything I do not know, I can ask Your Ladyship.’

Lady Wang found this reasonable and said no more. Seeing that Xi-feng had agreed, Jia Zhen smiled again. ‘There is no help for it: I must ask you to take the trouble, Sister. Let me pay my respects to you here first. When it is all over, I shall come to your house to thank you.’ He bowed with clasped hands, and Xi-feng hastened to return the courtesy.

Jia Zhen hurriedly took the Ning mansion’s authorisation tally from his sleeve and told Bao-yu to give it to Xi-feng. ‘Do exactly as you please, Sister. Whatever you need, use this to get it. There is no need to ask me. Only do not set your heart on saving my money: what matters above all is a fine show. And treat the people just as you do in your own house. Do not hold back for fear of their complaints. Those are the only two things I ask. I have no other worries.’

Xi-feng did not venture to take the tally at once, but looked at Lady Wang.

‘Since your cousin puts it that way, you may look after things for him,’ Lady Wang said. ‘Only do not act on your own judgement. If anything comes up, be sure to send someone to ask your cousin or his wife.’

Bao-yu had already taken the tally from Jia Zhen and pressed it into Xi-feng’s hand.

Jia Zhen went on, ‘Will you stay here, Sister, or come over every day? Coming daily would be still more tiring. Why not let me have a courtyard made ready at once? You could stay here for these few days in comfort.’

‘No need,’ Xi-feng said, smiling. ‘They cannot do without me over there either. I had better come every day.’

Jia Zhen could only accept this. After a little more conversation, they went out.

When the women visitors had dispersed, Lady Wang asked Xi-feng, ‘What are you going to do today?’

‘Please go back, Your Ladyship,’ Xi-feng replied. ‘I must first get some order into things before I can leave.’

Lady Wang accordingly went home with Lady Xing and the others. Of them we need say no more.

Xi-feng went to sit in a three-bay side hall and considered the position. First, there were so many people coming and going without order that things went missing. Second, no one had a definite responsibility, so when something had to be done they passed it off on one another. Third, expenditure was excessive, with reckless disbursements and fraudulent claims. Fourth, duties were assigned without regard to their importance, leaving some people overworked and others at ease. Fifth, the servants were insolent and unrestrained: those with influence would not submit to control, while those without it could never advance. These five abuses were indeed the established ways of the Ning mansion. How Xi-feng dealt with them you must hear in the next chapter. As the couplet has it:

Of thousands in gold and purple, who can rule the state?

One or two in skirts and hairpins can set a household straight.
