Chapter 16 Jia Yuan-chun’s talents win her a place in the Phoenix Splendour Palace; Qin Jing-qing dies young and takes the road to the Yellow Springs

Chapter end

Now Bao-yu, finding the outer study put in order, arranged to read there at night with Qin Zhong. But Qin Zhong had always been exceedingly frail. Exposure to wind and frost in the country, together with his secret meetings and intimacies with Sapientia, had left him little chance to care for his health. He came home with a cold and a cough, no appetite and scarcely the strength to keep going. He dared not venture out, but stayed at home to recuperate. Bao-yu’s pleasure was spoiled. There was nothing for it but to wait quietly until he was quite recovered before making another arrangement.

Xi-feng had meanwhile received Yun Guang’s reply: everything had been settled. The old nun informed the Zhang family, and the garrison commandant, swallowing his anger, duly accepted the return of the betrothal gifts. Who would have thought that parents so greedy for money and influence could have a daughter with such constancy and feeling? When she learnt that they had rejected the husband first promised to her, she quietly took a hempen cord and hanged herself.

The commandant’s son, who was no less devoted, heard that Jin-ge had hanged herself and drowned himself in the river, true to the pledge of the girl who should have been his wife. Neither the Zhangs nor the Lis had anything to rejoice at: they had lost both the girl and their money. Xi-feng, on the other hand, pocketed three thousand taels without stirring. Lady Wang and the others knew nothing whatever about it. Emboldened by this success, Xi-feng thereafter did as she pleased whenever such a matter arose. We need not record them all. One day, on Jia Zheng’s birthday, everyone from the Ning and Rong mansions had assembled to congratulate him, and the celebrations were in full swing, when a gatekeeper hurried up to the banquet. ‘Mr Xia, chief eunuch of the Six Palaces, has come with an imperial command!’ Jia She, Jia Zheng and the rest were alarmed, with no notion what it could mean. They hastily stopped the play, cleared away the feast, set out an incense table and opened the central gate, then knelt to receive the visitor. Xia Shou-zhong, chief eunuch of the Six Palaces, rode up with a host of palace eunuchs before, behind and on either side of him. He carried neither a written proclamation nor a sealed decree. Dismounting below the eaves, he walked into the hall, all smiles, and stood facing south. ‘By special command: Jia Zheng is summoned to court at once, for an audience in the Hall of Reverent Attendance.’ Without even stopping for tea, he mounted and rode away.

Jia Zheng and the others could not tell what this portended. He could only hurry to change his clothes and go to court. Grandmother Jia and the whole family remained in an agony of uncertainty, constantly sending riders back and forth for news. About two double-hours had passed when three or four stewards, including Lai Da, came panting through the ceremonial gate with congratulations. Their master had ordered them to ask Her Old Ladyship to bring the ladies to court at once to give thanks for the imperial favour. Grandmother Jia was standing anxiously under the verandah of the main hall. Lady Xing, Lady Wang, You-shi, Li Wan, Xi-feng, Ying-chun and her sisters, and Aunt Xue were all together there. On hearing the news, she called Lai Da in and questioned him closely. ‘We were waiting outside the Gate of Reverent Attendance,’ he reported, ‘and could learn nothing of what was going on within. At last the eunuch Xia came out to congratulate us. He said our eldest young lady had been promoted to Secretary of the Phoenix Splendour Palace, with the further title of Consort of Worthy Virtue.

‘Later, when the master came out, he gave us the same news and these instructions. He has now gone on to the Eastern Palace. Your Old Ladyship and the ladies are asked to go at once to offer thanks.’ Only then were Grandmother Jia and the rest reassured, and their faces shone with joy.

They put on the full ceremonial dress appropriate to their ranks. Grandmother Jia set off for court with Lady Xing, Lady Wang and You-shi in four great sedan-chairs. Jia She and Jia Zhen also changed into court dress and, with Jia Rong and Jia Qiang, attended Grandmother Jia’s chair on the journey. Throughout the Ning and Rong mansions, high and low, within and without, everyone was jubilant. Every face wore a look of triumph, and talk and laughter rose in a ceaseless clamour.

But in recent days Sapientia had secretly fled from Water-Moon Hermitage into the city and made her way to Qin Zhong’s house to see him. Qin Ye discovered her there, drove her out and gave his son a beating. His anger brought on his old illness, and within three to five days he died. Qin Zhong, frail to begin with and still not recovered, had endured the beating only to see his old father die of anger. His grief and remorse came too late, and many more symptoms now appeared. Bao-yu was deeply dejected, as though something had been taken from him. Even the news of Yuan-chun’s promotion did nothing to dispel his gloom. How Grandmother Jia and the others gave thanks, how they returned home, how friends and relatives came with congratulations, how busy and festive the two mansions had been lately, how pleased everyone was: he alone treated all this as though it were not happening and paid it no attention. People laughed at him for being more of a simpleton than ever. Happily, Jia Lian and Dai-yu were on their way back. A messenger sent ahead reported that they would reach home the next day, and at this Bao-yu brightened a little. On asking for particulars, he learnt that Jia Yu-cun too was coming to the capital for an imperial audience. Wang Zi-teng had repeatedly submitted memorials recommending him, and he was now to await a vacancy in the capital. Since he and Jia Lian acknowledged a common clan, and Dai-yu had been his pupil, he was travelling with them.

Lin Ru-hai had been buried in the ancestral graveyard, and Jia Lian had only set out for the capital after all the arrangements were complete. They had not expected to reach home until the following month, but news of Yuan-chun’s good fortune had made them press on day and night. All had gone safely on the journey. Bao-yu heard only that Dai-yu was safe: the rest did not concern him. At last, after a long wait, noon of the next day arrived, and with it the announcement: ‘Master Lian and Miss Lin are home!’ At their reunion grief and joy mingled, and there was another bout of weeping before they exchanged congratulations. Bao-yu thought Dai-yu had grown more ethereally lovely than ever. She had brought back many books and busied herself having her bedroom cleaned and her belongings arranged, as well as distributing paper, brushes and other things among Bao-chai, Ying-chun, Bao-yu and the rest. Bao-yu carefully brought out the string of fragrant jiling beads the Prince of Beijing had given him, to pass it on to her. ‘Some stinking man has handled them! I don’t want them.’ She flung them aside and would not take them. Bao-yu could only put them away again. We shall leave them for the moment. Jia Lian, meanwhile, had paid his respects to everyone and returned to his own rooms.

Xi-feng had been so busy lately that she scarcely had a moment to spare, but with Jia Lian home from his long journey she had to find time to receive him. No outsiders were in the room. ‘Congratulations, my lord, the imperial brother-in-law!’ she said, smiling. ‘What dust and hardship Your Lordship must have endured on the road! Yesterday your humble servant heard from the first rider sent ahead that your august person would return today. I have prepared a poor cup of wine to wash away the dust. I wonder whether you would condescend to honour it with your presence?’ ‘You are too kind! Too kind!’ Jia Lian answered with a laugh. ‘I hardly deserve it.’

Patience and the other maids had made their salutations and were serving tea. Jia Lian asked about everything that had happened at home in his absence and thanked Xi-feng for all the trouble she had taken. ‘As if I could manage such things!’ she said. ‘I know so little, I’m so clumsy with words, and I always take people at their word. Give me a washing-beater and I’ll believe it’s a needle. I haven’t the face to refuse anyone. Two pleasant words and my heart melts. Besides, I’ve never dealt with anything important, and I’m such a coward that the slightest sign of displeasure from Her Ladyship keeps me awake all night.

‘I begged her several times to let me off, but she wouldn’t hear of it. Instead she said I wanted an easy life and wouldn’t learn. Little did she know I was in a cold sweat all the time, afraid to say one word or take one step too many. You know those stewards’ wives of ours. Which of them is easy to deal with? Make the slightest mistake and they laugh and jeer. Show the least favour and they curse the mulberry to abuse the locust, letting you hear their complaints without naming you. “Sit on the hill and watch the tigers fight”, “Kill with a borrowed sword”, “Raise a wind to fan the fire”, “Stand safe on the bank”, “Never lift a hand to right a toppled oil bottle”: they’ve mastered every trick in the book.

‘And I’m so young. Above all, I haven’t the weight to keep them in order. No wonder they think nothing of me. The most ridiculous thing was when Rong’s wife suddenly died over there. Cousin Zhen went down on his knees to Her Ladyship again and again, pleading for me to help him for a few days. I refused over and over, but Her Ladyship wouldn’t allow it, and I had to obey. Of course I turned everything upside down and made it more of a shambles than ever. Cousin Zhen is still complaining and regretting it. Now you’re back, when you see him tomorrow, do try to smooth it over. Tell him how young I am and how little I’ve seen of the world. What possessed him to put me in charge?’

While she was speaking, voices were heard in the outer room. ‘Who’s that?’ Xi-feng asked. Patience came in. ‘Aunt Xue sent Caltrop to ask me something. I’ve answered her and sent her back.’

‘That reminds me,’ said Jia Lian, smiling. ‘When I went to see Aunt just now, I came face to face with a young married woman. A very pretty creature! I couldn’t think who she was: I’d never seen her in our household. So I asked Aunt as we were talking, and it turned out she was the little girl they bought when they came to the capital, Caltrop. She’s become that great fool Xue’s bedfellow. She’s had her bridal face-threading and grown prettier still. He really is a disgrace to her.’ ‘Oh!’ said Xi-feng. ‘You’d think a journey to Suzhou and Hangzhou would have taught you something, but your eyes are still hungry when your belly’s full. If you fancy her, that’s easily settled. Shall I take Patience and exchange her for you? That eldest Xue boy is another who keeps an eye on the pot while he eats from his bowl. Over the past year, what quarrels he’s had with Aunt because he couldn’t get Caltrop! Aunt thought her looks the least of it. The way she behaves is quite unlike other girls: so gentle and quiet that many a young lady of good family couldn’t match her. That was why Aunt went to all the trouble of a feast and guests, and had her made his concubine openly and properly.

‘Before half a month was out, he paid her no more attention than the wind blowing through a stable. I do feel sorry for her.’ She had scarcely finished when a page at the inner gate sent in word: ‘The master is waiting for Master Lian in the main study.’ Jia Lian hastily straightened his clothes and went out.

Xi-feng now asked Patience, ‘What did Aunt want, to send Caltrop all this way?’ ‘There wasn’t any Caltrop,’ Patience said, smiling. ‘I borrowed her name for a little lie. Really, ma’am, Wang-er’s wife has less sense every day.’ She came close to Xi-feng and lowered her voice. ‘That interest money of yours: of all the times to bring it, she had to choose now, when the master was here!

‘Luckily I ran into her in the hall. Otherwise she’d have walked straight in and told you, and if the master had asked what interest it was, naturally you wouldn’t have wanted to keep anything from him. You’d have had to tell him the truth. And with his ways, he’d fish money out of a pot of boiling oil if he could spend it. Once he knew you had these private savings, he’d spend without a care. So I took it from her at once and gave her a talking-to. Who would have thought you’d hear and ask? That was why I made up the story about Caltrop.’ ‘I thought as much!’ Xi-feng laughed. ‘Aunt knew your master was back. Why would she suddenly send a concubine over? So it was you playing your damned tricks, you little baggage!’ As they spoke Jia Lian came in. Xi-feng ordered the food and wine, and husband and wife sat facing each other. Though she could drink well, she did not dare indulge herself, but merely kept Jia Lian company. Presently his wet nurse, Nurse Zhao, came in. Both urged her to have some wine and sit up on the kang.

Nurse Zhao firmly refused. Patience and the others had already set a stool and a little footstool below the kang, and she sat on the footstool. Jia Lian chose two dishes from the table and put them on the stool for her to eat. ‘Nurse can hardly chew that,’ said Xi-feng. ‘You’ll only hurt her teeth.’ She turned to Patience. ‘I said this morning that the pork knuckle stewed with ham was beautifully tender, just the thing for Nurse. Why haven’t you taken it to be heated? Hurry and tell them to warm it up.’

Then she said, ‘Nurse, try the Huiquan wine your son has brought back.’ ‘I’m drinking it. You have a cup too, ma’am. What is there to be afraid of, so long as you don’t have too much? It wasn’t the wine that brought me here just now. There’s something serious I want you to remember, ma’am, and give me a little of your care. This young master of ours talks so prettily, but when it comes to doing anything, he forgets all about us. A fine reward for nursing you from a baby to this size! I’m old now, and all I have is those two sons. Give them a bit of special consideration and no one else would dare bare his teeth. I’ve asked you again and again. You promise beautifully, and to this day it’s all dried-up shit. Now here’s this wonderful good fortune dropped straight from heaven: where won’t people be needed?

‘So it’s to you I’d better speak, ma’am. If I depended on our young master, I’d starve.’ ‘Don’t you worry, Nurse,’ Xi-feng said with a laugh. ‘Leave both his foster-brothers to me. You nursed him from a baby: don’t you know his ways yet? He presses himself on outsiders who have nothing to do with him. Here are his own foster-brothers. Which of them isn’t better than anyone else? If you took care of them, who would dare say no? Why give outsiders all the benefit? No, I’ve got that wrong. We think of them as “outsiders”, but you treat them as your “inside people”.’ The whole room laughed. Nurse Zhao could not stop laughing either, and invoked the Buddha. ‘Well, we’ve a very upright judge sprung up in this room! As for all that nonsense about “inside people” and “outsiders”, our young master does nothing of the kind. He’s just too soft-hearted to refuse when someone asks him a favour.’

‘Exactly,’ said Xi-feng, smiling. ‘With his “inside people” he’s all softness. It’s only with us women at home that he’s hard!’ ‘You’ve said everything there is to say, ma’am!’ Nurse Zhao laughed. ‘It does me good. I’ll have another cup of this fine wine. Now our mistress has taken charge, I’ve nothing left to worry about.’

Jia Lian was embarrassed. He could only give a sheepish laugh, drink his wine and mutter, ‘Nonsense! Hurry up with the rice. I must have a bowl and go over to Master Zhen’s. We’ve things to discuss.’ ‘Yes, we mustn’t hold up important business,’ Xi-feng said.

‘What did the master want you for just now?’ ‘The home visit,’ Jia Lian replied. ‘Has permission really been given?’ she asked eagerly. ‘If it’s not absolutely certain,’ he said with a smile, ‘it’s eight parts out of ten.’

‘What generosity our Emperor shows!’ said Xi-feng. ‘All the old stories I’ve heard and plays I’ve seen, and never anything like it.’ Nurse Zhao broke in. ‘That’s just it. I must be growing old and muddle-headed. I’ve heard everyone, upstairs and down, making a commotion these last few days about a home visit, but I never troubled myself over it. Now here it is again. What does it all mean?’ ‘His Majesty understands the hearts of all his people,’ Jia Lian said. ‘There is nothing greater in the world than filial duty. The love between parents and children must surely be the same for everyone; rank makes no difference to that. Though His Majesty attends the Retired Emperor and the Empress Dowager day and night, he still feels he has not done enough. Seeing that the consorts and ladies of the palace have all been there for years, parted from the sight and sound of their parents, he thought: how could they fail to miss them? It is only right for children to long for their parents.

‘And he thought of the parents at home, always missing children they could never see. “If they should fall ill, or even die, on that account, it would be because I have kept their children confined, denying them the natural wish of every family. That would grievously offend the harmony of Heaven.” So he laid the matter before the Retired Emperor and the Empress Dowager, proposing that the families of the palace ladies should be allowed to visit them on dates ending in two or six each month. Their Majesties were delighted. They praised his perfect filial devotion and pure benevolence, his understanding of Heaven and the nature of things. Then the two elder sovereigns issued a further decree. When families came into the palace, they said, there must inevitably be rules of state and ceremonial restraints, so that even mothers and daughters could not speak their hearts freely. They therefore extended their gracious indulgence still further and specially informed the imperial ladies’ families that, in addition to the privilege of visits on dates ending in two or six, any family with suitable ranges of buildings and separate grounds, where an imperial visitor could lodge and be properly guarded, might petition to receive the imperial carriage from the inner palace at its own house. This would allow the affections of kin and the deepest feelings of family life some small fulfilment. Who could fail to rejoice and give thanks when that decree came out? Honoured Lady Zhou’s father has already begun building a villa at home for her visit.

‘And the family of Wu Tian-you, Noble Consort Wu’s father, have been looking at sites outside the city. Doesn’t that make it eight or nine parts certain?’ ‘Amitabha!’ said Nurse Zhao. ‘So that’s it! Then we too must prepare to receive our eldest young lady?’ ‘Of course!’ said Jia Lian.

‘What else do you suppose all this bustle is for?’ ‘If it really happens, I shall see something of the world at last,’ Xi-feng said, laughing. ‘What a pity I wasn’t born a few years earlier! Another twenty or thirty years and these old people wouldn’t look down on me for having seen nothing. When they talk about the founding emperor’s tours in imitation of Shun’s royal progresses, it sounds more exciting than a whole book of stories. I never had the luck to see it.’ ‘Oh, my!’ said Nurse Zhao. ‘That was something you might not see in a thousand years! I was just old enough to remember things then. Our Jia family were supervising the building of seagoing vessels and repairs to the sea walls around Suzhou and Yangzhou. Just to prepare for that one imperial visit, they poured out silver like seawater. Why, to tell you—’ ‘Our Wang family prepared for one too,’ Xi-feng broke in. ‘My grandfather had sole charge then of the tribute and ceremonial greetings from foreign countries. Every foreigner who came was maintained by our family. All the goods brought by foreign ships to Guangdong, Fujian, Yunnan and Zhejiang were ours.’

‘Who doesn’t know that?’ said Nurse Zhao. ‘There’s still a saying: “When the Eastern Sea lacks a white-jade bed, The Dragon King comes to ask the Wangs of Jiangnan.” That’s your family, ma’am. And the Zhens who are still down in Jiangnan: oh, what a display they made! That family alone received the Emperor four times. If we hadn’t seen it with our own eyes, no one we told would believe it. Never mind treating silver like dirt: everything in the world was piled up in mountains and seas. They hadn’t a thought to spare for such words as “waste” or “sin”.’ ‘I’ve often heard our old gentlemen say so,’ said Xi-feng. ‘Of course I believe it. What puzzles me is how that family came to be so rich and grand.’ ‘I’ll tell you one thing, ma’am. It was only the Emperor’s own money being spent on the Emperor! Who has that sort of money to buy a show of empty glory?’ They were talking animatedly when Lady Wang sent someone again to see whether Xi-feng had eaten.

Xi-feng knew this meant there was business waiting for her. She hurriedly ate half a bowl of rice, rinsed her mouth and was about to go when the pages at the inner gate reported that the two young gentlemen from the Ning mansion, Rong and Qiang, had arrived. Jia Lian had just rinsed his mouth too, and Patience was holding a basin for him to wash his hands. ‘What is it?’ he asked them. ‘Speak up.’ Xi-feng stopped to hear what they had to say. Jia Rong spoke first. ‘Father sent me to tell you, Uncle, that the masters have settled it. Beginning on the east, taking in our garden and turning round to the north, they’ve measured it all: three and a half li in extent. That will do for the villa for the imperial home visit. Someone has been sent for to draw the plans, and they’ll be ready tomorrow. You must be tired after your journey, so there’s no need for you to come over tonight. If there is anything to discuss, they’ll ask you over early tomorrow morning.’ Jia Lian smiled and answered at once. ‘Thank your father for his thoughtfulness. I won’t come over, then. This plan really does save trouble, and it will be easy to build there. Buying another site would be much more work, and the result wouldn’t be at all fitting.

‘Tell him I think this is excellent. If the masters want to change their minds, we must rely on him to dissuade them. On no account should we look elsewhere. I’ll come over early tomorrow to pay my respects and discuss the details.’ Jia Rong repeatedly assented. Jia Qiang now stepped forward. ‘Uncle, Master Zhen has put me in charge of going to Suzhou to engage instructors, buy girls and provide instruments, costumes and the rest. I’m to take the steward Lai’s two sons, and the two literary gentlemen Shan Pin-ren and Bu Gu-xiu. That’s why he told me to come and see you.’ Jia Lian looked him up and down and smiled. ‘Do you know anything about that line of business? It’s not a particularly large undertaking, but there are plenty of things hidden under the surface.’ ‘I can only learn as I go,’ Jia Qiang said, smiling.

Beside them, in the lamplight, Jia Rong secretly tugged at Xi-feng’s clothing. She took the hint. ‘You worry too much,’ she said with a smile. ‘Surely your father knows as well as you whom to employ? Yet here you are, afraid the boy doesn’t know the business. Who knows everything to begin with? These children are quite grown up now. Even if they’ve never eaten pork, they’ve seen a pig run.

‘Your cousin is only sending him to sit under the commander’s banner. Does he seriously expect him to bargain over prices and do all the trading himself? I think it’s a very good arrangement.’ ‘Of course,’ said Jia Lian. ‘I wasn’t objecting. But I ought to help him think it through.’ He asked Jia Qiang, ‘Where is the money for this to come from?’ ‘We were just discussing that too. Grandfather Lai says there’s no need to carry it down from the capital. The Zhen family in Jiangnan still hold fifty thousand taels of our silver. Tomorrow we’re to take a letter and a bill of exchange, draw thirty thousand first, and leave the other twenty thousand there to pay for candles, coloured lanterns, and all the curtains, blinds and hangings.’ ‘A good idea,’ Jia Lian said, nodding. Xi-feng at once turned to Jia Qiang.

‘In that case, I have two reliable men who know the business. Take them with you to do the work. That will make things easy for you.’ Jia Qiang was all smiles. ‘I was just going to ask you for a couple of men, Aunt. How fortunate!’ He asked their names, and Xi-feng turned to Nurse Zhao. The old woman had been listening in a daze. Patience laughed and nudged her, and she came to herself. ‘One’s Zhao Tian-liang, and the other’s Zhao Tian-dong,’ she said quickly.

‘Mind you don’t forget,’ said Xi-feng. ‘I must get on with my own business.’ She went out. Jia Rong hurried to escort her and spoke quietly. ‘If you want anything, Aunt, tell me. I’ll make a list for Qiang to take, and have him buy it all for you.’ ‘Don’t talk such bloody nonsense!’ Xi-feng said with a laugh. ‘I haven’t anywhere to put the things I have already. Do you think I want your sneaking little favours?’ And she went straight on.

Jia Qiang likewise asked Jia Lian quietly, ‘Is there anything you would like? I could have it woven while I’m there, as a little token of respect.’ ‘Don’t get carried away,’ Jia Lian said, smiling. ‘You’re only just learning to do business, and you’ve already learnt that trick. If I need anything, I’ll write and tell you. We needn’t talk about that now.’

He dismissed them both. People then came in to report on business three or four times and more, until Jia Lian, worn out, sent instructions to the inner gate that no further messages were to be admitted: everything must wait until tomorrow. It was the third watch before Xi-feng at last came back to bed. The night passed without further incident.

Next morning Jia Lian rose, paid his respects to Jia She and Jia Zheng, and went over to the Ning mansion. With the old stewards and several literary dependants and family friends, he inspected the grounds of both houses, planned and drew the buildings for the imperial visit, and considered how to arrange the labour. From then on, craftsmen of every trade gathered there. Gold, silver, copper and tin, earth, timber, bricks and tiles were carried in without a pause. First the workmen were told to take down walls and pavilions in the Ning mansion’s Garden of Gathered Fragrance, opening it directly into the Rong mansion’s great eastern courtyard. The whole range of servants’ quarters on the eastern side of the Rong mansion was demolished. Until then the two estates had been divided by a narrow lane, with no passage between them, but the lane was private property, not a public thoroughfare, so the grounds could be joined. A stream of running water already entered the Garden of Gathered Fragrance beneath the wall at its north corner. There was no need to bring in another supply.

There were not enough rocks and trees, but Jia She lived in what had once been the Rong mansion’s garden. Its bamboos, trees, rocks, pavilions, balustrades and other fittings could all be moved over. The two places were very close, and combining their resources would save a great deal of money and labour. Even if they did not provide enough, comparatively little would have to be added. All the planning and construction owed much to an elderly gentleman who went by the name of Shan Zi-ye.

Jia Zheng was unused to practical business and left the arrangements entirely to Jia She, Jia Zhen, Jia Lian, Lai Da, Lai Sheng, Lin Zhi-xiao, Wu Xin-deng, Zhan Guang, Cheng Ri-xing and the others. Shan Zi-ye designed the artificial hills and excavated pools, the towers and pavilions, the planting of bamboos and flowers, and every other feature of the landscape. When free after court, Jia Zheng merely looked round the various parts and discussed the most important points with Jia She and the rest. Jia She himself stayed comfortably at home. Jia Zhen and the others reported every trifling matter to him, either in person or in a brief written account. If he had anything to say, he sent for Jia Lian, Lai Da and the others to receive his instructions. Jia Rong’s sole charge was the making of gold and silver vessels. Jia Qiang had already left for Suzhou. Jia Zhen, Lai Da and the rest also had to assign the workers, draw up registers and supervise the work. No single pen could describe it all. Enough to say that for a time there was extraordinary bustle and commotion. Let us leave them there.

Now with such a great undertaking in the family, Jia Zheng had stopped examining Bao-yu on his books, which was a great relief to him. Yet Qin Zhong’s illness grew worse day by day, and Bao-yu was too anxious to enjoy his freedom. One morning he had just washed and combed his hair, and was intending to ask Grandmother Jia’s leave to visit Qin Zhong, when he saw Tealeaf peering round the spirit-screen at the inner gate, his head bobbing in and out. Bao-yu hurried out. ‘What is it?’ ‘Master Qin’s dying!’ Bao-yu started in alarm. ‘I only saw him yesterday, and he was quite clear-headed. How can he be dying?’ ‘I don’t know. The old man from his house came specially to tell me just now.’ Bao-yu turned at once to tell Grandmother Jia. ‘Choose reliable people to go with him,’ she ordered. ‘Go and show your affection for your schoolfellow, then come back. You mustn’t stay too long.’ Bao-yu hurried to change and came out again. The carriage was not ready, and in his impatience he paced wildly round the hall.

At last, under repeated urging, the carriage arrived. He scrambled in, with Li Gui, Tealeaf and the others attending him. No one was at Qin Zhong’s gate. They all crowded into the inner room, frightening Qin Zhong’s two distant aunts and several male cousins so much that they could scarcely get out of the way in time. Qin Zhong had already lost consciousness two or three times. His bed had long since been moved and its mat changed in preparation for death. At the sight Bao-yu could not help crying out. ‘No, no!’ Li Gui hastily urged him. ‘Master Qin is suffering from weakness. Lying stiffly up on the kang must have made his bones uncomfortable, so they’ve moved him down for a while to give him some ease. Carrying on like this will only make him worse, young master.’ Bao-yu restrained himself and went closer. Qin Zhong’s face was white as wax. He lay breathing on the pillow with his eyes shut. Bao-yu called urgently, ‘Brother Jing!

‘Bao-yu is here.’ He called two or three times, but Qin Zhong made no response. ‘Bao-yu is here,’ he said again. Qin Zhong’s soul had already left his body; only a faint last breath lingered in his chest. He was seeing a crowd of ghostly officers coming to seize him with warrants and ropes. His soul was unwilling to go. He thought of the household with no one left to manage it, of the three or four thousand taels his father had saved, and of Sapientia, whose whereabouts were still unknown. He pleaded with the officers in every possible way, but they would make no exception. Instead they rebuked him. ‘And you call yourself an educated man! Don’t you know the saying, “If King Yama orders you to die at the third watch, who dares keep you till the fifth?” Here in the world below we are all strictly impartial, from top to bottom. We’re not like you up among the living, always considering people’s feelings and connexions, with a thousand things to stand in your way.’ In the midst of this wrangle, Qin Zhong’s soul suddenly heard the words ‘Bao-yu is here’. He hastened to plead again. ‘Honoured messengers, have a little compassion. Let me go back and say one word to this dear friend, and then I’ll come.’ ‘What dear friend is this now?’ the ghosts asked.

‘To tell you the truth, gentlemen,’ said Qin Zhong, ‘he is the Duke of Rong’s grandson. His childhood name is Bao-yu.’ The chief judge was the first to take fright. He hastily rounded on the ghostly messengers. ‘I told you to let him go back for a little while, but you wouldn’t listen! You had to wait till he brought out the name of someone whose star is rising and whose luck is in.’ Seeing their chief judge so alarmed, the ghosts too became flustered, but they grumbled as well. ‘You were all thunder and lightning just now, sir. So it turns out you can’t bear to hear the name “Bao-yu”! In our humble opinion, he belongs to the living and we to the dead. Being afraid of them does us no good.’ The chief judge snapped, ‘Rubbish!

‘There’s a good saying: “An official of the world has a hand in all the world’s affairs.” Since ancient times, men and ghosts have followed the same ways. The living and the dead don’t have two different sets of rules. Never mind which world he belongs to. Let him go back, and we shan’t go wrong.’ The ghosts had no choice but to release Qin Zhong’s soul. He gave a groan and opened his eyes a little. Seeing Bao-yu beside him, he forced out a sigh. ‘Why wouldn’t you come sooner? A moment later and we couldn’t have seen each other.’ Bao-yu took his hand, tears falling. ‘Is there anything you want to say? Leave me a few words.’

‘There’s nothing else,’ Qin Zhong said. ‘You and I used to think our understanding was above everyone else’s. Only today do I see how I led myself astray. From now on you ought to set your heart on success in the examinations and an official career. Honour and distinction are what you should aim for.’ With that he gave a long sigh and quietly passed away.
