Chapter 39 A village granny talks as fancy takes her; A tender-hearted young cousin insists on getting to the bottom of it

Chapter end

When the others saw Patience arrive, they all asked, ‘What is your mistress doing? Why hasn’t she come back?’

‘How could she find the time?’ Patience said, smiling. ‘She says she didn’t get a proper chance to eat, and now she can’t come, so she’s sent me to ask whether there are any crabs left. I’m to take a few home for her.’

‘There are plenty,’ said Xiang-yun, and quickly had someone fetch ten of the very largest.

‘Make more of them the round-bellied ones,’ Patience said.

They tried to draw her down to sit with them, but she refused.

‘I insist,’ said Li Wan, laughing. She pulled Patience down beside her, took up a cup of wine and held it to her lips. Patience hurriedly drank a mouthful and was about to leave.

‘I won’t let you go,’ Li Wan said. ‘Plainly no one counts but Feng. You won’t listen to me.’ Then she told the older women, ‘Take the box over first. Say I’m keeping Patience here.’

Presently the woman returned with the box. ‘Mrs Lian says you and the young ladies mustn’t laugh at her for asking for something to eat, ma’am.

‘In this box are water-caltrop flour cakes and chicken-fat rolls that have just come from her uncle’s wife. They’re for you and the young ladies.’

Then she turned to Patience. ‘She says she sends you on an errand and you get so fond of the company you won’t come back. She advises you to have one cup less.’

‘And what will she do to me if I have one more?’ Patience said with a laugh, and went on drinking and eating crabs.

Li Wan put an arm round her and smiled. ‘What a pity, with such a pretty, respectable face, to have no better fortune than service in someone’s rooms. Who that didn’t know you wouldn’t take you for a mistress of the house?’

Eating and drinking with Bao-chai, Xiang-yun and the others, Patience turned her head and laughed. ‘Please don’t keep feeling me, ma’am. It tickles!’

‘Oh! What’s this hard thing?’ Li Wan asked.

‘Keys.’

‘What keys? So afraid the precious private things might be stolen that you have to keep the keys on you! I’m always joking with people: when the Tang Monk goes to fetch the scriptures, there’s a white horse to carry him; when Liu Zhi-yuan sets out to win an empire, there’s a melon spirit to bring him a helmet and armour; and where there’s Feng, there’s you. You’re your mistress’s master key. What do you need these keys for?’

‘You’ve had some wine, ma’am, and now you’re making fun of me,’ Patience said, laughing.

‘But it’s true,’ said Bao-chai with a smile. ‘When we have nothing else to do and start talking about people, we always say you few girls are each one in a hundred. The best of it is that each of you has her own particular virtues.’

‘There’s a providence in little things as well as great,’ said Li Wan.

‘Take the old lady’s rooms. What would they do without Faithful? From Her Ladyship down, who dares contradict the old lady? Yet Faithful does, and the old lady will listen to her alone.

‘All the old lady’s clothes and jewellery, things no one else remembers, she remembers every one. If she weren’t looking after them, who knows how much would have been wheedled away? And she’s fair-minded. For all her standing, she’s always putting in a good word for people, never using her influence to bully them.’

‘Only yesterday Grandmother said she was better than we are,’ Xi-chun said, smiling.

‘She really is a good one,’ said Patience. ‘We can’t compare with her.’

‘Cai-xia in Mother’s rooms is a quiet, honest girl,’ Bao-yu said.

‘Indeed she is,’ said Tan-chun. ‘She may look simple, but she knows what’s what. Mother is like a Buddha and doesn’t pay attention to things, but Cai-xia knows them all. She reminds Mother of everything that needs doing. Even Father’s affairs, whether he’s at home or away, great and small, she knows about them. When Mother forgets, she tells her privately.’

‘Quite so,’ said Li Wan. Pointing at Bao-yu, she went on, ‘And what do you suppose things would come to in this young gentleman’s rooms if it weren’t for Aroma?

‘Feng may be the Overlord of Chu, but she still needs those two good arms to lift her thousand-catty cauldron. Without this girl, could she attend to everything so thoroughly?’

‘There were four maids who came with her when she married,’ Patience said, smiling. ‘Some died, some left. I’m the only solitary ghost still here.’

‘Then you’re fortunate, and so is Feng,’ said Li Wan. ‘When Master Zhu was alive, didn’t he have two girls too? Do you think I’m the sort of woman who couldn’t tolerate them? Yet day after day they were unhappy. So when he died, I sent them both away while they were still young. If even one had been willing to stay, I would have had someone to help me.’

Tears fell as she spoke.

‘Why make yourself sad again?’ the others said. ‘We had better break up.’

They washed their hands and agreed to go together to pay their respects to Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang.

The older women and maids swept the pavilion and cleared away the cups and dishes. Aroma walked towards the front with Patience, inviting her to come into her room for a little while and have another cup of tea.

‘No more tea. Another time,’ Patience said, and was about to go out when Aroma called her back.

‘Why hasn’t this month’s money been paid yet? Even the old lady and Her Ladyship haven’t had theirs.’

Patience quickly turned and came close to her. Only when she had made sure no one was nearby did she whisper, ‘Don’t ask. It will be paid in a few days, anyway.’

Aroma laughed. ‘What can it be, to frighten you like that?’

Patience told her in a whisper. ‘Our mistress drew this month’s money long ago and lent it out. She’s waiting for interest to come in from elsewhere, and when she has enough together she’ll pay everyone. I’m only telling you because it’s you. You mustn’t tell a soul.’

‘Is she still short of money?’ said Aroma. ‘Will she never have enough? Why burden herself with all that worry?’

‘That’s what I say,’ Patience answered with a laugh.

‘Over these last few years she’s made several hundred taels out of this one lot of money. Then she never needs to spend her own household-expense allowance or monthly money, so she puts the little sums together, eight or ten taels at a time, and lends those out. The interest on her private money alone comes to over a thousand taels in less than a year.’

Aroma laughed. ‘So mistress and maid make interest on our money, while we’re fooled into sitting here waiting!’

‘What an ungrateful thing to say. Surely you aren’t short of money?’

‘I’m not. But then I have nowhere to spend it. I only keep it ready for our young gentleman.’

‘If you ever need money urgently, I have a few taels. Take those first, and I’ll deduct them from yours when it comes.’

‘I don’t need any now. If something comes up and I haven’t enough, I’ll send someone to fetch it.’

Patience agreed and went straight out of the garden gate to her own quarters. Xi-feng was not in her room. To Patience’s surprise, Grannie Liu, who had come soliciting help on her previous visit, had returned with Ban-er. They were sitting in the room on the other side, with Zhang Cai’s wife and Zhou Rui’s wife keeping them company. Two or three maids were emptying sacks of dates, pumpkins and wild vegetables on the floor.

Everyone stood up as Patience came in. Grannie Liu knew her standing from the last visit and hurriedly hopped down to greet her.

‘How are you, miss? Everyone at home sends their respects. I meant to come and pay my respects to Madam and see you long ago, but we’ve been busy on the farm. This year, at last, we’ve managed to harvest two shi of grain more than usual, and we’ve had plenty of fruit and vegetables too. These are the first pickings. We wouldn’t sell them. We saved the very best for Madam and you young ladies to taste. You must get tired of delicacies from the hills and seas every day. These will give you a little taste of the countryside. It’s our poor way of showing we think of you.’

‘Thank you for taking so much trouble,’ Patience said at once.

She asked Grannie Liu to sit again, sat down herself, then said, ‘Aunt Zhang, Mrs Zhou, do sit down.’ She also told a little maid to bring tea.

Zhou Rui’s wife and Zhang Cai’s wife laughed. ‘There’s a spring glow in your face today, miss. Even the rims of your eyes are red.’

‘No wonder!’ Patience said with a laugh. ‘I wasn’t going to have any, but Mrs Zhu and the young ladies kept hold of me and forced it on me. I had to drink two cups, and my face went red.’

‘I was thinking I’d like some,’ Zhang Cai’s wife said, laughing, ‘but no one invited me. Next time someone asks you, miss, take me along.’

They all laughed.

‘I saw those crabs this morning,’ Zhou Rui’s wife said. ‘Only two or three would make a catty. Three great baskets like that must have held seventy or eighty catties.’

‘Even that might not be enough for everyone, high and low,’ said Zhou Rui’s wife.

‘Of course it wasn’t,’ said Patience. ‘Only those of some standing got a couple each. Among the ordinary run, some got their hands on one and some didn’t.’

‘Crabs like that are worth five fen a catty this year,’ said Grannie Liu.

‘Ten catties, five qian. Five fives, two taels and five; three fives are fifteen. Then add the wine and the other dishes, and that’s more than twenty taels altogether. Bless the Buddha! The cost of that one meal would keep us country people for a year.’

‘I suppose you’ve seen our mistress?’ Patience asked.

‘Yes. She told us to wait.’ Grannie Liu looked out at the sky. ‘It’s getting late. We ought to go, or we’ll be in a fix if we can’t get out of the city.’

‘That’s true,’ said Zhou Rui’s wife. ‘I’ll go and see for you.’

She went straight off and returned after some time, smiling.

‘Your luck has come in, Grannie. You’ve taken the fancy of two people at once.’

Patience and the others asked what she meant.

‘Mrs Lian is with the old lady,’ Zhou Rui’s wife explained, smiling. ‘I only meant to tell her quietly, “Grannie Liu wants to go home. She’s afraid she’ll be too late to get out of the city.”

‘Mrs Lian said, “She’s come such a long way, and taken all the trouble of carrying those heavy things here. If it’s late, let her stay the night and go tomorrow.” Doesn’t that show you’ve taken her fancy? And that isn’t all. The old lady happened to hear and asked who Grannie Liu was. Mrs Lian explained, and the old lady said, “I was just wishing for someone really old to talk to. Ask her to come and see me.” Isn’t that a blessing from heaven you never expected?’

She urged Grannie Liu to get down and come along.

‘How can I go before her looking like this?’ Grannie Liu asked. ‘Be a good sister and say I’ve gone.’

‘Do go,’ Patience said quickly. ‘It doesn’t matter. Our old lady is the kindest of people to the old and poor. She isn’t like those blustering, deceitful creatures. You must be frightened of going before someone so grand. Mrs Zhou and I will take you.’

Together with Zhou Rui’s wife, she led Grannie Liu towards Grandmother Jia’s rooms.

The pages on duty at the inner gate all stood when they saw Patience come out. Two ran forward after her, calling, ‘Miss!’

‘What is it now?’ she asked.

One of the pages smiled. ‘It’s getting late, and my mother’s ill. She’s waiting for me to fetch a doctor. Please, miss, could I have half a day off?’

‘What a fine lot you are! You’ve arranged it all between you, one asking for leave every day, never going to our mistress, only pestering me. Zhu-er went off the other day, and of course the master wanted him. He couldn’t be found, so I had to answer for it, and was accused of showing favouritism into the bargain. Now here you are.’

‘His mother really is ill,’ Zhou Rui’s wife said. ‘Do answer for him, miss, and let him go.’

‘Be here first thing tomorrow,’ Patience said. ‘Listen: I have work for you. Just you try sleeping till the sun’s on your backside before you come! And on your way, take a message to Wang-er. Say it’s from our mistress. Ask him about the rest of that interest.

‘If he doesn’t bring it in tomorrow, she doesn’t want it at all. He might as well have it to spend himself.’

The page agreed with delight and went off. Patience and the others reached Grandmother Jia’s rooms, where the girls from Prospect Garden were keeping her company. Grannie Liu walked in to find the room full of pearls and jade, a bright flutter of finery, without knowing who anyone was. She saw an old lady reclining on a couch, with a beautiful maid all wrapped in gauze and silk, sitting behind her and massaging her legs. Xi-feng stood beside them, talking and laughing. Grannie Liu knew then that this must be Grandmother Jia. She hurried forward with an ingratiating smile and curtseyed several times.

‘My respects to our venerable Star of Longevity!’

Grandmother Jia raised herself a little to return the greeting and told Zhou Rui’s wife to bring a chair for her. Ban-er was still too shy with strangers to offer any greeting.

‘My old relation, how old are you this year?’ Grandmother Jia asked.

Grannie Liu quickly stood up. ‘Seventy-five.’

‘At her age, still so strong!’ Grandmother Jia said to the others. ‘She’s several years older than I am.

‘When I’m that old, heaven knows how little I’ll be able to move.’

‘We were born to work, Your Old Ladyship,’ Grannie Liu said with a smile, ‘and you were born to enjoy good fortune. If we lived as you do, there’d be no one to do the farm work.’

‘Are your eyes and teeth still good?’

‘Yes, they’re still good, except that this year a back tooth on the left has started to wobble.’

‘I’m old and none of me is any use now,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘My eyes are dim, my ears are deaf and my memory’s gone. I can’t even remember old relations like you. When relations call, I don’t see them, for fear they’ll laugh at me. I eat a little of whatever I can chew, have a sleep, and when I’m bored I joke for a while with these grandchildren. That’s all.’

‘That’s just your good fortune, Your Old Ladyship,’ Grannie Liu said, smiling. ‘We couldn’t do that if we wanted to.’

‘Good fortune? I’m only an old good-for-nothing.’

This made everyone laugh.

Grandmother Jia went on with a smile. ‘Feng tells me you’ve brought a great many pumpkins and vegetables. I’ve told her to have them prepared at once. I’ve been longing for vegetables freshly picked from the ground. What they buy outside never tastes as good as what you grow in your fields.’

‘It’s a little taste of the country,’ Grannie Liu said, smiling, ‘something fresh for a change. We’d like fish and meat, only we can’t afford them.’

‘Now we’ve met as relations, you mustn’t go away with nothing,’ Grandmother Jia said. ‘Unless you dislike it here, stay a day or two before going home. We have a garden too, and fruit in it. You can try some tomorrow, and take some home. Then you’ll have made a proper visit to your relations.’

Seeing how pleased Grandmother Jia was, Xi-feng quickly added her invitation. ‘Our place may not be as large as your farmyard, but we do have a couple of empty rooms.

‘Stay two days and tell our old lady some of the news and stories from your part of the world.’

‘Don’t tease her, Feng,’ Grandmother Jia said with a laugh. ‘She’s a plain country person. How is she to stand up to your jokes?’

She told someone to bring a handful of fruit for Ban-er, but with so many people there he was afraid to eat. She then had some money given to him and told the little pages to take him outside to play. After drinking her tea, Grannie Liu told Grandmother Jia some of the things she had seen and heard in the country, and the old lady became more and more interested. While they were talking, Xi-feng sent to invite Grannie Liu to supper. Grandmother Jia chose several dishes from her own meal and had them sent over for Grannie Liu to eat.

Seeing how much Grannie Liu had pleased Grandmother Jia, Xi-feng sent her back after supper. Faithful promptly told an older woman to take her for a bath, and herself chose a couple of everyday garments for her to change into. Grannie Liu had never known such treatment. She hurriedly changed, came out and sat before Grandmother Jia’s couch, searching her mind for more things to say. Bao-yu and the girls were all sitting there too. Having never heard anything of the kind, they found her tales more entertaining than the stories told by the blind professional storytellers. Country woman though she was, Grannie Liu had some natural shrewdness. She was old, moreover, and experienced in the ways of the world. Seeing, first, how much Grandmother Jia enjoyed it, and then how eagerly the young people listened, she invented things to tell them even when she had nothing left to say.

‘In our village we grow grain and vegetables, year in, year out, spring, summer, autumn and winter, wind or rain. When do we ever have time to sit down? The edge of a field is our wayside resting-pavilion every day. There’s no end to the strange things we see.

‘Last winter, for instance, it snowed for days on end, till the snow lay three or four chi deep on the ground. I was up early one morning. Before I’d left my room I heard a rustling outside in the firewood. Someone must be stealing it, I thought. I put my eye to a hole in the window, and it wasn’t anyone from our village.’

‘A traveller feeling cold, I expect,’ Grandmother Jia said. ‘Seeing firewood ready to hand, they might well take a little to make a fire.’

‘But it wasn’t a traveller, either,’ said Grannie Liu, smiling. ‘That’s what was so strange. Who do you think it was, venerable Star of Longevity? A lovely young girl of seventeen or eighteen, with her hair combed smooth and shining, in a scarlet jacket and a white figured-silk skirt—’

She had reached this point when a commotion broke out outside. Someone said, ‘It’s nothing. Don’t frighten the old lady.’

Grandmother Jia and the others quickly asked what was happening.

‘There was a fire in the stables in the south courtyard,’ a maid reported. ‘It’s nothing to worry about. It’s already been brought under control.’

Grandmother Jia was very easily frightened. She rose at once, leaning on someone for support, and went out on to the verandah to look. The glow of the fire was still bright in the south-east.

Frightened, Grandmother Jia invoked the Buddha and hurriedly sent someone to burn incense before the God of Fire. Lady Wang and the others hastened over to ask after her and assure her that the fire was already dying down.

‘Please go back inside, Your Old Ladyship.’

Grandmother Jia would not lead them back in until she had watched the last of the glow disappear. Bao-yu was meanwhile eager to question Grannie Liu.

‘Why was that girl taking firewood in the deep snow? What if the cold had made her ill?’

‘It was all that talk of taking firewood that brought on the fire,’ Grandmother Jia said. ‘And you’re still asking! No more of that. Tell us something else.’

Disappointed though he was, Bao-yu had to give up. Grannie Liu thought up another tale.

‘In the village east of ours there’s an old grannie in her nineties.

‘Every day she eats vegetarian food and prays to the Buddha. Well, she so moved the Bodhisattva Guanyin that one night Guanyin came to her in a dream and said, “You are so devout! Your family line was to have died out, but I have now petitioned the Jade Emperor, and you are to have a grandson.” You see, the old grannie had only one son, and that son had only one son of his own. They’d managed to bring him up to seventeen or eighteen, and then he died. They cried something dreadful. But afterwards another boy really was born. He’s only thirteen or fourteen this year, white as a ball of snow and ever so clever. So you see, those gods and Buddhas really do exist.’

This whole story answered exactly to what was in Grandmother Jia’s and Lady Wang’s hearts. Even Lady Wang listened spellbound.

Bao-yu was still preoccupied with the story about the firewood, brooding over it and turning it over in his mind. Tan-chun asked him, ‘After all the hospitality Xiang-yun gave us yesterday, shall we discuss another poetry meeting when we get back? We could repay her hospitality with a dinner and invite Grandmother to admire the chrysanthemums as well.’

‘Grandmother has already said she wants to give a dinner in return for Xiang-yun’s,’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘We’re to keep them company. It will be soon enough for us to give ours after we’ve eaten hers.’

‘It will only get colder as time goes on. Grandmother may not feel like it then.’

‘But Grandmother likes rain and snow.

‘Why don’t we wait for the first snowfall and invite her to admire the snow? Writing poems out in the snow would be more fun for us too.’

Dai-yu quickly smiled. ‘Writing poems in the snow? I think we’d do better to get a bundle of firewood and go pulling sticks out of it in the snow. That would be much more fun.’

Bao-chai and the others laughed. Bao-yu gave Dai-yu a look and said nothing.

When they broke up, Bao-yu made sure to take Grannie Liu aside and question her closely about who the girl was. Grannie Liu had to invent an answer.

‘She’s the one they worship in a little shrine on the field ridge north of our village. Not a god or a Buddha. There was once a gentleman called…’

She began trying to think of a name.

‘It doesn’t matter what he was called,’ Bao-yu said. ‘Don’t trouble to think of it. Just tell me what happened.’

‘This gentleman had no son, only a daughter called Ming-yu. She was educated and could read, and her father and mother loved her like a precious jewel.

‘Sadly, when Miss Ming-yu was seventeen, she fell ill and died.’

Bao-yu stamped his foot in grief and asked what happened next.

‘Her father and mother missed her so much that they built this shrine and had a statue made of Miss Ming-yu. They appointed someone to burn incense and tend the fire. But years and years have gone by. The people are gone, the temple is falling down, and the statue has turned into a spirit.’

‘Not turned into a spirit,’ Bao-yu said quickly. ‘People like her are, as a rule, alive even after they die.’

‘Bless the Buddha! So that’s it. If you hadn’t told us, young master, we’d all have thought she’d turned into a spirit. She often takes human shape and goes wandering through the villages and along the roads by the inns. The girl taking firewood that I told you about was her. The people in our village have been discussing whether to smash the statue and pull down the temple.’

‘You mustn’t do that! It would be a great sin to pull down the temple.’

‘Lucky you told me, young master. I’ll tell them when I go home tomorrow.’

‘Our old lady and my mother are both good people,’ Bao-yu said. ‘Everyone in the family, old and young, is fond of doing good and giving alms. They particularly like repairing temples and making images of the gods.

‘Tomorrow I’ll draw up an appeal and collect some donations for you. You can be in charge of the incense offerings. When you’ve collected enough, you can rebuild the temple and refurbish the clay image, and we’ll give you money every month to keep the incense burning. Wouldn’t that be a good thing?’

‘If you did that, the young lady would bring me a blessing too. I’d have a few coins to spend.’

Bao-yu asked the names of the place and village, how far away they were and in which direction. Grannie Liu made it all up as she went along.

Bao-yu believed every word. Back in his room, he spent the night planning it all. Early next morning he came out, gave Tealeaf several hundred cash and sent him first to look the place over carefully, following the place names and directions Grannie Liu had given. They would decide what to do when he returned. After Tealeaf had gone, Bao-yu waited and waited, but there was no sign of him. He grew as frantic as an ant on a hot pan. At last, when the sun was setting, Tealeaf came back in high spirits.

‘Was there a temple?’ Bao-yu asked at once.

‘You hadn’t heard her properly, sir,’ Tealeaf said with a smile. ‘You gave me a fine search. The place names and directions weren’t as you said, so I spent all day looking. I didn’t find a ruined temple till I got to a field ridge to the north-east.’

Bao-yu’s face lit up with joy. ‘Grannie Liu is old. She could easily have remembered it wrong for a moment. Tell me what you saw.’

‘The temple door did face south, and the place was in a terrible state.

‘I was thoroughly fed up with searching. When I saw it, I thought, “At last!” and hurried in. One look at the clay image and I was so frightened I ran out. It looked just like a living thing.’

Bao-yu laughed with delight. ‘If she can take human form, naturally there must be some life in her.’

Tealeaf clapped his hands. ‘What girl? It was a God of Plague, with a blue face and red hair!’

Bao-yu spat. ‘You useless wretch!

‘You can’t even manage a little job like that.’

‘Who knows what book you’ve been reading now, Master Bao, or whose nonsense you’ve heard and taken for truth? You send me off to knock my head against some wild errand with no sense to it, and then you call me useless?’

Seeing how angry he was, Bao-yu hastened to soothe him.

‘Don’t be upset. Go and look again another day when you have time. If she was making fools of us, of course there won’t be anything. But if it really exists, won’t you have earned some hidden merit too? I’ll reward you handsomely.’

While they were talking, a page from the inner gate came to say, ‘The girls from Her Old Ladyship’s rooms are standing at the inner gate asking for you, Master Bao.’
