Chapter 51 Young Miss Xue composes new poems on the past; Quack Hu recklessly prescribes tiger-and-wolf drugs

Chapter end

When they heard that Bao-qin had written ten quatrains on ancient sites she had visited in the different provinces, each concealing an object, they all agreed that this was a fresh and ingenious idea. They crowded round to read what she had written:

I. Meditation on Red Cliffs

Red Cliffs lie buried; the water does not flow.

Only names remain to ride an empty boat.

One torch amid the din; cold blows the grieving wind.

Countless heroic souls wander within.

II. Meditation on Jiaozhi

A mighty bell cast in bronze resounds, upholding rule;

Its voice goes overseas, among the Rong and Qiang.

Ma Yuan’s achievements surely were immense;

Why speak again of Zi-fang and his iron flute?

III. Meditation on Zhongshan

When did fame or profit ever keep you company?

Without cause, you were summoned from the mortal dust.

The ties that bind are seldom easy to break;

Do not blame others if they laugh at you so often.

IV. Meditation on Huaiyin

A hero must take care no vicious dog can harry him;

His place in the Three Qi is fixed when the coffin closes.

A word to worldly folk: withhold your scorn;

The kindness of one meal is known even in death.

V. Meditation on Guangling

Cicadas clamour, crows roost: a blink, and all is past.

How fares the scene along the Sui embankment now?

Simply winning a name for charm

Sets all those mouths and tongues astir.

VI. Meditation on Peach-leaf Ferry

Fading grass and idle flowers are mirrored in a shallow pool;

Peach Branch and Peach Leaf must always part.

So many beams and pillars of the Six Dynasties;

A little portrait hangs in vain, inscribed upon the wall.

VII. Meditation on the Green Mound

Black waters stretch away, choked with sobs, unable to flow;

Ice-cold strings are plucked to sound the song’s full sorrow.

The rules of the House of Han indeed deserve our sighs;

Ailanthus and oak should blush with everlasting shame.

VIII. Meditation on Mawei

Lonely rouge-marks stain the sheen of sweat;

Tenderness in a moment goes to the eastern ocean.

Only because some trace of charm remains,

Clothes and quilts still hold a fragrance to this day.

IX. Meditation on Pudong Temple

Little Hong-niang, lowly of bone and lightest of body,

By secret tucking, furtive carrying, forced a match.

Though her mistress time and again had her strung up,

She had already drawn the other to go along.

X. Meditation on Plum-blossom Convent

Not beside the plum, but beside the willow;

Who there picks up the painted beauty?

Reunited, do not think of Spring Fragrance coming;

A parting in the west wind, and another year.

When they had read the poems, they marvelled at their ingenuity. Bao-chai was the first to speak.

‘The first eight all have grounds in the histories. The last two have nothing to support them, and we don’t really know about them. I think it would be better to write two others instead.’

Dai-yu hastened to stop her:

‘Oh, Cousin Chai! How you do “glue down the bridges before playing the zither”! What an affectation! Granted, those two have no support in the histories. We haven’t read these unofficial tales and don’t know the full story, but surely we’ve seen the two plays? A child of three knows them, let alone us!’1

‘That’s quite true,’ said Tan-chun.

‘Besides, she has actually been to the places,’ Li Wan added. ‘There may be no evidence for the two stories, but false tales have been passed from mouth to mouth throughout the ages. People with nothing better to do have even deliberately made these ancient sites to fool others. When I came to the capital that year, for instance, I saw three or four tombs of Lord Guan alone. Everything he did in his lifetime is a matter of record. How can he have so many tombs? Naturally, people in later times admired the kind of man he had been. Some of these stories may well have been made up out of that very admiration. When I looked in the Comprehensive Geography, I found that it wasn’t only Lord Guan who had so many tombs. All sorts of famous people of the past had more than one, and there were still more ancient sites with no evidence behind them.

‘These two stories may have no historical evidence either, but storytellers tell them, they’re acted on the stage, and they’re even explained in the notes to the fortune-sticks people draw. Old and young, men and women, everyone knows them and talks of them in everyday speech. And it isn’t as though we’d been reading the words and songs of The Western Chamber and The Peony Pavilion and had to fear we were reading wicked books. There’s no harm in these at all. Leave them as they are.’

Only then did Bao-chai let the matter drop. They all spent some time guessing, but none of their answers was right.

Winter days are short. Before they knew it, it was time to go to the front of the house for the evening meal, and they all went to dinner together. Someone reported to Lady Wang:

‘Aroma’s brother, Hua Zi-fang, has come in to say that their mother is gravely ill and longs to see her daughter. He begs your permission to take Aroma home for a visit.’

‘They are mother and daughter, after all,’ said Lady Wang. ‘How could we refuse to let her go?’

She sent for Xi-feng, told her what had happened and instructed her to make whatever arrangements she thought fit.

Xi-feng agreed. Back in her rooms, she sent Zhou Rui’s wife to tell Aroma the news.

‘Send for one of the women who accompany people on visits,’ she added. ‘The two of you are to take two little maids and go with Aroma. Have four older men from outside assigned to the carriages. Order a large carriage for you to ride in with her, and a small one for the maids.’

Zhou Rui’s wife had agreed and was just going when Xi-feng spoke again.

‘Aroma is one for saving trouble. Tell her I said she is to put on some clothes in good, bright colours and take a really big bundle of clothes with her. The wrapper must be a good one too, and she must take a good hand-warmer. Tell her to come and let me see her before she leaves.’

Zhou Rui’s wife agreed and went off.

After a while Aroma duly appeared, dressed and ready to go, with the two maids and Zhou Rui’s wife carrying her hand-warmer and bundle of clothes. Xi-feng saw that the gold hairpins and pearl ornaments in her hair looked quite splendid. She then examined her clothes: a peach-pink silk-tapestry jacket with a hundred-boys pattern, lined with ermine; a padded skirt of spring green, worked in couched gold and coloured embroidery; and, over these, a blue satin coat lined with grey squirrel.

‘All three of those were Her Ladyship’s,’ Xi-feng said with a smile. ‘It was very good of her to give them to you. Only the coat is a little too plain, and too cold for this weather. You ought to wear one with a thicker fur.’

‘Her Ladyship only gave me this grey-squirrel one and an ermine one,’ Aroma replied, smiling. ‘She said she would give me one with a thicker fur for the New Year, but I haven’t had it yet.’

‘I have one with a thicker fur,’ said Xi-feng, smiling. ‘I don’t like the way the fur lies any more and was just going to have it altered. Never mind, you can have it to wear first. When Her Ladyship has yours made for the New Year, I’ll have another made for myself. We’ll call that your paying me back.’

The others all laughed. ‘You’re always saying things like that, ma’am.

‘All year round you spend so freely. Who knows how many things you’ve quietly paid for out of your own pocket on Her Ladyship’s behalf? More than anyone could count, and when do you ever settle up with her? Yet now you make us laugh by talking as though you begrudged the least little thing.’

‘How could Her Ladyship think of all these things?’ Xi-feng said, smiling. ‘They’re hardly serious business, after all. Still, however little attention we pay them, the whole family’s dignity is at stake. There’s nothing for it but for me to lose a little myself and see that everyone is properly turned out. I may as well get a good name for it. If they all look like burnt steamed rolls, the first thing people will do is laugh at me for managing the household so well that I’ve turned them into beggars.’

‘Who else has your wisdom, ma’am?’ they all exclaimed admiringly. ‘You think of Her Ladyship above you and care for the servants below.’

As they spoke, Xi-feng told Patience to bring out the dark-blue silk-tapestry coat with eight roundels, lined with sand-fox fur, from the day before, and gave it to Aroma. She then looked at the bundle. It had only a lined wrapper of ink-splashed patterned damask with pale-pink silk inside, and contained just two half-worn garments, a padded jacket and a fur coat.

Xi-feng told Patience to fetch a duoluo-cloth wrapper lined with pale jade-coloured silk, and to put in a snow-cloak as well. Patience went and brought out two: one a half-worn scarlet broadcloth cloak, the other a scarlet yusha-cloth cloak.

‘Even one is more than I deserve,’ said Aroma.

‘You take the broadcloth one,’ Patience said, smiling. ‘I brought this other one out at the same time so that someone can take it to Miss Xing. In all that snow yesterday, everyone else had one, broadcloth or yuduan or yusha. A dozen or so scarlet coats against the great expanse of snow: how splendid they looked! Only she had that old felt cloak, and it made her look all the more hunched and shrunken. I did feel sorry for her. Let her have this one now.’

‘My things, and she gives them away on her own authority!’ Xi-feng said, laughing. ‘As if I couldn’t spend enough by myself, now you’ve come to help me along. Better and better!’

‘It all comes of your constant devotion to Her Ladyship and kindness to the servants,’ the others said, laughing. ‘If you were mean and thought only of your possessions, without a thought for the people beneath you, how would Miss Patience ever dare do such a thing?’

‘That’s why I say she’s the only one who understands even a little of what I feel,’ Xi-feng said, smiling.

Then she gave Aroma further instructions. ‘If your mother gets better, well and good. If she can’t pull through, you must stay there. Send someone to tell me, and I’ll send your bedding over.

‘Mind you don’t use anyone else’s bedding or hairdressing things.’

She turned to Zhou Rui’s wife. ‘You know our rules, of course. I needn’t tell you.’

‘We know them all,’ Zhou Rui’s wife replied. ‘When we get there, we shall see that their people keep out of the way. If she stays, we shall certainly ask for one or two inner rooms to be set aside for her.’

With that she followed Aroma out, gave orders for lanterns to be made ready, and they drove off to Hua Zi-fang’s house. Of that we need say no more.

Xi-feng now sent for two of the mammas from the House of Green Delights.

‘Aroma may not come home,’ she told them. ‘You know the older girls. Pick out two with some sense to spend the night in Bao-yu’s room.

‘You must keep a good eye on things yourselves, too. Don’t let Bao-yu get up to any nonsense.’

The two mammas went away. Presently they returned to report, ‘We’ve put Skybright and Musk in his room. The four of us already take turns overseeing the night duty.’

Xi-feng nodded. ‘Make him go to bed early at night and get up early in the morning.’

The old mammas assented and went back to the Garden. Before long, sure enough, Zhou Rui’s wife sent word to Xi-feng that Aroma’s mother had already been laid on her deathbed, and Aroma could not return. Xi-feng reported this to Lady Wang and sent to Prospect Garden for Aroma’s bedding and dressing-case.

Bao-yu watched Skybright and Musk put everything in order and send it off. Then the two girls took off what remained of their make-up and changed their skirts and jackets. Skybright settled herself on the warming frame and stayed there.

‘Don’t play the young lady today,’ Musk said, laughing. ‘I advise you to bestir yourself a little.’

‘There’ll be time for me to bestir myself when you’ve all gone. As long as one of you is here, I mean to enjoy it.’

‘There’s a good sister,’ Musk said with a smile. ‘I’ll make the bed. You let down the cover over the long mirror and fasten the catch at the top. You’re taller than I am.’

She went to make Bao-yu’s bed.

Skybright gave an exasperated little sigh, then laughed. ‘I’d only just got warm, and now you have to come bothering me.’

Bao-yu had been sitting unhappily, wondering whether Aroma’s mother was alive or dead. Hearing Skybright’s complaint, he got up himself, went out, lowered the mirror-cover and secured the catch. He came back smiling.

‘You stay in the warm. It’s all done.’

‘There’s no staying in the warm for long,’ Skybright said, laughing. ‘I’ve just remembered we haven’t brought in the bed-warmer.’

‘How thoughtful of you to remember!’ said Musk. ‘He never wants a bed-warmer, and our warming frame is warm enough, not like the cold kang in that room. We can do without one tonight.’

‘But if both of you sleep up there,’ Bao-yu said, smiling, ‘there’ll be no one outside my bed. I shall be frightened. I shan’t sleep all night.’

‘I’m staying here,’ said Skybright. ‘Musk can go and sleep outside his bed.’

By this time it was the second watch. Musk lowered the bed-curtains, moved the lamp, lit incense and helped Bao-yu settle for the night. Only then did the two girls go to bed.

Skybright stayed on the warming frame, while Musk slept just outside the warm alcove. After the third watch, Bao-yu called for Aroma in his sleep. He called twice without an answer, then woke and remembered that she was away. He laughed at himself.

Skybright was awake already. Laughing, she called to Musk, ‘Even I’m awake, and she’s right beside him and hasn’t heard a thing. She lies there like a corpse!’

Musk rolled over and yawned. ‘He called for Aroma. What has that to do with me?’ she said with a laugh. Then she asked what he wanted.

Bao-yu wanted tea. Musk got up at once, wearing only her little padded jacket of red silk.

‘Put my jacket round you before you go,’ he said. ‘Mind you don’t catch cold.’

Reaching behind her, Musk took the warm, full-fronted sable-throat jacket he wore when he got up in the night and put it over her shoulders. She stepped down, washed her hands in the basin, then poured a cup of warm water and brought the large rinsing-bowl for Bao-yu to rinse his mouth. Only after that did she take a teacup from the tea-shelf, rinse it first with warm water, pour half a cup from the warm tea-pot and give it to him to drink. She rinsed her own mouth too and drank half a cup herself.

‘Be a good sister and grant me a sip too,’ Skybright said, laughing.

‘You get more shameless by the minute!’ Musk said with a laugh.

‘My dear little sister, tomorrow night you shan’t stir. I’ll wait on you the whole night. How’s that?’

Musk could only help her rinse her mouth too, and pour her half a cup of tea. When she had drunk it, Musk said, laughing, ‘Neither of you is to go to sleep. Keep talking. I’m going out for a little while.’

‘There’s a ghost outside waiting for you,’ Skybright said with a laugh.

‘There’s sure to be a great bright moon,’ said Bao-yu. ‘We’ll keep talking. Off you go.’

As he spoke, he coughed twice.

Musk opened the back door and lifted the felt curtain to look out. The moonlight was indeed beautiful. As soon as she had gone, Skybright decided to give her a fright for fun. Confident that she was stronger than other people and did not feel the cold, she slipped down from the warming frame and followed her out, wearing only her little jacket, without putting anything over it.

‘Mind you don’t get chilled,’ Bao-yu warned her with a smile. ‘It’s no joke.’

Skybright merely waved a hand and went out after Musk. The moonlight was like water. Suddenly a light breeze blew, piercing her flesh to the bone and making her hair stand on end.

‘No wonder people say you mustn’t let the wind blow on you when you’re warm,’ she thought. ‘This cold really is fierce.’

She was just about to frighten Musk when Bao-yu called loudly from inside, ‘Skybright’s gone out!’

Skybright hurried back into the room, laughing. ‘As if I could have frightened her to death! Trust you to fuss and flap like an old woman!’

‘It wasn’t really that I thought you’d frighten her too badly,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘First, it’s no good you getting chilled. And then, if she wasn’t expecting you, she might cry out. Suppose she woke the others? They wouldn’t say we’d been having a game. They’d say Aroma had only been away one night and already we were seeing ghosts and spirits. Come and tuck the quilt in on this side for me.’

Skybright came over and tucked it in, then slipped her hand underneath to warm it.

‘What a cold hand!’ Bao-yu said with a smile. ‘I told you to mind the cold.’

Seeing that her cheeks were as red as rouge, he touched them too. They were icy.

‘Quick, come under the quilt. Come and get warm,’ said Bao-yu.

Before he had finished, the door clacked and Musk came hurrying in, flustered and laughing.

‘I had such a fright! In the shadows behind the rockery, I saw someone crouching. I was just going to cry out, but it was that big ornamental pheasant. It flew up when it saw me and came out into the light, and only then could I see what it was. If I’d screamed without thinking, I’d have had everyone up.’

As she spoke, she washed her hands. Then she laughed again. ‘How is it I never saw Skybright outside? She must have been coming to frighten me.’

‘Here she is, getting warm,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘If I hadn’t called out quickly, you really would have had a fright.’

‘There was no need for me to frighten her,’ said Skybright, laughing. ‘The little hussy had already managed to frighten herself.’

She went back under her own quilt.

‘You don’t mean you went out rigged up as lightly as a trick-rider, just like that?’ said Musk.

‘Just like that,’ Bao-yu said, smiling.

‘Can’t you even choose a decent day to die?’ Musk said. ‘Stand outside a little longer and you’ll have the skin frozen off you.’

She lifted the copper cover from the brazier and used the ash-shovel to bury the glowing charcoal again. She put two pieces of plain incense on it, replaced the cover and went behind the screen to trim the lamp once more. Then she lay down.

After the chill outside, Skybright was warm again, and before she knew it she had sneezed twice.

‘There, you see?’ Bao-yu sighed. ‘You have caught cold after all.’

‘She was complaining of feeling unwell this morning,’ Musk said, laughing, ‘and hasn’t eaten all day.

‘Instead of taking care of herself now, she still has to go playing tricks on people. If she’s ill tomorrow, she’ll only have herself to thank.’

‘Does your head feel hot?’ Bao-yu asked.

Skybright coughed twice. ‘It’s nothing. Since when have I been so delicate?’

Just then the clock on the display shelves in the outer room struck twice. The old mamma on night duty there coughed twice as well.

‘Go to sleep, girls. There’ll be time to talk tomorrow.’

‘We’d better stop talking,’ Bao-yu whispered with a smile. ‘We’ve set them talking again.’

At last they all went to sleep.

When they got up next morning, Skybright did indeed have a stuffy nose and a thick voice, and felt too listless to move.

‘Don’t let anyone know,’ Bao-yu said quickly. ‘If Mother hears, she’ll make you go home to recover. Home may be all very well, but it’s colder there. You’re better off here. Lie down in the inner room, and I’ll have a doctor brought quietly through the back gate to see you.’

‘Even so, you must tell Mrs Zhu,’ Skybright said. ‘Otherwise, when the doctor comes and someone asks, what are you going to say?’

Bao-yu saw that she was right. He called an old mamma and instructed her:

‘Go and tell Mrs Zhu that Skybright has only taken a little chill. It’s nothing serious. Aroma is away, and if Skybright goes home to recover, there’ll be fewer people here than ever. Ask for a doctor to come quietly through the back gate and see her. There’s no need to tell Mother.’

The old mamma was gone some time. When she returned, she said, ‘Mrs Zhu knows. She says if a couple of doses put her right, well and good, but if not, she had better go outside after all.

‘The season is unhealthy just now. It would be bad enough if she gave it to anyone else, but the young ladies’ health is the important thing.’

Skybright was lying in the warm alcove, coughing all the time. At these words she cried out angrily, ‘Have I got the plague, then, that you’re all so afraid of catching it? Once I’ve left here, let’s see you go the rest of your lives without a headache or a fever!’

She actually began to get up. Bao-yu hurried to hold her down, smiling.

‘Don’t be angry. It is her responsibility, after all. She’s afraid Mother will hear and blame her, so she just says it to be on the safe side. You’re always so quick to get angry. No wonder the fire in your liver is so strong now.’

As they were talking, word came that the doctor had arrived. Bao-yu went across and hid behind the bookcase. Two or three old mammas from the back gate brought the doctor in. All the maids had withdrawn. Three or four old mammas lowered the scarlet embroidered curtain across the warm alcove, and Skybright put out just one hand. The doctor saw two fingernails on it a full three cun long, still bearing traces of the deep red dye of balsam flowers, and hurriedly turned his head away. One of the old mammas hastened to cover the hand with a handkerchief. Only then did he feel her pulse for a while. He rose and went into the outer room to speak to the mammas.

‘The young lady has taken an external chill, with stagnation within. The season is unhealthy at present; it amounts to a mild cold-induced fever. Fortunately the young lady is always sparing in her diet, and the wind and cold have not affected her severely. Her qi and blood are simply weak by nature, and she has happened to catch a little of what is about. A couple of doses to disperse it should put her right.’

He followed the women out again.

Li Wan had already sent word to the people at the back gate and to the maids throughout the Garden to keep out of the way. The doctor had seen the Garden’s scenery, but not a single woman. Outside the gate he sat down in the gatekeepers’ duty room and wrote his prescription.

‘Don’t go yet, sir,’ the old mamma told him. ‘Our young master is a great one for questions. He may have something more to say.’

‘Was that not a young lady just now?’ the doctor asked hurriedly. ‘Was it a young gentleman? The room was just like a lady’s bedchamber, and they lowered the curtain. How could it have been a gentleman?’

The old mamma laughed quietly. ‘My dear sir! No wonder the boys said just now that they’d fetched a new doctor today. You really don’t know anything about our household. The room belongs to our young master. The person in it is his maid, one of the senior girls. What young lady? If it had been a young lady ill in her own bedchamber, do you suppose you’d have got in so easily?’

She took the prescription inside.

Bao-yu examined it. It listed zisu, jiegeng, fangfeng, jingjie and other drugs, with zhishi and mahuang further down.

‘Damn him, damn him!’ he said. ‘He treats girls just as he treats us. How can that be right? Whatever stagnation she may have inside, how could she stand zhishi and mahuang? Who fetched him? Send him away at once and get one we know.’

‘We don’t understand whether the medicine’s good or bad,’ the old woman said. ‘It’s easy enough to send the boys for Doctor Wang instead. Only this doctor wasn’t called through the steward’s office, so we shall have to give him something for his chair or horse.’

‘How much?’

‘Too little wouldn’t look well. It ought to be a tael of silver, at least. That’s the proper thing for a household of our standing.’

‘How much do we give Doctor Wang when he comes?’

‘Doctor Wang and Doctor Zhang don’t get money each time they come,’ the woman said, smiling. ‘They’re sent substantial gifts at the four festivals every year. That’s a fixed annual custom.

‘This man is here for the first time. He must have a tael of silver to take away.’

Bao-yu told Musk to fetch the silver.

‘Who knows where Madam Hua has put it?’ said Musk.

‘I’ve often seen her getting money from the little cabinet with mother-of-pearl inlay. Let’s go and look.’

They went to the room where Bao-yu kept his things and opened the inlaid cabinet. The top compartment was full of brushes, ink, fans, incense cakes, purses and sashes of every kind. The lower one held several strings of cash. They opened a drawer and at last found a little winnowing-basket with a few pieces of silver in it. There was a small balance as well. Musk took a piece of silver and picked up the balance.

‘Which mark is one tael?’ she asked Bao-yu.

‘You’re asking me?’ Bao-yu said, laughing. ‘That’s funny. You might have only just come here.’

Musk laughed too and was about to ask someone else.

‘Just pick out a big piece and give him that,’ said Bao-yu. ‘We’re not doing business. Why bother reckoning it up?’

Musk put down the balance, chose a piece and weighed it in her hand.

‘I should think this is about a tael,’ she said with a laugh. ‘Better too much than too little. We don’t want the poor wretch laughing at us. He won’t say we don’t know how to read a balance. He’ll think we’re being mean on purpose.’

The old woman was standing outside on the stone step.

‘That’s a five-tael ingot with half snipped off,’ she said, smiling. ‘There must still be at least two taels in that piece. We haven’t any silver-shears here now. Put it back, miss, and choose a smaller one.’

Musk had already shut the cabinet and come out.

‘Who wants to go looking again?’ she said, laughing. ‘Never mind if it’s a little more. Take it.’

‘Just hurry and tell Tealeaf to fetch Doctor Wang,’ Bao-yu said.

The woman took the silver and went to see to it.

Presently Tealeaf did bring Doctor Wang. After feeling Skybright’s pulse, he gave much the same account of her illness as the first doctor. His prescription, however, did indeed omit such drugs as zhishi and mahuang. Instead it contained danggui, chenpi, baishao and others, in somewhat smaller quantities than before.

‘Now that’s medicine for girls!’ Bao-yu said delightedly. ‘Even when you need to disperse an illness, you mustn’t go too far.

‘When I was ill last year, I had a cold-induced fever and food lying stagnant inside. He saw me, and even then he said I couldn’t stand tiger-and-wolf drugs like mahuang, shigao and zhishi. Beside you, I’m like an old poplar that’s been growing for decades among the graves out in the wilds. You’re like those white begonias Yun brought me in the autumn, just opening into flower. How could you stand medicines that even I can’t bear?’

‘Are poplars the only trees that grow among wild graves?’ Musk and the others asked, laughing. ‘Aren’t there pines and cypresses too? I can’t stand poplars. Such great clumsy trees, with such tiny leaves, and even when there isn’t a breath of wind they keep up that rustling. Fancy choosing one of those to compare yourself to! How low you’ve sunk.’

‘I wouldn’t dare compare myself to pines and cypresses,’ Bao-yu said, smiling. ‘Even Confucius said, “Only when the year turns cold do we know that pine and cypress are the last to wither.” That shows how noble those two are. Only someone with no sense of shame would lightly compare himself to them.’

As he spoke, the old woman came back with the medicine. Bao-yu had the silver medicine-pot brought out and ordered them to boil it on the brazier.

‘Send it to the tea-room to be boiled properly,’ Skybright said. ‘How can we have this room smelling of medicine?’

‘The scent of medicine is finer than any scent of flowers or fruit,’ said Bao-yu. ‘Immortals gather herbs and brew them, and lofty scholars and recluses gather and prepare them too. Medicine is the most wonderful thing. I was just thinking that we had everything in this room, with only the scent of medicine missing. Now it’s complete.’

As he spoke, he had already told someone to set it on to simmer. He also told Musk to get some things ready and sent an old mamma to see Aroma and urge her not to cry so much. Once everything had been attended to, he went to the front of the house to pay his respects to Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang and have his meal.

Xi-feng was just discussing a plan with Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang.

‘The days are so short and cold. Wouldn’t it do just as well for my elder sister-in-law to have her meals in the Garden with the girls from now on? When the days grow longer and warmer, it won’t matter if they come backwards and forwards again.’

‘That’s a good idea,’ Lady Wang said, smiling. ‘It would be much more convenient in wind or snow. Eating something and then being chilled isn’t good for them. Nor is walking here on an empty stomach, filling it with cold wind, and putting food on top of it. Why not use the five large rooms just inside the back gate of the Garden? There are women on night duty there in any case. We can choose two women cooks to work there and prepare meals just for them. They have their regular allowance of fresh vegetables. They can draw it from the steward’s office, in money or in provisions, as they please. And they can have a share of the pheasants, zhang deer, roe deer and all the other game.’

‘I’ve been thinking the same thing,’ said Grandmother Jia. ‘Only I was afraid another kitchen would mean more trouble.’

‘No extra trouble at all,’ said Xi-feng. ‘It’s the same allowance. What we add here, we take away there. Even if it did mean a little more trouble, with the girls out in that bitter north wind, the others might manage, but what about Cousin Lin? How could she stand it? Even Cousin Bao couldn’t stand it, let alone the girls.’

‘That’s just it,’ said Grandmother Jia.

‘I meant to say so last time, but you had so many important matters to attend to, and now all these other things have come up…’
