Chapter 68 Unhappy Er-jie is tricked into Prospect Garden; Jealous Xi-feng raises an uproar at the Ning mansion

Chapter end

Now, after Jia Lian set out, it happened that the military commissioner of Ping-an was away inspecting the frontier and would not return for about a month. Unable to get any definite word, Jia Lian had to wait at his lodgings. By the time the commissioner had returned, received him and settled the business, nearly two months had passed. Only then could Jia Lian set out for home.

Xi-feng, meanwhile, had worked everything out beforehand. No sooner had Jia Lian left than she summoned craftsmen of every trade to put three rooms in the east wing in order, decorating and furnishing them just like her own principal rooms. On the fourteenth she told Grandmother Jia and Lady Wang that she wished to go to a nunnery early on the fifteenth to offer incense. She took only Patience, Felicity, Zhou Rui's wife and Wang-er's wife, and before getting into the carriage told the four women what was afoot. She ordered the men to wear plain clothes and use a plain carriage cover, and they set off straight for the house.

Joker led the way to Er-jie's gate and knocked. Bao Er's wife opened it.

‘Quick, tell the Second Mistress the First Mistress is here,’ he said with a grin.

At these words Bao Er's wife felt her soul fly clean out through the top of her head. She rushed in to tell Er-jie. Er-jie was startled too, but the visitor was already there, and there was nothing to do but receive her courteously. She hastily straightened her clothes and came out to meet her. As she reached the gate, Xi-feng was just alighting from the carriage. All the ornaments in her hair were plain white silver. She wore a pale-blue satin jacket, a dark satin cloak and a plain skirt of white damask. Her brows curved like willow leaves, their tips lifted high; her long phoenix eyes held a keen, triangular glint. She was as lovely as peach blossom in spring, as pure and fresh as chrysanthemums in autumn. Zhou Rui's wife and Wang-er's wife supported her into the courtyard. Er-jie hurried forward, smiling, to make her greeting.

‘Sister, you honour me with your visit. Forgive me for not coming farther to meet you, and for receiving you so unprepared.’

She sank into a curtsey. Xi-feng hastened to return her smile and her salutations, and the two went indoors hand in hand.

Xi-feng took the place of honour. Er-jie told a maid to bring a kneeling cushion and prepared to make her formal obeisance.

‘I am young, and everything to do with my coming here was discussed and decided by my mother and elder sister. Now that I have the good fortune to meet you, if you will not despise my humble station, I beg you to guide and instruct me in everything. I shall open my whole heart to you and devote myself to your service.’

As she bowed, Xi-feng hurried down from her seat to return the courtesy.

‘It is all because of my foolish woman's notions,’ she said. ‘I have always urged my husband to be careful, not to spend his nights with women in the pleasure quarters, for fear of distressing his parents. You and I are alike in our foolish devotion, but Master Lian has misunderstood me. He might perhaps conceal his visits to those places, but marrying you as a secondary wife is a great event, a solemn family ceremony, and even of that he told me nothing. I myself have urged him to take this step early, so that he might have children.

‘Instead, he took me for one of those jealous wives, went through with this great affair on his own, and never let me know. I have been wronged and have nowhere to plead my case. Heaven and earth alone can bear me witness. I heard a rumour ten days ago, but was afraid he would be displeased, and dared not speak first.

‘Now that he happens to be away, I have come in person to pay my respects and ask you to understand my feelings. Will you trouble yourself to move into our house? We sisters could live together, with one heart between us, and urge him to attend carefully to worldly affairs and look after his health. That would be the proper way. If you stay outside while I remain within, how can I be at ease, however foolish and unworthy a companion I may be?

‘Besides, it would look very bad if outsiders came to hear of it. His reputation matters. Let them talk about me instead; I shall not complain. So you see, my good name for the rest of my life is entirely in your hands. As for what those petty-minded servants say, they find me too strict in running the household and add a little here, subtract a little there, behind my back. It is only human nature. A woman of your understanding could surely never take it all for truth. If I were really so bad, with three ranks of parents-in-law above me and countless sisters and sisters-in-law beside me, could the Jias, a family distinguished for generations, have tolerated me to this day? Another woman might be angry that he had secretly married you and set you up outside. I count it my good fortune. Heaven and earth, gods and Buddhas could not bear to see me slandered by those creatures, and so they brought this about. I have come to beg you to live with me, on the same footing, with the same allowance, serving our elders together and advising our husband together. We shall share our joys and share our sorrows, as close as sisters, more united than flesh and blood. Not only will those petty people be ashamed of having misjudged me, but when he comes home and sees us, even our husband must feel some secret remorse.

‘You will be my great benefactress, sister. You will wash away every stain on my name. If you will not come with me, I am willing to stay here with you. Let me be the younger sister and wait on you every day while you wash and dress your hair. All I ask is that you speak kindly of me to him and win me a little favour, so that I may keep a corner to live in. I would die content.’

With this she began to sob. At the sight of her, Er-jie could not help shedding tears too.

They exchanged formal salutations and sat down in order of precedence. Patience at once came forward to pay her respects. Seeing her fine clothes, her pleasing looks and her uncommon bearing, Er-jie guessed who she was. She caught her by the arm before she could bow.

‘Don't, sister, please! You and I are in the same position.’

Xi-feng rose too, smiling.

‘You'll be the death of her! Let her pay her respects, sister. She's our maid, after all. You mustn't do that again.’

She then told Zhou Rui's wife to take from a bundle four bolts of fine cloth and four pairs of gold-and-pearl hairpins and rings, as gifts on their first meeting. Er-jie bowed to receive them. Over tea they told one another what had passed. Everything Xi-feng said was an admission of her own fault, a reproach to herself: ‘I can't blame anyone else. All I ask now is that you should care for me, sister,’ and so forth. Er-jie took her for the best of women. It was only natural for disgruntled servants to slander their mistress. She opened her whole heart to Xi-feng as they talked, and came to regard her as a true confidante. Zhou Rui's wife and the other women standing by praised all the good their mistress did in managing the household. Her only fault, they said, was caring too much, which made people resent her.

‘The rooms are ready already. You'll see for yourself when you come, ma'am.’

Er-jie had long thought it would be better to live in the mansion. After such a reception, how could she refuse?

‘Of course I ought to come with you, sister,’ she said. ‘But what is to happen to this place?’

‘Nothing could be easier. Have the pages carry your trunks and valuables over.

‘There's no use taking all this heavy stuff. We can leave someone to look after it. Whoever you think suitable can stay here.’

‘Now that I've met you, sister, I shall leave everything to you once I come in,’ Er-jie said. ‘I haven't been here long, and I've never managed a household. I know nothing of the world. How could I venture to decide? Just take these few trunks. I have nothing of my own. Even those things belong to him.’

Xi-feng told Zhou Rui's wife to make a careful list and see that everything was safely carried to the east-wing rooms. She then urged Er-jie to put on her things. Hand in hand they got into the carriage and sat together. Xi-feng spoke to her softly.

‘Our family is very strict. The old lady knows nothing whatever about this. If she heard that he had married you during mourning, she would have him beaten to death. You had better not see her or Her Ladyship just yet. We have a very large garden where the girls live. People seldom go there. Stay in the garden for a couple of days while I find some way of explaining things. Then you can be presented to them properly.’

‘I leave it entirely to you, sister,’ Er-jie said.

The pages attending the carriage had all been instructed beforehand. They made straight for the back entrance instead of the main gate.

When they had alighted and sent everyone away, Xi-feng took Er-jie through the back gate of Prospect Garden to call on Li Wan. By now nine out of ten people in the garden knew about the affair. The unexpected sight of Xi-feng bringing her in drew a crowd of visitors with questions, and Er-jie was introduced to them all. Everyone praised her beauty and her pleasant manner.

Xi-feng gave them each her orders. ‘Not a word of this is to get outside. If the old lady or Her Ladyship hears of it, I'll see you dead first.’

The women and maids of the garden had always feared Xi-feng. Since Jia Lian had done this during both national and family mourning, they knew how serious it was, and all kept out of the business. Xi-feng quietly begged Li Wan to take Er-jie in for a few days.

‘Once I've explained things, we'll move over, of course.’

Li Wan knew that the rooms at Xi-feng's had already been put in order. And since the family was still in mourning, it was only right not to make the affair public. She could hardly refuse to let Er-jie stay for the time being. Xi-feng then found ways of dismissing all Er-jie's own maids and gave her one of her own instead.

She secretly instructed the married women in the garden: ‘Keep a good watch on her. If she goes missing or runs away, I'll hold every one of you to account.’

Then she went off to pursue her schemes in secret. All over the household people marvelled privately: ‘Look how good and virtuous she's become!’

Er-jie was delighted with her new home. The girls in the garden were all friendly, and she settled down contentedly, believing she had found her proper place. But after three days her maid Shan-jie began to baulk at her orders.

‘I've run out of hair oil,’ Er-jie told her. ‘Go and ask the First Mistress for some.’

‘Second Mistress, how can you be so ungrateful and have so little sense?’ Shan-jie said. ‘Our mistress has to wait on Her Old Ladyship every day, as well as Lady Wang here and Lady Xing over there. All those sisters-in-law and young ladies, and hundreds of men and women, high and low, get up every morning and wait for her word.

‘There are ten or twenty big matters to attend to every day, to say the least, and thirty to fifty little ones. Outside the house, beginning with Her Highness, think of all the courtesies owed to princes, dukes, marquesses and earls. Inside, there are all these friends and relations to arrange things for. Thousands in silver, tens of thousands in cash, all pass through her hands every day, and it is her one head that must think of it all and her one mouth that must give the orders. How could I bother her with a trifle like this? I advise you to make do as best you can. You didn't come here as a properly wedded wife, did you? Only a good, virtuous woman such as you scarcely find once in all the ages would treat you as she has. Anyone a little less so would have raised an uproar as soon as she heard, and left you outside to live or die. What could you have done about it?’

Er-jie lowered her head under this lecture. There was something in it, she thought. She would have to put up with things. Shan-jie gradually grew too lazy even to bring her meals. One came early, another late, and whatever she brought was left over. Er-jie spoke about it twice, only to have Shan-jie start shouting before she could say more.

Afraid that people would laugh at her for not knowing her place, Er-jie had to bear it. She saw Xi-feng only at intervals of five or eight days. Then Xi-feng was all smiles and pleasant looks, with ‘sister’ forever on her lips.

‘If any of the servants fail in their duty and you can't manage them, just tell me. I'll beat them.’

She scolded the maids and married women too.

‘I know you. You bully the gentle ones and cower before the strong. Once you're out of my sight, whom do you fear? If I hear a single word of complaint from the Second Mistress, I'll have your lives.’

Seeing such kindness, Er-jie thought, ‘With her to look after things, why should I make trouble? Servants often don't know how to behave. If I complain and they suffer for it, people will only call me unkind.’ She therefore covered up for them instead.

Meanwhile Xi-feng had Wang-er make inquiries outside, and learnt every detail of Er-jie's affairs. She had previously been promised to another family. The intended husband, now only nineteen, spent his days whoring and gambling, neglected his livelihood and had squandered all the family property. His father had turned him out, and he was living at a gambling house. The father had accepted ten taels from Old Mrs You to cancel the betrothal, but the young man still knew nothing about it. His name was Zhang Hua.

Once Xi-feng knew the whole story, she wrapped up twenty taels and gave them to Wang-er, secretly ordering him to lure Zhang Hua into his keeping and support him. He was to have the young man draw up a petition and lodge it with the authorities, accusing Master Lian of ‘marrying during national and family mourning, defying the imperial decree and deceiving his parents, using wealth and power to force the cancellation of a betrothal, and taking another wife while retaining the first’.

Zhang Hua understood the danger well enough and at first dared not act rashly. When Wang-er reported this, Xi-feng swore in a rage.

‘A mangy dog! You couldn't prop that wretch up against a wall. Explain it to him carefully. He could accuse our family of rebellion and still come to no harm.

‘I only want him to make a disturbance and disgrace them all. If the case grows serious, I can settle it.’

Wang-er had his orders, and could only go and explain it all to Zhang Hua. Xi-feng gave him further instructions.

‘If he accuses you, go and answer him.’

He was to do such-and-such, and say so-and-so.

‘I have my own reasons.’

Knowing that she would stand behind him, Wang-er told Zhang Hua to add his name to the petition.

‘Just accuse me of going between them and handling the transfers, and of putting Master Lian up to the whole thing.’

Zhang Hua now knew what to do. He and Wang-er settled their plans, wrote out a petition, and the next day he went to the Censorate to cry for justice.

The censor, sitting in the hall, read the petition. Seeing that it accused Jia Lian and named a household servant, Wang-er, he had no choice but to send men to the Jia mansion to summon Wang-er to answer. The blue-clad runners did not dare enter on their own authority and merely asked for word to be taken in. But Wang-er had been expecting them. He needed no message, for he was already waiting in the street. As soon as he saw the runners, he went forward to meet them with a smile.

‘Sorry to put you to the trouble, brothers. My little offence must have come to light. Nothing for it, then. Come and put the chains on me.’

The runners dared do no such thing.

‘Just come along, sir. Don't make a joke of it.’

He went with them and knelt before the censor, who ordered the petition to be shown to him. Wang-er made a show of reading it through, then knocked his head on the ground.

‘I know all about this, Your Honour. My master did indeed do these things. But Zhang Hua has an old grudge against me and has deliberately dragged me into it. There are others involved. I beg Your Honour to question him further.’

Zhang Hua knocked his head on the ground.

‘There is someone else, Your Honour, but I dare not accuse him. That is why I have accused only his servant.’

Wang-er pretended to lose patience.

‘You fool! Out with it at once. This is an imperial tribunal. Even if it's a master, you must name him.’

Zhang Hua then named Jia Rong. The censor had no choice but to send for him.

Xi-feng had also sent Qing-er to keep watch in secret. As soon as the case was brought, she hurriedly summoned Wang Xin and told him about it. He was to ask the censor to put on a show of severity merely to frighten them, and she gave him three hundred taels to smooth the way. That night Wang Xin went to the censor's private residence and laid the groundwork. Fully informed of the affair, the censor accepted the bribe. When he returned to the hall next day, he merely declared that Zhang Hua was a scoundrel who, owing money to the Jia family, had fabricated charges to slander respectable people. The censor was already on close terms with Wang Zi-teng, and Wang Xin had only to put in a word at the Wang house. With the Jias involved as well, the censor was only too glad to dispose of the matter. He said no more about it, accepted what came his way, and confined himself to summoning Jia Rong to answer.

Jia Rong and the others were busy attending to Jia Zhen's affairs when someone suddenly brought word that charges had been laid against them: such-and-such had been alleged, and they must act quickly. In a panic, Jia Rong went to tell his father.

‘I had thought of that danger,’ Jia Zhen said. ‘But I never expected him to have the nerve.’

He at once wrapped up two hundred taels and sent it off with someone to smooth matters over with the censor. He also ordered a servant to answer the charges. While they were still discussing it, a servant announced:

‘Mrs Lian from the Rong mansion is here.’

This gave Jia Zhen a real fright. He was about to hide with Jia Rong when Xi-feng came in.

‘My fine elder brother! What splendid things you've been leading your younger brothers into!’

Jia Rong hastened to pay his respects. Xi-feng caught him by the arm and pulled him along with her. Jia Zhen still managed a smile.

‘Look after your aunt properly. Tell them to slaughter an animal and get a meal ready.’

Then he hurriedly called for his horse and took refuge elsewhere.

Xi-feng led Jia Rong towards the principal rooms. You-shi came out to meet them and, seeing the look on Xi-feng's face, hastened to smile.

‘What's the matter? Why such a hurry?’

Xi-feng spat full in her face.

‘Can't you find anyone to take the girls of your You family, that you have to smuggle them into ours? Are the Jia men all so wonderful? Has every other man on earth died? Even if you were determined to give her to him, there should have been the proper matchmakers and witnesses, everything openly agreed and decently done. Phlegm has addled your brains and fat has stopped up your wits! With national mourning and family mourning both upon us, you send a woman in. Now someone has brought a case against us, and I'm a crab without legs. Even the officials know me as a jealous shrew. They've named me and want me divorced. What wrong have I done since I married into this family, that you should treat me like this? Or have the old lady and Her Ladyship dropped a word in your ear, and you've laid this trap to squeeze me out? You and I will go before the officials together now and have it all made clear.

‘Then we'll call the whole clan together and say it out to one another's faces. Give me a bill of divorce, and I'll go.’

She burst into tears as she spoke, pulling at You-shi and insisting that they go to the tribunal. Jia Rong was so desperate that he fell to his knees and knocked his head on the floor, begging, ‘Dear aunt, dear aunt, please don't be angry!’

Xi-feng turned her abuse on him.

‘You conscienceless wretch! May thunder split your skull and five devils tear your body apart! You don't know how high heaven is or how deep the earth. Stirring up trouble all day, getting into these shameless, lawless doings that will ruin the family! Your dead mother's spirit won't forgive you, nor will your ancestors. And you dare try to reason with me!’

Still weeping and cursing, she raised her hand to strike him. Jia Rong knocked his head down so hard it sounded on the floor.

‘Don't be angry, Aunt. Mind your hand. Let me hit myself. Please don't be angry.’

He raised his own hands and gave himself a volley of slaps, right and left, then began questioning himself.

‘Will you meddle in other people's business again, looking one way and forgetting the other?

‘Will you listen only to Uncle and not to Aunt again?’

The onlookers tried to soothe Xi-feng. They wanted to laugh, but dared not. Xi-feng flung herself into You-shi's arms and howled loud enough to shake heaven and earth.

‘I'm not angry that you found your younger brother another wife. Why did you let him defy the decree and deceive his parents, and leave me to bear the disgrace? Let's go before the officials ourselves and save the constables and runners a journey. And then we'll go to the old lady, Her Ladyship and all the clan, and let them judge between us. Since I'm so unvirtuous, since I won't let my husband marry another wife or buy a concubine, all I ask is a bill of divorce, and I'll leave at once. I went myself to bring your sister home. I was so afraid of angering the old lady and Her Ladyship that I haven't dared tell them yet. She's living in the garden now, with her three teas and six meals, and menservants and maids worth their weight in gold and silver to wait on her. I've been hurrying to get rooms ready for her, with everything on the same footing as my own, and only waiting for the old lady to be told. I meant to bring her in so we could all live quietly and properly, without my bringing up the past. And now it turns out she belonged to somebody else already.

‘I don't know what you've been up to. No one ever tells me anything. Now they've brought charges against me. Yesterday I was frantic. Even if I went out to face the officials, it would be your Jia family's name I disgraced. I had no choice but to steal five hundred taels of Her Ladyship's money to smooth things over. And my man is still there in chains.’

From talking she turned to crying, from crying to cursing. At last she began to wail aloud for her ancestors and her father and mother, threatening to kill herself and dash her head against something. She had kneaded You-shi into a lump of dough and covered her clothes in tears and snot. You-shi could say nothing except to scold Jia Rong.

‘You wicked wretch! Fine work you and your father have done! I said it would never do.’

At this Xi-feng, still crying, seized You-shi's face in both hands and pulled it round to confront her.

‘Were you out of your mind? Was there an aubergine stuffed in your mouth? Or had they put a bit between your teeth? Why didn't you come and tell me? If you had told me, wouldn't everything be peaceful now? Would the officials have been brought into it? Would things have come to this? And now you blame them!

‘As the old sayings go, “A good wife spares her husband trouble” and “Strength outside is nothing beside strength within.” If you were any good at all, how could they have got into this mess? You have neither ability nor a tongue in your head. A gourd with its mouth sawn off, that's what you are! All you can do is fuss and cringe, hoping to be thought a virtuous wife. And still they neither fear you nor listen to you.’

She spat several times. You-shi was crying too.

‘Isn't that just how it is? Ask the people who were there, if you don't believe me. Of course I tried to dissuade them, but what use was that if they wouldn't listen? What could I do? I can't blame you for being angry, sister. I can only bear it.’

The concubines, maids and married women were now kneeling in a dense mass all over the floor, pleading with conciliatory smiles.

‘You are the most understanding of ladies, ma'am. Our mistress may have been wrong, but you've punished her enough. Before all us servants, too! You two have always been so close. Please spare her some dignity.’

They offered tea, but Xi-feng flung that down too. She stopped crying long enough to tuck up her hair, then turned on Jia Rong again, weeping and cursing.

‘Go and fetch your father.

‘I'll ask him to his face. A nephew marrying when he is only in the fifth seven-day period of mourning for his own elder uncle! That's a point of etiquette I never learnt. I'd like to ask about it, so I'll know how to teach the younger generation in future.’

Jia Rong remained on his knees, knocking his head against the floor.

‘My parents had nothing to do with it. Your son here must have eaten shit and lost his senses for a moment. It was I who put Uncle up to it. My father knew nothing about it either. He's busy now arranging Grandfather's funeral procession. If you make a scene, Aunt, it will be the death of me. Punish me yourself, I beg you. I'll accept whatever you give me. But you must take care of this lawsuit. I'm not capable of handling anything so serious. A woman like you must know the saying, “A broken arm stays inside the sleeve.” I've been an utter fool. After behaving so badly, I'm no better than a cat or dog. Once you've taught me my lesson, you won't lower yourself to quarrel with the likes of me. I must still ask you to take the trouble to keep the people outside quiet. I am your unworthy son, after all. Now that I've made trouble, you must put up with it and care for me still.’

He went on kowtowing without cease.

Seeing mother and son in this posture, Xi-feng could hardly carry her attack any further. She changed her face and her manner of speaking, and began apologising to You-shi instead.

‘I'm young and know nothing of affairs. The moment I heard that someone had brought charges, I was frightened out of my wits. I don't know what I may have said to offend you just now, sister-in-law. Rong is right: “Hide a broken arm inside your sleeve.” You must make allowances for me. And please speak to your husband for me. The first thing is to hush up this lawsuit.’

You-shi and Jia Rong both assured her that she need not worry.

‘Whatever happens, Uncle won't be involved in the slightest. You said you had spent five hundred taels, Aunt. Mother and I will have to put five hundred together and send it over for you to replace the money. How could we let you be accused of making away with funds as well? We should deserve to die for that. There's just one other thing. You must help us with the old lady and Their Ladyships, Aunt. Please don't mention any of this to them.’

Xi-feng gave a cold laugh.

‘You did it all over my head, and now you want to coax me into covering for you.

‘I may be a fool, but I'm not as big a fool as that. Your brother-in-law is my husband, sister-in-law. If you were afraid he'd have no heir, surely I had still more reason to be afraid. Your sister is as much a sister to me. When I first heard about it, I was so happy I couldn't sleep all night. I hurried to call people in to prepare the rooms, meaning to bring her home and live with her. It was the servants who had the petty notions. “You're far too good-natured, ma'am,” they said. “If we had our way, we'd tell the old lady and Her Ladyship first and see what they said. There'd be time enough afterwards to prepare rooms and fetch her.” I threatened to beat them and scolded them for saying it, and only then did they stop. But nothing would go as I wished. I had to be made a fool of. Out of thin air this Zhang Hua appeared and filed a complaint. When I heard, I was so frightened I didn't close my eyes for two nights. I dared not make it known, so I had to ask people to find out who this Zhang Hua was, to be so bold. After two days of inquiries, he turned out to be a worthless beggar.

‘Young and ignorant as I am, I actually laughed. “What can he complain of?” I said. But the pages told me, “The Second Mistress was originally promised to him. Now he's desperate. Freezing or starving to death is death all the same. With this right to lay hold of, even if he dies for it, his death will be worth more than dying of cold or hunger. How can you blame him for bringing a complaint? The master really was too hasty about it.

‘“Marrying during national mourning is one offence, during family mourning another, marrying secretly without his parents' knowledge a third, and taking another wife while the first remains a fourth. As the saying goes, ‘A man prepared to be flayed alive will dare to drag the emperor from his horse.’ Poverty has driven him mad. What wouldn't he do? And with right so completely on his side, why shouldn't he complain? Was he to wait for an invitation?” Tell me, sister-in-law, even if I'd been Han Xin or Zhang Liang, wouldn't that have frightened all the stratagems out of me? Your younger brother wasn't home. I had no one to consult, and could only put up money to cover the trouble. But the more I spent, the more I gave them a handle against me, and the more they tried to extort. I'm like a mouse with a sore on its tail: how much pus and blood can there be to squeeze out? I was so worried and angry that I had to come to you.’

Before she had finished, both You-shi and Jia Rong were assuring her, ‘Don't trouble yourself. Of course we'll see to it.’

Jia Rong added, ‘Zhang Hua only risked his life to bring the case because poverty had driven him to desperation. We can find a way. Promise him some silver. All he need do is plead guilty to bringing false charges, and we'll arrange for the case against him to be settled.

‘Then, when he comes out, give him a little more silver, and that will be the end of it.’

Xi-feng smiled.

‘My dear boy, no wonder you can do these things, seeing one side and forgetting the other. You really are a fool. Suppose you tell him that, and he agrees for the time being. He gets clear of the lawsuit and gets his silver. Naturally that settles it for the moment. But people like him are rogues. Once the money's gone, he'll find some pretext to extort more. If he rakes this up again, we may not be afraid of him, but it will still be a worry.

‘He has only to ask why we gave him money if there was nothing wrong. It will never end.’

Jia Rong was no fool. Hearing this, he smiled.

‘I have another idea. “A bearer of tales when he comes, a bearer of tales when he goes.” I'd better settle this myself. I'll go and ask Zhang Hua what he wants: whether he insists on having the woman, or would rather take money, drop the matter and marry someone else. If he insists on having her, I'll have to persuade my Second Aunt to leave and marry him after all. If it's money he wants, we'll have to give it to him.’

‘Whatever you say, I couldn't bear to let your aunt leave,’ Xi-feng said quickly. ‘I would never consent to her going. My dear nephew, if you care for me, you must give him more money instead.’

Jia Rong knew very well that, for all her words, what Xi-feng wanted most was to have the woman herself leave, while she played the virtuous wife. He now agreed to whatever she said. Xi-feng was pleased.

‘The outside business is easy enough to settle. But what are we to do about things at home?

‘You must come over with me and explain it all.’

You-shi grew alarmed again. Catching hold of Xi-feng, she begged her to think of a suitable lie.

Xi-feng gave a cold laugh.

‘If you weren't capable of handling it, who asked you to do it? Now you take that tone again, and I can't bear it. I'd like not to help you think of a way out, but I'm soft-hearted and can't say no. However people play upon me, I go on caring for them just as foolishly. There's nothing for it. I'll have to shoulder it myself.

‘Now, you two must keep out of sight. I'll take your sister alone to kowtow to the old lady and Their Ladyships. I'll simply say she is your sister, that I took a fancy to her and thought her a very good girl. As I haven't been very fruitful, I'd been thinking of buying a couple of girls to put in our rooms. Now that I've seen how good your sister is, and as it would make another marriage within the family, I want to take her as a secondary wife for my husband. Her parents and sisters have all died recently, I'll say, and she is in such straitened circumstances that she cannot keep herself. She cannot wait until the hundred days are over: she has neither a home nor a livelihood, and really cannot hold out. So it was my idea to bring her in. I've had the east-wing rooms made ready for her to stay in for the time being, and the marriage won't be consummated until mourning is over. I'll brazen it out, with this shameless face of mine, and stick to my story whatever happens. If there is any blame, it won't fall on you. Think it over, both of you. Will that do?’

You-shi and Jia Rong answered together, smiling.

‘No one can match your generosity and ingenuity, Aunt. Once it's settled, Mother and I will come over to thank you properly.’

You-shi hurriedly ordered the maids to help Xi-feng wash her face and put her hair and clothes to rights. She had wine and food brought in, and herself handed Xi-feng the wine and chose morsels for her. Xi-feng did not stay long, but insisted on leaving. Back in the garden, she told Er-jie what had happened, going on to describe how she had worried and made inquiries, how she had devised her plans, and how only by doing such-and-such could everyone be saved from punishment.

‘I shall have to prise this fish-head apart myself, so that everyone can be at peace.’

For the outcome, you must listen to the next chapter.
