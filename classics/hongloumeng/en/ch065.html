Chapter 65 Jia the Second secretly takes You Er-jie to wife; You San-jie longs to marry Liu the Second

Chapter end

Now Jia Lian, Jia Zhen and Jia Rong had settled everything between them. On the second day of the lunar month, Old Mrs You and San-jie were taken to the new house first. Though it was not quite what Jia Rong had made it out to be, Old Mrs You found it very well provided, and both mother and daughter were satisfied. Bao Er and his wife welcomed them with blazing warmth. They bustled round Old Mrs You, calling her now ‘Mother’, now ‘Your Old Ladyship’, and round San-jie, calling her ‘Third Aunt’ or ‘Madam’.

Before dawn next morning, during the fifth watch, a plain sedan-chair brought Er-jie. Incense, candles and paper offerings of every kind, bedding, wine and food had all been carefully prepared. Presently Jia Lian arrived in a small chair, dressed in plain mourning clothes. They bowed to Heaven and Earth and burned the paper offerings. Old Mrs You was delighted to see Er-jie in new clothes and head ornaments, quite transformed from the girl she had been at home. The bride was helped into the bridal chamber. That night Jia Lian and she tumbled together like phoenix and mate in all the delights of love, of which we need give no detailed account.

The more Jia Lian looked at her, the more he loved her; the more he studied her, the more she pleased him. He could not think how to make enough of her. He ordered Bao Er and the others to call her simply ‘the mistress’, with none of their seconds and thirds. He called her ‘my lady’ himself, striking Xi-feng clean out of the reckoning.

On his occasional visits home, he merely said that business at the Ning mansion kept him occupied. Knowing how close he was to Jia Zhen, Xi-feng and the others naturally supposed the two had something to discuss and suspected nothing. Numerous though the household servants were, none concerned themselves with such affairs. Even those idlers who made it their business to ferret out every little thing preferred to flatter Jia Lian and turn the opportunity to profit. Who would have let the secret out? Jia Lian's gratitude to Jia Zhen therefore knew no bounds.

He provided five taels a month for the household's daily expenses. When he was absent, the mother and her two daughters ate together. When he came, husband and wife ate alone, and the mother and younger daughter took their meal in their own room. He also brought over all the private savings he had accumulated over the years and gave them to Er-jie to keep. Under the bedclothes, with their heads on the pillow, he told her freely what Xi-feng was like and how she behaved. Once she died, he would bring Er-jie into the mansion. Er-jie was, of course, willing. The ten or so people in the little household thus settled into a life of considerable comfort.

Before they knew it, some two months had passed. One day, after completing the Buddhist observances at the Temple of the Iron Threshold, Jia Zhen was on his way home in the evening when he decided to visit his wife's younger sister, whom he had not seen for so long. He first sent a page to find out whether Jia Lian was there. The boy returned to say he was not. Delighted, Jia Zhen sent all his attendants home ahead of him except for two trusted pages to lead his horse. It was lamplighting time when he reached the new house and slipped quietly inside. The two pages tethered his horse in the stable and went to wait in the servants' quarters.

The lamps had just been lit indoors. Jia Zhen first saw Old Mrs You and San-jie; then Er-jie came out to greet him. He still called her ‘Second Aunt’. They had tea and chatted for a while.

‘Well,’ he said with a smile, ‘what do you think of my matchmaking? Miss this one, and you could search with a lantern and never find another. Your sister is getting some presents ready to bring when she comes to see you in a few days.’

While they were talking, You Er-jie had ordered wine and food. With the doors shut they were all family, and there was no need for restraint. Bao Er came to pay his respects.

‘You do have some conscience,’ Jia Zhen told him. ‘That is why I put you here to look after them. I'll have greater use for you one day. Don't go drinking outside and making trouble. I shall see you rewarded. If anything is short here, Master Lian has a great deal to do and too many people round him at home, so come straight to me. We brothers aren't like other people.’

‘Yes, sir. I understand. If I didn't do my best, I might as well throw my head away.’

‘See that you remember it,’ said Jia Zhen, nodding.

The four of them sat drinking together. Er-jie knew what was wanted.

‘I'm rather frightened, Mother,’ she said. ‘Come over to the other side with me for a little while.’

Old Mrs You understood too, and actually went out with her, leaving only the little maids. Jia Zhen began pressing his shoulder against San-jie's and rubbing his face against hers, taking every sort of liberty.

The little maids could not bear to watch and slipped out too, leaving the pair to enjoy themselves at their ease. What they got up to we do not know. The two pages were drinking with Bao Er in the kitchen, while his wife attended to the cooking. Suddenly the two maids came in, laughing and teasing, and asked for wine.

‘So you've slipped away too, instead of waiting on them up there,’ Bao Er said.

‘If they call and no one's there, there'll be trouble.’

‘You muddled, blithering cuckold!’ his wife cursed him. ‘Go on, drink yourself to death on that yellow swill! And when you're drunk, tuck your prick between your legs and go and lie like a corpse. What the fuck has it to do with you whether they call or not? I'll answer for everything. Whatever wind or rain there is, none of it will land on your head.’

Bao Er owed his rise in the world to his wife, and lately he had been more indebted to her than ever. Apart from making money and drinking, he concerned himself with nothing. Jia Lian and the others would not rebuke him either. He therefore treated his wife like a mother, obeying her in everything, and went to bed as soon as he had drunk his fill. She stayed drinking with the maids and pages, currying favour with them so that they would put in a good word for her with Jia Zhen.

The four were enjoying their drinking when they heard a knock at the gate. Bao Er's wife hurried out to open it and found Jia Lian dismounting. He asked whether anything was the matter.

‘The elder master is here, in the west courtyard,’ she told him quietly.

Jia Lian went to the bedroom, where he found Er-jie and her mother. Both looked somewhat embarrassed at his arrival. He pretended to know nothing.

‘Bring some wine, quickly,’ was all he said. ‘We'll have a couple of cups and go to bed. I'm very tired today.’

Er-jie hurried up with a welcoming smile, took his outer clothes, offered him tea and asked him one solicitous question after another. Jia Lian tingled with delight. Presently Bao Er's wife brought the wine, and the couple drank together. His mother-in-law would not join them and went back to her own room to sleep. Of the two little maids, one was sent over to wait on them.

When Jia Lian's trusted page Long-er went to tether the horse, he found another already there. A closer look told him it was Jia Zhen's. Understanding the situation, he too made for the kitchen. Xi-er and Shou-er were sitting there drinking. They understood at once when they saw him, and laughed.

‘You've come at just the right moment. We couldn't keep up with our master's horse, and we were afraid of being caught out after curfew, so we came to beg a bed for the night.’

‘Plenty of kang to sleep on,’ Long-er said, laughing. ‘Make yourselves at home.

‘Master Lian sent me with the monthly allowance. I've handed it to the mistress, and I'm not going back either.’

‘We've had plenty,’ said Xi-er. ‘Come and have a cup.’

Long-er had only just sat down and raised his cup when a commotion broke out in the stable. Sharing a manger, the two horses had taken exception to each other and begun lashing out with their hind legs. Long-er and the others hastily put down their cups and ran out, shouting at the horses. At last they quieted them and tethered them separately before coming back inside.

‘You three can stay here,’ Bao Er's wife said with a smile. ‘There's tea ready too. I'm off.’

She went out and closed the door behind her. After a few more cups, Xi-er was staring glassily. Long-er and Shou-er shut the door and turned to find him stretched flat on his back across the kang. They shook him.

‘Come, brother, get up and lie properly. If you only think of yourself, what are we supposed to do?’

‘Tonight we're all going to lie pressed together fair and square, like a batch of cakes in an oven,’ Xi-er said. ‘If either of you plays the proper gentleman, I'll fuck your mother good and hard!’

Seeing that he was drunk, Long-er and Shou-er wasted no more words. They blew out the light and settled down as best they could.

The noise of the horses made Er-jie uneasy, and she kept talking to distract Jia Lian. A few cups had roused his amorous appetite. He ordered the wine and fruit cleared away, shut the door and began to undress. Er-jie wore only a little scarlet jacket, her cloud of black hair loosely pinned up. Desire suffused her face, making her still lovelier than by daylight. Jia Lian put his arms round her and laughed.

‘Everyone says that yaksha of a wife of mine is handsome. Looking at you now, I wouldn't have her pick up your shoes.’

‘I may be pretty,’ said Er-jie, ‘but I have no virtue. It seems to me one would be better off without good looks.’

‘What do you mean by that?’ he asked quickly. ‘I don't understand.’

Tears fell from her eyes.

‘You all treat me as a fool, but what is there I don't know? We've been husband and wife for two months now. It isn't long, but I know you're no fool either. In life I am yours, and in death my ghost will be yours. Now that we are married, I depend on you for the rest of my days. How could I dare to hide a single word from you? I have someone to look to. But what is to become of my sister?

‘As I see it, this way of carrying on cannot last. We must find some lasting provision for her.’

‘Don't worry,’ Jia Lian said with a smile. ‘I'm not one to grow sour with jealousy. I know all about the past. There's no need to be frightened. You feel awkward because your sister's man happens to be your husband's elder brother. I'd better go and break down the barrier myself.’

He went off to the west courtyard. Through the window he could see the bright lights and the two of them drinking and making merry.

Jia Lian pushed open the door and entered with a smile.

‘So you're here, elder brother. I've come to pay my respects.’

Jia Zhen was too ashamed to speak and could only rise to offer him a seat.

‘Why put on this manner with me?’ Jia Lian said at once, still smiling. ‘What have we brothers been to each other all these years? You've taken so much trouble on my account that I could be ground to dust and never repay you. How can I rest easy if you stand on ceremony with me? From now on, I beg you to go on just as before. Otherwise, I'd sooner have no heir than ever dare to come here again.’

He was about to kneel. Jia Zhen caught him in alarm and pulled him up.

‘Whatever you say, brother. I shall do exactly as you wish.’

‘Bring some wine,’ Jia Lian called. ‘I'll have a couple of cups with my elder brother.’

He pulled at You San-jie too.

‘Come here and have a cup with your husband's younger brother.’

‘That's my brother!’ Jia Zhen laughed. ‘Trust you, Lian. I must drain this cup.’

He tossed his head back. San-jie stood on the kang, pointing at Jia Lian and laughing.

‘Don't give me your smooth-tongued nonsense. Mixed-flour noodles in clear water: you may be eating them, but I can see what you're at. Since you've brought your shadow-puppets on stage, at least don't poke through the paper screen. Don't let the grease clog your heart and imagine we know nothing about your household. You've spent a few stinking coins, and now you two brothers think you can use us two sisters as whores for your amusement. You've got your sums wrong there! I know what a handful that wife of yours is. You've lured my sister here to be your second wife, and a stolen gong is one you daren't beat. I'd like a meeting with that Mrs Feng myself, to see how many heads and hands she has. If we can all get on, well and good. But if she makes things impossible for us, I'll tear out those wicked guts of yours, ox-bezoars and dog-bezoars and all, then fight that shrew to the death, or I'm no Third Lady You!

‘What's there to be afraid of in drinking? Let's drink!’

She seized the pot and poured herself a cup. After drinking half, she hooked an arm round Jia Lian's neck and tipped the rest into his mouth.

‘I've had a drink with your elder brother already. Now let's get close, you and me.’

Jia Lian was so frightened that all the wine left his head. Nor had Jia Zhen expected such shameless, seasoned boldness from her. The two brothers were old hands in the world of pleasure, but today this girl's speech had left them speechless. She called again and again for her sister.

‘Ask her to come over. If we're going to enjoy ourselves, let's have all four of us together.

‘As they say, keep the good things in the family. They're brothers and we're sisters; no outsiders here. Come along.’

Er-jie was embarrassed. Jia Zhen tried to seize his chance to slip away, but San-jie would not let him. Only now did he regret it. He had never expected this kind of woman; now neither he nor Jia Lian quite knew how to take liberties with her.

San-jie's hair was loosely pinned up, and her scarlet jacket hung half open, showing a spring-green breast-cover and a glimpse of snowy bosom. Below were green trousers and red shoes. Her two little bound feet, her ‘golden lotuses’, were now cocked up, now pressed together; she was not decorous for half a moment. Her two earrings swayed like swings. In the lamplight her willow brows seemed wrapped in green mist, her delicate mouth touched with cinnabar. Her eyes had always been clear pools of autumn water. Wine had now given them a languid, wanton look. Not only did she eclipse her elder sister: in the judgment of Zhen and Lian, none of the many women they had seen, high or low, noble or base, had ever possessed such seductive grace.

The two men were already numb with delight, as if drunk. They could not resist trying to draw her on, but her very wantonness and allure held them in check. San-jie had only to try a little of her skill on them to discover that neither had any judgment or insight worth speaking of. They could not even produce one spirited word. Wine and women: that was all there was to them. She held forth, talking and acting as freely as she pleased, making the two brothers her objects of ridicule and amusement. In truth, it was she who had used the men as her whores, not they who had debauched her.

When she had drunk her fill and tired of the amusement, she would not let the brothers stay any longer. She drove them out, shut her door and went to bed. From then on, the slightest failing on the part of a maid or serving-woman would set her abusing Jia Lian, Jia Zhen and Jia Rong at the top of her voice, charging the three of them, father, son and uncle, with cheating a widow and her fatherless girls. After that visit Jia Zhen no longer dared come lightly. Only when San-jie was in the mood and secretly sent a page to invite him would he venture over for a while; once there, he could only do as she wished.

San-jie had, as it happened, been born with an intolerable temper. Relying on her beauty and allure, she insisted on dressing to outshine everyone else and devised wanton airs and gestures that ten thousand women could not have matched. She delighted in making men drool and lose their wits, unable to approach her yet unwilling to draw away, utterly bewildered. Her mother and sister urged her repeatedly to stop, but she retorted:

‘Sister, you're a fool. We're worth gold and jade, and to let those two laughing-stocks soil us for nothing would show we had no spirit at all.

‘Besides, there's that formidable woman in their family. We're safe only because they're keeping her in the dark. If she finds out one day, do you think she'll let it rest? There'll be an uproar, and who knows who will live and who will die? If I don't make my sport of them now and torment them to square the account, all we'll have left then will be a foul name. It'll be too late for regrets.’

Seeing that she would not listen to reason, her mother and sister had to leave it at that. San-jie was choosy every day about what she wore and ate. Have silver ornaments made, and she wanted gold; give her pearls, and she wanted precious stones. Once she had eaten a fat goose, a fat duck must be killed. If something did not please her, she pushed the whole table away. Clothes she disliked she cut to pieces with scissors, no matter how new or fine their damask or satin, tearing off a strip and cursing with every tear. When did Jia Zhen and the others ever have a single day to suit themselves? Yet they had spent a great deal of money on this shameful business.

When Jia Lian came, he stayed in Er-jie's room. He too was beginning to regret the affair. But Er-jie was an affectionate creature. Having taken him for her lord for life, she was attentive to his every comfort. In gentleness and obedience, always consulting him, never trusting in her cleverness to act on her own, she was ten times better than Xi-feng. In beauty, conversation and deportment she was half as good again. She had reformed now, but she had already lost her footing. Once the word ‘unchaste’ had been set against her, none of her good qualities counted for anything.

Jia Lian, however, would say: ‘Who has never done wrong? Recognise your fault and mend it: that's enough.’

He therefore said nothing of her past unchastity and valued only her present goodness. They were inseparable as glue and lacquer, as fish and water, united in heart and purpose, vowing to live and die together. What place was left in his thoughts for Xi-feng or Patience?

In the privacy of their bed Er-jie often urged him:

‘Speak to Cousin Zhen and find someone you know to marry San-jie. We can't keep her here for ever. Sooner or later she'll bring trouble on us. What shall we do then?’

‘I did speak to him the other day,’ said Jia Lian. ‘He simply can't bear to give her up.

‘I told him, “It's a lovely piece of fat mutton, but too hot to handle. A rose is charming, but its thorns are big enough to pierce your hand. We may not be able to master her. The sensible thing is to find someone for her to marry.” He just hummed and hawed, and dropped the matter. What can I do?’

‘Don't worry,’ said Er-jie. ‘Tomorrow we'll talk to San-jie first. Once she agrees, leave her to make a fuss herself. When there's nothing else for it, he'll have to marry her off.’

‘That's exactly what we should do,’ said Jia Lian.

Next day Er-jie laid on wine for the occasion, and Jia Lian stayed at home. At noon they expressly invited her younger sister to join them and seated her with their mother in the places of honour. San-jie knew what they intended. After three rounds of wine, before her sister could begin, she started to weep.

‘You have something very solemn to say to me, sister. That's why you've asked me here today. But I'm not a fool. There's no need to go on and on about the shameful things of the past. I know them all, and talking will do no good. Now that you've found a good home for yourself, and Mother has a home too, it's only right that I should find some settlement for myself. But marriage is a matter for one's whole life, from now until death. It's no child's play. I'm changing my ways now and will keep to my proper place. Only, I must choose a man I've long liked and wanted before I'll go with him. If you choose for me, he may be as rich as Shi Chong, more gifted than Zi-jian and as handsome as Pan An, but if I can't take him into my heart, I shall have wasted my life.’

‘That's easily arranged,’ Jia Lian said, smiling. ‘Name the man, and you shall have him. We'll see to all the marriage gifts. Mother needn't trouble herself about a thing.’

‘My sister knows,’ San-jie said through her tears. ‘I needn't say it.’

Jia Lian smilingly asked Er-jie who it was, but for the moment she could not remember. They all racked their brains. Then he exclaimed:

‘It must be him! No doubt about it!’

He clapped his hands and laughed.

‘I know now. He's a good choice, certainly. You do have an eye!’

‘Who?’ Er-jie asked with a smile.

‘Who else could have caught her fancy? It must be Bao-yu.’

Er-jie and Old Mrs You thought this reasonable too. San-jie spat.

‘If there were ten of us sisters, would we all have to marry ten of you brothers? Are there no decent men in the world outside your family?’

They were all astonished.

‘If it isn't him, who else can it be?’

‘Don't think only of what's under your nose,’ San-jie said, laughing. ‘Sister, think back five years. That's all you need to do.’

While they were speaking, Jia Lian's trusted page Joker arrived to fetch him.

‘Your father is waiting for you most urgently, sir.

‘I said you'd gone to your uncle's, and hurried straight here to fetch you.’

‘Did anyone ask for me at home yesterday?’ Jia Lian asked quickly.

‘I told the mistress you were at the family temple with Master Zhen, discussing the hundredth-day observances, and probably wouldn't be home.’

Jia Lian ordered his horse at once and went off with Long-er, leaving Joker to attend to any callers or business.

Er-jie took two dishes of food, ordered a large cup filled with wine, and told Joker to squat at the foot of the kang and eat. She kept up a stream of questions: how old was his mistress at home, how was she so formidable, how old were the old lady and Her Ladyship, how many young ladies were there? She asked about every sort of household matter. Grinning as he ate below the edge of the kang, Joker gave mother and daughters a detailed account of the Rong mansion.

‘I'm one of the men on duty at the second gate,’ he said. ‘There are two shifts of us, four to a shift, eight altogether. Some of the eight are the mistress's confidants, and some are the master's. We daren't touch her men, but her men can do what they like to his. As for our mistress, she has a vicious heart and a sharp tongue. Master Lian's a good enough sort, but what chance has he against her? Miss Patience, the girl who waits on her, is a very good person. She sides with the mistress, but behind her back she often does a good turn.

‘If any of us has done something wrong, the mistress won't let it pass. We only have to beg Miss Patience, and it's settled. Nowadays there isn't a soul in the whole household, high or low, who doesn't hate our mistress, except the old lady and Her Ladyship. People only keep up appearances because they're afraid of her. She never thinks anyone else as good as herself. All she cares about is keeping those two ladies pleased. If she says one, it's one; if she says two, it's two. No one dares stand in her way. She'd like to save every scrap of silver and copper and pile it into a mountain, so the old lady and Her Ladyship will say how well she manages. What they don't see is that the servants suffer and she gets the credit. If there's anything good in the offing, she won't wait for someone else to mention it: she pushes herself to the front. If there's trouble, or she's made a mistake herself, she ducks her head and pushes it onto someone else, then stands by fanning the flames. Even her own mother-in-law, Lady Xing, is disgusted with her now. “A sparrow flies to the flourishing branch,” she says. “Black hens stick to their own nest! Neglecting her own affairs to bustle about blindly for other people!”

‘If the old lady weren't protecting her, her mother-in-law would have called her back long ago.’

‘If you say all this about her behind her back,’ Er-jie said, smiling, ‘who knows what you'll say about me one day? I'm a whole degree beneath her. You'll have still more to say.’

Joker hastily knelt.

‘How can you say that, ma'am? Do you think I have no fear of being struck by lightning? If only we'd had the good fortune to have someone like you when the master first married. We'd have had fewer beatings and scoldings, and less reason to tremble for our skins. Every one of us who follows the master praises your kindness and mercy to us servants, whether you're there to hear it or not. We've been talking of asking Master Lian to get us transferred. We'd willingly come and serve you.’

‘You monkey-fucked rascal,’ Er-jie said with a laugh. ‘Get up.

‘It was only a joke. Look how it frightened you! What would you come here for? I mean to go and call on your mistress myself.’

Joker waved his hands urgently.

‘You mustn't go, ma'am. I tell you, the best thing would be never to see her in your life.

‘Sweet on the tongue, bitter at heart; two faces and three knives. Smiling above while her foot trips you below. A bowl of fire in the open, a knife in the dark. That's her, every bit of it. I'm afraid even Third Aunt's tongue would be no match for hers. As for a gentle, kind lady like you, how could you stand against her?’

‘I shall simply treat her with courtesy,’ Er-jie said, smiling. ‘What would she dare do?’

‘This isn't the wine making me insolent or talking nonsense, ma'am,’ said Joker. ‘However courteous and yielding you are, when she sees you're prettier and better liked than she is, how could she let it rest? Other women are little vinegar pots of jealousy. She's a whole vat, a great stoneware jar of it.

‘Let Master Lian look twice at a maid, and she'll batter the girl's head to a pulp in front of him. Miss Patience may be his concubine, but if the two get together perhaps once in a year or two, the mistress will still find ten faults to harp on. She makes Miss Patience so angry that she loses her temper and cries and makes a scene. “It wasn't I who asked for this,” she says. “You were the one shamelessly urging me on. I wouldn't agree at first, and you said I was rebelling. Now you behave like this!” Then the mistress has to leave it alone, just like anyone else. She even begs Miss Patience to be reconciled.’

‘Are you making this up?’ Er-jie asked with a laugh. ‘How can a yaksha like that be afraid of her own maid-concubine?’

‘That's what the saying means: no one in the world can escape what's right. Patience has been her maid since she was little. Four of them came with her when she married. Some married, some died, and Patience's the only confidante she has left. She originally had her taken as a concubine for two reasons: to win a name for wifely virtue, and to keep the master tied to her, so he wouldn't stray outside.

‘There's another part to the story. In our family, when the young masters grow up, before they marry they're given two girls to attend to them. Master Lian had two, but within six months of her arrival she'd found fault with both and sent them away. No one else liked to say anything, but she couldn't face it herself. That's why she forced Miss Patience to become his chamber concubine. Miss Patience is a decent person. She's never set any store by that side of things, and she isn't one to set a husband against his wife. She just serves the mistress with absolute loyalty and devotion. That's why she's been allowed to stay.’

‘So that's how it is,’ said Er-jie, smiling. ‘But I've heard there's a widowed young mistress in your household, and several young ladies. If she's so fierce, how do they put up with her?’

Joker clapped his hands and laughed.

‘So you don't know, ma'am. That widowed mistress of ours is nicknamed “the Great Bodhisattva”. She's the kindest, most virtuous person of all. Our family has very strict rules. The widowed ladies don't manage affairs. They're supposed to live quietly and remain faithful to their dead husbands. Luckily there are plenty of young ladies. They're put in her charge to read and write, learn their needlework and learn how to behave. That's her responsibility.

‘Apart from that, ask her about anything and she knows nothing; tell her about it and she won't interfere. It's only because our mistress has been ill lately and there's so much to do that the elder mistress has taken charge for a few days. After all, there's nothing much to manage: just follow the established rules. She's not like our mistress, making work for herself to show off her cleverness. As for the eldest young lady, there's no need to say anything. If she weren't good, she could never have had such great good fortune. The second is nicknamed “Blockhead Number Two”. You could stick a needle into her and she wouldn't so much as say “Ow!” The third is nicknamed “the Rose”.’

Both You sisters eagerly asked, laughing, what that meant.

‘A rose is red and fragrant,’ Joker said with a grin. ‘Who doesn't love it? Only its thorns prick your hand. She's another formidable spirit. It's a pity Her Ladyship didn't bear her: a phoenix out of a crow's nest! The fourth is still little. She's actually Master Zhen's own sister. She's been motherless since she was little, so the old lady told Her Ladyship to take her over and bring her up. She's another who won't interfere in anything. You don't know, ma'am, but apart from our own young ladies there are two more, rare even in Heaven and without equal on earth. One is Miss Lin, the daughter of our master's aunt. Her little name is Dai-yu or something. Her face and figure are not unlike Third Aunt's. She's full of literary learning, but ill from head to foot. Even in weather like this she wears lined clothes. She only has to come out for the wind to knock her over. Those lawless mouths of ours call her “the Ailing Xi Shi” behind her back. The other is Her Ladyship's sister's daughter, Miss Xue, called Bao-chai. She looks as if she'd been heaped together out of snow.

‘Whenever we see them going out or getting into a carriage, or catch a glimpse of them in the courtyard, it's as though some spirit had taken hold of us. We daren't even breathe.’

‘That's the rule in a great household,’ Er-jie said with a smile. ‘Even if you boys are allowed inside, when you meet the young ladies you ought to hide well out of the way.’

‘No, no,’ said Joker, waving his hands.

‘Of course we keep well out of the way. That's proper respect, and goes without saying. But even once we're hidden, we daren't breathe, for fear a strong breath would blow Miss Lin down and a warm one would melt Miss Xue.’

The whole room burst out laughing.
