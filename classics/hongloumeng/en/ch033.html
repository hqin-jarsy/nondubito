Chapter 33 A hostile brother watches his chance and speaks a few words; An unworthy son is soundly beaten for his many faults

Chapter end

Now Lady Wang called Golden’s mother forward and gave her several hairpins and rings in person. She also ordered several groups of monks to be invited to recite scriptures in rites for the release of the dead. The mother kowtowed her thanks and withdrew.

Bao-yu had heard the news on returning from his meeting with Yu-cun. Realising that Golden had killed herself in shame and anger, he had been stricken to the heart. When he came in and Lady Wang scolded and admonished him, he could find nothing to say. Bao-chai’s arrival gave him a chance to leave. He had no idea where to go. With his hands behind his back and his head bent, he walked slowly on, sighing as he went, until his wandering steps brought him to the hall. He had just rounded the screen-door when someone coming in from the other direction collided with him full on.

‘Stand still!’ the man shouted.

Bao-yu started and looked up. It was none other than his father. He caught his breath, then could only stand to one side with his hands hanging down.

‘What is the matter with you?’ Jia Zheng asked. ‘Why are you moping about with your head down, sighing? When Yu-cun came just now and wanted to see you, you took an age to answer the summons. And when you did come, there was nothing spirited or easy about your conversation. You were as listless and drooping as ever.

‘Your face is full of longing and discontent, and now here you are sighing again. Haven’t you enough of everything? Aren’t you comfortable enough? What can you possibly have to behave like this about?’

For all his usual quickness of tongue, Bao-yu could think only of his grief for Golden. He wished he could die that very moment and follow her. He heard none of what his father was saying, but stood staring blankly.

Jia Zheng had not been angry to begin with, but the boy’s frightened manner and unaccustomed failure to answer now began to irritate him. He was about to speak again when a servant came to report that someone from the Prince of Zhongshun’s mansion wished to see him.

Jia Zheng was puzzled. He had never had dealings with the prince’s household: why should they send someone today? Still wondering, he ordered the visitor to be shown in at once and hurried out. Finding that it was the prince’s chief secretary, he hastened to welcome him into the hall, where he had him seated and served with tea.

Before they could exchange civilities, the chief secretary began:

‘I should not have presumed to intrude upon your distinguished household on my own account. I come at the prince’s command, with a favour to ask. For His Highness’s sake, may I venture to ask you to help us? His Highness will appreciate it, and we who serve him shall be endlessly grateful too.’

Unable to make anything of this, Jia Zheng rose with a courteous smile.

‘Since you come at the prince’s command, sir, may I ask what instructions you bring? Pray explain, so that I may carry them out.’

The chief secretary gave a cold laugh.

‘There is nothing to carry out. One word from you will settle it.

‘We have an actor in our household who plays young female roles, a certain Bijou. He has always lived quite contentedly at the mansion, but now he has been away for three to five days. We have looked everywhere and can find no trace of him, so we have been making enquiries in all quarters. Eight people out of ten in this city say that lately he has become very close to that son of yours who was born with the jade in his mouth. Once we heard this, we reported it to the prince: yours is not a house like any other, where we might simply walk in and demand him. His Highness said, “If it were any other actor, I should not mind losing a hundred of them. But Bijou is so ready with an answer, so discreet and dependable, so entirely to this old man’s liking, that I simply cannot do without him.” That is why we ask you to instruct your son to let Bijou return. His Highness’s earnest wishes will be satisfied, and we shall be spared all the trouble of searching for him.’

He ended with a prompt bow.

Shocked and furious, Jia Zheng sent for Bao-yu at once. Bao-yu hurried in, not knowing why he was wanted.

‘You damned wretch!’ Jia Zheng demanded. ‘It is bad enough that you do no studying at home. Must you go out and commit these lawless outrages as well? Bijou is in attendance on the Prince of Zhongshun himself. What are you, you miserable nobody, to lure him away for no reason and bring this trouble down on me?’

Bao-yu was terrified.

‘I truly know nothing about it,’ he answered hurriedly. ‘I don’t even know what “Bijou” means. How can there be any question of my “luring him away”?’

He began to cry. Before Jia Zheng could speak, the chief secretary gave another cold laugh.

‘There is no need to conceal it, young sir. Whether you are hiding him here or know where he is, tell us now and spare us the trouble. We should certainly be grateful.’

Bao-yu repeated that he knew nothing about it.

‘It may only be a false rumour.’

‘We have evidence,’ the chief secretary said with a cold laugh. ‘Why go on denying it?

‘Must I bring it out before your father? Wouldn’t that be rather awkward for you? If you don’t know the man, how did his scarlet sash come to be round your waist?’

Bao-yu was thunderstruck. He stared, speechless.

How had the man found that out? If he knew something so secret, there was probably little else Bao-yu could hide from him. Better send him away before he brought up anything more.

‘Since you know all about him, sir,’ he said, ‘how is it you haven’t heard of such an important thing as his buying a house? I’ve heard that out in the eastern suburbs, twenty li from the city, there’s a place called Zitan Fort, or something of the sort. He has bought a few mu of land and a few rooms there. Perhaps that is where he is.’

The chief secretary smiled.

‘Then that must be the place. I shall go and look. If we find him, all well and good. If not, I shall have to come and consult you again.’

With that, he hurried away.

By now Jia Zheng’s eyes were staring and his mouth was twisted with rage. As he escorted the chief secretary out, he turned to order Bao-yu:

‘Don’t move! I have questions to ask you when I get back.’

He saw the official all the way out. He had only just turned back when he caught sight of Jia Huan rushing about with several pages. He shouted to the pages:

‘Beat him! Beat him!’

At the sight of his father, Jia Huan went limp with fear and stopped at once with his head lowered.

‘What are you running about for?’ Jia Zheng demanded. ‘Where have the people in charge of you gone wandering off to, leaving you to gallop about like a wild horse?’

He ordered the servants who accompanied Jia Huan to school to be fetched. Seeing how angry his father was, Jia Huan seized his chance.

‘I wasn’t running before. Only, as I was passing the well, there was a maid drowned in it. I saw her head, this big, and her body, this thick, all swollen up. It was so frightening that I ran over here.’

Alarmed and bewildered, Jia Zheng asked:

‘Who would jump into a well for no reason?

‘Nothing like this has ever happened in our family. From our ancestors’ time we have always treated our servants kindly and leniently. I suppose I have neglected household affairs these past few years, and naturally the people in charge have taken the opportunity to oppress and extort until they have brought about this waste of a life. If outsiders hear of it, where will our ancestors’ honour be?’

He shouted for Jia Lian, Lai Da and Lai Xing to be summoned at once. The pages answered and were about to go when Jia Huan hurried forward, caught the skirt of his father’s robe and knelt close against his knees.

‘Don’t be angry, Father. No one knows a thing about it except the people in Her Ladyship’s rooms. I heard my mother say…’

He broke off and looked round in every direction. Jia Zheng understood, and glanced at the pages. Taking the hint, they all drew back to either side and behind him.

Jia Huan then whispered:

‘My mother told me that the other day, in Her Ladyship’s room, my elder brother Bao-yu seized her maid Golden and tried to rape her. When he couldn’t, he beat her. Golden was so angry that she threw herself into the well and died.’

Before he had finished, Jia Zheng’s face had turned the colour of gold paper with fury.

‘Bring Bao-yu here at once!’ he roared.

He strode towards the study farther inside, shouting as he went:

‘If anyone tries to reason with me today, I shall hand over my cap and girdle of office, the household property, everything, and let him live with Bao-yu! Sinner that I am, I shall shave off these few troublesome hairs at my temples and find some peaceful place to end my days. At least I shall be free of the guilt of disgracing my ancestors and begetting a rebellious son.’

The gentlemen who were his guests and the servants knew from his manner that Bao-yu was once more the cause. In dismay, biting their fingers and tongues, they hastily withdrew. Jia Zheng sat bolt upright in his chair, panting, his face streaked with tears. His orders came one upon another:

‘Fetch Bao-yu! Bring a heavy cudgel! Get a rope and tie him up! Shut every gate! If anyone takes word to the women’s quarters, beat him to death on the spot!’

The pages could only answer in chorus. Several went to find Bao-yu.

When Jia Zheng had ordered him not to move, Bao-yu had known that things were likely to go badly for him. He could not have guessed how much more Jia Huan had added. He was pacing helplessly round the hall, longing for someone to carry a message inside, but there was no one to be seen. Even Tealeaf was nowhere about.

As he watched and waited, an old woman servant came out. He rushed to catch hold of her as though he had found a treasure.

‘Go in quickly and tell them! The Master’s going to beat me! Hurry, hurry! It’s urgent, urgent!’

In his agitation Bao-yu could not speak clearly, and the old woman happened to be deaf. She made out nothing of his message. His cry of ‘Urgent!’ (yaojin) reached her as ‘Jump in the well!’ (tiaojing).

‘Let her jump in the well if she wants to,’ she said with a smile. ‘What have you to be frightened of, Master Bao?’

Realising that she was deaf, Bao-yu said desperately:

‘Go out and fetch my page, then!’

‘What is there left to settle?’ she answered. ‘It was all settled long ago.

‘Her Ladyship has given clothes and money too. How can you say it isn’t settled?’

Bao-yu stamped in desperation. Before he could think where else to turn, Jia Zheng’s pages arrived and forced him out.

At the sight of him Jia Zheng’s eyes turned dark red. He had no time now to question the boy about the charges: consorting with actors outside, exchanging private gifts, neglecting his studies at home and violating his mother’s maid. He merely shouted:

‘Gag him! Beat him to death!’

Not daring to disobey, the pages pressed Bao-yu down on a bench and struck him ten or so times with a heavy paddle. Even this was too gentle for Jia Zheng. He kicked the man wielding it aside, seized it himself and, with his teeth clenched, brought it down with all his strength thirty or forty times.

Seeing that the beating was taking an ominous turn, the gentlemen hurried forward to wrest the paddle away and plead with him. Jia Zheng would not listen.

‘Ask him what he has done! Ask yourselves whether it can be forgiven! You are the very people who have let him grow so rotten, and even now you come to intercede.

‘Will you wait until you have brought him to murder his sovereign and his father before you stop pleading for him?’

The men recoiled at these words. Knowing that he was beside himself with rage, they hurried out again and could only find someone to take word inside. Lady Wang did not dare to tell Grandmother Jia first. She hastily put on her clothes and hurried straight to the study without stopping to consider who might be there. The gentlemen and pages scarcely had time to scramble out of her way.

Lady Wang’s entrance was like oil poured on Jia Zheng’s fire. The paddle came down harder and faster than ever. The two pages holding Bao-yu hastily let go and moved away, but by now he could no longer stir. Jia Zheng was about to strike again when Lady Wang seized the paddle and held it in her arms.

‘Enough! Enough!’ he cried. ‘You will not be satisfied until you have driven me to my death today!’

‘Bao-yu may deserve a beating,’ Lady Wang said through her tears, ‘but you must take care of yourself too. In any case, the weather is so terribly hot, and Her Old Ladyship is not very well. Bao-yu’s death would be a small matter. But if anything should happen to her, wouldn’t that be serious?’

Jia Zheng gave a cold laugh.

‘Don’t bring that up. I have already failed in my duty as a son by raising this unworthy wretch. Yet whenever I try to discipline him, everyone comes to his defence. I had better strangle him outright today and put an end to any trouble he may cause in future!’

He called for a rope to strangle the boy. Lady Wang hurriedly threw her arms round him, weeping.

‘It is right for you to discipline your son, but you must have some regard for me as your wife. I am nearly fifty now, and this wretched child is all I have. If you are determined to punish him so harshly, I dare not plead too much. But if you insist on his death today, aren’t you deliberately trying to put an end to me? If you must strangle him, fetch the rope and strangle me first. Then strangle him. Neither of us will dare bear you any resentment, and at least we shall have each other to depend on in the world below.’

With that she flung herself over Bao-yu’s body and wept aloud.

At these words Jia Zheng gave a deep sigh and sat down, tears falling like rain.

Lady Wang held Bao-yu in her arms. His face was white, his breathing faint, and the green gauze drawers he wore were covered in bloodstains. Unable to bear it, she untied his sash to look. From buttocks to shins he was bruised blue and purple; here the skin was whole, there broken. Not a single patch was unhurt. She burst into loud sobs.

‘My poor, ill-fated child!’

The cry brought Jia Zhu suddenly to mind. She called his name through her tears.

‘If only you were alive, I wouldn’t care if I lost a hundred like him!’

Hearing that Lady Wang had gone out, Li Gong-cai, Wang Xi-feng, Ying-chun and her sisters had already come from the inner quarters. The others could bear to hear Lady Wang crying out Jia Zhu’s name, but Gong-cai could not contain herself and began to weep aloud too. At the sound, Jia Zheng’s tears rolled down in great drops.

While the scene was still at its height, a maid came to announce:

‘Her Old Ladyship is here.’

She had not finished when a quavering voice could be heard outside the window:

‘Kill me first, then kill him! Wouldn’t that settle everything?’

At his mother’s arrival Jia Zheng was overcome with distress and alarm, and hurried out to meet her. She was coming towards him, leaning on a maid and panting for breath. He bowed before her with a placating smile.

‘What has upset you so, Mother, that you should come out yourself in this dreadful heat? If you had anything to say, you need only have sent for me and given me your instructions.’

Grandmother Jia stopped and caught her breath. Then she spoke sharply:

‘Oh, it is me you are speaking to! I do have instructions to give, but the pity is that in all my life I have never raised a good son. Who am I to give them to?’

Dismayed by her words, Jia Zheng fell to his knees.

‘In disciplining my son,’ he said tearfully, ‘I am only trying to bring honour to our ancestors. How can I bear to hear you speak like this, Mother?’

Grandmother Jia spat in contempt.

‘You can’t bear a single word from me, but Bao-yu is supposed to bear those murderous blows of yours?

‘You say disciplining your son brings honour to our ancestors. Is this how your father disciplined you?’

Tears began to roll down her face. Jia Zheng once more attempted a smile.

‘Please don’t grieve, Mother. It was only that I lost my temper for a moment. I shall never beat him again.’

Grandmother Jia gave a cold laugh.

‘There is no need to take that sulky tone with me. He is your son. I have no business interfering, whether you beat him or not. I suppose you are tired of the lot of us. We had better leave you as soon as we can, and then we shall all have some peace.’

She ordered the chairs and horses to be made ready.

‘Your wife, Bao-yu and I are going back to Nanjing this instant!’

The household servants could only murmur assent, without doing anything. Grandmother Jia called to Lady Wang:

‘There is no need for you to cry either. Bao-yu is still young, so you feel for him. When he grows up and becomes some grand official, he may not even remember that you are his mother. You had better not feel for him now. It may save you some vexation later.’

Jia Zheng hastily knocked his head on the ground and wept.

‘When you speak like this, Mother, you leave me nowhere to stand.’

Grandmother Jia laughed coldly.

‘You have plainly left me nowhere to stand, and now you make it all about yourself!

‘Once we have gone back, you can have your peace of mind. Then we shall see who comes to give you leave to beat him.’

As she spoke she kept ordering the luggage, carriage and chairs to be got ready at once for their return. Jia Zheng went on kowtowing, desperately imploring her forgiveness and admitting his fault.

Even as she spoke, Grandmother Jia was anxious about Bao-yu, and hurried in to see him. This beating was nothing like the others. In grief and anger she too took him in her arms and wept without stopping. Only after Lady Wang, Xi-feng and the others had comforted her for some time did she gradually grow calmer.

Maids and married women servants came forward to help Bao-yu to his feet. Xi-feng rounded on them.

‘Stupid creatures! Open your eyes and look at him! Beaten like this, and you expect him to walk with your help? Go in this instant and fetch that long cane-seated bench!’

They hurried in and brought it out, lifted Bao-yu onto it, then followed Grandmother Jia, Lady Wang and the others into the inner quarters and carried him to Grandmother Jia’s room.

Seeing that Grandmother Jia’s anger had not quite subsided, Jia Zheng dared not go about his own affairs and followed them in. He looked at Bao-yu. He had indeed beaten him too severely. Then he looked at Lady Wang. Over and over she was calling, ‘My child! My own flesh and blood!’

‘If you had died young in Zhu’s place, and he had been left alive, your father would have been spared this anger, and I wouldn’t have wasted half a lifetime worrying. If anything happens to you now, and you leave me behind, who will I have to depend on?’

After a torrent of reproaches, she went back to weeping over her ‘good-for-nothing child’.

Jia Zheng’s spirit sank as he listened. He regretted having struck so cruelly and brought the boy to this pass. He first tried to soothe Grandmother Jia, but she said through her tears:

‘Why don’t you go? What are you still doing here? Are you not satisfied yet? Must you watch him die before you will leave?’

Only then did Jia Zheng withdraw.

By this time Aunt Xue, Bao-chai, Caltrop, Aroma and Shi Xiang-yun were all there too. Aroma’s heart was full of grief and resentment, but she could not give it free expression. Everyone was crowded round Bao-yu, some giving him water, others fanning him, and she could find no way to help. She decided instead to go out to the inner gate and have the pages fetch Tealeaf, whom she questioned closely.

‘Everything was perfectly all right just now. Why did the beating start? And why didn’t you come and warn us sooner?’

‘That’s just it! I wasn’t there,’ Tealeaf said in agitation. ‘I only heard when the beating was half over. I hurried to find out why. It was over Bijou and Sister Golden.’

‘How did the Master come to hear about them?’

‘The Bijou business was most likely Master Xue’s doing. He’s always jealous and couldn’t find any way of getting his own back, so I suppose he stirred up someone outside to come and set the Master against him. As for Golden, it was Master Huan who told him. I heard that from the Master’s own servants.’

Both explanations fitted what Aroma knew, and she was largely convinced that they were true.

She went back to find everyone still tending Bao-yu. When everything had been seen to, Grandmother Jia ordered them to carry him carefully to his own room. They answered and, with many hands helping, hurried him to the House of Green Delights and laid him comfortably on his own bed. There was another long spell of bustle before they gradually dispersed. Only then could Aroma come forward to tend him with care and ask what had really happened.
