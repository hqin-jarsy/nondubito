Chapter 21 Worthy Aroma chides Bao-yu with tender displeasure; Pretty Patience saves Jia Lian with gentle words

Chapter end

Now Shi Xiang-yun ran outside, afraid that Lin Dai-yu would catch her. Behind her Bao-yu called hurriedly, ‘Mind you don’t trip! She’s nowhere near you yet!’

When Dai-yu reached the door, he stretched his arms across the frame to bar her way.

‘Let her off this once,’ he urged with a smile.

Dai-yu tugged at his arms. ‘If I let Yun-er off, may I never live another day!’

Seeing Bao-yu block the doorway, Xiang-yun judged that Dai-yu could not get out, and stopped to laugh.

‘Dear sister, let me off this once!’

Just then Bao-chai came up behind her and said with a smile, ‘For Cousin Bao’s sake, I think you should both let go.’

‘I won’t!’ Dai-yu said. ‘You’re all in league against me. Do you mean to make fun of me together?’

‘Who would dare make fun of you?’ Bao-yu coaxed. ‘If you hadn’t teased her, would she have dared say anything about you?’

The four were still at an impasse when someone came to call them to dinner, and only then did they go to the front rooms. By lamplighting time that evening, Lady Wang, Li Wan, Xi-feng, Ying-chun, Tan-chun, Xi-chun and the others had all come to Grandmother Jia’s. They chatted for a while, then went their separate ways to bed. Xiang-yun again slept in Dai-yu’s room. Bao-yu saw the two of them back to their room. It was well into the second watch, and Aroma had come several times to hurry him, before he returned to his own room to sleep.

At daybreak he threw on his clothes, slipped his feet into his shoes and went over to Dai-yu’s room. Neither Nightingale nor Kingfisher was there. The two cousins were still in bed. Dai-yu lay peacefully asleep, her eyes closed, wrapped snugly in an apricot-red figured-silk quilt. Xiang-yun’s black hair trailed beside her pillow; her quilt came only to her chest, and a snow-white arm curved outside it, with two gold bracelets on it.

Bao-yu sighed at the sight.

‘You can’t keep still even in your sleep! When a draught catches you, you’ll be complaining of a sore shoulder again.’

As he spoke, he gently covered her up. Dai-yu was already awake. Sensing someone there, she guessed it must be Bao-yu, and turned over to find that she was right.

‘What have you come running over for at this hour?’

‘You call this early?’ he said with a smile. ‘Get up and have a look.’

‘Go outside first and let us get up.’

Bao-yu turned and went out.

Dai-yu rose and woke Xiang-yun, and both dressed. Bao-yu came back and sat beside the dressing-table as Nightingale and Snowgoose came in to help them wash and do their hair. When Xiang-yun had washed her face, Kingfisher was about to throw out the used water.

‘Wait,’ Bao-yu said. ‘I’ll wash in it while it’s here. It will save the trouble of going back.’

He came over, bent down and splashed his face twice. Nightingale offered him scented soap.

‘There’s plenty in the basin already. No need to rub any more.’

He splashed his face twice more, then asked for a towel.

‘Still the same old habit!’ said Kingfisher. ‘When will you ever give it up?’

He took no notice, but hurriedly asked for green salt, cleaned his teeth and rinsed his mouth. Xiang-yun’s hair was now finished. He went over to her with a smile.

‘Dear cousin, do my hair for me.’

‘No, I can’t do that.’

‘Dear cousin, why did you do it for me before, then?’

‘I’ve forgotten how. How can I do it now?’

‘I’m not going out. I shan’t wear a cap and headband. You only need to put it in a few loose plaits.’

He went on begging her, with a thousand ‘dear cousins’ and ten thousand more.

Xiang-yun had to draw his head towards her and work through his hair, first with a comb and then with a fine-toothed one. At home, without a cap, he did not wear his hair in paired tufts. The short hairs round his head were simply made into little plaits, gathered in with the hair at the crown, and braided into one large plait tied with red cord. Four pearls were set along it from crown to tip, with a gold pendant at the bottom.

As she plaited, Xiang-yun said, ‘There are only three of these pearls now. This one is different. I remember they all matched. What happened to the other one?’

‘I lost it.’

‘It must have fallen off when you were out, and someone picked it up without your knowing. What a piece of luck for them!’

Dai-yu was washing her hands beside them. She gave a cold laugh.

‘Who knows whether it was really lost, or given to someone to have set in something to wear?’

Bao-yu did not answer. The dressing-table was flanked by cosmetic boxes and other toilet things, which he picked up idly to examine. Without thinking, he took up some rouge and was about to put it to his lips, but feared that Xiang-yun would scold him. While he hesitated, she caught sight of it from behind. Holding his plait in one hand, she reached round with the other and knocked the rouge out of his hand with a smack.

‘When will you ever grow out of that wretched habit?’

Before she had finished speaking, Aroma came in. Seeing what was going on, she knew that his washing and grooming were already attended to, and could only go back to do her own.

Suddenly Bao-chai appeared and asked, ‘Where has Cousin Bao gone?’

‘As if Cousin Bao had any time to stay at home!’ Aroma replied with a smile.

Bao-chai understood. Then she heard Aroma sigh.

‘It’s all very well for cousins to be fond of one another, but there are limits, and there are proprieties. They can’t go romping about together night and day! Whatever anyone says to him goes in one ear and out the other.’

‘I mustn’t misjudge this maid,’ Bao-chai thought. ‘There’s some sense in what she says.’

She sat down on the kang.

In casual conversation she gradually drew Aroma out, asking about her age, her home and so on. She watched her closely, and found much to respect and like in both her conversation and her outlook. Presently Bao-yu came in, and Bao-chai left.

‘What were you and Cousin Bao-chai talking about so merrily?’ he asked Aroma. ‘Why did she run away as soon as I came in?’

His first question went unanswered. When he asked again, Aroma said, ‘Are you asking me? How should I know what goes on between you?’

Hearing this, and seeing an expression quite unlike her usual one, Bao-yu smiled.

‘What, are you really angry?’

Aroma gave a cold laugh. ‘How should I dare be angry? Only don’t come into this room again from now on. You have people to look after you. Don’t come ordering me about. I’ll go back to waiting on Her Old Ladyship.’

As she spoke she lay down on the kang and closed her eyes. Deeply dismayed, Bao-yu hurried over to comfort her, but she kept her eyes shut and paid him no attention. He was at a loss. Seeing Musk come in, he asked, ‘What’s the matter with your sister?’

‘How should I know? Ask yourself, and you’ll have your answer.’

Bao-yu sat dumbfounded for a while. Feeling thoroughly rebuffed, he rose with a sigh.

‘Don’t speak to me, then. I’ll go to sleep too.’

He got down from the kang and lay on his own bed. After a long silence Aroma heard him snoring softly. Supposing that he was asleep, she rose and brought a cloak. She had only just laid it over him when, with a sudden swish, he flung it off, still keeping his eyes closed and pretending to sleep.

Knowing perfectly well what he meant, Aroma nodded with a cold laugh.

‘There’s no need for you to be angry either. From now on I’ll pretend I’m dumb and never say another word against you. Will that do?’

Bao-yu could bear it no longer. He sat up.

‘What have I done now?

‘There you go, advising me again! I wouldn’t mind your advice, but you didn’t give me any just now. As soon as I came in, you wouldn’t speak to me and lay down in a huff. I still don’t know why, and now you say I’m angry. When did I hear you give me any advice?’

‘Don’t you know in your own heart?’ said Aroma. ‘Must you wait for me to tell you?’

While they were quarrelling, Grandmother Jia sent for him to eat, and he went to the front rooms. He hastily swallowed half a bowlful, then returned to his own room. Aroma was lying on the outer kang, with Musk beside her playing dominoes. Knowing how close the two were, he would have nothing to say to Musk either. He lifted the soft curtain and went into the inner room. Musk had to follow him. He pushed her out.

‘I wouldn’t dream of troubling any of you.’

She could only come out smiling and send in two little maids. Bao-yu took a book and lay reading for some time. Wanting tea, he looked up to find the two young maids standing on the floor before him.

The older of the two had a particularly fresh, pretty face.

‘What’s your name?’ Bao-yu asked her.

‘Hui-xiang.’

‘Who gave you that name?’

‘I was called Yun-xiang before. Sister Aroma changed it to Hui-xiang.’

‘Hui-qi, Bad Luck, is what you ought to be called. Hui-xiang indeed!’

Then he asked, ‘How many sisters are there in your family?’1

‘Four,’ Hui-xiang said.

‘Which are you?’

‘The fourth.’

‘Then from tomorrow you’re Si-er, Number Four. None of this Hui-xiang and orchid fragrance. Which of you deserves to be compared with such flowers? You only disgrace good names.’

As he spoke he told her to pour him some tea. Aroma and Musk heard him from the outer room and pressed their lips together to hide their smiles.

That day Bao-yu scarcely left his room. He neither romped with his cousins nor teased the maids, but sat gloomily by himself, turning to a book for distraction or playing with brush and ink. He gave the others no orders, calling only on Si-er. She proved a remarkably quick-witted, obliging girl and, finding herself in favour, tried every means of winning him over.

After supper, two cups of wine had left his eyes drowsy and his ears warm. On any other evening he would have had Aroma and the rest about him, laughing and enjoying themselves. Now he sat alone before the lamp in dismal silence, with no pleasure in anything. He wanted to go after them, but feared that this would only gratify them and encourage still more admonitions. To assert his authority as master and frighten them into obedience seemed altogether too unfeeling.

There was nothing for it but to harden his heart and suppose them dead. He would have to go on living even then. So he imagined for the moment that they were dead, and found that, with nothing to tie him to them, he could actually be quite content. He told Si-er to trim the lamp and brew tea, and read for a while in The Classic of Nanhua. He came to this passage in ‘Breaking Open Chests’, one of the Outer Chapters:

‘Therefore, cut off sagehood and cast out knowledge, and great thieves will cease. Cast away jade and destroy pearls, and petty thieves will not arise. Burn tallies and break seals, and the people will be simple and unsophisticated. Smash measures and snap scales, and the people will not contend. Destroy utterly the sages’ laws throughout the world, and only then can one begin to reason with the people. Disrupt the six pitch-pipes, melt down and destroy the reed-organs and zithers, stop the ears of Kuang the blind musician, and only then will everyone in the world retain their own powers of hearing. Blot out ornament, disperse the five colours, glue shut the eyes of Li Zhu, and only then will everyone in the world retain their own powers of sight. Destroy hooks and marking-lines, throw away compasses and squares, break the fingers of the craftsman Chui, and only then will everyone in the world possess skill of their own.’

Delighted by what he read, and carried along by the wine, he could not help taking up his brush to add a continuation:

‘Burn the flowers and scatter the musk, and only then will everyone in the women’s chambers keep their counsel to themselves. Mutilate Bao-chai’s immortal beauty, reduce Dai-yu’s subtle understanding to ashes, destroy and diminish love and affection, and only then will fair and foul in the women’s chambers be alike.2

‘If they keep their counsel to themselves, there will be no fear of estrangement, like the stars Shen and Shang that never meet. Mutilate their immortal beauty, and there will be no heart for love. Reduce their subtle understanding to ashes, and there will be no feeling for wit and invention. For hairpin and jade, flowers and musk, all spread their nets and dig their pitfalls, to bewilder, entangle and ensnare the world.’

His continuation finished, Bao-yu threw down the brush and went to bed. His head had scarcely touched the pillow before he was fast asleep. All night he knew nothing of where he had gone, and did not wake until daybreak. Turning over, he saw Aroma lying fully dressed on top of the quilt. Yesterday’s quarrel had already gone out of his mind. He nudged her.

‘Get up and go to bed properly. You’ll catch cold.’

Now Aroma, seeing him romp with his cousins at all hours of the day and night, had judged that plain remonstrance would never change him. She therefore used his affection to bring him to his senses, supposing that in half a day, or even a little while, they would be on good terms again. Instead, Bao-yu had held out all day and all night. It was she who no longer knew what to do, and she had scarcely slept. Now that he behaved like this, she judged that his feelings had softened, and ignored him all the more.

When she did not answer, Bao-yu reached out to undo her clothes. He had just unfastened the buttons when she pushed his hand away and did them up again. Helpless, he could only take her hand and ask with a smile, ‘What is the matter with you?’

After he had asked several times, Aroma opened her eyes.

‘Nothing is the matter with me. You’re awake now. Go over to the other room to wash and have your hair done. If you wait much longer, you’ll be too late.’

‘Where am I to go?’

Aroma gave a cold laugh. ‘You ask me! How should I know? Go wherever you please. From now on, let’s have nothing to do with each other. It will save all this cock-and-goose squabbling for other people to laugh at.

‘When you’ve had your fill over there, you can come back here and have some Number Four or Number Five wait on you. Creatures like us do nothing but “disgrace good names”.’

‘You still remember that today!’ Bao-yu said with a smile.

‘I’ll remember it in a hundred years! I’m not like you, who let my words blow past your ears. What I say at night you’ve forgotten by morning.’

The sight of her face, so full of tender displeasure, stirred him beyond restraint. He picked up a jade hairpin beside the pillow and dashed it down, breaking it in two.

‘If I ever refuse to listen to you again, may I end up like this!’

Aroma hurriedly gathered up the pieces.

‘Why do a thing like that first thing in the morning? What does it matter whether you listen? Is it worth such a fuss?’

‘You don’t know how desperate I feel!’

‘So you know what it is to feel desperate?’ she said with a smile. ‘Then you can imagine how I feel. Come along, get up and wash your face.’

Only then did they both get up and attend to their washing and grooming.

After Bao-yu had gone to the main rooms, Dai-yu happened to call. Finding him out, she turned over the books on his desk and chanced upon the volume of Zhuang-zi he had been reading the day before. When she reached his continuation, she was both angry and amused. She too could not resist taking up a brush and adding a quatrain:

Who idly takes his brush in hand To mock Nanhua’s Zhuangzi yin?

He feels no shame for his own lack of sense, But heaps foul words on others, laying blame.

When she had finished, she too went to the main rooms to see Grandmother Jia, and afterwards to Lady Wang’s. As it happened, Xi-feng’s daughter Da-jie had fallen ill, and there was a flurry of people fetching a doctor to feel her pulse.

‘My congratulations to Her Ladyship and the ladies,’ the doctor said. ‘The little girl’s fever is the auspicious eruption of smallpox, not some other illness.’

Lady Wang and Xi-feng hurriedly sent someone to ask whether she would be all right.

‘The illness is dangerous, but it is taking a favourable course,’ he replied. ‘There should be no great difficulty. The important thing is to have mulberry worms and pig tails ready.’

Xi-feng sprang into action. Rooms were cleaned for worship of the Smallpox Goddess, and orders went round the household that nothing was to be fried or stir-fried. She told Patience to put together bedding and clothes so that Jia Lian could sleep in a separate room, and brought out lengths of scarlet cloth to make clothes for the wet-nurse, the maids and all who attended the child closely. A clean room was also prepared in the outer quarters, where the two doctors were hospitably lodged. They took turns considering the case, feeling the pulse and prescribing medicines, and were not allowed home for twelve days. Jia Lian had to move into the outer study and observe fasting and abstinence. Every day Xi-feng and Patience joined Lady Wang in worshipping the goddess.

As for Jia Lian, he had only to be away from Xi-feng to start looking for mischief. After two nights alone he found it unbearable, and for the time being picked out the better-looking male page-servants to relieve his desire.

Now in the Rong mansion there was a thoroughly worthless, disreputable drunkard of a cook called Duo-guan. He was so weak and ineffectual that everyone called him Duo the Dullard. While he was still young, his parents had found him a wife from outside the household. She was now in her early twenties, with looks that won the admiration of everyone who saw her. Flighty by nature, she delighted in sexual adventures. Duo the Dullard took no notice: so long as he had wine, meat and money, he let everything else go. Thus the men of both the Rong and Ning mansions had access to her. For her remarkable beauty and boundless wantonness, everyone knew her as ‘Mrs Duo’.

Jia Lian, now chafing in the outer quarters, had seen her before and lost his head over her. But fear of his lovely wife at home and his male favourites outside had kept him from making an approach. Mrs Duo had designs on him too; she had only lacked the opportunity.

When she heard that Jia Lian had moved into the outer study, she began finding excuses to go that way and provoke him. He was soon like a starving rat. He had to consult his trusted pages, promising them liberal rewards of money and silk if they would arrange matters and cover for him. Why would the pages refuse? Besides, they were all on intimate terms with the woman themselves. One word settled it.

That night, at the second watch, when everyone had gone to rest and Duo the Dullard lay dead drunk on the kang, Jia Lian slipped over to meet her. One glimpse of her as he came in sent his senses flying. There was no need for affectionate talk or tender preliminaries; he undressed and set to work. The woman had a peculiar gift by nature: at a man’s touch her whole body became so soft and yielding that he seemed to be lying on cotton. Her lewd manner and licentious talk outdid a prostitute’s. What man at such a moment would spare his very life? Jia Lian wished he could melt his whole body into hers.

From beneath him, deliberately teasing him with wanton talk, she said, ‘Your daughter has the pox, and you’re worshipping the goddess at home. You ought to abstain for a couple of days, yet here you are, defiling yourself for my sake.

‘Go on, get away from me.’

Moving vigorously, Jia Lian answered between gasps, ‘You’re my goddess! What do I care about any other goddess?’

The more wanton the woman grew, the more completely Jia Lian abandoned all restraint. Afterwards they pledged their love by sea and mountain, unable to bear parting, and from that time became lovers.

In due course the poison left Da-jie’s body and the spots subsided. At the end of twelve days the goddess was ceremonially sent away. The whole family made offerings to Heaven and the ancestors, fulfilled their vows, burned incense, celebrated and distributed rewards. Jia Lian moved back into the bedroom. At his reunion with Xi-feng, it was just as the saying has it: ‘Newlyweds cannot match a couple long parted.’ Their tenderness was boundless, and need not detain us.

Early next morning, after Xi-feng had gone to the main rooms, Patience was putting away the clothes and bedding Jia Lian had used outside when a lock of black hair unexpectedly fell from his pillowcase. She understood at once and hastily tucked it into her sleeve. Then she went into the other room, took it out and held it up to Jia Lian with a smile.

‘What’s this?’

He was alarmed and rushed to snatch it. Patience ran, but he caught her, pinned her to the kang and tried to prise her hand open.

‘You little baggage,’ he said, laughing, ‘give it here at once, or I’ll snap your arm!’

‘You heartless thing!’ she laughed. ‘I was kind enough to hide it from her and ask you privately, and now you threaten me!

‘Go on threatening. When she comes back I’ll tell her, and then we’ll see what you do.’

Jia Lian promptly put on a coaxing smile. ‘Be a darling and let me have it. I won’t threaten you again.’

Before he had finished, Xi-feng’s voice was heard approaching. He released his hold. Patience had only just got up when Xi-feng came in and told her to open the box at once and find a pattern for Lady Wang. Patience quickly assented and began to look. The sight of Jia Lian suddenly reminded Xi-feng of something.

‘Have you brought back everything that was taken out?’ she asked Patience.

‘Yes.’

‘Nothing missing?’

‘I was afraid a thing or two might have been left behind, so I checked very carefully. Nothing’s missing.’

‘That’s good. So long as nothing extra has turned up.’

‘It’s lucky enough not to have lost anything,’ Patience laughed. ‘Who would be adding to it?’

Xi-feng gave a cold laugh. ‘There’s no guarantee he’s kept himself clean this fortnight. Some intimate friend might have left something behind. A ring, a kerchief, a scent-bag, even hair or fingernails. They all count.’

Her words turned Jia Lian’s face yellow. Behind her back he stared at Patience, grimacing and jerking his head in frantic signals. Patience pretended not to see.

‘Why, ma’am, you and I think exactly alike!’ she said with a smile. ‘I was afraid of just such things. I searched very carefully, but there wasn’t the least sign of anything. If you don’t believe me, I haven’t put the things away yet. You can go through them yourself.’

‘Silly girl!’ Xi-feng laughed. ‘If he had anything like that, would he leave it where we could find it?’

With that, she found the pattern and went back to the main rooms.

Pointing at Jia Lian’s nose, Patience shook her head and laughed.

‘How are you going to thank me for this?’

Jia Lian was beside himself with delight. He rushed over and embraced her, pouring out thanks and calling her his heart, his darling, his own flesh. Still holding the hair, Patience smiled.

‘I’ve got something to hold over you for the rest of my life.

‘Treat me well, and all’s well. If you don’t, I’ll let this out.’

‘Just keep it safe,’ Jia Lian said with a smile. ‘Whatever you do, don’t let her know.’

As he spoke, he watched for an unguarded moment and snatched it from her.

‘It will always be a danger in your hands,’ he said, laughing. ‘Better burn it and have done with it.’

But as he said this, he tucked it inside the top of his boot.

Patience gritted her teeth. ‘You heartless creature! Pull down the bridge as soon as you’re across! And you expect me to lie for you again?’

Her pretty, spirited manner stirred Jia Lian. He clasped her and asked her to make love, but she wrenched herself free and ran. Bent double in his frustration, he cursed.

‘You damned teasing little slut! You have to set a man on fire, and then you run away.’

From outside the window Patience laughed. ‘I can tease if I please. Who told you to catch fire? Am I to give you one moment’s pleasure and have her turn against me again when she finds out?’

‘You needn’t be afraid of her,’ Jia Lian said. ‘One of these days, when my temper’s up, I’ll smash that vinegar-jar to pieces. Then she’ll know who I am! She watches me as if I were a thief. She may talk to men, but I mustn’t talk to women. Let me go anywhere near a woman and she’s suspicious. Yet she laughs and jokes with younger brothers-in-law and nephews, old or young, without worrying that I might be jealous.

‘From now on I won’t let her see anyone either!’

‘She has a right to be jealous of you,’ Patience said. ‘You have no right to be jealous of her. Everything she does is above board. You can’t move without some wicked idea in your head. Even I don’t trust you, never mind her.’

‘You’re a pair of rogues, singing the same tune! Everything you do is right, and every move I make has some wicked idea behind it. One of these days I’ll kill the pair of you!’

He had not finished speaking when Xi-feng came into the courtyard. Seeing Patience outside the window, she asked, ‘If you two want to talk, why don’t you talk indoors? What does it mean, one of you running outside and talking through the window?’

From inside, Jia Lian answered, ‘Ask her. You’d think there was a tiger in here waiting to eat her.’

‘There’s no one else in the room,’ Patience said. ‘What should I stay beside him for?’

‘No one else there? All the better,’ Xi-feng said, smiling.

‘Is that meant for me?’

‘Who else would I mean?’

‘Don’t make me say something nice in return.’

Without holding up the door-curtain for Xi-feng, Patience went in first, let it fall sharply behind her and went off into the other room. Xi-feng lifted it herself and came in.

‘Patience has gone mad! The little baggage really means to get the upper hand of me. You’d better look out for your skin!’

Jia Lian had collapsed on the kang, clapping his hands and laughing.

‘I never knew Patience had such a temper! From now on I bow to her.’

‘You’ve spoiled her,’ Xi-feng said. ‘I shall have it out with you!’

‘You two fall out, and you make me the scapegoat again!’ Jia Lian said hastily. ‘I’m keeping out of your way.’

‘I’d like to see where you can hide.’

‘I’ll be right back.’

‘I have something to discuss with you,’ said Xi-feng.

What she wished to discuss, you must hear in the next chapter. Truly:

Virtuous ladies have always had complaints in store;

And lovely wives have tasted jealousy since days of yore.

Translator’s notes

  1. Hui-xiang means ‘orchid fragrance’; Bao-yu plays on its first syllable with huiqi, ‘bad luck’. ↩
  2. The four objects also allude to names: flower (hua) is Aroma’s surname; musk points to Musk; hairpin (chai) and jade (yu) point to Bao-chai and Dai-yu. ↩
