## Finding

Precision findings reported to the coordinator:

- **06, paragraph 6:** Chinese “而且会比她更得体” is rendered as “y que le sentará mejor que a ella”. The Spanish idiom tends toward clothing fitting or looking better, while the Chinese stresses more appropriate/proper dress. Consider “y que irá vestida con más propiedad que ella”. The following sentence already preserves the contrast between lived circumstances and competent stage costume, so this is a local nuance, not a blocking argument error. This report does not claim that the suggested change has been applied.

- **08, paragraph 9, pronoun clarification recommended:** “Ella retrocede hacia su marido. Él le permite responder...” can make “Él” refer to the husband just mentioned. The Chinese continues to describe the visitor allowing an answer while assuming its content. Recommend “El visitante le permite responder...”. This parallel issue was noticed on a focused recheck prompted by the German reviewer’s same finding in the French version, and reported immediately. No Spanish edit was made by this reviewer.

No missing paragraph, summary substitution, material reversal, unsupported resolution or other actionable semantic omission was found in the five full readings. The pronoun should be clarified as above.

## Complete-reading conclusions

### 06 — Seis personajes en busca de autor

The Spanish preserves the opening interrupted greeting and the character’s claim that a different delivery would have elicited a different answer. The need for theatre, incomplete authored existence, the Father’s actual family relation to the girl, her biological father’s death and forced reception of clients, and the Mother’s intervention are complete. Hat actions, black mourning clothes, the omitted sentence, non-parodic actor skill, hopeful-versus-confirmatory delivery, laughter through the fingers and actors’ refusal to continue remain.

The Father’s objection does not become a workable demand for perfect copying. The director’s practical need to select and give room to other characters remains valid but does not justify every deletion. The proposal to replace undressing with sympathetic inquiry is preserved as a change in what the man does to the girl. The Father’s suffering does not erase hers. The Mother and Hijastra have different wishes about replaying the encounter; recurrent suffering is not treated as merely a rehearsal inconvenience. All final actions, including the Mother separating them, the director withdrawing to the stage opening, the actual mistaken curtain fall, lifting its corner and going back inside with the Father, remain. The source note explicitly preserves the 1921 Florence Bemporad text, facsimile index, exclusion of the 1925 revision, and stopping at the rehearsal interruption rather than the whole play’s ending. Only the costume nuance above merits an optional adjustment.

### 07 — El pabellón de las peonías

The Spanish preserves both the father’s understandable limits of knowledge and the danger of his proposed killing as a test. Three years, actual death and resurrection, Chen Zuiliang’s earlier report and revised testimony, mirror, traces, flower shade and bodily verification remain. The age-sixteen portrait, dream, love, illness, burial, portrait discovery and encounter across life and death are all included.

The marriage discussion remains separate from bodily verification. Gratitude does not replace Liniang’s love predating resurrection. The medicinal-name and areca wordplay is described without invented Spanish jokes. The mother’s separate account of wartime survival and meeting in Lin’an is preserved. The first decree gives meaningful public protection but does not finish the play or resolve the father’s rejection. His divorce condition, the impossibility of discarding Liniang’s intervening life, Mengmei’s own hurt, the use of his examination rank, the father’s cry at her collapse, Chunxiang and Shi, hand-taking, shoulder pressure and urging the bow all remain. The second decree and actual family return are explicit. Affection does not make the divorce condition harmless, and no comparison with Nora becomes a ranking of freedom. Waseda shelfmark, two volumes, reprint history, unknown date, p0100–p0111 and the post-first-decree continuation are all retained in the source note.

### 08 — La dama del mar

The Spanish preserves the half-hour deadline and why postponement until tomorrow removes one real choice. Ellida’s lighthouse background, second marriage, earlier vow and cancellation by letter, the sailor’s refusal to accept termination, attraction and fear are present. The husband’s concern about a violent death and her distress is acknowledged, while his protective plan also predetermines the outcome. The purchase metaphor remains Ellida’s account beside his sincere invitation and her acknowledgement of good care.

Release from mutual obligations is not a completed legal divorce, explicitly in both the conversation and source note. The sailor is not idealized: crossing the fence, predicting her decision and her retreat toward Wangel remain. The threatening escalation, body/thought distinction, repeated verification of sincere freedom, sadness and shared years, and the available cabin and still-departable ship remain. Responsibility is not erased by autonomy. Ellida first refuses the visitor and then affirms staying; neither forced departure as proof of freedom nor calculated permission as a retention technique is endorsed. She retains uncertainty about Wangel’s diagnosis while appreciating his action. The daughters remain children of his earlier marriage, and their acceptance is still needed. Hilde’s response, the hand extended, final ship movement and the fact that the ship no longer decides for them are complete. All act and translator details remain.

### 09 — El misántropo

The Spanish preserves the blocked initial “señor”, friendship offered before acquaintance, court access offered without an explicit threat, and the difficulty of giving an unpredetermined answer. The sonnet’s theme, claimed quarter-hour composition, successive praise and Alceste’s unheard-or-possibly-unheard apartes remain. The anonymous-poet detour is neither omitted nor misrepresented as immediate perfect frankness. It keeps the widening threat to the writer’s reputation, concrete criticisms, the old Paris/love song and Alceste’s reaction to laughter at something he values.

His legitimate wish not to be ruled by majority praise does not justify declaring every admirer false. His aesthetic reasons and hurtful manner remain separate. Philinte’s intervening action has value without vindicating all his praise; Alceste’s refusal to hear him remains. The summons does not authorize imposed aesthetic agreement. The fourth-act mediation is explicitly Philinte’s report to Éliante, not a fully staged scene. The catalogue of Oronte’s other merits, the still-barbed concession, sincere but potentially wounding apology and arranged embrace preserve the distinction between stopping offenses and agreeing about the poem. The end gives neither true friendship nor complete reconciliation. Louandre, Charpentier 1910 and all act/scene references are correct against the Chinese metadata.

### 10 — Filoctetes

The Spanish explicitly says that taking Troy requires “ese arquero”, not merely possession of the bow. True lineage, invented shared grievance, the youth’s initial preference for force, ten years of abandonment, hunting for food, trust-based entrusting during pain and the warning against handing the bow to captors are all complete. Restitution occurs after refusal, without requiring gratitude or assent, and restores food security independently of cooperation.

Odysseus remains an active threat, while restraining the proposed shot remains distinct from confiscating the bow. Renewed trust in the youth is not renewed trust in the whole army. The real weight of the oracle, promise of cure, continuing reproach, future coexistence with wrongdoers and the injured man’s concern about his own peaceable conduct remain. The earlier promise to take him home becomes a genuine alternative to the false two-option framing. The youth accepts that route before any divine guarantee. Protection of his homeland is proposed after the bow has been returned, not as a prior condition.

Heracles’s appearance, Zeus’s order, mutual indispensability, paired lions and post-conquest respect for the gods are preserved. The final change of route is explicitly due to the divine command, not successful human persuasion. The same final destination does not erase differences of method. Farewell to the cave, springs and sounds does not negate the injustice or the island’s ambivalent refuge. The final argument preserves the value of restitution even if the harmed person remains unwilling to cooperate. Johnston’s revised 2012 translation, Jebb comparison, approximate Greek lines 1220–1471 and the bracketed/unbracketed numbering distinction remain.
