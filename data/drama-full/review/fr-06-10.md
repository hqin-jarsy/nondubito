### 06 — Six personnages en quête d’auteur

All 25 paragraphs retain the repeated greeting, interrupted rehearsal, characters’ need for theatre, and the difference between actor skill and fidelity to the lived event. Preserved the Father’s actual relation to the young woman (his wife’s daughter by another man), her biological father’s death, forced reception of clients, the Mother’s intervention, the hat actions and mourning clothes, the omitted final line, and the actors’ non-parodic beautification. The laughter’s different meanings for the girl, actors and director remain distinct. The actors’ need to interpret is acknowledged alongside the characters’ right to object; neither perfect reproduction nor arbitrary substitution is endorsed.

Particular drift risks checked: the demand to remove mourning clothes is not softened into an attentive question about bereavement; the director’s valid need to select does not automatically justify this selection; the Father’s fuller biography does not subtract the daughter’s harm; the Mother and Belle-Fille have incompatible wishes. Preserved the Mother’s experience of recurrent pain, the physical separation, the director’s move toward the footlights, the accidental actual curtain fall, and his return behind the curtain with the Father. This interruption is explicitly not the play’s ending. The source note retains Florence, R. Bemporad & figlio, 1921, the linked facsimile index, and exclusion of the 1925 revision.

Self-review edit: clarified an ambiguous “ils ne sont pas eux” as “ils ne sont pas les personnages qu’ils jouent”, keeping the actors/characters distinction explicit rather than creating an unclear reflexive reference.

Liniang’s portrait at sixteen, dream, illness, burial, Mengmei’s discovery of the portrait and their meeting across the living/dead divide remain explicit.

Particular drift risks checked: grateful obligation does not replace prior love and her own choice; the mother’s survival brings a separate narrated history rather than mere maternal authority; the initial imperial order ends neither the scene nor the father’s objections. The demand for marital separation, husband’s anger at mistreatment, Liniang’s use of the top examination rank, her love predating that rank, her collapse and her father’s cry, Chunxiang and the Taoist nun Shi, hands/shoulder/exhortations to bow, and the second final edict all remain. Chinese edition and scene names are romanized where needed, not replaced with an unverified French edition title. Source note retains Waseda, shelfmark bunko 06 01071, Yumingtang huanhun ji, the Nuanhongshi/Mengfenglou reprint, unknown publication year, Bingsiguan original edition, lower-volume p0100–p0111, the public transcription, and the full post-first-edict continuation.

### 10 — Philoctète

All 24 paragraphs preserve ten years on Lemnos, painful abandonment, hunting for food, the army’s renewed need, true parentage versus fabricated shared grievance, Neoptolemus’s original preference for force, and the arc of his own change. The entrusting of the bow during a crisis and express warning not to hand it to captors remain. Restoration takes place after a refusal and restores subsistence independently of assent.

Particular drift risks checked: Odysseus is still actively threatening when the shot is prevented; restraining retaliation does not justify confiscation. Renewed trust in the youth is not trust in the army. The oracle has real weight in the play, while cure and glory do not answer how to live with former perpetrators. Kept the still-reproachful tone, Philoctetes’s fear of his own difficulty remaining peaceful, the false two-option framing, and the earlier promise to take him home. The youth accepts the homeward journey before divine intervention or any guarantee of success; Philoctetes’s protection of his helper’s homeland is volunteered after restitution, not a prior price for it.

Heracles’s appearance, Zeus’s direction, cure and glory, mutual indispensability, paired lions, and reverence for the gods after conquest remain. The final redirection comes from divine command, not a claim that the youth finally persuaded Philoctetes. The farewell’s caves, springs and sounds retain the island’s ambivalent value. Identical destination does not erase different methods or prior wrong. Source note retains Johnston’s 2012 revision, comparison with Jebb, Greek lines approximately 1220–1471, and the distinction between bracketed Greek and unbracketed English line numbers.
