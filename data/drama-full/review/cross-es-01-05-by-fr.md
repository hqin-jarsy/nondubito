# Independent Chinese–Spanish cross-review, essays 01–05

Reviewer: French translation worker. Scope: **all 105 body paragraphs**, in their entirety, compared against canonical Chinese `body_zh`; not a sample. Also read all Spanish titles, decks, source notes and source labels against the Chinese material. An initially truncated combined display was followed by a targeted reread of the omitted portion (01 paragraphs 17–19 and 02 paragraphs 1–2), so the scope statement does not count truncated text as reviewed. This is a semantic review, not a conclusion inferred from word counts.

No Spanish file was edited. Findings were sent immediately to the coordinator. The French essay 01 paragraph 13 precision correction previously requested by the coordinator has already been applied and recorded in `fr-01-05.md`.

## Result

The batch is substantively faithful: no missing paragraph, omitted argument, inverted conclusion, substantial speaker misattribution, or removed limiting qualification found. Recommended one semantic precision correction and three localized language/detail corrections before final acceptance. These are not reasons to retranslate the batch.

### Corrections recommended

1. **01, paragraph 6: standing to judge versus competence.** Chinese: “能够承认她也有资格判断这段婚姻”. Spanish: “reconocer que ella también está capacitada para juzgar ese matrimonio”. `Está capacitada` emphasizes ability or competence, whereas the contrast here concerns recognizing her standing/right to judge the relationship. Suggested: **“reconocer que ella también tiene derecho a juzgar ese matrimonio”**. This is the most important precision issue, given the essay’s argument about who is permitted to judge.
2. **01, paragraph 18: pronoun construction.** “ella responde que recordará a él, a los niños y a esta casa” is awkward Spanish, especially the stressed personal pronoun without its object clitic. Suggested: **“ella responde que se acordará de él, de los niños y de esta casa”**. No plot change intended.
3. **03, paragraph 17: what earns laughter.** “el bufón sin talento, al que solo se reía por condescendencia de su señora” has an awkward relative construction and may obscure that people laugh at his performance out of deference to the mistress. Suggested: **“el bufón sin talento, cuyas gracias solo provocaban risa por deferencia hacia su señora”**. Preserve that this is Malvolio’s past disparagement being recalled by Feste, not the essay’s own assessment of his talent.
4. **04, paragraph 12: concrete meal detail.** The Chinese ends with tired people once again having something to sit down and eat at the proper time. Spanish instead says “vuelvan a tener una mesa a la que sentarse puntualmente”. This retains the schedule but substitutes a table for the meal. Suggested ending: **“y que las personas cansadas vuelvan a tener una comida que puedan sentarse a tomar a su hora”**. A small detail, but worth retaining because the paragraph explicitly gives practical domestic care its weight.

## Limits
