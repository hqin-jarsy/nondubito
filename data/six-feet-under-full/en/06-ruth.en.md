# Ruth

*English edition, expanded from the Chinese essay and revised alongside it. Full-series spoilers.*

## 6.1 The Phone Call

In the first episode Ruth Fisher is in the kitchen, cooking and talking to her husband on the phone. It's Christmas Eve. She's making food for the holiday and they're talking about the sort of things you talk about at home.

On the road, a bus hits the new hearse he's driving.

The news reaches the kitchen. The meal Ruth meant to make can't go on. Over the next few episodes she arranges a funeral, sees to visitors, cooks and tidies up.

## 6.2 Thirty Years

Ruth got pregnant in her teens and married before she was twenty. After the wedding she spent years making a home. In that house she ran the household and kept the accounts, brought up three children and took care of a husband who spent every day downstairs handling bodies.

For thirty years she was Nathaniel's wife. After his death she admits that her involvement with Hiram had already started while Nathaniel was alive.

You can make a list of an ordinary day: shop, cook, wash clothes, clean, keep track of what the house spends, see to mourners, do a little work for the church. Again and again for thirty years.

It would be easy to say she was trapped. That's not how she understood it. The life she was living was what a woman in her world was expected to do, and she did it very well.

Nobody ever asked her what she might do instead.

## 6.3 The Question

In the first season she comes up against a question she hasn't had much reason to ask. It might sound like this:

I've been a wife and a mother all my life.

What now?

This is the first essay's question, asked inside the family and a little ahead of time. She's in her fifties. Her husband has died, her children have grown up, and the thing that filled her days is suddenly gone.

She doesn't know the answer.

## 6.4 She Begins Trying Things

Over the next few seasons she tries things. The show treats these attempts almost as comedy. Most of them aren't pretty.

She tries again with Hiram, the hairdresser. It began while Nathaniel was alive. She's had pleasure from it and she's felt guilty about it.

She takes up with Nikolai, a Russian florist. He's brusque and direct and doesn't understand why she cares about the things she cares about. She does paid work at his flower shop. When he gets hurt, she takes care of him. Then she realizes that she's back to being the person who takes care of someone.

She goes to a personal-growth workshop. It's filmed with a good deal of acid: a room full of people, a leader, a set of phrases about setting yourself free, a few days enclosed with it all. Still, she comes out a little different. She starts taking classes. Flower arranging, gardening, all sorts of things.

She meets George Sibley, a geology professor, and marries him.

None of these attempts turns out as she hoped. Neither Hiram nor Nikolai becomes her lasting partner. The workshop doesn't give her a life she can simply follow. Not long after the wedding George has a breakdown. There's a serious history of illness he hasn't told her about. She's taking care of a husband who's undergoing electroconvulsive therapy.

## 6.5 Her Answer

If you look only at those five seasons, Ruth Fisher seems to be failing. She's tried a lot of things and keeps running into walls.

The finale shows when she dies. The fictional obituary HBO published alongside it adds something. Ruth O'Connor Fisher, born in Pasadena in 1946, dies in 2025. She raised three children after high school. Twenty years before she died, she opened a pet boarding business.

Twenty years. Count back from 2025 and you get 2005, the year the show ends.

So after all those attempts that seemed to go nowhere, Ruth goes into the business of looking after animals. The finale already lets us see her beginning it. By the time of the obituary, she has a full twenty years on the other side of that beginning.

## 6.6 What Those Failed Attempts Were

Those attempts look different when you can see what they went toward. They were what those twenty years cost.

Consider what Ruth got out of them. With Hiram she found out she could want something for herself. In the florist's she earned money and saw that she could do a job outside the house well. The workshop was dubious in its methods, yet in a room full of strangers it made room for her to say something about herself.

Taking care of George is the hardest part. She finds herself back in the old role, and this time she recognizes it. In the fifth season she's able, in effect, to say that she can't keep doing this.

None of these things is an achievement she can hold up on its own. They're pieces. You can almost see the boarding business put together from them. She knows something about what she wants. She knows she can do a job. She can decide what she's willing to take care of.

In her fifties she was asked what now and couldn't answer. In the five years that followed she put an answer together piece by piece. At the time it looked like a mess.

## 6.7 She Had Time

The question comes to Ruth Fisher in 2025. She's seventy-nine. What does she have? A business she started twenty years earlier. George, still her companion. A sister. Two children still living, David in Los Angeles and Claire in New York. Four grandchildren.

And those twenty years. That's what matters most.

In her fifties, from where she stood, she had a house that didn't feel hers, a business that didn't feel hers and three children who didn't seem to need her anymore. At seventy-nine she has something she made.

She had time.

The answer isn't settled at twenty. When Ruth was in her fifties, it was “I'm a wife and a mother. What now?” When she died, it wasn't that answer anymore.

The relationships, the jobs and the bad choices had looked like nothing but disorder. Years later you can see what each taught her. Starting late takes more than being told you still can. It takes years of making pieces you may not know what to do with, until one day what you have in front of you is twenty years that were your own.

Six Feet Under was created by Alan Ball and aired from 2001 to 2005, for five seasons and sixty-three episodes.
