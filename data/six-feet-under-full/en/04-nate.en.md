# Nate

*English edition, expanded from the Chinese essay and revised alongside it. Full-series spoilers.*

## 4.1 He Left

Nate Fisher left Los Angeles young. He went to Seattle and worked at an organic grocery store. He wasn't building a career. He didn't move up, save much or make plans. He stayed there selling organic food for years.

It comes up again and again in the show and sounds different depending on who's saying it. Ruth hears that he abandoned the family. David hears that Nate ran and he stayed. Nate just says he got out.

In the first episode he flies home for the holidays. At the airport he meets a woman he doesn't know and they have sex in a storage room. That's Brenda, whom we'll come back to in the seventh essay. He learns of his father's death afterward.

This is worth noticing. He isn't escaping the news about his father. He hasn't heard it yet. But when things come along that he doesn't know how to handle, the place where he is never seems to be where he wants to stay.

## 4.2 A Knot in a Blood Vessel

Early in the series Nate finds out he has an arteriovenous malformation. It's a malformation of blood vessels in the brain, and his doctor tells him it might bleed. The difficulty is that nobody can tell him when something might happen. Surgery has risks. So does leaving it alone. Nate has to decide.

Most episodes start with a person dying who didn't know it was about to happen. Nate gets something the show usually withholds until it's too late: advance notice.

## 4.3 What He Does

What might someone do with a warning like that? They might start getting things in order. They might think through what matters, do the things they've always said they'd get to later, and say what needs saying. We call that getting ready.

Nate does something else.

Lisa gets pregnant with his child. When we see them married in the third season, Maya has already been born. What happened seems to set the terms of a marriage he didn't particularly want. He's unhappy in it. He sits in the house staring at nothing; the space between them grows. Lisa disappears and is found dead.

He and Brenda come together and split apart, then come together again. They're together in the first season; in the second, he learns some things about her and they break up. In the third he's married to Lisa. In the fourth, after Lisa's death, he comes back to Brenda. In the fifth they marry. He sleeps with other people.

He has an operation and recovers well. His first move is to resume the life he was living before.

It's hard to find something he begins in those years because he thinks he might not have much time.

## 4.4 He Keeps Running

The pattern repeats. He runs from Los Angeles to Seattle, then back again, where he gets stuck. He runs inside the marriage, keeping in touch with Brenda while Lisa's alive. He runs after Lisa's death, to her parents and back. In the fifth season he sleeps with Maggie, George's daughter and Ruth's stepdaughter. By then he's married to Brenda, who's pregnant.

He always has a reason. He needs something real. He doesn't want to live a false life. He's tired of pretending.

These are good things to want. They even sound like what these nine essays have been asking about: how do you stop living by somebody else's plans and become yourself?

But listen to the uses Nate finds for the words. “I want to be myself” can give someone a direction, or it can show them to the exit. For a direction, you need some idea who that self is. For an exit, all you have to know is that this isn't it.

Nate keeps finding the exit. He can tell you exactly why it isn't here. He never says what it would look like there.

## 4.5 That Last Night

In the fifth season Nate collapses after sleeping with Maggie. There's been bleeding in his brain. He has surgery and, in the hospital afterward, seems to be recovering. Brenda comes to see him.

What he tells her is the last thing she'll hear from him. He can't keep going like this. He's going to leave her. She's carrying his child, and he's telling her, from his hospital bed, that he's going to go.

He appears to have made it through. He dies in that bed.

## 4.6 When the Question Comes for Him

When the question arrives for Nate Fisher, what does he have?

A wife he's just told he's leaving, who's pregnant with his child. Maya, his daughter from the marriage to Lisa. A job at a funeral home where he has spent years without feeling he belonged, a job he's found it hard to give his full attention to. Years of talk about living honestly.

The show gives three more episodes to the people still alive. They can love him and be angry with him at the same time. David is angry at being left with the work. Brenda is angry about the last thing he said. Ruth is angry with him too.

They're falling apart because they love him. Both things can be true, and the show leaves them together.

## 4.7 Knowing and Being Ready

Nate Fisher knew early on that he might not have much time. It's hard to think of anyone in the show who's less ready when it runs out.

Knowing the question is coming doesn't in itself prepare you for it. Being ready isn't a feeling, even a strong feeling about how short life is. It takes things you do: ordinary, often boring things, done over many years.

A craft you learn. A relationship you keep up. A child you actually raise. A piece of work you don't leave halfway through.

Nate knows plenty about mortality. He talks about life and death more often than anyone. Of the three siblings, he's the one who treats the business as a philosophical problem. There's little in his hands to show for all that awareness. We need his story here, before the next five essays, to see why awareness alone won't do.

You can take “I don't know how much time I have” as a reason to begin today. You can take it as a reason never to begin. The fact is the same. What changes is where you let it take you.

Six Feet Under was created by Alan Ball and aired from 2001 to 2005, for five seasons and sixty-three episodes.
