The director explicitly identifies the infant as Wander and discusses Mono’s gradually audible restraining voice. Discarded endings and the proposed horse-memory flashback are not treated as released events. The guidebook distinguishes player-controlled resistance from inevitable absorption and supports the infant identity. Its account does not justify asserting either Mono’s total ignorance or omniscience after awakening.

## Resolved findings
- Emon inherits the sealing tradition; he is not identified as the original ancient sealer. Doctrinal continuity and classification are retained as the essay’s reading, not invented institutional history.
- Mono not being consulted remains central; it is no longer confused with never making a sound or certainly knowing nothing afterward.
- Infant Wander remains Wander. The conclusion describes changed bodily form, without adding a purified-remnant or future-identity theory.
- Agro’s fall no longer incorrectly includes the rider already thrown to safety; the thirteenth of sixteen is not called one third. Unverified exact colossus dimensions were removed.
- Optional garden access, spoken character names, and the distinction between non-instrumental actions and an exclusive count of such actions were corrected.
- Final resistance and movement toward the altar are player possibilities, not asserted universal fixed actions.
- The exact eighth-battle face shot was generalized because it was not independently verified here.

## Interpretive limits retained
The account of instrumentalization, the morally troubling exchange, Mono’s care and Agro’s cry remains the author’s analysis. It is not recast as a comprehensive lore explanation. The horn/classification passage and ICO parallel are read within their explicitly interpretive context. No new cinematic details, psychology or abandoned ending material was introduced. No source edits were made by this reviewer; catch22 applied the consolidated changes and synchronized Chinese variants.
