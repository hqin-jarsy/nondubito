# Korean Fables 21–23: translation and final review

## Scope and method

Translated directly from the complete Chinese `texts` arrays in `source/21.json`, `22.json`, and `23.json`, including titles and every source note. Read CLAUDE.md, the writing-style guidance, and opening register samples from Korean essays 01–05. The Korean keeps non-honorific literary narration, natural dialogue, short paragraph beats, repeated questions, and the source's irony. Dialogue politeness follows who is speaking to whom; it does not turn the essay narration into honorific prose.

After all three drafts were written, reread all 420 source/translation pairs in order, with the Chinese and Korean printed side by side. This was a complete semantic and idiomatic self-review, not merely a JSON check or a sample review. No independent second translator or external historical-source verification was used.

## Essay 21: consent, responsibility, and predation

Preserved the contrast between being able to act and acting for the frogs, the log's lack of public function, and the crane's use of ability for predation. Initial consent establishes authority without prospectively justifying every later action. The frogs retain responsibility for their choice, but Jupiter's restricted alternatives and the crane's actual predation also remain separate sources of responsibility.

The counterweight is intact: disliking a decision does not itself establish a violation, and arbitrary withdrawal undermines durable shared rules. This is not rewritten as unconditional exit or as a rejection of all authority. The log/crane punch line and Jupiter's accusatory refrain retain their short beats.

Actual review fixes, with zero-based `texts` indices:

- [2] Restored the frogs' subjective assessment with `느껴졌다` and `부족해 보였다`, rather than presenting their dissatisfaction as an objective description.
- [56] Replaced `내 취향에 맞지` with `내가 원하는 바와 다르다`, avoiding an overly aesthetic sense of preference.
- [91] Replaced the stiff `책임을 지나치게 전부인 것처럼 말하는` with `책임이 전부 있는 것처럼 과장하는`, making the excessive attribution of responsibility clear.
- [102] Made the frogs the grammatical subject of the unknown past, instead of making the pond itself appear to have lived a life.

## Essay 22: procedure is not substantive justification

Kept both judgments distinct: a fair allocation procedure and whether the demand it implements is justified. The lottery is genuine harm reduction under coercive conditions, not proof that a daily death is acceptable. The first leader's refusal is given its actual reason: another nonconsenting deer would have to die early. The pregnancy is not used to erase that competing burden.

The Banyan Deer King's voluntary sacrifice is not converted into a leader's standing duty. His protected position opens a third option, then a progressively wider challenge to the boundary of protection. The people's benefit and the cost transferred to the deer remain distinct, without equating the people's responsibility with the king's. The source's Buddhist ethical direction and distinction between the prose commentary tradition and early canonical verses remain intact.

Actual review fixes:

- [9] Changed `잡힐 차례가 되기도 전에` to `사냥감으로 골라지기도 전에`. The earlier wording risked implying a fixed turn-taking system before the lottery was introduced.
- [39] Replaced the overly jaunty `덤으로 다치는` with `추가로 다치는` for additional injuries.
- [68] Corrected the case construction to `남에게 대신하라고` and clarified the coordination of the powerful and well-connected.
- [69] Expanded the literal name `가지 사슴왕` to `나뭇가지 사슴왕`, avoiding the Korean word's possible eggplant reading. The source's name was retained semantically rather than replaced with an unprovided Pali name.
- [101] Restored the explicit category sense of `这一类生命` with `이런 종류의 생명들`.
- [131] Corrected a mismatched subject in the king's question. It now reads `그리고 내게는 왜 날마다 여기에 누운 사슴 한 마리가 필요한가?`, clearly asking why the king needs the daily victim.

## Essay 23: care without compulsory dependence

Retained the opening value of emergency mutual aid, the salivary-water metaphor, and the dry-land/river-and-lake contrast. `상유이말` is introduced through the story before the four-syllable expression is discussed. `江湖` is rendered as `강과 호수` to keep the literal water conditions visible rather than introducing an unrelated martial-world association.

All limits remain: enduring dependence can be real and valuable; no one is absolutely independent; not every care relationship can end in no longer needing help; some losses are irreversible; better conditions may not be available. Seeking water and continuing present aid are not forced alternatives. The rebuke to abandoning someone on dry land is explicit. Gratitude does not create permanent authority, and freedom from desperate dependence does not erase memory, later meetings, or renewed mutual help.

Actual review fixes:

- [13], [53], [101], and [128] Replaced several bureaucratic or religious-sounding uses of `구제` with natural verbal phrases such as `서로를 구하는` and `과거에 구해 준 일`. The rescue/help meaning remains, while the prose reads less mechanically.
- [80] Changed the ambiguous `자신이 물러날` to `돕는 이가 ... 물러날`. The helper is now explicit, and good long-term care is not accidentally framed as deficient because the recipient cannot become independent.
- [85] Repaired the awkward compliment construction to `얼마나 위대한지 감탄만 하고`.
- [123] Recast the sentence with 『장자』 as the active subject, making the interruption of the dependency ideal idiomatic Korean.
