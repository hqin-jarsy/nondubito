## 阅读方向

每一列从上往下，整件手卷从右往左。苏正文从“自我来黄州”读到“右黄州寒食二首”。其左侧更大的黄庭坚后跋，以及两者之间后来加入的乾隆小字，均不是苏轼正文。红印另属收藏、鉴赏等留下的印记。

## English guide to the seven shared images

All eight essays use the same seven JPEG files. Their Chinese labels have been retained. The six non-Chinese essays provide their own figure captions, character glosses and directions; this guide also explains the two navigation images. Read every calligraphic column from top to bottom and move between columns from right to left.

- [00 · Su Shi text overview](images/00_su_text_overview.jpg): three museum photographs arranged for orientation, with photograph 03 on the right, 04 in the centre and 05 on the left. Their overlapping regions remain visible. This is not a seamless reconstruction, and its width cannot be used to measure the manuscript. The small edge of later Qianlong writing visible at the far left does not belong to Su Shi’s text.
- [Figure 1 · Ending](images/01_ending_detail.jpg): 不, the negative word “not,” is at the bottom of the middle column; 起, “rise” or here “be rekindled,” stands at the top of the column to its left. The crop retains the long blank area beneath 起. The inscription following Su’s poems lies outside this crop.
- [Figure 2 · Opening](images/02_opening_detail.jpg): the word 寒食, “Cold Food,” crosses from 寒 at the bottom of the rightmost column to 食 at the top of the next. The sequence 不／容惜 also crosses a column boundary. Small repetition marks below 年 (“year”) and 春 (“spring”) instruct the reader to repeat those characters; they are not punctuation.
- [Figure 3 · Five adjacent columns](images/03_ink_spacing_detail.jpg): the rightmost column begins 破竈, “broken stove,” and continues through 燒濕葦那, “burn / wet / reeds / how.” The next reads 知是寒食但見烏, “know / is / Cold Food / only / see / crows.” The other three retain the compressed column endings and renewed expansion discussed in the essay. The simplified forms 灶、湿、苇、见、乌 correspond to 竈、濕、葦、見、烏 in the manuscript; typed characters are finding aids rather than facsimile shapes.
- [Figure 4 · Revision](images/04_revision_detail.jpg): a small 病 (“ill”) is inserted beside 少 (“young”) in the right column; 子, an abandoned character, is cancelled below 年 in the left column. The accepted phrase reads 何殊病少年, “how unlike a young person who falls ill?” The next line has 頭 (“head,” here referring to hair), not the reading 鬚 (“beard”) printed on one museum explanatory page. Both readings and the basis for this edition are recorded in the essay.
- [Figure 5 · Huang Tingjian’s colophon](images/05_huang_colophon_detail.jpg): the opening 東坡此詩似李太白 means “These poems by Dongpo resemble Li Taibai [Li Bai]…”. Dongpo is Su Shi’s literary name. These larger characters were written by Huang Tingjian, not by Su Shi. They are a later response to the manuscript.
- [06 · Full-scroll photograph index](images/06_full_scroll_gallery_index.jpg): all 19 photographs from the museum gallery, reduced to thumbnails. Images 01–17 show the opened scroll and are arranged from right to left within each row, with rows proceeding from top to bottom. Images 18–19, shown separately, depict the outer wrapping and ties. The photographs include overlaps and blank paper. This is an index, not a continuous, measurable image of the complete mounted scroll. Photos 01–02 include the frontispiece, 03–05 Su Shi’s poems and final inscription, 06–07 Huang’s colophon, and 08–17 later inscriptions and blank areas.

The five detail images have only been rectangularly cropped, proportionally reduced and JPEG-encoded. No strokes, seals or paper damage have been removed or invented, and no colour adjustment, sharpening, warping or perspective reconstruction has been applied. The overview and index are explicitly labelled arrangements of separate museum photographs. Exact crop rectangles, pixel dimensions and checksums appear in [IMAGE_MANIFEST.json](IMAGE_MANIFEST.json).
