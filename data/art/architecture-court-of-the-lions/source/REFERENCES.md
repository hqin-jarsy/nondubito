## English

The Palace of the Lions belongs to the Nasrid court of Muhammad V, during his second reign, 1362–1391. The essay distinguishes the central supporting cylinder from the visible lions, and describes the inscribed poem as courtly praise, not hydraulic evidence.

The linked institutional records provide historical and material context. Interpretive comparisons belong to the essay and are not claims about an architect’s unrecorded intentions.
