# German restoration: preliminary independent translation review

Status: preliminary only; not a final certificate or independent historical fact-check. The seven known source decisions remain pending. No German translation, Chinese source, or live HTML was edited during this review.

## Scope and method

Read every Chinese source unit and every German translation unit in the five essays, and all five baseline/de essay bodies. Compared argument coverage, examples, narrative order, historical qualifications, reader sensitivity, and the distinction between explanation and excuse. The German drafts contain 253 units in total (47 / 51 / 45 / 56 / 54). Each has the same count and outer h2/p sequence as its current source. This structural match supports, but does not alone establish, semantic completeness.

The review is independent of the German translator. It is a language/argument review, not a claim of verification against archival or specialist historical sources. Findings apply only to the byte snapshots below; later edits require a delta review.

## New findings, separate from the seven known source decisions

### D1. Restore the source’s order of magnitude for deaths

Location: 1-weimar, body unit 46 (one-based).

Chinese: “到1945年吞噬了几千万条人命”. German: “nach einem Krieg und Verbrechen, die Millionen Menschenleben gekostet hatten.”

The German is literally compatible with tens of millions but loses the source’s explicit order of magnitude. This is a small but material loss in a paragraph assessing the catastrophe, not a demand to introduce a new casualty estimate. Recommended minimal correction: “die mehrere zehn Millionen Menschenleben gekostet hatten” or another idiomatic German rendering of tens of millions. Keep the existing distinction between political appointment, violent dictatorship, and military defeat.

### D2. Preserve the baseline’s explicit allocation of imperial responsibility

Location: 3-four-systems, body unit 24, compared with baseline/de/3-four-systems.html, section “Das japanische Kaiserreich”.

Current German names “Führung, Kaiser, Heer, Marine, Kabinett und Bürokratie” and rightly differentiates their agency from that of conscripts and civilians. But the baseline’s more precise safeguard has been compressed away: the emperor sanctioned state decisions and occupied the institutional and symbolic center, while decisions also arose through interactions of army, navy, cabinet, and bureaucracy. The baseline explicitly rejects both blaming every decision on one person and claiming the military deceived everyone else.

Recommended: restore that qualification concisely within unit 24, without adding a new unit or treating all Japanese people as one agent. This is retention of the existing German baseline, not one of the pending seven Chinese changes. The current draft does not exonerate the emperor, but preserving the explicit wording will better meet the instruction to retain historical qualifications.

### D3. Minor stylistic edit, optional

Location: 4-promise, body unit 33: “das internationale Versagen bestand darin, ihnen keinen wirksamen Schutz der Bedrohten entgegenzusetzen.”

The referent “ihnen” is the perpetrators, but the collocation “Schutz ... entgegensetzen” is awkward and makes the actor/action relation harder to follow. Suggested: “Das internationale Versagen bestand darin, die Bedrohten nicht wirksam vor den Tätern zu schützen.” This keeps perpetrator responsibility distinct from the international failure to protect.

## Completeness and reader-sensitive handling

- Essay 1 retains the carriage, treaty and reparations, worker/map example, inflation, savings, unemployment, Hans, group-conditioned value, anti-Semitic dehumanization, and final borrowed-subjectivity argument. Propaganda is consistently attributed to Nazis rather than asserted as truth. Alternatives, resistance, individual agency, and no inherited guilt are explicit. Deterministic domino language is qualified in line with baseline.
- Essay 2 retains Versailles, League cases, Munich’s calculation, the Czech dismemberment correction, Weimar’s procedural destruction, institutional self-preservation, and conditional trust. It preserves appointment versus violent consolidation, free-election limits, SPD dissent, and the role of Article 48. It does not turn the argument into blanket contempt for democratic institutions.
- Essay 3 restores all four opening scenes, Nazi administrative murder, the future-sacrifice mechanism, Japanese death-as-self-completion, Anglo-American abuses and slow correction, and all four structural comparison questions. It explicitly rejects equivalence of systems and crimes. The baseline’s imperial responsibility wording needs the small restoration noted in D2.
- Essay 4 retains the personal-responsibility argument, legal principles, victor selectivity, declaration and enforcement distinction, UN veto compromise, Rwanda, selective reconstruction, colonialism, nuclear hostage logic, and concluding cracks metaphor. It keeps legal/moral authority distinct from military power and avoids invalidating Nuremberg judgments by comparison with Allied actions.
- Essay 5 retains exit barriers, Soviet achievements and costs, Hungary/Prague, American crises and corrective channels, the peaceful revolution and border opening, reform and legitimacy, triumphalism, narrowing channels, finance/platform examples, and the final demand for individual action. Baseline qualifications on Soviet collapse and limited structural analogies remain.

The prose is fluent, serious German rather than a short summary. It sometimes repeats qualifications at greater length than necessary, but that is a style issue, not missing argument. No untranslated Chinese body units or editorial pending placeholders were found in these German snapshots. No instance requiring translation of the protected expression Self-as-an-End was introduced.

## Seven known pending source decisions: not new German defects

1. Hans: unit 31 of essay 1 is expressly a thought example.
2. Rhineland determinism: unit 4 of essay 2 rejects a certain alternate history.
3. Denazification speed: unit 25 of essay 3 rejects a completed rapid German transformation versus a uniquely difficult Japanese one.
4. Bengal famine: unit 29 of essay 3 is multicausal and retains colonial responsibility.
5. Göring/Dresden attribution: unit 15 of essay 4 uses the question as analysis, not an asserted quotation.
6. GDR departure count: unit 4 of essay 5 scopes an approximately 2.7 million estimate to 1949–August 1961, rather than repeating the disputed 3.5 million twelve-year total. That narrower new number still requires source/provenance reconciliation after approval; this review does not independently verify it.
7. Fukuyama: units 33–34, 38 and 40 of essay 5 distinguish his argument from triumphalist reception.

These treatments are mostly already grounded in baseline German qualifications, but Chinese approval and synchronized source/hash changes are still unresolved. Do not label this review a final full-language certificate before that reconciliation and a final delta review.
