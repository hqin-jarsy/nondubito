## Scope and method

I read every Chinese source unit and every available English body unit in essays 1–4, checking the argument, scenes, examples, paragraph sequence, and approved amendments. The four sources have 199 units: 47 / 51 / 45 / 56. The English files contain 196 translated units and three explicit null slots. Essay 5 was not present at this review cutoff and has not been reviewed.

This review is independent of the English translator. It was performed by the German translator. It does not claim new archival research or independent specialist historical verification.

## New translation correction

### E1. Identify Article 1, not the start of the entire Declaration

Location: 4-promise, body unit 21 (one-based).

Chinese explicitly identifies the sentence as 第一条, Article 1. English says: “The final text began: ...”. This drops that precision and can wrongly imply that the quoted sentence precedes the Declaration’s preamble. Minimal correction: “Article 1 of the final text reads: ...”. Retain the existing official English quotation. This is a source-fidelity correction, not a new historical thesis. No edit applied.

## Historical and reader-sensitivity cautions inherited from the Chinese source

These are not asserted as newly authorized edits. They should be kept distinct from the seven pending decisions and from translation defects. If the author wants wider source changes, seek the appropriate scope rather than silently importing the more qualified German adaptation.

- 1-weimar units 4, 11–12, 24, 37–40 and 45 retain broad psychological generalizations and a deterministic domino metaphor. They can suggest that Versailles and collective humiliation made the Holocaust inevitable. German safeguards expressly avoid this. English remains close to the current Chinese source, so a full semantic pass is not an endorsement of those causal claims. Unit 29’s “everyone” also needs to be understood as a promise to those included in the Nazi racial community, not literal inclusion of all people.
- 2-institutions unit 51 says every institution was “functioning normally” and none protecting people. This reproduces the Chinese closing sentence but sits uneasily with the approved account of coercion and rights destruction. Consider a separately authorized minimum clarification rather than treating the conclusion as proof of normal democratic functioning. Units 10–22 and 42–45 also retain broad institutional-purpose claims, not independently demonstrated laws.
- 3-four-systems units 4 and 16 retain sweeping assertions about Soviet generals and Zhukov; the million Stalingrad casualties should not be read as a million deaths. Units 22–24 retain an overly categorical model of kamikaze assent and internally impossible resistance. Units 6 and 30 risk moral equivalence unless readers keep the narrow instrumentalization comparison distinct from differing intents, scale and responsibility. These risks are inherited from the source; no English rewrite was performed.
- 4-promise unit 11 can be read as a general right of military intervention arising from Nuremberg, which is broader than the tribunal’s actual legal innovation. Unit 38’s “today’s terms” dollar conversion has no base year or method. Unit 47’s “worked” does not prove the counterfactual claim that nuclear deterrence caused the absence of world war. Units 22, 52 and 54 retain categorical historical-first assertions requiring caution.

The English prose is readable and substantially full-length. No untranslated Chinese body paragraphs were found among the 196 available texts. No translated replacement of the protected term Self-as-an-End was introduced.

### E2. Kamikaze causal wording changes the source claim

Location: 3-four-systems, zero-based body index 21 / one-based unit 22.

Source: “不是因为他们会被枪毙（虽然拒绝确实会有严重后果），而是因为……”. English snapshot reviewed: “The argument here is not that execution alone forced them, although refusal could have serious consequences.” Adding “alone” suggests execution may have compelled them as one of several causes; the source denies execution as the explanation and separately recognizes serious consequences. Minimal faithful rendering: “Not because they would be executed (although refusal could indeed have serious consequences), but because ...”. This is a fidelity observation, not independent verification of the source’s categorical historical explanation. Retaining or qualifying that explanation as history is a distinct editorial/source decision. No English edit applied by this reviewer.
