# Chapter 89 publication review

The November author manuscript anchors this chapter. The Simplified source differs from the supplied text only by a terminal newline; the Traditional edition changes script and contextual orthography only. The six translated editions map to the same 125 body units. English follows the author-approved reading draft.

## Reading and continuity

- All 24 new lines stay with their individual speakers, including the interjections about the out-of-season osmanthus, the shadow on a sleeve, and pear broth in place of wine. The earlier two-line poem is carried over from each Chapter 76 reading edition. The final image of the old gallery or verandah returns in Xiangyun’s invitation, without forced rhyme at the cost of the scene.
- Yuan-chun’s silence, the slow removal of the crown, the succession of doors, and the turn from Jia Zheng's courtly form of address to “Father” remain distinct. Her short reply recalls the road from Chapter 5 without explaining or resolving the dream.
- Grandmother Jia’s three statements retain the sense and voice of the earlier chapter. Chapter 88’s purse and cloak, Chapter 3’s old flowered bed-curtain, and the distinction between ginseng rootlets and the small reserve of good ginseng stay recognizable. The cord may recall the hair-knot but no character’s knowledge is supplied by the narrator.
- The wedding rumor and the wedding Jia Zheng actually orders stay distinct; the date is nine days away. Nightingale does not disclose here what “when it matters” means. The palace messenger has not returned by lamplighting; Xi-feng's request remains the chapter's last turn.
