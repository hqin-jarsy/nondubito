## Editorial comparison

Compared the complete Chinese source with the exact six translation payloads after successful serialization, then ran saved-file structure checks. Preserved both competing readings (civilizing progress and corrupting domestication) without adopting either as the conclusion. Retained irreversible loss, incomplete prior consent, the circular difficulty of consenting to the capacities needed for consent, and the distinction between guidance and taking over. Preserved Enkidu's opposition to Gilgamesh, friendship, shared violence, curse and blessing, and the refusal to settle his life into a single balance sheet.

Shamhat remains a guide with power and responsibility, not merely a seductress or an innocent doorway. The final educational promise retains both adaptation and the capacity to refuse and choose again. Version notes distinguish Standard Babylonian Tablets I–II and VII from the Old Babylonian Tablet II passage. No extra golden-age, clinical, or modern psychological narrative added.

Minor editorial refinements: German wording about the hunter and city's aim avoids suggesting elimination of Enkidu; French wording about the world beyond the door avoids confusing a broader world with more people.
