# Ancient Stories: full-translation review, episodes 11–15

## Coverage

All 30 translation files exist in en, ja, fr, de, es, ko. Source block counts per language: 11 = 131; 12 = 127; 13 = 148; 14 = 96; 15 = 118. Total: 620 source blocks, 3,720 translated blocks. Every block retains its tag, sequence, nonempty/empty status, and emphasis markers. Self-as-an-End was checked for literal preservation; none of these source blocks uses the term. Narrative, short transitional lines, objections, qualifications, endings, and italicized textual notes all remain present.

## Review method

Episodes 11–12: full Chinese source compared after writing with the exact six saved translation payloads, plus saved-file validation. Episodes 13–15: translator reread every saved translation against the Chinese source in all six languages and reported corrections. Batch reviewer independently read all three Chinese essays and inspected, in each language, the passages most vulnerable to argument flattening, role reversal, or lost textual qualification. This batch-level independent inspection supplements, rather than claims to duplicate, the full block-by-block review of those eighteen files.

All 30 saved files passed JSON, tag, block-count, nonempty, emphasis, and protected-term validation. The later Korean 15[83] refinement was reread and verified in the saved file.

## Editorial findings

- 11, Cassandra: preserved the differences between knowing, intelligible speech, credibility, actionable understanding, and testimony. Retained the objection that unverified warnings cannot simply command obedience, and the intermediate response to credible risk. Social position is not made the only cause. Testimony does not substitute for prevention, and walking into the palace is not romanticized as unconstrained freedom. Corrected an English attachment ambiguity at block 43 so that activity is halted because of an untestable judgment, rather than that judgment ceasing to guide action.
- 12, Savitri: preserved the sequence of boons and the argument about power answering for its own words. Kept the limits of dialogue against power, consent and acting on behalf of the dead, tradition as both enabling context and a role that must not be universalized, and the warning against demanding that someone live to fulfill others' hopes. The result is neither a mere verbal trick nor proof that love conquers everything.
- 13, Inanna: maintained simultaneously her right to escape and her responsibility toward the substituted third person. Preserved compelled choice as different from no choice, without absolving the rule that creates the dilemma. Retained uncertainty about the composite text, the possible connection of different traditions, and the explicit refusal of a modern seven-stage spiritual-growth gloss.
- 14, Midas: retained wealth's genuine usefulness and the distinction between means and irreplaceable ends. The wish really succeeds before its cost becomes apparent. Reconsideration is not treated as weakness, and the essay does not become a blanket denunciation of wealth. Ovid source note explicitly excludes the later golden-daughter addition.
- 15, Echo/Narcissus: preserved Echo's interior life, Narcissus's right to refuse intimacy, the distinction between responsiveness and reflection, and recognition without automatic transformation. No clinical diagnosis added. Refined Korean block 83 to express accepting another person's answer without having scripted it in advance.

Across the five essays, retained the changing rhythm between scene, short emphatic paragraphs, objection, qualification, and ending. No generic substitute summaries or repetitive moralizing conclusions were introduced.

## Source issue

Episode 15, source block 31 (zero-based), originally says 善待拒绝者 although the surrounding argument concerns kindness to the rejected person. The integration record documents the minimal clarification 善待被拒绝者, with Traditional Chinese synchronized, in `data/ancient-full/minor-source-correction.json`. The correction is applied by the renderer; original source snapshots remain untouched. Translations follow the clear contextual role. No other substantive source problem was identified in this batch.

## Limits

This is a faithful translation/editorial review of the supplied Chinese, not independent philological verification against Greek, Sanskrit, or Sumerian source editions. The essays' own textual cautions are retained. HTML regeneration, multilingual navigation, rendered-page inspection, repository-wide tests, and commits belong to the integration stage and are not claimed here. No Chinese source snapshots, HTML, scripts, or git state were changed by the translators.
