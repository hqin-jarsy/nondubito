# Ancient stories 06–10: translation review

## Status and coverage

All five essays are complete in English, Japanese, French, German, Spanish, and Korean: 30 translation files. Episodes 06–10 contain respectively 144, 172, 103, 131, and 140 blocks per language. Titles, short rhetorical paragraphs, separators, emphasis, counterarguments, and full source notes are retained. Chinese source files were not edited.

Every source block in episodes 06–09 was compared with all six saved translations after writing. Episode 10 received a separate complete source-aligned review in all six languages, documented in `episode-10.md`. The batch review additionally compared its complete Chinese and English texts and checked blocks 0, 21, 44, 60, 61, 68, 89, 98, 101, 133, 136, and 139 in the other five languages. This distinction records the review coverage accurately.

Final integration validation of all 30 JSON files passed exact block-count and tag-order checks, nonempty-block parity, emphasis-run parity, and unchanged Self-as-an-End occurrence counts. These mechanical checks supplement rather than replace the editorial comparisons.

## Editorial review

### 06: Prometheus

Preserved the distinction between Hesiod and Prometheus Bound, disputed authorship, recipients bearing unchosen risks, the informed-consent circularity, and distributed responsibility. Post-review corrections improved the Japanese title and closing phrasing, clarified the English risk question and permanent denial of growth, repaired German syntax and the distinction between possible misuse and obligatory responsibility, and replaced an awkward Spanish misuse verb. All 144 blocks were read in aligned ranges; a clipped initial passage was reread separately.

### 07: Gilgamesh

Preserved abstract versus personal mortality, a non-replicable exception, sleep versus death, rejuvenation versus immortality, the explicitly identified Old Babylonian Siduri tradition, the non-exclusive reading of the city walls, resistance to preventable deaths, and Enkidu as a person rather than an instrument of the hero's growth. Corrections repaired French negation, German chronology and plant-trial wording, future-death phrasing, Korean perspective, and Japanese and Spanish readability. All 172 blocks were compared in six aligned ranges.

### 08: Odysseus and the Sirens

Preserved the two commands as separate beats, the temptation of knowledge, narrow and terminable authority, new knowledge versus foreseen temptation, past-self domination, the crew's work and shared risk, and release as the decisive final action. Corrections clarified German permission rather than prohibition, restored a French degree question and attribution, and smoothed Japanese, Korean, and Spanish phrasing. All 103 blocks were compared in four aligned ranges.

### 09: Amaterasu

Preserved the specific Kojiki sequence and its distinction from Nihon Shoki variants. The narrative retains injury, justified withdrawal, real consequences for others, invitation and deception alongside physical force, Susanoo's punishment, and the absence of evidence for full reconciliation. Neither individual protection nor collective urgency erases the competing claim. The final questions about dependence becoming ownership and a community's reliance on a single provider of light remain intact. All 131 blocks were compared against all six saved translations in bounded ranges. Corrections clarified an English relative clause, Japanese attraction rather than literal gravity, French forced emergence and burdens, German sentence order, Spanish reflexive syntax, and Korean roof-path and concluding metaphor. No content was condensed.

### 10: Ekalavya

Confirmed the full sequence of exclusion, clay image, practice, dog episode, Arjuna's complaint, right-thumb demand, and reduced speed and skill rather than total inability. The review retains the counterarguments about Drona's name and image, Ekalavya's agency without exemption of the demand from judgment, responsibility for the dog without justification of Drona, and the distinction between developing a pupil and suppressing rivals. The qualified conclusion and ancient Nishada identity caveat remain. Title refinements in English, French, and German make surrendering skill explicit. Full language-specific corrections and review details are in `episode-10.md`.

## Batch consistency and source observations

Across the five essays, sources of ability and relationships of dependence are not silently converted into ownership. Consent, responsibility, coercion, and community needs retain their tensions rather than being flattened into a single slogan. Short narrative beats and questions preserve the Chinese pacing; natural target-language syntax is preferred over literal constructions.

Episode 07's note identifies mainly Tablets VIII–XI while including Enkidu's death, which occurs at the end of VII, with mourning in VIII. Because the note explicitly says “mainly,” its reference has been retained unchanged. This is a minor bibliographical scope imprecision, not a narrative contradiction or an unresolved correction request. No other substantive source issue was found during this translation review.

This review establishes translation coverage and editorial fidelity to the supplied Chinese. It is not a fresh collation against ancient-language editions, a historical fact-check of every claim, or a browser-layout review. HTML, rendering scripts, and git were outside this translation review's editing scope.
