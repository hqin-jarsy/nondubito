# English 01–05: semantic and editorial review

Reviewed against all five complete Chinese source essays and their five-language `notes.zh.md`, not the former English summaries. Reviewed each written translation for its sequence of arguments, individual scenes, rhetorical oppositions, qualifications, names, classical quotations, and source footer. Word counts including metadata: 01 1460; 02 1333; 03 1118; 04 964; 05 1000. Length follows the source rather than a word-count target.

- 01: Preserved opposite prescriptions, Zilu's courage and death, Ran You's self-imposed limit, excess/deficiency, Gongxi Hua as a third recipient, non-ranking, recorders, and names-as-medicine conclusion. Corrected the source's mistaken “Zilu, courtesy name Zhong You” to “also known as Zhong You.” Identified You/Qiu/Chi. Removed the erroneous “ten characters” count, not any quoted content. Preserved the poison assertion and “seldom,” not a universal prohibition.
- 02: Checked all three student cases and the final causal indictment of memorization. Preserved “most important,” the lowest-entry/open-door argument, board games without inventing a type, and inability versus refusal. Clarified Ci/Zigong and Shang/Zixia. Omitted the source's repeated character count, which does not transfer usefully to English. The “cheapest gift” assertion is retained as the author's description, not a verified historical price comparison. The white-ground reading of 3.8 is explicitly the reading used here; alternatives remain an academic question, not a reason to change the thesis.
- 03: Preserved the empty-teacher reading of 9.8 and the three-items/seldom reading of 9.1, the independent Athens analogy, each negative record, temple questioning, charioteering joke, failed all-night thinking, and empty-space conclusion. Retained the author’s assertion that charioteering ranks last among the Six Arts. This remains an unresolved author-decision issue: the common enumerated order is ritual, music, archery, charioteering, writing, calculation; the source supplies no separate ranking basis. An initial neutralization was reverted after checking the parent’s correction scope. The rendering is an essay's interpretation, not a claim to settle alternative ancient-text readings.
- 04: Corrected “a student asked him twice” to two exchanges, matching the actual speakers in 15.3 and 4.15. Preserved Zeng's honesty, the not-wrong direction of his interpretation, and the separate criticism of “nothing more.” Clarified Shen/Zeng and Ci/Zigong. Kept all three scenes and the teacher's cannot-give rather than will-not-give distinction. “Wholeheartedness and reciprocity” translates zhong/shu consistently within this essay.
- 05: Preserved the author's genuine-dissatisfaction reading of 11.4, not ironic praise; Yan Hui's status and death; the circuit of questioning; strongest anti-authoritarian claim; ruler conversations and slow medicine; advice to Zilu; recorders; and bilateral growth. “Humaneness” is the ethical criterion, not species membership. Retained certain national-ruin judgment without inserting “might.”

All classical passages are translated directly for these essays; no modern translator is named or implied. Source DOI identifiers, original chapter-number conventions, CC BY 4.0, and overview titles are retained. No Chinese text, builders, or other language files changed. Remaining scholarly questions (polysemy/segmentation, gift pricing, interpretive universals) are not independently settled by this translation review.
