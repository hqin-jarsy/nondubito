# Without the Office, May You Still Think?

A limit on deciding for others is not a limit on thought.

Han Qin (秦汉) · 2026

“If you do not hold the position, do not plan its affairs.” People still use this sentence today, almost always in one way: this is none of your business. You do not occupy that position, so you have no right to discuss the matter. It tells people to be quiet, to sweep only the snow before their own doors, to protect themselves by prudent withdrawal. And it comes with “The Master said.”

The sentence occurs twice in the *Analects*: once by itself, once with a comment attached.

> The Master said, “If you do not hold the position, do not plan its affairs.” Zengzi said, “The exemplary person's thoughts do not go beyond his position.”

Confucius says: if you do not occupy that position, do not plan the government affairs belonging to it. Zengzi says: an exemplary person's thinking does not extend beyond his office.

First look at Confucius's own sentence. This series reads “plan its affairs” as making plans on behalf of the holder of that position: making the decisions that belong to someone else's office. “Do not plan” means not taking over another's task, not making the decisions they ought to make. It is a matter of proportion in action, pointing in the same direction as “Take the lead before the responsible officials” and “Let the ruler be a ruler, the minister a minister; let each fulfill his task.” Each person should hold to their own position rather than reach into another's.

It cannot be a prohibition on discussion or thought. The evidence is his own life. He scarcely held a position throughout his life, yet spent his life discussing the government of the world. When the Ji family went to sacrifice at Mount Tai, he challenged Ran You to say whether he could stop them. When Chen Heng murdered the ruler of Qi, Confucius was already retired, yet he bathed, went to court, and formally asked Lu to send troops to punish the crime. The ruler of Lu told him to approach the three great families. He said: I have served as a high official and did not dare leave this unreported. He criticized Duke Ling of Wei for lacking the Way. He never declined when rulers of different states asked him about government. **If “do not plan its affairs” prohibits discussion and thought, the first person to violate it was Confucius himself.** But if it means “Do not make the decisions belonging to someone else's position,” every one of his actions fits. He discussed, advised, petitioned. He never usurped anyone's position to decide for them.

Where, then, did the “Be quiet” reading come from? Look at Zengzi's sentence.

“The exemplary person's thoughts do not go beyond his position.” Thought is not to exceed one's office. Zengzi's immediately following sentence pushes “planning” into “thinking.” The distinction this series reads here is this: the teacher says not to plan in another person's place, a matter of action; Zengzi says not to think beyond one's place, a matter of thought. **One concerns the bounds of action; the other makes a forbidden zone of thought. They are entirely different in kind.** Action can have limits: one must not make other people's decisions, and that is right. Thought has no limits. “An ordinary person cannot be deprived of his resolve.” Confucius himself thought all day and all night: “I once went the whole day without eating and the whole night without sleeping, in order to think.” Once the two sentences are read together in this way, the student's words do not merely strengthen the teacher's. They change their direction. A rule of proportion, “Do not exceed your authority in acting,” becomes a discipline of “Do not think what is not yours to think.” The later machinery of “not overstepping one's place” can grow from that shift.

Fairness requires a word for Zengzi. He signed his own name. “Zengzi said” is plain to see. Readers can recognize at once that this is not “The Master said.” He did not impersonate his teacher. He placed his own understanding beside the teacher's words. The understanding was wrong, but it was his own. The problem with this sentence is therefore limited: it is enough to point out where it twists the meaning; there is no need to refute it as a fabricated quotation. The people to challenge are not Zengzi but those who later squeezed the two sentences together and used them as “Confucius says discussion is forbidden.”

This chapter has another value. It places both layers in one frame, the teacher's original sentence immediately beside the student's twist. How can words that originally impose no confinement become confinement in transmission? The scene has been preserved.

Return to the present. If someone uses “If you do not hold the position, do not plan its affairs” to tell you to keep out, you can return the original sentence to them. It says not to decide for other people. It does not say not to think or speak. Thinking and speaking have never required an office.

Have you ever been silenced with “You do not hold the position”? Was it your hands that were restrained, or your mind?
