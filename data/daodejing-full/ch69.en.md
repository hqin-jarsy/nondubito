# The Danger of Feeling Invincible

## I. The News a Victory Stops Hearing

A company has been growing for years. Customers arrive, results seem to confirm every decision, and the leadership starts discussing the market as a possession. When someone identifies a problem, the answer no longer examines what they say. It recalls how many times the company has been right.

The risk is not having succeeded. It begins when success stops being a result and becomes permission not to look. “We have already won” can slowly empty out the habits that helped bring us this far.

Chapter 69 of the *Daodejing* speaks of war, but lets us hear that warning beyond the battlefield. “祸莫大于无敌”: no calamity is greater than what this reading understands as believing oneself without a rival. Its concern is not our enjoyment of a good result. It is confusing advantage with an absence of limits.

Warfare is not merely decorative language for business. Its presence adds gravity. When people in command misjudge their strength, others may pay for the error with their lives. An everyday application should preserve something of that responsibility, not turn the chapter into a recipe for elegant domination.

## II. Not Needing to Initiate the Confrontation

“用兵有言曰：吾不敢为主而为客，吾不进寸而退尺.” The chapter cites a military saying: not daring to be host, but guest; not advancing an inch, but retreating a foot.

Host and guest help us consider the difference between initiating a confrontation and responding to a situation already present. The imbalance between a small advance and a larger retreat emphasizes restraint. Every opportunity to prevail need not become an obligation to seize it.

Chapters [67](ch67.html) and [68](ch68.html) prepared this disposition through not claiming first place and not accepting every offered collision. Here we can ask whether escalation answers a need for protection or our discomfort at appearing less strong.

Declining to initiate a confrontation can prevent conflicts we would have manufactured. It does not give us control over what others do. A person under attack cannot always choose whether fighting will occur or when it begins. Defense, assistance, and resistance may be necessary despite every reasonable precaution.

Retreat does not prove us right either. It can be sensible, or abandon someone depending on us. The measure is not appearing restrained; it is recognizing the lives and responsibilities at stake. The chapter invites us not to initiate unnecessary harm. It does not transfer to victims the duty of preventing violence someone else chose to inflict.

## III. Four Images That Reduce the Display of Threat

“行无行，攘无臂，执无兵，乃无敌矣.” Marching without a deployed formation, reaching without presenting an arm, grasping without displaying weapons, arriving at no enemy. The military images are paradoxical: action remains while its apparatus of display is withdrawn.

In the interpretation followed here, they suggest strength that need not arrange every encounter as a threat. Not because actual weapons become imaginary, but because possessing capacity does not oblige us to turn it into continual provocation.

The final expression, 无敌, introduces a difficulty. Here I read it through not manufacturing a relationship of enmity; later, through believing that no opponent can resist us. This develops two uses interpretively. It does not demonstrate that the text admits only those meanings.

Not manufacturing enemies does not mean nobody will be hostile. Someone can refrain from attacking and still be attacked. The practical thought is more limited and more useful: do not add an unnecessary dispute for vanity's sake, or make every disagreement a test of power.

In a team, pointing out a defect can begin a correction or a trial over who deserves respect. The difference does not depend on concealing the defect. It depends on whether we add humiliation to affirm our position. Strength able to forgo that addition retains more than one possible response.

## IV. Invincibility Begins Spending the Treasures

The adopted panel continues, “祸莫大于无敌，无敌近亡吾，葆矣.” Its punctuation remains as displayed. For the argument here, I join 吾葆 and read “近亡吾葆” as approaching the loss of what we preserve. This arrangement of the sense should not be attributed to a uniquely established ancient punctuation.

The received text speaks of 轻敌, underestimating an enemy, and losing the treasure. [Wang Bi's commentary](https://www.chineseclassic.com/LauTzu/LaoTzu_wongbee/ch69.htm) connects it with the three treasures. That reading is not merely a shallow tactical warning. Underestimating another person can express precisely an excessive estimate of ourselves.

In the reading of presumed invincibility, danger enters decisions from within. We stop caring because loyalty seems assured. We abandon measure because resources will surely return. We no longer stand behind because disagreement feels insolent in the presence of our superiority.

The company at the beginning can continue showing good results throughout this process. That is why we should not wait for failure to ask what is being lost. Who no longer reports a problem? Which expenditure has become unquestionable? Whom do we treat as replaceable because we imagine nobody can offer them anything better?

The correction is not to pretend weakness. It is to remember that capacity depends on conditions. Preserving compassion, moderation, and humility after victory may be harder than invoking them when we need help. They are practices, not protective charms that make injury impossible.

## V. Winning Does Not Make Destruction Good

“故乘兵相若，则哀者胜矣.” When forces are comparable, victory belongs to the one retaining 哀: sorrow, compassion, grief at warfare and at those subjected to it. We need not exclude compassion for soldiers on the other side in order to emphasize the refusal to enjoy killing.

[Chapter 31](ch31.html) asks that death, and even victory, be met with mourning. Its criticism of weapons leaves room for force considered unavoidable, while refusing to turn force into a festival. Winning does not automatically make everything done to win just.

The claim that the compassionate prevail is a moral ideal, not an empirical law of warfare. A cruel army can win. A population that wanted no war can be defeated. The sentence matters for what it asks us to preserve when exercising force, not for predicting which side will triumph.

A literary scene makes this discomfort visible. In [Chapter 90 of *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/三國演義/第090回), Zhuge Liang brings about the destruction by fire of an army wearing rattan armor. The novel depicts him weeping and lamenting the moral cost. The scene belongs to fiction, not verified biography.

The tears do not absolve the character or return the lost lives. That is precisely why the scene helps: a victory can contain harm that does not vanish when the fighting ends. Sorrow matters if it prevents that harm from becoming a source of pride and requires reconsideration of what happens next.

Outside warfare, too, we can resolve a conflict without savoring someone else's degradation. We can defend a right, prevail in a dispute, or end an abuse without making the opponent's humiliation the emotional reward we were seeking all along.

## VI. A Review After Winning

The first question belongs before confrontation. What needs protection, and which part of the escalation are we adding to avoid looking weak? Where danger is real, the answer may be seeking support and acting. If only our image is threatened, there may be more room than we imagined.

The second arrives with “just a little further.” One more step may look small and commit us to a great deal. Which boundary will it cross? Who will have to sustain it? What would be needed to turn back? Retreat will not always be right, but should remain thinkable.

The third question belongs to success. Are we still listening to those who made it possible? Which conditions have we begun treating as permanent? Can we recognize another person's strength without feeling that our own has been diminished?

The fourth examines what we enjoy in victory. Perhaps it is relief at being safe, repair of an injustice, or completion of difficult work. These can be celebrated. Needing someone to remain humiliated before we feel we have won enough is something else.

Such questions do not replace agreements, safeguards, or collective decisions. They help a powerful position remain open to information its satisfaction might exclude. They also make it easier to end a confrontation once its legitimate purpose has been met, rather than finding another reason to keep enjoying the advantage.

## VII. Do Not Disappear Inside the Result

The three chapters make a sequence in this reading. [Chapter 67](ch67.html) names what to preserve; [Chapter 68](ch68.html) asks how strength avoids becoming display; Chapter 69 asks what happens when advantage threatens to make us forget both.

They do not teach that whoever least wants to fight always wins. They ask that outcome not become the only measure of action. Restraint, necessary defense, and compassion must remain in conversation even when no available response is clean.

Return to the company imagining that the market belongs to it. The next uncomfortable signal may arrive in a small sentence. Hearing it does not cancel success. It may keep success from consuming what sustained it.

[Chapter 70](ch70.html) changes the tone. After so many recommendations, it asks why what sounds easy to understand can be difficult to live. Perhaps part of the answer is already here. We do not always lack another idea. Sometimes we need the satisfaction of arrival not to prevent us from attending again to what we already knew.

The most important thing a victory leaves us may be the ability to notice what the word victory does not describe.
