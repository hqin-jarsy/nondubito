# The One That Looks Least Like It

## I. Not Wearing the Look of the Best

Some craft is so well judged that it hardly draws attention to itself. You sit in a chair and nothing is uncomfortable; you may never think about the person who made it. Some people's reliability works that way too. Something has been taken care of before you notice how much attention it required.

We tend to expect the best to look unmistakably best: most complete, most full, sharpest, cleverest, most triumphant. Chapter 45 approaches from the other side. Great completion seems deficient; great fullness seems empty; great straightness seems bent; great skill seems clumsy; great winning seems to dissolve. Those are the readings followed in this series.

The chapter echoes [Chapter 41](ch41.html), rather than immediately following it. There, familiar appearances of greatness began to loosen. Here achievement, fullness, integrity, skill and victory each step outside the image through which we expect to recognize them.

This does not make every unpromising appearance a sign of hidden excellence. A defect can be a defect. Clumsiness can mean more practice is needed. What deserves questioning is the authority of a prescribed appearance to tell us, before we look, whether something has value.

## II. Completion That Leaves Room

“大成若缺，其用不弊.” Great completion seems deficient; its use is not worn out. How can something accomplished still look as though something is missing? Start by separating completion from a prohibition on further change.

A product can be delivered on time and solve a real problem while remaining repairable and open to correction. If every difficulty reported by a user is answered with “The design is perfect; you are using it wrongly,” completion has become a wall against experience.

Responding to those difficulties does not invalidate everything already achieved. The thing is worth maintaining precisely because it has a use. Seeming deficient does not mean deliberately leaving defects, or permanently shipping unfinished work. It means accepting that one act of completion cannot anticipate every future need.

The second half of the sentence directs attention beyond the impressive moment of delivery. Can the thing continue to serve? The ancient phrase need not be turned into a guarantee that a particular object will never wear out. Nor does constant updating ensure success. Continued usefulness depends on particular acts of care and judgment.

A mature person can have the same room for correction. You can know what you do well and still allow someone to point out what you have not seen. There is no need to pretend you know nothing, or to treat every remaining limitation as proof that your previous life was wasted.

## III. Fullness That Does Not Fill Every Space

“大盈若冲，其用不穷.” Great fullness seems empty; its usefulness is not exhausted. The hollow space suggested here recalls the Dao in Chapter 4 and the bellows in Chapter 5. An interior can be useful because it has not been packed solid.

A room does not become most habitable when every corner is occupied. The apparently unused space lets somebody move, sit down or welcome a person who arrives later. It is not merely a failure to acquire enough furniture.

Knowledge has a related discipline. Someone who knows a field well may be particularly able to distinguish what is established from what remains uncertain. This is not an obligation to empty out their expertise or pretend every opinion is equally supported. It is an ability to prevent existing knowledge from making new evidence inaudible.

The familiar image of an empty cup can become misleading if it means having a cup and never putting anything in it. Without learning and accumulation, humility can itself become a performance. We need both: something to bring to the encounter, and enough room for the encounter to matter.

This connects with contentment in [Chapter 44](ch44.html). Enough need not mean the disappearance of every wish. It can mean that you do not have to fill every available space to establish that your life has substance. Room is already part of the substance of a life.

## IV. Straightness Can Turn; Skill Need Not Show Off

“大直如诎” places straightness beside bending. Holding a direction does not require assuming the same posture at every step. You can insist that a matter be handled fairly while changing the setting, timing or manner of a conversation so that the facts can be heard.

This is not permission to bend away the principle. If flexibility means abandoning a vulnerable person whenever somebody powerful objects, the relevant straightness has been lost. Direction and method need to be considered together. Calling yourself adaptable does not justify every retreat.

“大巧如拙” brings us back to unobtrusive skill. Chapter 27 speaks of good travelling leaving no tracks. A carefully fitted joint may make a piece of furniture feel obvious to use. An explanation may be so well arranged that the listener wonders why the subject ever seemed difficult, without seeing the preparation that made the explanation possible.

Naturalness is not the absence of training. Apparent effortlessness can come from having practiced enough to recognize where one more flourish would get in the way. The invitation is not to abandon craft, but to keep its display from displacing the thing it serves.

This series next adopts “大赢如溶,” approached here as great winning that seems to dissolve. The familiar received passage has “大辩若讷,” about great eloquence appearing hesitant. Following the author's reading, winning and dissolving raise a further question. Can there be a successful outcome that does not need to leave another person permanently underneath it?

Two people dispute a course of action. If they find an arrangement that takes both of their real concerns seriously, the shape of the contest may fade. Not because one quietly swallows an injury so that the other can appear magnanimous, but because establishing one's own purpose no longer requires the other's defeat.

Chapter 68's direction away from contention offers a later echo. Yet not every conflict can be reconciled, and just resistance can require a clear result. Sometimes harm must be stopped and responsibility stated. Celebrating an achievement is not automatically domination. The danger here is making victory an identity that needs the defeated to be repeatedly reminded of their place.

## V. A Response Appropriate to the Occasion

“躁胜寒，静胜炅.” Movement counters cold; stillness counters heat. The chapter suddenly offers an observation drawn from ordinary bodily experience. Different conditions call for different responses.

It is not an emergency guide to dangerous temperatures, nor a claim that calmness cures every problem associated with heat. Its value in the argument lies in refusing to make one posture universally sufficient. When movement is required, sitting still is not automatically wise. When agitation is already driving a situation, adding more agitation may accomplish little.

This prepares the chapter's final appeal to clarity and stillness. Stillness need not be worship of immobility. It may give us enough pause not to let urgency decide how to intervene before we have seen what intervention is needed.

## VI. Clarity and Stillness in a Shared World

“清静可以为天下正.” Clarity and stillness can help set the world aright. The sentence also closes the nine chapters gathered in the fifth paper of this commentary. It answers, within that arrangement, the “heaven and earth will right themselves” of [Chapter 37](ch37.html).

The earlier passage asks whether things can have room to develop without constant imposition. This one turns attention toward the posture of those involved, especially those with influence. Less anxiety-driven interference can let other people do their work instead of spending the day responding to your latest change of mind.

A leader who stops changing instructions every hour may allow a team to recover a direction. But the leader still has to provide resources, clarify responsibilities, address conflict and protect people from mistreatment. Handing every necessary decision back to the team is not necessarily stillness. It may mean that others must bear the cost of your refusal to act.

A family presents a similar distinction. Less relentless checking can give a child room to try. Care, safety and intelligible boundaries do not therefore disappear. People need space, and they need others to be reliably present when presence matters.

Self-correction cannot be assumed to mean automatic improvement. An injustice nobody interrupts can also perpetuate itself. We cannot declare every spontaneous result correct and then treat a person harmed by it as an interruption of the natural order.

Clarity and stillness become, in this reading, a responsible restraint. Do not spread your own agitation everywhere; do not dismiss other people's needs as noise either. Recognize when to leave room, when to speak and when action is necessary. Responsibility remains, but it need not always take the form of control.

## VII. Five Ways to Look Again

The chapter's five images can become five practical questions rather than five flattering labels.

Does a completed work still allow somebody to report a problem? That is the opening left by great completion seeming deficient: a door for repair, not an excuse for carelessness.

Can accumulated knowledge and experience still receive a new fact? That is the space in great fullness seeming empty. It does not require denying the abilities you genuinely have.

When you defend a direction, have you made one particular way of expressing it equally non-negotiable? That is the discernment suggested by great straightness seeming bent. Not every bend preserves a direction, but refusing every bend can lose it too.

Are you making the thing work, or making sure everyone notices how skilfully you work? That is the reminder in great skill seeming clumsy. Display can serve a purpose; it need not take priority over use.

After a success, are you interested in what can happen next, or do you still need somebody to be told that they lost? That is the question this essay draws from winning that dissolves. A victory maintained by repeatedly producing a loser has difficulty bringing the contest to an end.

None of these questions proves, “I do not look good enough, so I must secretly be excellent.” They return judgment to actions, uses and relationships. Has a difficulty actually been addressed? Has somebody been harmed? Is there a way to acknowledge a mistake and make a correction?

## VIII. Closing the Fifth Paper

Chapters 37 through 45 form a group in the fifth paper of this series. That is the commentary's arrangement; it should not be confused with the division of the silk manuscripts into their Dao and De parts. Read together, the nine chapters produce a set of related reminders.

[Chapter 37](ch37.html) leaves room for things to develop, but room may need tending. Abandonment does not ensure that everything goes well. [Chapter 38](ch38.html) distinguishes conduct from the maintenance of a virtuous image without making rules and commitments worthless.

[Chapter 39](ch39.html) attends to the relationships that sustain something, rather than elevating one conspicuous attribute into its whole identity. [Chapter 40](ch40.html) allows return and continuing growth after maturity, not just growth before it.

[Chapter 41](ch41.html) lets work reach completion without declaring a person finally complete. [Chapter 42](ch42.html) questions power that can function only by subduing others, without entrusting justice to automatic retribution.

[Chapter 43](ch43.html) looks for paths besides forcing through, while respecting boundaries that should not be crossed. [Chapter 44](ch44.html) asks seriously about enough and stopping, without turning contentment into immunity from injury.

By Chapter 45, achievement, substance, integrity, craft and successful outcomes can all remain real. What we can release is the requirement that they forever preserve a single approved face.

So, the next time you feel incomplete, you need not use these sentences to reassure yourself that you are already great. A plainer response may help more. Repair what needs repair. Take responsibility for what is yours to do. And do not pretend that having done so will leave no further opening.

Some room is not the blank space before life begins. It is what a life already seriously lived can still leave available for living.
