# Source review scope

The primary-source editor read the relevant passages in the Italian Einaudi 1961 text transcribed at https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/La_luna_e_i_fal%C3%B2 . The integration editor separately read all four original Chinese/English essays and the full four-essay revised candidate, then checked the five final wording refinements. The latter is not a claim to have independently collated the entire Italian novel.

Verified clusters: chapters I/X/XVIII on the hospital payment, five lire collected at the municipal office, foster household, possible file and the nickname Emilia gives him; X/XIV on the two dead foster sisters, absence from the valley and remaining/changing inhabitants; XXIX on Irene's resistance and her father's initial opposition to Arturo, Elvira/dowry, subsequent abuse, and Silvia's hemorrhage after abortion; XXVII on crop division, Valino's household, assault/fire, Cinto's escape and walnut-tree hanging; XXXI on Nuto actually taking Cinto in, carpentry/music, the doctor's examination discussed and Anguilla's intended departure for Genoa; XXXII on Baracca's accusation/sentence, Santa's attempt to flee, Nuto's participation and the novel's actual final visible ash mark.

The previous account of an absent birth record/name, an arranged obedient marriage, a fixed rent unaffected by harvest, an absent exit plan, and a merely proposed apprenticeship could not remain as factual premises. Direct consequences in the argument were narrowed without replacing the essay's viewpoint. The grave/memory reading remains an interpretation, not an invented universal intention attributed to the participants. Irene's death is not asserted to have a verified marital cause.

The source note's claim that no Chinese translation is available was removed. Translator Chen Ying's 2025 CITIC edition is documented by her university: https://xyxy.sisu.edu.cn/kxyj/8792d95300414be18a9823b3486bc63c.htm . The essay series retains Han's chosen Chinese title and names. No claim is made that the whole essay is a scholarly critical edition or that every inherited rhetorical assertion has been exhaustively authenticated.
