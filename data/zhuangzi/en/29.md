## Before the next person enters

A difficult conversation has just ended. The door opens again. The next visitor has done nothing wrong, but his first sentence already irritates you.

No philosophical training is needed to recognize the situation. The previous conversation is over; its mood still occupies a chair. The newcomer does not know that he is now speaking to two conversations at once.

Near the end of Responding to Rulers, the Zhuangzi offers an image of the mind as a mirror. It does not escort what leaves or anticipate what arrives. It responds without storing, and can thus meet things without injury.

A mirror usually makes us think of clarity: seeing what is there. This passage also draws attention to what happens afterward. The image does not stay in place, waiting to cover the next person who appears.

## Keep the responding in the sentence

The negatives are conspicuous: not escorting, not welcoming in advance, not retaining. Yet the sentence is not made entirely of refusals. At its center is a response.

Something stands before a mirror, and an image appears. Applied to the mind, the comparison need not describe a person who never judges, never reacts, and regards every event with identical indifference. There is still someone at home when life knocks.

This distinguishes non-retention from numbness. After being hurt, a person may close off pleasure as well as pain, avoiding anyone who might require a response. Such protection has a history and deserves understanding. It is not the only kind of life the mirror can help us imagine.

The passage places contact alongside an absence of fixed accumulation. An encounter need not take permanent possession of a room in the mind. That is an attractive possibility, but failing to achieve it immediately should not become another reason to punish oneself.

## Not anticipating does not forbid preparation

The first verb is commonly understood as escorting or sending off. Together, the two negatives suggest neither pursuing what departs nor reaching ahead to capture what has not come. In ordinary life, they can prompt a question about how thoroughly we arrange reality before it arrives.

Preparation remains useful. A medical appointment calls for remembering symptoms. A meeting requires reading the material. Collecting a child requires knowing the time. Refusing all anticipation would often leave difficulties for other people, not create philosophical openness.

The difference is whether the expectation can change. You may have considered an objection someone is likely to raise without deciding that everything he says must be another version of that objection.

Sometimes a conversation needs less rehearsal and one small interruption in our certainty: that was not quite what I expected him to say. Let me listen again. The preparation can remain available without dictating every line of the encounter.

## Where do you put the past?

A colleague submitted incorrect material last year. This year he receives a similar assignment. You remember the mistake. That is normal and may be necessary.

You could arrange another check, clarify the requirements, and see what has changed. Or you could decide that he is simply unreliable. Everything done correctly then counts as luck; every fresh error becomes confirmation of a conclusion already complete.

The same memory occupies two different places. As relevant information, it can work alongside new evidence. As a sealed verdict, it prevents new evidence from entering.

Responding without storing cannot sensibly become a requirement to lose memory. Here it can remind us not to let an accumulated impression speak permanently on behalf of someone standing before us.

Nor is there a universal number of seconds within which trust must return after one good performance. Repeated deception differs from an isolated error, and the stakes differ too. Waiting for more evidence may be reasonable. Caution need not be condemned as an inability to let go.

## Some images need to be remembered

If someone has harmed you, keeping records, recognizing patterns, and maintaining boundaries may be necessary. No one must become a clean slate at every meeting so that the same harm can be offered another first chance.

Not declaring a person permanently incapable of change is different from restoring access or trust. You can acknowledge that change is possible while deciding not to bear the cost of testing it yourself.

The mirror cannot issue an order to forgive. It also cannot guarantee that injuries leave no trace if only you refrain from retaining them. Human bodies, memories, and relationships are not panes of glass wiped clean at will.

We can hear the ancient aspiration to meet things without injury without converting it into an infallible psychological prescription. Someone who repeatedly remembers a painful event has not thereby failed a philosophy test. Sometimes stepping away and finding support is more practical than forcing the mind to announce that it has already released everything.

## Don't send the next visitor the previous one's bill

The phrase about remaining uninjured can first be read as protecting the one who meets the world. Asking how the metaphor might also help us injure others less is an extension made by this essay, not something every word of the original explicitly states.

A teacher has just been deceived by a student. The next student explaining a late assignment is received as another liar. Someone has experienced betrayal in a relationship; every delayed reply in a later relationship begins to feel like the same story returning.

These reactions may be understandable. Yet the person now present should not automatically become responsible for someone else's past actions. Memory needs care, while judgment needs distinctions: the fear has a history; this event still requires its own examination.

No instantaneous mastery is necessary. Saying “I feel very tense and would like to return to this conversation later” may already create more room than immediately turning the tension into an accusation. A pause can protect both people without denying what either is experiencing.

## You need not become the headquarters of everything

The mirror does not appear alone. It follows instructions not to become the seat of reputation, the storehouse of schemes, the bearer of every affair, or the master of knowledge. The exact force of each phrase invites study, but together they unsettle the self that must gather everything around it.

That does not cancel responsibilities already accepted. It questions what happens when accepting responsibility becomes the only way to demonstrate that one is indispensable. No workload is then sufficient. An offer to take over causes anxiety. Even when the task ends, it must continue telling you who you are.

Non-retention can concern more than bad impressions. Each success need not be collected as another badge requiring permanent defense. You may enjoy accomplishing something. Someone else accomplishing it differently need not feel like the removal of a piece of yourself.

We will remember what we have done. We need not require every remembered achievement to take a shift guarding the present.

## After the mirror comes Hundun

In the received arrangement of the Inner Chapters, the mirror passage is followed by the story of Hundun. The rulers Shu and Hu wish to repay his kindness by giving him the seven openings human beings possess. They bore one opening each day. On the seventh day, Hundun dies.

Reading the passages together produces a powerful contrast. First, responsiveness without holding possession; then, help shaped by the helpers' own template. The mirror does not require the visitor to acquire a standard form before appearing. The grateful rulers bring their standard and begin work.

The adjacency supports the comparison. It does not prove that an ancient author designed the seven-lesson curriculum proposed by this reading series. The formation and arrangement of the book remain historical questions.

Nor is Hundun simply an undeveloped student awaiting improvement. He has a way of existing that the intervention destroys. At the end of this route, the person who most urgently needs to stop may be the one eager to help.

## You do not have to become someone without a past

Our route through the Inner Chapters pauses here. The mirror has not erased the stories we encountered along the way. We remember fasting the mind, the shrine oak, the order in which two classmates were allowed to leave, and the changing presentations Liezi watched beside his teacher.

The conclusion, therefore, need not be that nothing should ever remain. A more useful question is what those remains will do. Will they help us see a new event, or only make us quicker to decide that every event has happened already?

The next person enters. You bring your history; he brings his. There is no need to pretend that either begins from zero. Yet the meeting may still contain something that has not previously occurred between you.

That possibility does not demand perfect clarity. It asks for enough room to be noticed. An old interpretation may be useful, but it need not occupy the only available place before the person has even sat down.

Leave a seat for what is happening now.
