After some conversations, it is difficult to explain what felt wrong. The other person was clear and patient. They kept asking for your opinion. But whenever your opinion differed, they changed the example and explained again, until you finally said the sentence they had been waiting for.

You took part in many rounds without quite being allowed to participate.

The opening of “Borrowed Words” considers how language reaches people. What interests me most is not whether the speaker uses a story, but what the listener is actually allowed to do. That question belongs to these essays as well.

## Three names, not a recipe

The passage begins with three kinds of speech: borrowed words, weighty words, and goblet words. The first places speech elsewhere, often in another voice. The second is commonly understood as drawing on a respected speaker. The third uses the image of a vessel to suggest language responsive to changing circumstances rather than fixed permanently in one position.

These are starting points, not uncontested definitions. The collected commentaries also preserve a reading of weighty words as repeated speech and different explanations of goblet words. Guo Xiang describes a vessel's changing inclination. We can follow the image without pretending to have reconstructed an ancient mechanical experiment.

The accompanying numbers, nine in ten and seven in ten, should not simply be added into a statistical breakdown of the book. Guo reads them as proportions believed by listeners. A compositional reading is also possible. Neither supplies a verified count of the text before us.

The important opening is that speech involves more than sentences. Who speaks, from what position, and how someone approaches the words can change the resistance those words encounter.

## Why the father does not act as matchmaker

The chapter offers a familiar comparison. A father's praise of his son is less persuasive than another person's praise. That need not mean the father is lying. The listener notices the relationship and wonders what it leaves out.

We respond similarly to recommendations and introductions. Why is this person saying this? What might be missing? Such suspicion can be excessive, but it can also be reasonable.

The passage then describes people responding to what agrees with them and resisting what differs, calling their own side right and the other wrong. It recognizes the moment when allegiance has arrived before examination.

But a tendency is not an exceptionless law. A direct disagreement may awaken thought. A story's allegiance may be recognized and rejected immediately. Clarity does not guarantee acceptance, yet the two need not belong to worlds with no connection.

Sometimes expression is the obstacle. Sometimes it is the relationship. Sometimes the listener has sound reasons to disagree. We should not treat every refusal as a psychological barrier that better packaging must eventually overcome.

That would preserve the speaker's infallibility by making every unresolved difference a problem inside someone else's mind.

## Explanation has not been prohibited

The opening explains methods of speaking. Does that invalidate it? If borrowed speech is effective, should the chapter not teach the whole point through a story instead?

The challenge is worth considering, but the verdict would be premature. The passage has not established a rule requiring every utterance to be a narrative. It also uses the father's matchmaking as a comparison. Describing a method does not require employing only that method in every sentence.

Its later treatment of weighty words questions age without the understanding that might justify respect. Its discussion of goblet words develops the relations between speaking and not speaking, acceptance and rejection. It is not merely an instruction to recruit an authority and overpower a listener.

Whether the explanation opens something for a reader must be tested in reading. Its explanatory form alone cannot prove failure. Conversely, the absence of a final moral cannot guarantee a story's generosity.

People teaching communication should certainly be answerable for their practice. Welcoming disagreement and then punishing it is a substantive contradiction. But describing a communicative practice and using the identical form on every occasion are not the same requirement.

A useful test becomes less useful when its terms grow so absolute that they decide the case before anyone reads.

## Back to the mat

The chapter ends with Yang Ziju, encountered in essay 5. On his outward journey, people welcome him ceremoniously and yield their places. On his return, fellow guests compete with him for a seat.

Between the two pictures, words have been spoken. Laozi directly criticizes his self-important bearing and offers sayings about great purity appearing blemished and abundant virtue appearing insufficient. Yang changes expression and respectfully accepts the teaching.

Direct instruction is therefore part of the scene. What the ending withholds is not every explanation but a certificate declaring that enlightenment has been achieved. The reader returns to how people now share space with him.

The speech has not replaced the life after it. Something must still be noticed in another encounter.

Reading the opening discussion beside this final scene allows us to feel differences of form and ways the forms may help each other. Calling the juxtaposition a larger parable is an interesting interpretation. It does not establish that an editor deliberately arranged the ending to refute the beginning.

A chapter can contain different voices without requiring one winner. We can ask what the explanation helps us see, and what the scene makes us reconsider within the explanation.

## Another voice can conceal another intention

A story can move a dispute slightly away from its immediate loyalties. We look first at what happens to a character, then return to our own question. That distance can be valuable.

Borrowing another voice can also hide an agenda. Carefully selected examples, questions admitting only one answer, and endorsements disguised as independent testimony may be harder to inspect than a plainly stated position.

The difference between a story and a persuasive trick cannot depend only on whether a conclusion is announced. A story without a moral can arrange its materials so that only one route remains. A direct explanation can openly provide evidence and allow omissions to be challenged.

One practical test is what happens after the listener says no. Can the premise be examined again? Can the example be admitted to be unsuitable? Can someone defer judgment without being diagnosed as immature?

If hesitation always becomes evidence of the listener's deficiency, the story's gentleness may be another way of hurrying them.

Nor should the wish to inspire make help unnecessarily difficult. A newcomer asking which button to press may deserve a clear answer. Someone awaiting a commitment needs a reply. Requiring people to guess your meaning on every occasion is not more respectful than deciding everything for them.

## These essays are inside the question

By essay 65, the question cannot remain directed only at ancient writers. This series has explained, selected details, made judgments, and suggested where readers might pause. None of that is simply the original text speaking by itself.

“Written for ordinary readers” cannot exempt every explanation from criticism. Commentary can make a work approachable; it can also take away something a reader might have discovered. Both possibilities require attention to the actual writing, not confidence in the commentator's good intentions.

So I should state the responsibility here rather than postpone it entirely until the final essay. Quotations should be checkable. Added scenes and interpretations should remain distinguishable from the original. Judgments should have reasons, and disputed readings should not disappear merely because they complicate my account.

If an explanation makes the story narrower, it should be open to revision.

That does not require a disclaimer in every paragraph. Sources can be available at the end, important disagreements visible where they matter. The main account still needs a voice. Otherwise a reader receives only someone continually retreating from the possibility of being wrong.

I can state what I find without asking you to treat it as the only thing available to find. If you return to the original with a different question, these essays need not call you back into line.

The mat at the end remains a useful image. Many words may be spoken; what they make possible appears in another encounter. An essay has done something worthwhile if, after it finishes, a reader has another path toward the work, rather than another interpreter they must obey.
