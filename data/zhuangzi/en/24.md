## Nothing on the usual list

Duke Ai of Lu has met a man called Ai Taituo and cannot explain his attraction. The man's appearance, as the story describes it, is alarming. Yet men who spend time with him are reluctant to leave, and women seek his company. The text goes so far as to imagine women preferring to become his concubines rather than someone else's principal wife.

That exaggeration belongs to an ancient hierarchy of marriage, not a romantic ideal we need to adopt. What matters for the question being staged is the reversal of expectation. The familiar reasons for wanting to be near someone seem absent.

Ai Taituo has no commanding office through which to rescue people from danger, no stored wealth with which to feed them, and no reputation as a widely learned authority. Why do people stay?

The duke is not merely puzzled by other people's behavior. Within a month of meeting him, he has become interested himself. Before a year passes, he trusts him deeply enough to want to entrust him with the country's affairs.

## He is not mute

The text says that Ai Taituo responds to others without taking the lead. One useful rendering of the contrast is that he joins in without initiating the tune. That is not the same as never speaking, and it does not establish that he lacks opinions.

We probably know the opposite kind of encounter. Mention a book, and the other person begins describing the ten more important books he has already read. Start explaining a difficulty, and the conversation becomes an account of the larger difficulty he once solved brilliantly.

There need be no ill intent. He may be genuinely knowledgeable and eager to help. Yet when you leave, you realize that you have once again served as an audience. The thing you brought has barely reached the table.

Ai Taituo suggests another possibility. Being with him does not first require finding a gap in his performance. Your unfinished words might arrive without immediately being reorganized into his answer. This is a way of approaching the image, not a transcript of his method for receiving visitors. The story does not give us one.

## The duke finds him a position

The duke's trust eventually takes a familiar form: an appointment. With the relevant governing post vacant, he wants Ai Taituo to take responsibility for state affairs. The passage is not an account of a completed abdication or a transfer of the throne.

Nor does Ai Taituo eagerly seize the opportunity. His manner suggests reluctance or withdrawal. The duke feels ashamed, but entrusts him with the government anyway. Before long, Ai Taituo leaves. The duke is desolate, as though someone with whom he could be at ease has vanished from his life.

We are not told why Ai Taituo goes. It would be convenient to supply a reason: his teaching was finished, or he was protesting the appointment. Neither explanation is given in the episode.

The sequence nevertheless leaves a question available. When we discover that someone is valuable to us, how quickly do we find him a role in which he can continue being valuable? Admiration can become an assignment before we notice the change.

## Good intentions still need to ask

A colleague listens exceptionally well. Whenever the team has a conflict, everyone goes to her. At first this is trust; gradually it becomes the arrangement. One day she does not want to listen, and her colleagues are startled. Isn't she supposed to be the understanding one?

A friend writes beautifully, so you ask him to improve a draft. Then another. Eventually his own writing time is gone, and declining requires an explanation for why he has become less friendly.

The mistake is not asking for help. Nor do appointments and payment automatically corrupt a relationship. Fair payment and clear responsibility may improve one. The question is whether the person wants the work, how much is reasonable, and whether the answer may change.

Recognizing an ability is one thing. Treating it as a resource available on demand is another. The duke's affection may be sincere; we do not need to discover a calculating motive behind every expression of appreciation. Sincere admiration is exactly what can make the impulse to decide for someone else difficult to notice.

## Why the piglets leave

Confucius responds to the duke with an unsettling image. Piglets suckle at a mother who has died. After a little while they startle and leave. Her bodily shape is still present; the life that drew them to her is not.

The comparison turns attention from visible form toward what animates it. It does not require us to regard bodies as disposable shells. Pain, hunger, illness, and physical need are real; praising what lies within does not excuse failing to attend to them.

Its function in this exchange is more particular. An inventory of appearance, position, and material benefits has not yet explained what people actually experience in Ai Taituo's company. Something important to the relationship is missing from the duke's usual account.

That missing thing may be less conspicuous than charisma. Perhaps you are not judged before you have finished a sentence. Perhaps disagreement can be spoken. Perhaps you do not have to arrive looking successful before you are allowed to sit down. These are ordinary possibilities through which we can begin to approach the story's more elusive description.

## Goodness without a signboard

Confucius describes Ai Taituo as complete in his capacities, while his virtue takes no outwardly displayed form. “Capacity” here is broader than professional competence. And unadvertised virtue need not mean a person has no recognizable character at all.

Think of a goodness that does not repeatedly require its own introduction. I need not be told how tolerant my host is, how unpossessive, or how free I am supposed to feel in her presence. The room can simply become easier to inhabit.

The passage uses still water as an image. It also speaks beautifully of being springtime to things. Spring does not make every plant grow into the same shape; it provides conditions in which growth can occur. Between people, the image might suggest this: to grow well beside you, I do not first have to grow into you.

But that person must be allowed needs of her own. She can be tired, disagree, or ask to talk another day. If we require her always to accept us, then enjoy the freedom her endless acceptance provides, we have dressed a burden in the language of praise.

## Silence is not automatically wise

“Not taking the lead” can be turned into a fantasy of advanced instruction: nod when the student is right, remain silent when he is wrong, and leave him to discover the truth. The text does not describe such a procedure.

In life, withholding explanations is not inherently more respectful than giving them. A learner may need a reason, a demonstration, or an explicit disagreement. Making someone guess indefinitely what he has failed to understand is not proof of the teacher's depth.

Ai Taituo is not presented as a reproducible training program. Rather, he makes the duke's habits of assessment falter. The familiar advantages have all been counted, and they still do not explain why the duke wants his company.

We need not repair that uncertainty by inventing another invisible metric for evaluating our friends. A person may be welcoming or socially awkward. Lacking this remarkable power to attract others does not make his life worth less.

## The sentence that comes afterward

The episode does not finish with the duke longing for the departed man. Later he tells Minzi that he once regarded holding the ruler's place and managing the country's affairs as the height of accomplishment. Confucius's words have exposed the narrowness of that view.

He then describes his relationship with Confucius not as ruler and subject, but as a friendship in virtue. Whether he will consistently live up to that description is another question. Yet the line matters: the story does not simply say that entering an official role makes genuine encounter impossible.

Responsibilities remain. So do differences in power. But neither need constitute everything that can be said about the people involved. A relationship can be reconsidered from within its circumstances, not only preserved by disappearing from them.

## A seat without an appointment

You may finish the story thinking of someone whose company makes life easier. There is no immediate need to investigate her secret or give her a larger part to play.

Next time, ask how she is doing. Make time for her own concerns. Allow her to be less patient than usual, and allow some things to be things she does not want to hear today.

Being able to help is a valuable part of a relationship. Being allowed to remain when you are not helping is another.

Ai Taituo leaves, and the explanation remains unwritten. We cannot bring him back for the duke. But we can refrain from one small habit in our own lives: the moment someone seems precious, we need not appoint him to a job in which he must keep proving it.
