Chisel-Construct Cycle: Economics · 二十三篇 · 双语
凿构周期律 · 经济 · 23 Essays · Bilingual

这个系列用凿构周期律重读经济思想与经济制度的历史，从没有货币的礼物经济，一路追到中本聪与评分社会。

凿构周期律的核心命题：任何秩序都是对某种凿（旧秩序的破裂）的应答构。构有它能做的，也有它做不到的。构做不到的，是它的余项。余项不消灭，只转移，只累积，最终引发下一次凿。

经济这套构，从第一天起就想把人和万物压进同一把可结算的尺子：从库拉圈里名望的分量，到神庙的账簿，到铸币的成色，到复式记账的借贷两栏，到边际效用的最后一单位,到金融衍生品的合成结构。可它每一次想把账做平，都被自己盖不住的余项顶开——不肯被折算的人，洗不干净的礼物，算不清的相关性，救不进系统性风险那把尺子的普通家庭。二十三篇，追着这套构在几千年里，一次次想合上账本，又一次次被余项顶开的历史。

每篇都有独立的英文版，不是翻译，是用英文直接重写的平行文章。

This series re-reads the history of economic thought and economic institutions through the chisel-construct cycle — from moneyless gift economies to Satoshi Nakamoto and the scored society.

The framework's core claim: every order is a construct built in response to a chisel — the fracture of an earlier order. Constructs have what they can do and what they cannot. What they cannot do generates remainders. Remainders do not disappear; they shift, accumulate, and eventually force the next chisel.

The economy, as a construct, has wanted from its very first day to press people and things into a single settleable scale: from the weight of prestige in a Kula ring, to a temple's ledger, to a coin's purity, to the two columns of double-entry bookkeeping, to the last unit of marginal utility, to the synthetic structure of a derivative. And every time it tries to close its books, its own unabsorbable remainder pries it back open — the person who won't be converted into a number, the gift that won't wash clean, the correlation no model can quite estimate, the ordinary family that never enters the scale of systemic risk. These twenty-three essays follow that construct across several thousand years, closing its ledger again and again, and pried open again and again.

Each essay has an independent English version — not a translation, but a parallel essay written directly in English.

    互惠与馈赠:不结清,是它的运转方式
    Reciprocity and the Gift — Never Settling Was the Point
    结清账目，在最亲近的人之间反而是冒犯。从库拉圈到夸富宴，从易洛魁人的长屋到南非的采集者，礼物经济靠的不是不算计，而是一种判断：有些账不该当场算清。而每一次想把关系压进一把共同的尺子，都会剩下一截露在外面，量不进去，也抹不掉的人。
    Among people closest to you, settling accounts is an insult. From the Kula ring to the potlatch, from the Iroquois longhouse to foragers in southern Africa, gift economies ran not on an absence of calculation but on a judgment: some accounts should never be settled on the spot. Every attempt to compress a relationship into a shared scale leaves something sticking out past its edge — a person who won't be measured away.
