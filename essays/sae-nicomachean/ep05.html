过到什么份上才算过

How Far Is Too Far?

Han Qin (秦汉)

第二卷第九章一开口，他就把前面收了尾：性格上的德性是个中道，怎么是的，它是两种毛病中间的那一个，一头过一头不及，说到这儿够了。

收完他没有往下推。他做了一件在这本书里不多见的事：他给了三条上手的办法。

给之前先说了一句：所以做个好人是件力气活。

他说得很实在。在每一样事情上取那个中间，都是力气活；就像一个圆的圆心，不是谁都找得着，得是懂的人才找得着。

接下来那一句写得很利落：

发火这件事人人会，也容易；给钱花钱也是。可对谁发，发多少，什么时候发，为着什么事发，怎么个发法，这就不是人人会的了，也不容易。所以做得好这件事既少见，又值得夸，又漂亮。

这句话里那五样，一样也不是刻度。它们只是把"该发多少火"这个问题拆成了五个更细的问题，而每一个还是同样难。

然后是三条办法。

第一条：先躲开更歪的那一头。他引了卡吕普索一句：把船开出这团烟和这片浪。两个极端里总有一个错得更厉害。既然正中间极难打中，那就照人说的走第二条水路，在祸害里头挑小的。

第二条：看清自己往哪边倒，然后往反方向拽自己。怎么知道自己往哪边倒，看什么让你快活，什么让你难受，就知道了。往反方向使劲，拽得远一点，反倒能落到中间。他说这就像扳弯木头的人干的活。

第三条：处处防着快活。因为我们评判快活的时候，不是不受贿的。特洛伊城头那些老人看见海伦的时候是什么心思，我们对着快活就该是什么心思，把他们那句话对它念一遍；这么把她送走，就少错一点。

这三条有个共同的样子：一条也不给你一把量完照数执行的尺。第一条还明明白白在比较两个极端哪个错得更厉害，所以它们不是没有理由的手艺动作；它们是三条上得了手的办法，不是一套能自动算出中间点的公式。

而收尾那一段，是他给这套办法自己标的界。

他说，这也许难，尤其在一件一件具体的事上。因为怎么发怒，对谁发，为着什么事发，发多久，这些不容易划出来。

他还举了个我们自己嘴上的证据：有时候我们夸那些不足的人，说他和气；有时候又管发狠的人叫有男子气。

接着他把责备的线往下描：偏一点的不受责备，不管往哪边偏；偏得多的才受责备，因为偏得多瞒不住。

然后是这一章真正的那一句：

可到什么份上，过了多少，才算该受责备，不容易用道理划出线来。因为凡是要靠觉察的东西，都没有一样是容易划的。这一类事情落在一件一件的具体情形里，而判断落在觉察上。

他不是只在说发怒。发怒是他的例子，例子说完就丢开了。他接下去写的是凡是要靠觉察的东西，一句都没有留在发怒上。

所以这不是一个题材上的小注。这是他给整套中道说法标的界：这套办法能把你带到那一处，带不进那一处里面去。

他标完界，最后一句还是往回收的：这么多至少说清了，中间那个状态在一切事上都值得夸，而人得有时候朝过头那边偏一点，有时候朝不及那边偏一点，因为这样最容易打中中间和那个好。

他没有给那条线一把通用的刻度，也没有因为通用的刻度给不出来就把中道扔掉。中间和那个好照旧在它们的位置上；只是它在具体一件事里怎么落，得由个案的觉察来判。

他没有说个案本身没有边。他说的是普遍的道理量不出每一个个案。

他站住的那一处，这边也站得住。

两边都不认那一句：一条普遍的公式已经替每一个个案把线画完了。

这边不认它，用的是一个先摆出来才看得见的分法。

一次划界落在一个场子里。场子里的每一样东西受一次判，而判下来只有四种落法：落在这一边，落在那一边，两边都落上，一边也不落。四种是判下来的形式，不是关于那件事的四种真假。

这一篇要的是第三格：两边都落上。

要紧的是这一格的地位。它本身只是一种落法，不是一个待清理的错。只有在收下了"同一个地址上不许两边都命中"这一条的那一支上，它才带着一个负担：接着往下分条件。而这边把那一条登记成可以换掉的。换掉了，那一格就是一个正当的落法，不承诺散开。

日常里的对照也在用，只是不点名。

一件事既算迟到又算没迟到，因为"哪一刻算到"还没有定下来。这不是那件事本身没有边，也不是谁看走了眼。这时候要么把"到"定下来，要么承认这一格就这么记着。

而表格上另有一栏画横杠。那是第四格：一边也不落。填"没有"是一次判断，画横杠是说这儿轮不到判断。这一格跟第三格不是一回事，摆在一起是为了不混。

可这条线怎么才落得下来，两边接下来那一步不一样。

他把个案的收尾交给觉察。这一类事情落在一件一件的具体情形里，而具体的东西要靠觉察。这一章只说到这儿；至于那份觉察为什么跟经历和明智的人连着，是第六卷才补的。

这边接下来那一步不是去找一个看得准的人，是把这一次的地址再摊开一格：面对的是什么，在哪一层上说的，认了哪几条前提，哪一样还没定。

而摊开本身不替你判。摊到第几格算够，还是一次判断。

他把最后那一下交给一个人的眼力，这边把它交给下一次判断。而下一次判断，还是判断。

带过孩子就知道这两步的分别。

孩子闹到什么份上该拦，什么份上该由他去，这件事上没有一条线。有经验的人确实看得出来，可你问他凭什么看出来的，他未必说得出来，只能说这回不一样。

说不出来这件事，他那边有个正面的说法：判断落在觉察上，而觉察是那个人的觉察。（这一章只说到觉察；觉察和经历的关系，第六卷才写。）

这边会往另一头走：这回哪里不一样。是孩子今天没睡够，还是场合不同，还是这一次拦了以后你自己接不接得住。这些一条条摆出来，原先记在同一个地址上的两次命中，可能就分到两个地址上去了。

可摆出来够不够分得开，仍然是下一次判断的事，不由这张单子先替你保住。

他在那条界上留了东西给人用。他给了三条办法：先躲开更歪的那一头，看清自己往哪边倒然后往反方向拽，处处防着快活。这三条一条也不给你刻度，可它们是能上手的：你今天就能照着做一件。

这边在那条界上留的是四种落法。

四种落法定的是这一判怎么入账。它没有从入账的形式里长出一条上手的办法来。

这一句不是说上游漏写了一样本该写的东西。那张表本来就是定入账形式的，它没有许诺产出行动上的办法。是这一篇拿他那三条量过来，量出的一处类型差，而这一量是本篇自己下的判。

也不是说这边欠一套应当。这边不出应当，这一条写在门口。差的不是应当，是上手。

而中道那件事，恰恰是要下手的。孩子这会儿正闹着。

他那三条办法本身也不保证打中。他自己写着：第二条水路，在祸害里头挑小的。第二条水路的意思是风停了，划桨走，比不上顺风。

所以他给的不是一把尺，是三个方向感。

而方向感把你带到那一处，最后那一下还得由觉察来判。三条办法帮你走近个案，走不完个案。

他在那一处站住了，还回头递了三样东西出来。

这边站在同一处，手里拿着一张定入账的表。

At the opening of Book II, chapter 9, he closes the preceding discussion: virtue of character is a mean, and how it is a mean, between two faults, one of excess and one of deficiency, has now been said sufficiently.

Having closed it, he does not push the argument further. He does something relatively rare in this book: he offers three ways to get started.

Before offering them, he says: being good, then, is hard work.

He is quite concrete. Finding the middle in anything is hard work, just as not everyone can find the center of a circle. It takes someone who knows.

The next sentence is brisk:

Anyone can get angry, and it is easy. So too with giving and spending money. But at whom, how much, when, for what, and in what way: that is not within everyone’s reach, nor is it easy. That is why doing it well is rare, praiseworthy, and noble.

None of those five things is a scale. They merely divide “how angry should one be?” into five finer questions, each as difficult as before.

Then come the three ways.

First: keep clear of the more crooked extreme. He quotes a line attributed to Calypso: steer the ship clear of this smoke and surge. One of the two extremes is always more wrong than the other. Since hitting the exact middle is exceedingly hard, take the proverbial second-best voyage and choose the lesser evil.

Second: recognize which way you lean, then pull yourself the other way. How do you discover your lean? Notice what delights you and what pains you. Pull hard in the opposite direction, a little further, and you are more likely to land in the middle. He compares it to the work of people straightening bent wood.

Third: everywhere be on guard against pleasure. We are not unbribed judges of pleasure. We should feel toward it as the Trojan elders felt when they saw Helen, and repeat their words to it. By sending her away in that fashion, we will go less wrong.

These three ways share a shape: none gives you a ruler to measure with and then obey the number. The first explicitly compares which extreme is more wrong, so they are not craft movements without reasons. They are three usable ways forward, not a formula that automatically calculates the midpoint.

The closing passage marks the limit he gives these ways himself.

This may be hard, he says, especially in particular cases. It is not easy to determine how to be angry, at whom, for what, and for how long.

He even offers evidence from our own speech. Sometimes we praise people who fall short and call them gentle; sometimes we call those who are fierce manly.

Then he traces the boundary of blame. A slight deviation is not blamed, whichever direction it takes. A large deviation is, because it cannot go unnoticed.

Then comes the chapter’s real sentence:

But at what point, and by how much, a person becomes blameworthy is not easy to draw out in an account. Nothing that depends on perception is easy to determine in this way. These things lie in particular cases, and the judgment rests on perception.

He is not speaking only of anger. Anger is his example, and he leaves it behind when it has served its purpose. What he writes next concerns everything that depends on perception. Not a word of that claim remains confined to anger.

So this is no small note about a particular subject. It marks the boundary of his whole account of the mean: the method can bring you to that place, but it cannot take you inside it.

After marking the boundary, his last sentence still draws matters together. This much, at least, is clear: the middle state is praiseworthy in everything. We must sometimes incline toward excess and sometimes toward deficiency, since this is the easiest way to hit the middle and the good.

He supplies no universal scale for the line, but he does not discard the mean because no universal scale can be supplied. The middle and the good remain where they are. How they fall in a particular case must be judged through perception of that case.

He has not said that the case itself has no boundary. He says that a universal account cannot measure out every particular case.

This side can stand where he stands.

Neither side accepts the claim that a universal formula has already drawn the line for every particular case.

This side rejects it through a distinction that must first be set out before it can be seen.

A boundary is drawn in a field. Each thing in the field receives a judgment, and there are only four ways for it to fall: on this side, on that side, on both sides, or on neither. These are forms of the judgment’s result, not four truth values of the thing itself.

The third cell is what this essay needs: falling on both sides.

Its status matters. By itself it is simply one result, not an error waiting to be cleared away. Only in the branch that accepts the rule “both sides may not hit at the same address” does it carry a burden: go on distinguishing the conditions. And this side registers that rule as replaceable. Once it is replaced, the cell is a legitimate result, with no promise that it will split apart.

Everyday comparisons use this too, without naming it.

Something counts as both late and not late because “at what moment does arrival count?” has not yet been settled. This does not mean the event has no boundary, nor that someone has misperceived it. At that point, either define “arrival,” or allow this cell to remain entered as it is.

Another cell on the form contains a dash. That is the fourth cell: falling on neither side. Writing “none” is a judgment; drawing a dash says that judgment does not apply here. This is not the same as the third cell. They are placed together so they will not be confused.

But how the line can finally be drawn leads the two sides to different next steps.

He gives the completion of the particular case to perception. These matters lie in particular cases, and particulars require perception. That is as far as this chapter goes. Why that perception is connected with experience and the practically wise person is supplied only in Book VI.

The next step on this side is not to find a person with a good eye. It is to unfold another part of this judgment’s address: what it faces, at what level it speaks, which premises it accepts, and what remains unsettled.

Unfolding the address does not judge for you. Deciding how far is enough is still a judgment.

He gives the last move to a person’s eye; this side gives it to the next judgment. And the next judgment is still a judgment.

Anyone who has cared for children knows the difference between these two steps.

How much fussing calls for intervention, and how much should be left alone? There is no ready line for that. An experienced person really can see it. Ask what they saw it by, though, and they may not be able to say. Only: this time is different.

His side has a positive account of that inability to say: the judgment rests on perception, and the perception belongs to that person. (This chapter goes only as far as perception; its connection with experience is written in Book VI.)

This side heads the other way. What is different this time? Did the child sleep too little today? Is the setting different? Or, if you intervene now, can you handle what follows? Set these out one by one, and two hits previously entered at the same address may separate into two addresses.

Whether setting them out is enough to separate them remains for the next judgment. The list cannot guarantee it in advance.

At that boundary he leaves something people can use. He gives three ways forward: first avoid the more crooked extreme; see which way you lean and pull the other way; everywhere guard against pleasure. None supplies a scale, but each is usable. You could follow one of them today.

At that boundary, this side leaves four possible results.

The four results determine how a judgment is entered in the account. No practical way to begin has grown out of the form of entry itself.

This does not mean the earlier account omitted something it ought to have written. The table is for defining forms of entry. It never promised to produce practical methods. It is this essay, measuring it against his three ways, that finds a difference of type. That comparison is the present essay’s own judgment.

Nor does this mean this side owes us a set of oughts. It produces no oughts; that is written at the entrance. What differs is not obligation but getting started.

And the mean is precisely something one has to act on. The child is making a fuss right now.

His three ways do not themselves guarantee a hit either. He writes it himself: take the second-best voyage, choose the lesser evil. A second-best voyage means rowing when the wind has stopped. It is not as good as sailing with a fair wind.

So what he gives is not a ruler, but three senses of direction.

Direction brings you to the place; the last move must still be judged by perception. The three ways help you approach the particular case. They do not finish the case for you.

He stops there, and turns back to hand out three things.

This side stands at the same place, holding a form that determines how the account is entered.
