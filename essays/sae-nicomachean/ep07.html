能不能自己对自己不正义

Can One Be Unjust to Oneself?

Han Qin (秦汉)

第五卷第九章，他一口气摆了一串难处，开头就是"有人会问"。

头一个他引的是欧里庇得斯，而且他说那句话说得古怪：

我杀了我母亲，话就这么短。她愿意我也愿意，还是她不愿意我也不愿意。

引完他问：一个人到底能不能自愿地被人不正义地对待。做不正义的事全是自愿的，那么受不正义的事呢，是全不自愿，还是有的自愿有的不自愿。

接着他往"被正义地对待"那一头也推了一遍，说要是那也全是自愿的，就说不过去，因为有些人明明是不情愿地被正义地对待的。

再往下他又起了一道：一个受了那件不正义的事的人，是不是就一定算被不正义地对待了。还是说受这一头也跟做那一头一样，可以只是碰上，不是那么回事。

这一章就是这么一串往下滚的，一道接一道，都是他自己起的。而其中一道他点了名。

这也是那些难处里的一个：一个人能不能对自己不正义。

它是这么冒出来的。要是所谓做不正义的事就是明知故犯地害一个人，而明知故犯的意思是知道对谁，拿什么，怎么做；而不能自制的人正是明知故犯地在害自己，那么他就是自愿地被不正义地对待了，而且等于自己对自己做了不正义的事。

第十一章他回来答。

开口第一句就是：这一问从前面说过的那些已经明白了。所以整章是答，不是问。

他的答不是一句，是几层。

第一层是法。有些正义的事是法照着整个德性安排下来的。比方法不叫人自杀，而法不叫做的事，就是它禁止的事。

再往下一步：凡是违着法明知故犯地害人而对方没有先害他的，就是做了不正义的事；而明知故犯就是知道对谁，拿什么。那么一个因为一时火气把自己抹了脖子的人，是明知故犯地做了正当道理不许的事，而法不许这么做。所以他做了不正义的事。

然后是这一章里最好看的三句。

那么他对谁不正义了呢。是对城邦，不是对他自己吧。因为他是自愿受的，而没有人是自愿被不正义地对待的。

所以城邦也罚他。自毁的人身上还带着一份不名誉，就当他是对城邦做了不正义的事。

第二层更硬，是笔算术。就一个人只是不正义而还不到整个人下作的那一层说，他没法对自己不正义。因为那样一来，同一样东西就得同时从同一个人身上被拿走，又被加到同一个人身上，而这不可能。所以正义和不正义总得落在一个人以上。

第三层是数具体的事。没有那些一件一件的不正义事，谁也做不成不正义的事；而没有人跟自己的妻子通奸，没有人挖自己家的墙，没有人偷自己的东西。

这三层下完，"自己对自己不正义"在严格的意思上已经不成立了。

可他没有把这一问一脚踢开。

他留了一条，而且留得很仔细：

按比方说，按相似说，是有一种正义的。不是一个人对他自己的那种，是他身上某些部分之间的那种。而且不是随便哪一种正义，是主人对奴仆那一种，或者当家的那一种。

为什么是这两种。因为在这些说法里，灵魂中讲理的那一部分跟不讲理的那一部分是分开站着的。人们正是看着这个，才觉得有一种对自己的不正义：因为在这些部分里头，确实可能有一部分受着违背它自己想望的事。

所以就像管的和被管的之间有一种正义，这两部分之间也有一种。

他没有说"其实是有的"，也没有说"根本没有"。他做的是：把一个严格要求两个人的说法，往一个人里头移；移进去以后，他明说那只剩比方。

而这一个动作，这边也做过。

《第一批判》登记公设那一章里，第六条叫反身，写的是：否定能以自身的施行者和产物为对象，工具和对象可以重合。

所以"移得进去"这件事，两边都认。他认，因为灵魂里本来就分着几部分；这边认，因为那把工具本来就转得过来。

而要说准的是这边认下的到底是哪一件。这边没有说过"追问这件事本来要两个人"。说过的是另一句，在讲幻相那一章里：自问担保不了发现自己的全部盲区，所以他者的追问提供一种自问自己拿不到的独立校验。

要一个不同的位置，跟要两个人，不是同一句话。

移进去以后剩下的是什么，两边不一样。

他剩下的是比方。严格说不成立，打个比方说得通。名分变了：那不再是正义，那是像正义。

这边剩下的不是比方，是一样担保不住的东西。动作照旧成立，只是它拿不到那个不同的位置。

差别就在这儿。他说移进去以后那个关系换了名分；这边说移进去以后那个动作还是同一个动作，只是它做不到它在有两个位置的时候做得到的那一件事。

一个是名分变了，一个是效力短了。

而两边收手的地方是同一处，收手的理由不是同一个。

他的理由是关系的语法。拿走和加上不能同时落在同一个人身上；受和作，多得和少得，这一套要两方才摆得开。前面那三层他一层一层数过了，末了压成一句：正义和不正义总得落在一个人以上。

这边的理由是位置。不是一个人身上不能分成两部分，是那两部分共用着同一套看不见的地方。你转过身来查自己，转的还是你那双眼睛。

他那一头短的是人数，这边这一头短的是位置。

自己校自己写的东西，是这件事最平常的样子。

一段话你写完读三遍，那个错字还在。不是你不认真，是你读的不是纸上写的，是你以为你写的。换个人来，一眼就看见了。

这一处两件事同时成立：你确实在校，这是真的，工具转得过来；而你确实校不出那一个，这也是真的。动作成立，效力短一截。

另一头那句话也很平常，只是它成立的方式是打比方。

一个人说好了不再拖，结果又拖了，事后会说一句"我对不起自己"。

这句话说得通，听的人也懂。可它说得通的方式，正是他讲的那一种：你没法真的告自己，也没法真的赔自己。那句话里的"我"和"自己"，是拿主人和奴仆那个样子借来的两方。

而一问"那份校验为什么短"，事情就不再只停在效力上了。

这边登记的是一个上限：自问担保不了发现全部盲区。上限是一个结果。它没有说这个结果是怎么来的。

来的路子是明摆着可问的：校验的那一头和被校验的那一头，共用了哪些东西。共用的是同一批前提，还是同一套地址，还是同一条取信息的路。共用得越多，那份校验就越短；而共用到什么程度，这边一个字也没写。

这一问得写清是谁在问。它不是说上游本该给出一套内部的两方。反身那一条许的只是工具和对象可以重合，它没有许诺把一个人拆成动手的那一份，受着的那一份，看不见的那一份，也不欠这一套。是这一篇看着他那张图，问出了这边这一处。

而他那张图确实是一张图。

讲理的那一部分，不讲理的那一部分，主人和奴仆的那个样子，一部分受着违背它自己想望的事。这些拼起来，是一张能指得出"哪一头短在哪儿"的关系图。图对不对是另一回事，灵魂分成几部分是他自己那一套，这边不接。

可这边手上现在只有一个上限，还没有一张图。

上限告诉你走不到哪儿。图告诉你为什么走不到。

Why Self-Injustice Sounds Obvious

Ordinary speech has no difficulty with self-injustice. A person wastes a talent, remains in an abusive situation, betrays a long-held commitment, or destroys a body through addiction. We say, “You are being unfair to yourself.” The phrase appears to describe a familiar moral fact.

Aristotle resists it. In Book V, justice is relational. Injustice in the strict sense involves one person receiving more and another less, or one party acting against another contrary to law and equality. The roles of doer and sufferer cannot simply collapse into one.

His sharpest case is suicide. A person who kills himself in anger acts contrary to right reason and does what the law does not permit. But whom does he wrong? Aristotle's answer is the city, not himself. The act violates a public relation. It is not an instance of one citizen unjustly taking from another citizen when both are the same person.

Likewise, a person cannot literally commit adultery with his own spouse against himself or steal his own property from himself. The grammar of strict justice requires parties.

The Return of Justice by Analogy

Aristotle does not deny inner conflict. He allows a metaphorical or analogical justice within the soul, between the rational and nonrational parts, resembling the justice between ruler and ruled. The name returns after its status has changed.

That move is methodologically exact. Instead of stretching “justice” until every harmful relation fits, Aristotle marks the point at which the concept becomes an analogy. The inner case resembles political relation but is not identical to it. One person is not secretly two legal subjects.

The distinction helps with contemporary language. “I violated my own boundary” can name something real, but the boundary did not function as an agreement between independent parties. The same person set it, crossed it, interpreted the crossing, and may now rewrite its meaning. The lack of separation changes what verification is possible.

Reflexivity Is Not Independence

SAE permits a judgment to turn on itself. A subject can examine its own premises, revise a prior registration, and become the object of its own claim. That reflexivity is essential. Yet self-application should not be confused with external review.

The same apparatus that produced a blind spot cannot guarantee that it has found the whole blind spot. When I audit my own action, I supply the evidence, define the address, decide which objections matter, and issue the revised account. I may do all of this conscientiously. I still do not occupy a noncoincident perspective.

Aristotle changes the conceptual status: strict justice becomes justice by analogy. SAE changes the practical expectation: the operation of review can be the same, but its evidential force is weaker when reviewer and reviewed position coincide. Both preserve the inner case while refusing to pretend that nothing has changed.

Shared Premises and Manufactured Opponents

Self-criticism often feels rigorous because it stages two voices. One part prosecutes; another defends. But the voices may share the premise that most needs examination. A perfectionist can accuse herself of laziness and defend herself by listing hours worked while both sides assume that worth depends on output. The debate is lively and the ground remains untouched.

This is why “be your own harshest critic” is unreliable advice. Harshness is not difference. A cruel internal judge may be less capable of finding a blind spot than a generous friend, because cruelty and defense are organized around the same inherited terms.

SAE can model the problem as overlapping addresses. If claimant, respondent, and reviewer all inherit the same unexamined premise, procedural multiplicity does not create epistemic independence. What is missing is not another voice count but another location from which the premise itself can appear.

Aristotle's relational map helps here even if SAE does not adopt his partition of the soul. Justice is not merely a rule applied to content; it is a relation among positions. Change the positions and the name, force, or scope of the judgment may change.

What We Mean When We Say “You Owe Yourself”

The ordinary phrase should not be banned. It can register continuity through time: the present self inherits commitments made by an earlier self, or the future self will bear costs imposed now. Temporal separation supplies something like distinct parties, though not full independence. The phrase can also identify a standard a person accepts but fails to honor.

Still, its moral force is different from what is owed to another person. A promise to myself may be revised by me in a way that a promise to someone else cannot. My later consent does not retroactively settle the other's claim. The relation matters.

Aristotle's caution therefore survives even when his psychology is not adopted. A person can damage herself, conflict with herself, and fail standards she gives herself. Calling all of that “injustice” may illuminate, provided we remember the change of status. The term is doing analogical work.

SAE adds a corresponding caution about audit. Self-review is real and necessary. It is not self-certifying. The procedure can turn inward; the perspective cannot make itself independent merely by changing seats.

Can one be unjust to oneself? In ordinary moral speech, yes. In Aristotle's strict account, not in the same way one is unjust to another. The difference is not pedantry. It tells us why self-forgiveness, self-punishment, and self-correction can become closed circuits—and why another person's answer sometimes matters even when the original harm seems to be entirely one's own.
