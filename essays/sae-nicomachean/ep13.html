知道了为什么还不够

Why Knowing Is Not Enough

Han Qin (秦汉)

书快完了。德性讲完了，友爱讲完了，快乐讲完了，幸福讲完了。

第十卷第九章一开口，他问了一句：

关于这些，关于那些德性，还有友爱和快乐，要是已经说得够了，说了个大概，那么这件事该不该算是做到头了。

这里有一个问号。而后面紧跟着一个"还是"，那一支后头还有第二个问号：

还是像人们说的那样，在做的事情上，终点不是把每一样都看明白弄清楚，而是把它们做出来；关于德性也一样，知道还不够，得试着有它，用它，要么就得有别的什么法子让我们变好。

这两支都还是问句。第二支已经把方向摆了出来，可它还没有论证。论证从下一句才起。

要是那些道理本身足以把人做成好人，它们理当挣到许多大笔的酬劳，照忒奥格尼斯说的；那样的话，弄到那笔酬劳才是要紧的事。

"照忒奥格尼斯说的"这半句，贴的是他刚说完的那几个字，不是泛泛地引他的看法。

可事实上，那些道理看来只有这么点力气：它们能把年轻人里性子疏朗的那一些鼓动起来，能让一副本来就高贵又真心爱好东西的性子，被好东西整个占住；至于多数人，那些道理没有本事把他们劝到好上头去。

为什么。他答得很直：

因为他们生来就不是服羞的，是服怕的；他们不碰坏事，不是因为那事丑，是因为有罚。

他们照着情绪过日子，追自己那一份快活，追能带来这份快活的东西，躲开与之相反的难受；至于什么是好的，什么是真正甜的，他们连个想头都没有，因为他们压根没尝过。

然后是这一章的第三个问号，也是整段的转轴：

这样的人，什么样的道理能把他们重新塑一遍。

他自己接了一句：那些从老早起就长进性子里的东西，靠一句道理挪不动，就算挪得动也不容易。能指望的顶多是，该有的都有了的时候，我们分得着一点德性。

讲完这一段，他把人怎么变好这一问重新摆了一遍，摆成三条路：有人说靠天生，有人说靠习惯，有人说靠教。

书从这儿转弯。三条路怎么处置，往下那一段归另外两篇。

而这一篇停在这儿，不是收得早。是他这一段里本来就还没有法，没有强制，没有立法的人。罚这个字在这儿只以"他们怕的是罚"的样子出现，是在说那些人怎么被推动的；至于该拿什么去推，一个字还没有。

他在这一段里做的只有一件事：一个诊断。

两本书在最后一页做了同一个动作：把自己交出去。

《第一批判》那本书的收尾是这么写的：这部批判至此写毕，随即入册，立于被追问之下，此后照亮它的光来自读它的人。它开篇还写过一句更硬的：写完那一刻起它就立在被追问之下，你翻开它的这一刻起，追问的是你。

它连怎么被查都替人备好了。凡例那一节明写：这几条写作之法录在这儿不是自陈，是武装读者，法在明处，追问才有抓手；读者要是在书里抓到违反此法的地方，那正是这几条法等着的用处。

它还把这件事立成了整本书的建筑：它没有把一次论证当成一张永久的执照，而是把每一条主张的来路和可以换掉的地方都留在明面上，让后来的人随时重审。它自己给这个安排下过一句判词：批判这个名分不由一纸判决书担保，由永不休庭的法庭担保。

它还写过这个法庭为什么散不了：当下的这一问，尚未被问。不闭合的那一处，由他者和后来者接手。

两边都不肯让"讲完了"等于"办完了"。

分开的地方，就在他做完那个诊断之后的下一步。

他诊断完就往外走。对着服怕的那些人讲道理不起作用，所以他去找一样能对着"怕"起作用的东西。找的是什么，怎么找，从下一句起，归另一篇。

这边交出去的时候，手上只有说理。开篇那一句，凡例那一节，收尾那一句，还有那座永不休庭的法庭。每一样都是把话说清楚，把规矩摆到明处，把可以驳的地方先标出来。全是说理。

一个诊断出说理不够，就掉头去找别的；一个把全部指望留在说理上。

羞和怕这一对，带过孩子的人分得出来。

小孩不碰某样东西，可以有两种。一种是他知道那样不好。一种是他知道碰了要挨说。

表面上一模一样，都是没碰。可你一转身，两种就分开了。

而要紧的不是他嫌服怕的那些人不好。他把这件事当成一个关于办法的事实来处理：对着服怕的人讲道理，讲得再对也不起作用，所以得换一样东西。

这边欠他的那一笔，就在这个位置上。

先要撤掉一句容易顺手写下的话：这边没有失败条件。这句话不成立。这边的失败条件写得很清楚：一样东西被搬去用的地方越多，没标地址的越界处就越多；长期缺少外头的复核，那些没结清的东西会一笔垫着一笔往下走。

空着的那一格在别处。

把这边为着被人追问所做的安排点一遍。把规矩写在明处，武装读者。不把一次论证当成永久执照，改成随时可以重审。发问者的身份本身不算凭证，谁问都算，问题的成色由那一问自己的对账结算。而且它明写过：自问担保不了发现自己的全部盲区，所以外头那一道校验是自问替代不了的。承担的人还可以不是一个人，是一伙人依着公开的程序一起作判。

这几样各管各的。有的说清为什么非得有人来，有的把来了以后的手续备齐，有的把门槛压到最低。

没有一样是让人来。

把成本压低和把动机给出，是两件事。这本书把前一件做得很足，后一件一个字没写。

而这边不是漏了，是拆过。这边在讲动力的地方专门拆掉过一样东西：没有哪一片没做完的地方，逼得着你去做下一步。做不做，在做的人。

拆得对。可拆完以后，这本书自己的最后一步，要的正是那个没人保得住的下一步。

这一层在办公室里也见得着，而且不好看。

一份写得很好的东西发出去，没有人回。不是被驳了，是没人回。写的人会当作是自己写得不够好，于是改，改完再发，还是没人回。

问题不一定在那份东西上。它落在了一个没有什么东西保着有人会来读的位置上。

这一本十三篇，每一篇接他亲手摆上桌的一道。摆完了。

照他那一问：这算办完了吗。

按他的答法，讲完了不能算办完。而按他那个分法，这十三篇整个都在"看明白"那一头，一篇也没到"做出来"那一头。它们做成的是把两边并排摆开，让碰得正的地方和岔开的地方都露在外头。

而他那个词还在那儿等着。

自足这个词，他在这本书里最后一次用，是问道理能不能独自把人做成好人。而那是一个假设，被他下一句当场否掉了。

否掉的是"独自"，不是道理。

这一本十三篇，是道理。

两句话摆在一处，谁也不替谁作证。一句说：这本书的效力不由自家宣布，得有人来参与结算。一句说：道理说得再对，也不能独自把人做成好人。

而这一本自足不自足，照它自己那条规矩，不由它自己说。

The book is almost over. Virtue has been discussed, friendship has been discussed, pleasure has been discussed, happiness has been discussed.

At the opening of Book X, chapter 9, he asks:

If enough has been said in outline about these things, about the virtues, friendship, and pleasure, should we count the undertaking complete?

There is a question mark here. Immediately afterward comes an “or,” and a second question mark at the end of that branch:

Or, as people say, is the end in practical matters not to contemplate and understand each thing but to do it, and must we, in the case of virtue too, go beyond knowing to try to possess and use it, or find some other means of becoming good?

Both branches are still questions. The second has already indicated a direction, but it has not argued for it. The argument begins in the next sentence.

If arguments by themselves were enough to make people good, they would rightly earn many great rewards, as Theognis says; then securing those rewards would be the thing to do.

“As Theognis says” attaches to the words he has just used. It is not a general reference to Theognis’s views.

But as things stand, arguments seem to have only this much power: they can encourage the generous among the young, and let a character already noble and truly in love with the good become wholly possessed by it. They cannot persuade the many toward goodness.

Why? His answer is direct:

Because they naturally obey fear, not shame. They refrain from bad things not because those things are ugly, but because there is punishment.

They live by their passions, pursuing their own pleasures and what produces them, avoiding the contrary pains. Of what is noble and truly pleasant they have not even a notion, because they have never tasted it.

Then comes the chapter’s third question mark, the pivot of the passage:

What argument could reshape people like that?

He adds the answer himself. Things that have long grown into a person’s character cannot be moved by an argument, or at least not easily. At best, with everything that ought to be there in place, we may hope to gain some share of virtue.

After this passage he poses the question of becoming good again, now in three routes: some say by nature, some by habit, some by teaching.

The book turns here. How those routes are handled in the following passage belongs to two other essays.

This essay does not stop here because it is ending too soon. There is as yet no law, no compulsion, and no legislator in this passage. Punishment appears only as “what they fear,” describing how those people are moved. Not a word has yet said what should be used to move them.

He does just one thing in this passage: he diagnoses.

On their final pages the two books make the same move: they hand themselves over.

The First Critique ends this way: this critique is now written, immediately entered in the record and placed under questioning. From now on, the light that illuminates it comes from its readers. Its opening was firmer still: from the moment it is finished it stands under questioning; from the moment you open it, the questioner is you.

It even prepares the means of examination. The section on writing conventions states explicitly that these rules are recorded not as self-description but to arm the reader. With the rules in view, questioning has something to grip. If the reader catches a violation in the book, that is precisely the use for which these rules were waiting.

It also makes this the architecture of the whole book. It does not treat one argument as a permanent license. It keeps the grounds of every claim and the points open to replacement visible, so later people can reopen the examination at any time. It has passed its own verdict on this arrangement: the designation “critique” is guaranteed not by one written judgment, but by a court that never adjourns.

It has also written why the court cannot disperse: the present question has not yet been asked. Where closure fails, others and later readers take over.

Neither side will let “finished saying” mean “finished doing.”

They part at his next step after the diagnosis.

Having diagnosed, he moves outward. Reasoning with people who obey fear does not work, so he looks for something that can work on fear. What he looks for, and how, begins with the next sentence and belongs to another essay.

When this side hands itself over, argument is all it holds. The opening sentence, the section on conventions, the closing sentence, and the court that never adjourns: each makes the words clear, brings the rules into view, and marks in advance where objections can be made. All of it is argument.

One diagnoses that argument is insufficient and turns to seek something else. The other leaves its whole hope with argument.

Anyone who has cared for children can distinguish shame from fear.

A child may leave something alone for two reasons. One is knowing that touching it would be wrong. The other is knowing that touching it will bring a scolding.

They look exactly alike: in both cases the child does not touch. Turn your back, and the two separate.

The point is not that he dislikes people who obey fear. He treats it as a fact about methods: however sound the reasoning, it does not work on people moved by fear, so another means is needed.

This side’s debt to him lies at that spot.

First we must withdraw a sentence that is easy to write without thinking: this side has no conditions of failure. That is untrue. Its failure conditions are clear. The more places something is carried into use, the more unaddressed overreaches appear. When outside review is absent for a long time, unsettled items keep propping one another up as they proceed.

The empty cell is elsewhere.

Count the arrangements this side makes to be questioned. The rules are visible, arming the reader. No argument is a permanent license; each can be reopened at any time. The questioner’s identity is not a credential: anyone may ask, and the question’s quality is settled by checking its own account. It also states that self-questioning cannot guarantee discovery of all one’s blind spots, so the outside check cannot be replaced by questioning oneself. The bearer need not be one person either; it can be a group judging together through public procedures.

Each of these handles its own task. Some explain why someone must come; some prepare the procedures for when they do; some lower the threshold as far as possible.

None makes someone come.

Lowering the cost and providing the motive are two different things. This book does a great deal of the first. Of the second it writes not a word.

And this was not an omission; this side has taken the matter apart. In discussing motive, it explicitly dismantled one thing: no unfinished area can compel you to take the next step. Whether to act belongs to the one who acts.

That dismantling is right. But afterward the book’s own last step needs precisely the next step that nobody can guarantee.

This layer is visible in offices too, and it is not pretty.

A very well-written document goes out. Nobody replies. It has not been refuted; nobody replies. The writer takes this to mean it was not good enough, revises, sends it again, and still nobody replies.

The problem need not be in the document. It has landed somewhere with nothing ensuring that anyone will come to read it.

Thirteen essays in this book, each taking up a difficulty he placed on the table himself. They have all been laid out.

By his question: does that count as done?

By his answer, finishing the account does not count as finishing the work. By his distinction, all thirteen essays stand on the side of “seeing clearly,” none on the side of “doing.” What they have accomplished is to set the two sides alongside each other, exposing where they meet squarely and where they diverge.

And his word is still waiting there.

The final time he uses “self-sufficient” in this book is to ask whether arguments can make people good by themselves. It is a supposition he rejects in the very next sentence.

What he rejects is “by themselves,” not arguments.

The thirteen essays in this book are arguments.

Put the two sentences beside each other, and neither proves the other. One says this book cannot declare its own efficacy: someone must come and participate in settling the account. The other says that however sound the arguments, they cannot make people good by themselves.

Whether this book is self-sufficient, by its own rule, is not for it to say.
