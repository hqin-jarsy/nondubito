失踪的音乐：软件如何成为余项

Missing Music: How Software Becomes Remainder

Han Qin (秦汉)

Suren Seneviratne（艺名 My Panda Shall Fly），英国声音艺术家，多年来持续追踪一类特殊的软件：曾经运行、曾经产生声音、而今在时间与平台迁移中悄然失踪的音乐工具。他的项目 Missing Music 积累了 250 余条档案，记录着 Turbosynth、Argeïphontes Lyre、Th0nk 等为 Macintosh 系统写成的程序——它们随苹果从 PowerPC 和 Mac OS 9 的退出而陷入无法运行的困境，既未被博物馆收藏，也未被历史书写，只是消失了。然而 Suren 不止于记录：他购买二手 iBook 和 PowerBook，在伦敦 Café OTO 等演出场所，以这些旧机器为乐器进行实时即兴表演，从仍能运行的旧软件中榨取此前从未产生过的新声音。2026 年 7 月 31 日，Cycling '74 与他合作，将四枚经典 Max patch 升级为当代 Max 9 兼容版本并免费发布：Leafcutter John 的 CDMF（CD Mini F**k）、jasch 的 erratica、jhno 的 looper、Lewis Keller 的 punkin300_2026。

在 SAE 的凿构循环框架里，每一块余项（余项）最终都会被命名，并固化为已构。"计算机音乐"是已构：从 Stockhausen 的早期电子声学实验，到 Max/MSP 在学术机构中的规范化，这条谱系有名字、有历史、有制度。1990—2010 年代的笔记本电脑音乐也是已构：IDM、microsound、glitch——类别是清晰的，重要人物有记录，评论话语已然成形。但 Suren 的实践落在了这些已构的夹缝之中。他的 Missing Music 不是保存（preservation）：他不把软件归入档案库便了事，他在演出。他的工作不是怀旧（nostalgia）：旧机器产生的是以前从未产生过的声音，而非过去作品的复现。他的路径不是考古学（archaeology）：没有"还原历史真相"的叙事框架，只有工具与此刻的直接接触。他的输出不是历史再现（recreation）：2026 年在 Margate Electronics 发行的卡带 4.0，是完全意义上的新音乐。这个夹缝——其实践所在但无法被命名之处——正是 Missing Music 作为余项的结构所在。

最能将这一结构具体化的，是 Leafcutter John 在 2000 年代初建造的 CDMF（CD Mini F**k）。这个 patch 的逻辑极其简单：四个粒子合成引擎，随机从一个音频文件夹中抓取片段，随机化播放位置、音高与速度。John 最初的念头是制造一台"自动混音机器"——不是产生混音作品，而是将混音这个姿态本身自动化。后来他更进一步：为什么只限于一首曲子？可以将整张专辑压缩成一个混音。这个逻辑不是粒子合成（granular synthesis）在学术意义上的含义，后者强调的是设计过的材料与有意识的手势控制。它不是 AI 生成：没有模型，没有训练集，没有语义推断层。它不是混音（remix）：没有任何保留原始作品身份的意图。它产生的声音——某张专辑在随机速度和随机位置被无数次中断、截取、叠置——至今占据着一个没有名字的地带。2026 年 CDMF 被重写为 gen~ 版本，艺术家 littlescale 随即用它驱动了 35 台联网电脑，整个机房成为一件可被指挥的乐器，erratica patch 在 34 台机器上同时运行，第 35 台通过 OSC 控制其他所有机器的参数。使用者描述它产生的声音"既熟悉又陌生，带有一种通过混沌产生的有机变化感"——这是余项尚未固化时才有的触感。

为什么是现在？因为这个余项四周的领土正在被快速封闭。生成式 AI 音乐（Suno、Udio 等平台）正在占领"生成音乐"这个词本身：生成音乐 = 文字提示 → 音频模型 → 输出。一旦这个等式完全固化，没有 AI 介入的程序化与随机音乐生成将只剩两种可读的位置：历史计算机音乐（博物馆意义上的），或电子音乐创作（制作意义上的）。CDMF 所在的地带——工具驱动的、无意图的、基于随机材料的生成——将找不到立足点。从另一侧，"计算机音乐保存"也正在形成自己的已构话语（媒体考古学、数字人文等），一旦这个话语成熟，Suren 的实践将被归入其中，其演出性、即兴性、生产新声音的能量将被压平。现在，这些 patch 刚刚重新进入当代 Max 环境，正在被新一代艺术家（littlescale、Alex Roldan 等）使用，还没有被命名。Cycling '74 的文章在描述这个项目时绕圈子，找不到合适的类别词。Peter Kirn 在 CDM 上写道"一切旧的又是新的"——这是余项感受到命名压力但名字尚未落地时特有的说法。这是感知这个余项的最后窗口。

Missing Music 的档案在 missingmusic.medium.com；Suren 作为 My Panda Shall Fly 的音乐发布在 missingmusic.bandcamp.com；patch 的免费下载通过 Cycling '74 的文章"Missing Music Modernization"提供。如果你现在下载 CDMF，放入一张专辑，听那些被随机截取和随机变速的碎片——你会听到一种从未被命名的东西。这就是余项之美：不是理论上的存在，是可以打开的文件，是此刻仍在生长的逻辑。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

Suren Seneviratne, performing as My Panda Shall Fly, is a London-based sound artist who has spent years tracking a specific category of software: music tools that ran, produced sounds, and then vanished—not through critical failure but through platform migration. His project Missing Music catalogs over 250 programs—VST plugins, Max/MSP patches, and Macintosh-specific audio tools from the 1990s and 2000s—that became inaccessible when Apple moved away from PowerPC and Mac OS 9. They were never archived by institutions, never entered the histories, they simply disappeared. But Suren doesn't only document. He performs: buying secondhand iBooks and PowerBooks, he plays these old machines live at venues like London's Café OTO, generating new improvised sounds from still-functional obsolete software. On July 31, 2026, Cycling '74 collaborated with him to release modernized versions of four beloved Max patches for current Max 9 as free downloads: Leafcutter John's CDMF (CD Mini F**k), jasch's erratica, jhno's looper, and Lewis Keller's punkin300_2026. The accompanying CDM feature is the most thorough public accounting of the project to date.

In SAE's chisel-construct framework, every remainder eventually gets named and hardens into construct. "Computer music" is a settled construct: from Stockhausen's early electronic studies to the institutionalization of Max/MSP in academic music departments, the lineage is clear, named, and institutionalized. Laptop music from 1990 to 2010 is a construct: IDM, microsound, glitch—the categories exist, the major figures are documented, the critical discourse has formed. But Suren's practice falls precisely into the gap between these settled territories. Missing Music is not preservation: he doesn't archive and shelve, he performs. It's not nostalgia: the old machines generate sounds they never generated before, not reenactments of past works. It's not archaeology: there's no "recovering the lost historical truth" narrative structure—only the direct contact between tool and now. The output is not recreation: his 2026 cassette 4.0 (on Margate Electronics) is genuinely new music. This gap—the exact shape of what his practice is but cannot be named—is where the remainder lives.

The patch that makes this most concrete is Leafcutter John's CDMF (CD Mini F**k), built in the early 2000s and rebuilt for gen~ in 2026. Its logic is disarmingly simple: four granular synthesis engines that randomly grab audio chunks from a folder, randomizing playback position, pitch, and speed on each grab. John originally conceived it as an "automatic remix machine"—not a remix work, but the automation of the remix gesture itself. He then extended the thought: why stop at one track? You could distill an entire album into a single remix. This logic is not granular synthesis in the academic sense (which emphasizes designed materials and controlled gesture). It is not AI generation (no model, no training corpus, no semantic inference layer). It is not remix (no intent to preserve the original's identity). The sounds it produces—an album repeatedly interrupted, grabbed, and restacked at random speeds and positions—occupy a territory that still has no name. When artist littlescale ran 35 networked computers as an orchestra of erratica patches—34 instances running simultaneously, a 35th conducting via OSC—the result was described as "both familiar and alien, with this strong sense of organic change through chaos." That feeling—familiar-but-unnameable—is what remainder feels like before it hardens into construct.

Why now? Because the surrounding territory is closing fast. AI music generation (Suno, Udio) is absorbing the word "generative": generative music now defaults to text prompt → audio model → output. Once that equation fully hardens, programmatic and random music generation without AI will have only two legible readings: historical computer music (museum), or electronic music composition (production). The zone CDMF inhabits—tool-driven, intentionless, random-material generation—will lose its footing. From the other side, "computer music preservation" is forming its own construct discourse (media archaeology, digital humanities), and once that discourse matures, Suren's practice will be absorbed into it, its performativity and generative aliveness flattened into archive. Right now, these patches have just re-entered the contemporary Max environment, are being picked up by a new generation of artists, and have not yet been named. The Cycling '74 article circles around without landing on a category. Peter Kirn on CDM writes "everything old is new again"—which is precisely what a remainder sounds like when the name is felt but hasn't landed. This is the last window for perceiving it as remainder.

The Missing Music archive is at missingmusic.medium.com; Suren's music as My Panda Shall Fly is at missingmusic.bandcamp.com; the patch downloads are available through Cycling '74's "Missing Music Modernization" article. If you download CDMF now, drop in an album, and listen to the randomly grabbed and randomly pitched fragments—you will hear something that has never been named. That is the beauty of the remainder: not a theoretical existence, but a file you can open, a logic still growing.
