把自己翻过来的球

A Sphere That Turns Itself Inside Out

Han Qin (秦汉)

这是剑桥大学应用数学与理论物理系 Raymond Goldstein 组的一项观察，第一作者是 Stephanie Höhn，合作者包括 Aurelia Honerkamp-Smith、Pierre Haas 和 Philipp Khuc Trong，2015 年 4 月 27 日发表于《物理评论快报》第 114 卷，文章编号 178101。研究对象是团藻（Volvox）的胚胎：一个中空的细胞球，要把自己整个翻过来，里面朝外，外面朝内。他们用光片显微镜，第一次拍下这个过程的活体三维影像。整个翻转约一小时：球先在中部向内凹陷，成为两个半球，一个半球被收进另一个里面，顶部的开口慢慢撑大，外层半球从内层半球上滑过去，最后回到球形。

他们不只是拍下来。论文开头说的是：球形胚胎翻转自己，据称“仅由细胞形状的变化”引起。他们把细胞形状变化当作一张弹性壳的内禀曲率变化来建模，然后问：只靠这个，够不够？答案是：一个半球要主动收缩，另一个半球要主动伸展，而且两者要在时间和空间上按次序配合。剑桥的新闻稿引用 Goldstein 的话，说此前没有人能给出定量的力学理解，回答这些形状变化是否足以解释胚胎的形状。

这是第七条光线（繁殖之美）的生成之美，几乎就是该条目自己举的例子：“一个小球把自己翻过来”。这一条光线的句子是：各部分不同，各在其位，合起来才是它；它会死，但它的模式不随它死去，整个它从一个细胞里重新长出来。成熟的团藻有大约两千个带鞭毛的小细胞负责划水，还有大约十几个大的生殖细胞；母体的划水细胞一起死去，而每个生殖细胞已经分裂成一个完整的新球，翻转之后离开母体。翻转正是这个“重新长出来”的一步：一层薄薄的、没有任何外部模具的细胞片，让不同区域各自领到不同的角色（这边收缩，那边伸展），整个形状于是被翻了过来。判断力在这里不能不承认的是：这不是被外面压成的，也不是一个细胞的简单分裂；差异先成了分工，分工又成了一个形状。它也不只是“有用”或“聪明”：就算没有人关心团藻，一层薄壳靠自己各处不同的动作把自己翻过来，这件事本身站得住。

需要说清它不是什么。它不是对生命力的赞美，也不是“进化设计得多巧”之类的解释；这篇论文的力量恰恰在于，它把这件事收到可以被检验的力学语言里，同时没有让力学吞掉事件本身。在这里，机制与美不是同一个维度，前者越清楚，后者越不被削弱。

它的余项也写在论文里。这个模型说明细胞形状变化可以够用，但活体里这些主动收缩与伸展是怎样被协调的，各个半球的变化究竟来自凹陷区域内部的力，还是来自别处，仍是开放的；作者也提到，这套方法或许可以延伸去看动物的神经胚形成等过程。另一层余项在第七条光线本身：每一代重新长出来的，并不完全相同。这个翻转的一小时，正是模式再一次从零开始、而且不被外面完全决定的那一小时。

现在值得看，是因为这是一个可以直接指给人看的、活的、一小时的事件：一个球，没有手，没有模具，把自己翻过来。看过它，就很难再把“形状”只当作结果了。原始论文：

Höhn et al., Physical Review Letters 114, 178101 (2015) ↗

This is an observation from Raymond Goldstein's group in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics at Cambridge. The first author is Stephanie Höhn, with Aurelia Honerkamp-Smith, Pierre Haas and Philipp Khuc Trong, published 27 April 2015 in Physical Review Letters, volume 114, article 178101. The subject is the embryo of Volvox: a hollow ball of cells that has to turn itself completely inside out. Using light-sheet microscopy, they recorded the first live three-dimensional images of the process. The inversion takes about an hour: the sphere folds inward around its middle into two hemispheres, one hemisphere moves inside the other, an opening at the top widens, and the outer hemisphere glides over the inner one until the sphere is restored.

They did not stop at filming. The paper opens by saying that spherical embryos turn themselves inside out through a process hypothesized to arise from cell shape changes alone. They modelled those shape changes as changes of intrinsic curvature in an elastic shell, and asked: is that enough? The answer: one hemisphere must actively contract and the other actively expand, in a coordinated pattern in time and space. In the Cambridge press release Goldstein says that until then there was no quantitative mechanical understanding of whether those cell shape changes were sufficient.

This is the generative face of the seventh ray, the Beauty of Reproduction, and nearly the ray's own example: a small sphere turning itself inside out. The ray's sentence runs: its parts differ, each in its own place, and only together are it; it dies, but its pattern does not die with it, and the whole of it grows again from a single cell. A mature Volvox has about two thousand small flagellated cells that row, and about a dozen large germ cells. The rowers die together, but each germ cell has already divided into a complete new sphere, which inverts and leaves the mother. The inversion is exactly the step of growing again: a thin sheet of cells with no outside mould, whose regions take up different roles (this side contracts, that side stretches), so that the whole shape is turned over. What a judge cannot not acknowledge here is that this was not pressed into shape from outside, and is not one cell simply splitting in two: difference became division of labour, and division of labour became a form. Nor is it merely useful or clever. If no one cared about Volvox, a thin shell turning itself over through the differing actions of its own parts would still stand.

It is worth saying what this is not. It is not praise of vital force, nor of how cleverly evolution designed it. The strength of the paper is that it brings the event into mechanics that can be tested, without letting the mechanics swallow the event. Mechanism and beauty are not the same dimension here; the clearer the first becomes, the less the second is diminished.

The remainder is also stated in the paper's own terms. The model shows that cell shape changes can suffice, but how the active contraction and expansion are coordinated in the living embryo, and whether the hemisphere changes come from forces inside the invaginating region or from elsewhere, is still open; the authors also suggest the approach may extend to processes such as neurulation in animals. A second remainder belongs to the ray itself: what grows again each generation is not entirely the same. That hour of inversion is the hour in which the pattern starts from nothing once more, and is not fully fixed by what is outside it.

It is worth seeing now because it can be pointed to directly: a living event of one hour, a ball with no hands and no mould that turns itself over. After seeing it, it is hard to treat shape as only a result. The original paper:
