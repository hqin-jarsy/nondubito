The Note Has Not Sounded. You Are Already Waiting.

A note next door, a pharmacy around the corner: memory does not only look backward. It is also in the half-step not yet taken.

Han Qin · 2026-09-28

In this essay

Someone is practicing the piano next door. You are sorting papers at a table.

At first you are not listening closely. The piece is familiar from childhood, although you have not thought about it for years. Sound comes through the wall, phrase by phrase, without interrupting your hands.

Then a hand stops.

That is not the note that belongs there.

What is peculiar is that the note you expected has not sounded. A different one is in the room. Something absent has nevertheless been definite enough to make what is present seem wrong.

Next door, the player pauses and begins again from the preceding phrase. This time the music reaches the place you remember. You return to the papers.

A small incident. Yet in that interruption, years ago and a moment still to come have met.

Not an old photograph on the wall

Store a recording on a phone, and the file remains when nobody plays it. It does not wait for the next note. It may preserve a performance, but it does not turn in surprise when another performance diverges.

Something different happened at the table. What you once heard is doing work in the present.

This persistence is not quite an unaltered recording inside the head. Asked to hum the whole piece immediately, you might lose a phrase or take it at a different pace. You might check the score and discover that even the note you felt most certain about belongs to another version.

Remembering does not guarantee remembering correctly.

The interruption was real nonetheless. You brought something learned through earlier listening into this moment. Recalling it was not merely retrieving an untouched object from storage. You took it up again: to recognize, to anticipate and, if necessary, to put it differently in relation to what you now heard.

There is no need to rush to the opposite conclusion and declare all memory untrustworthy. Parts of the tune can be checked. Familiar melodies can indeed be continued. Not being an exact preserved copy is a long way from having no basis at all.

Your feet slow before the corner

Elsewhere, the way memory reaches forward becomes easier to follow.

Years later, you return to a street where you used to live. Before reaching the corner you slow down. There should be a little breakfast place beyond it. The doorway is narrow; people used to emerge carrying bowls, and you often had to make room. Today, before the doorway is visible, your body has already left it space.

You turn. There is a pharmacy.

Perhaps you feel disappointed. Perhaps you find it amusing that the shop has changed while your knowledge has remained behind. Set the feeling aside briefly. Something quite specific is happening too. The remembered street has extended beyond the reach of your eyes. That extension has encountered the street as it is today.

When you next think of the corner, the breakfast place need not have vanished. It can remain under “there used to be.” The pharmacy enters the present; the old shop acquires a more definite place in the past. Revision is not simply deleting one picture. It can be a rearrangement of what belongs where.

Your feet may still slow out of habit on another visit. But you need not demand that the pharmacy become the breakfast place again before conceding that the street is there.

Expectation, then, need not be a solemn prediction. Sometimes it is a slowing step, a hand reaching toward a familiar switch, or half a melody already inviting its continuation.

The past has not stayed entirely in the past. It helps arrange our reception of what comes next.

When things disagree, which side changes?

The corner and the piano differ in one respect.

Once we have established that the shop really has changed, our expectation of the present street needs revising. With the practice next door, if the score confirms the remembered note and the player returns to correct it, there is no reason to revise the tune at every slip of a finger.

A discrepancy may expose a mistaken memory, a mistaken performance, or two different versions being confused. “I am used to hearing it this way” is not sufficient grounds for convicting the world. Nor must every encounter with difference erase everything previously learned.

We have to check, listen and compare.

This movement is what deserves attention: memory reaches forward; its extension can meet something it did not anticipate; that encounter sends us back to what we remember, to decide what should remain and what should change.

Some statements sound predictive while accommodating any outcome. “There will eventually be changes” may never be wrong, but it tells us nothing about which note we are awaiting.

An expectation that has genuinely arrived ahead of events can fail to fit them. Surprise makes the shape of what we had been waiting for briefly visible.

Beauty here is therefore not the reward for a successful guess. Before the pharmacy, the guess failed and the movement remains available to attention. Saying only that human memory is impressive misses its most interesting passage: reaching out, meeting resistance, returning to rearrange.

Familiar does not mean used up

A thoroughly familiar song need not unfold in the mind as a simultaneous display of every note. The opening comes, you continue, and you know where it ends. Familiarity can be a route you can follow rather than a screen crowded with the whole journey.

There may be little suspense and still something worth hearing. You sing from beginning to end without mastering a difficulty or improving on the previous attempt. A complete passage does not require a report of progress to justify itself.

At another time, a phrase approaches what seems to be its ending and turns away. It continues a little longer. The arrival you anticipated remains suspended while the sounding notes move on, altering the wait.

This need not be a mistake. A composition can delay an expectation it has established. Whether the result is good requires further judgment; surprising someone is not automatically an accomplishment.

Your sense that the music should now settle may also come from years of hearing a particular tradition. A listener formed by different music may not be waiting at the same place. Your own acquired habits cannot simply be renamed the world's rules.

There is still something we can discuss together. We need not expect the same note to understand what expecting is like. You can point out where you begin to wait. I may then hear, for the first time, why that passage carries tension for you.

Rather than demanding one answer, we make a route available for another person to follow a little way.

The other side of the wall falls quiet

The piano lid closes. You continue clearing the table. No revelation about life is required, and you need not immediately revisit your childhood.

The song may carry no desire to return. You may dislike that period and still remember how the melody goes. Wanting the past back and having it continue to matter are different things.

A little later you might hum a phrase without the neighbor to lead you. Something that has passed has a next line again. Before the line is sung, your mouth is already preparing for it.

Memory does not only face backward. Sometimes it is in the half-step we have not yet taken.

Who has been listening and anticipating through these years, and how that person has become who they are now, is another question we can approach next.

For the moment, there is this phrase to finish. It has not arrived, and you have already left it a place.

About this essay and its source

Based on The Beauty of Cognition, developing its piano-practice and street-corner scenes. The people and actions are illustrative; no actual composition or performer is being judged. Reconstructive memory does not make all recollection unreliable, and acquired musical expectations are not universal laws. A mismatch may prompt revision or, after checking, retention of a memory. The source paper's experimental history and discussion of Augustine are left to the original.
