单一用途的屏幕：当约束系统走进客厅

Single-Purpose Screens: When Constraint Systems Walk Into the Living Room

Han Qin (秦汉)

Grant Custer是一个设计师兼程序员，白天在Google Creative Lab做大语言模型的界面原型，业余时间经营着一个叫Constraint Systems的副项目——一组实验性的、基于浏览器的创意工具，每个工具都围绕一条特定的约束展开：像素只能流动、文字只能朝你指定的方向生长、排版只能在网格上变形。这些工具从2019年做到现在，已经积累了二十多个。但最近发生了一件事：这些约束系统开始离开浏览器，进入他的房间。

2026年5月，Custer在自己的办公区搭了一面墙：三块单一用途的屏幕。最下面是一个树莓派连着一台没有回显屏的"Camp Snap"相机——拍照时你看不到自己拍了什么，照片直接进入循环播放的幻灯片。中间是另一个树莓派，全屏显示一个他自建的时间块可视化器，从本地服务器读取markdown文件里的当日计划，画出进度条。最上面不是屏幕，是一个手写表盘的实体时钟——他从一个陶艺工作室的橱窗里学到，钟表机芯可以单独买，后面放什么都行。手写的数字是占位符，但他说"也越来越顺眼了"。

这为什么是余项之美？因为这个实践落在三个已构范畴的缝隙里，哪个都装不下它。它不是"智能家居"——智能家居的凿构循环已经完成了，它的美学已经被命名为"无缝"和"自动化"，已经沉淀为构。它也不是"新媒体艺术装置"——装置的凿构循环也早已完成，白盒子空间、策展话语、技术奇观的三角关系已经是沉积物。它更不是"DIY极客文化"——那里的已构美学是效率和功能的最大化。Custer的做法不符合这三条中的任何一条。他的屏幕不无缝（他坦承没有一块屏幕能持续很久，因为"无限的可能性一直在暗处潜伏"），不壮观（就是几块树莓派加木头），也不追求效率（他专门选了一台看不到拍摄结果的相机）。

余项在这里的含义是双重的。表层上，他从Constraint Systems的浏览器约束出发，把"约束"这个概念推向了物理空间——单一用途本身就是一种约束，而约束产生余项。深层上，他自己承认了一个核心矛盾：他追求的是数字物件那种"它就是它自己、不是别的东西"的平静感，但这种平静对于软件来说几乎不可能实现，因为软件永远可以变成别的东西。这个矛盾没有解决。他没有假装解决了它。他只是继续实验。这正是余项之美的核心标志：逻辑还在生长，矛盾没有被消化，命名还不完整。

现在看到这个比以后看到它重要。如果等到这种"嵌入日常的单一用途计算美学"被策展话语正式命名——大概会叫"calm computing"或者"domestic interface"之类的东西——它就变成了构，变成了另一个DD的美，安全但不活。现在它还是余项：还在矛盾中，还在失败中，还在生长。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

Grant Custer is a designer-programmer who prototypes LLM interfaces at Google Creative Lab by day and runs Constraint Systems on the side — a growing collection of experimental browser-based creative tools, each built around a single, specific constraint: pixels can only flow, text can only grow in the direction you choose, layout can only warp on a grid. He has been building these since 2019, accumulating over twenty. But something recent happened: the constraint systems started leaving the browser and entering his room.

In May 2026, Custer set up a wall in his home office: three single-purpose screens. The bottom one is a Raspberry Pi connected to a screenless "Camp Snap" camera — you cannot see what you shot; photos go straight into a looping slideshow. The middle one is another Pi, displaying a full-screen time-block visualizer that reads markdown files from his home server and draws a progress bar for the day. The top piece is not a screen at all — it is a physical clock with a handwritten face, inspired by a pottery studio window where he learned you can buy clock mechanisms separately and put whatever you want behind them. The handwriting is a placeholder, but he says it is growing on him.

Why is this the beauty of the remainder? Because this practice falls into the gap between three already-constructed categories, and none of them can contain it. It is not "smart home" — the smart home's chisel-construct cycle is complete; its aesthetics have been named as "seamless" and "automated," already sediment, already construct. It is not "new media art installation" — that cycle is also done; the white-cube-space, curatorial-discourse, and technical-spectacle triangle is settled sediment. And it is not "DIY maker culture" — whose constructed beauty is the maximization of efficiency and function. Custer's practice fits none of these. His screens are not seamless (he openly admits none have lasted long because "the unbounded possibilities are still always kind of lurking"). They are not spectacular (just some Pis and wood). And they do not maximize efficiency (he deliberately chose a camera that cannot show you what you shot).

The remainder here is double-layered. On the surface, he extends the Constraint Systems concept from browser constraints into physical space — single-purpose itself is a constraint, and constraint produces remainder. At a deeper level, he confesses a core contradiction: he seeks the calm of a digital object that simply "is what it is and nothing else," but this calm is nearly impossible for software, because software can always become something else. This contradiction is not resolved. He does not pretend to have resolved it. He just keeps experimenting. This is the core signature of the beauty of the remainder: the logic is still growing, the contradiction has not been digested, the naming is still incomplete.

Seeing this now matters more than seeing it later. Once this aesthetic of "single-purpose domestic computing embedded in everyday life" gets formally named by curatorial discourse — it will probably be called "calm computing" or "domestic interface" or something like that — it becomes construct, becomes the beauty of yet another DD, safe but no longer alive. Right now it is still remainder: still in contradiction, still failing, still growing.
