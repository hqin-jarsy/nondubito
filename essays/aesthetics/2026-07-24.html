Kuro Kasetto：击打与共鸣之间的那个余项

Kuro Kasetto: The Remainder Between Strike and Resonance

Han Qin (秦汉)

Kuro Kasetto 是来自墨西哥的艺术家，用电磁铁敲击真实的声学乐器制造音乐——木琴、卡林巴。技术栈很具体：五个电磁铁通过 Arduino 接收 MIDI 信号，MIDI 数据来自运行在 Norns Shield 上的 Bloom 脚本，一个生成性的、不断变化的节奏算法；击打后的声音再经过 Chase Bliss MOOD MKII 效果器实时处理。乐曲是环境音乐，抵抗音网和歌曲结构，Peter Kirn 今天在 CDM 写道，"像植物而非机器，它似乎是随风漂来的。"自我介绍只有一行：Someone having fun making little sounds。

两个已构在2026年都已硬化。其一：机器打击乐的传统——Conlon Nancarrow 的机械钢琴、Pat Metheny 的 Orchestrion、所有 MIDI 机器人打击乐——整个范式建立在精密上：机器能做人类做不到的事，超速、超准、超复杂。电磁铁在这个传统里是精密仪器，它消除人的不精确。其二：环境音乐作为类型，从 Eno 的《Airports》到今日 AI 生成环境音乐，四十年的沉淀在2026年完成了最后一块：描述一种氛围→大模型→输出。两个已构从两端同时封闭，在中间留了一道缝。

凿去的是精密本身，但不是用人类的不精确替代。Kuro Kasetto 的电磁铁"处在运作边缘"——机械不稳定性成了乐器本身。凿构循环在此以非典型的方式展开：Bloom 脚本发出 MIDI 模式→电磁铁将其转化为机械敲击→声学琴体（木琴键、卡林巴弦）用自己的共鸣、衰减、泛音结构作出回应。三个层次各有自己的逻辑，没有一个完全掌控另外两个。数字的精度在物质的体积里消失了。

余项就是击打与共鸣之间的那个东西——电磁铁弹簧的回弹声、过强击打造成的泛音暴发、每次衰减方式略微不同的余音，因为物理世界不保持静止。这既不是采样（声音不是录好再播放的），也不是合成，也不是鼓机。是一个特定的声音质地，只在电磁铁的时序精密遇见声学乐器的物质不精密时才产生。"像植物而非机器"是一个悖论，不是类别名。这个类别名还不存在：不是"机器人音乐"（那需要精密），不是"环境音乐"（那是已产、已混、已制作的），不是"电声音乐"（那是欧洲学院传统），不是"声音艺术"（那在美术馆里），不是任何现有的名字。"Tiny sounds"是 Kuro 自己的描述——一种感觉，不是范畴。

今天（2026年7月24日）是 CDM 对 Kuro Kasetto 的第一次主要英语报道。Kirn 写道 Instagram 帖子"在我的圈子里流传"——还在社区发现阶段，命名还没有开始。AI 音乐生成正在把"生成性环境音乐"吸收进单一公式，一旦封闭，通过 DIY 机械装置制造生成性音乐的具体实践就会被密封为历史类别，不再是活的余项。现在是看它的时候。它的逻辑还在演化：不同的乐器（木琴、卡林巴）、不同的装置配置、Patreon 还在活跃。还没有一个名字。还是余项。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

Kuro Kasetto is an artist from Mexico who makes music by having solenoids — electromagnetic actuators — physically strike real acoustic instruments: a xylophone, a kalimba. The setup is specific: five solenoids receive MIDI signals via Arduino, the MIDI generated by the Bloom script running on a Norns Shield, a generative, constantly shifting rhythmic algorithm; the acoustic result passes through a Chase Bliss MOOD MKII pedal for real-time texture. The music is ambient in the truest sense, resisting grids and song structures, floating in something that Peter Kirn described today on CDM as "more flora than machine, as if it's floated in on a breeze." The artist's self-description is one line: Someone having fun making little sounds.

Two already-constructs have hardened by 2026. First: the robotic percussion tradition — Conlon Nancarrow's player pianos, Pat Metheny's Orchestrion, every MIDI robotic drum rig — built entirely on precision. The machine does what humans cannot: superhuman speed, regularity, complexity. A solenoid in this tradition is a precision instrument; it eliminates human error. Second: ambient music as genre, sedimented from Eno's Music for Airports to the AI-generated present. In 2026, this second construct completed its final brick: describe a mood, large model outputs atmosphere, end of conversation. Both constructs are closing from opposite ends, leaving a gap in the middle.

The chisel removes precision as the goal — without replacing it with human imprecision. Kuro Kasetto's machines are "on the very edge of working." Mechanical unreliability becomes the instrument itself. The chisel-construct cycle unfolds here in an atypical form: Bloom script generates MIDI patterns → solenoid translates them into mechanical strikes → the acoustic body of the xylophone or kalimba responds with its own resonance, damping, overtone structure. Three layers, each with its own logic, none fully controlling the others. The digital precision dissolves into material volume.

The remainder lives in the gap between strike and resonance — the spring-return clatter of the solenoid mechanism, the overtone bloom from a strike that was slightly too hard, the sustain that fades differently each time because the physical world does not hold still. This is not sampling (the sound is live, not pre-recorded), not synthesis (there is a real xylophone bar), not drum machine (there is no fixed loop). It is a specific sound quality that only emerges when the solenoid's mechanical timing meets the acoustic instrument's material unpredictability. "More flora than machine" is a paradox, not a category name. The category does not yet exist: not "robotic music" (that requires precision), not "ambient music" (that is produced, arranged, finished), not "electroacoustic" (that is a European academic tradition), not "sound art" (that lives in galleries). "Tiny sounds" is Kuro's own descriptor — a feeling, not a category.

Today, July 24, 2026, is CDM's first major English-language coverage of Kuro Kasetto. Kirn notes that Instagram posts are "making the rounds in my circle" — the community discovery phase, not yet naming. AI music generation is absorbing "generative ambient" into a single formula; once sealed, the specific practice of making generative music through DIY mechanical apparatus becomes a historical footnote rather than a living remainder. Now is the time to see it. Its logic is still evolving: different instruments (xylophone, kalimba), different apparatus configurations, Patreon still active. No name yet. Still a remainder.
