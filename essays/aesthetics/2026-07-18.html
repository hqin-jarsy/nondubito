Otomata Labs《Triplet》：2600芯片的余项时刻

Otomata Labs' "Triplet": The Remainder Moment of the 2600 Chip

Han Qin (秦汉)

2026年4月的Revision国际demoscene派对上，一个叫做Otomata Labs的小组展示了一件32KB的程序作品，运行平台是1977年的Atari VCS/2600游戏机。这件作品名为《Triplet》，在数分钟内呈现出3D旋转效果、水面倒影、对角线滚动字幕，以及一套完整的合成音乐引擎——全部压缩在那颗叫做TIA（电视接口适配器）的芯片所允许的范围内。TIA没有帧缓冲区，没有精灵表，没有PCM音频通道。程序员必须在CRT显像管电子束横扫屏幕的过程中逐周期地喂指令，让图像在射线飞行中诞生——这种技巧在圈内被称为"racing the beam"（追赶射线）。《Triplet》在Oldskool Demo组别中获得第一名。

TIA芯片在1977年已经被完全"凿定"：其约束条件、时序结构、所有的极限都固化在硅片里。1980年代的雅达利游戏产业将这颗芯片推向当时所能触及的边界，留下《太空侵略者》《探险》《Pitfall!》——这些是已构，是沉淀的构造物，是从前一次凿构循环里析出的美。它们的美学已经定型，已经有名字，已经可以被博物馆收藏。但芯片本身的余项从未被完全消化。余项不在芯片外部，而在芯片的逻辑与人类理解之间那道仍在生长的裂缝里。

Otomata Labs所做的，是在这道裂缝里又凿进了一刀。TIA只提供两个精灵寄存器、一个球、一个场地图形系统；而《Triplet》利用中扫描线精灵复位（mid-scanline sprite resets）和借自《宇宙方舟》的星空效果，让一颗1977年的芯片在2026年说出了它从未说过的话。demoscene社区的评论揭示了这道裂缝的真实深度："不是一个周期被浪费了。你们真正理解了硬件，并且为它写了这个demo。"另一条评论说："有追赶射线这回事，也有把射线按在地上揍的事——这是后者。"这种惊叫不是对华丽效果的惊叫，而是对一个未被预期的结构性可能性的认知：硬件的余项又一次从裂缝里渗了出来。

从SAE的视角来看，《Triplet》是余项之美的教科书案例，原因在于它的结构与艺术史叙事之间的错位。媒体考古学和平台研究已经知道demoscene的存在，有学者在写retrocomputing aesthetics的论文。但《Triplet》本身——这次具体的技术突破、这个特定的合成音乐引擎、这组对TIA边界的特殊重新绘制——尚未被任何话语完全命名。demoscene社区已经惊叫，艺术界尚未抵达。这正是余项存在的证明：技艺圈已经看到，命名圈尚未跟上。

这也是为什么现在看比以后看更重要。《Triplet》正处于那个短暂的窗口期：余项在被建构为"遗产"之前的时刻，在VCS demoscene被媒体艺术机构正式命名为一个类别之前的时刻。凿构循环仍在进行中——下一组程序员已经在看着这件作品，思考下一道裂缝在哪里。此刻接触它，就是接触一个仍在生长的余项，而非一件已经固定的历史文物。

At the Revision 2026 demoparty in Saarbrücken, a group called Otomata Labs showed a 32-kilobyte program running on a 1977 Atari VCS/2600 console. The production, "Triplet," unfolds over several minutes: 3D-seeming rotations, water-surface reflections, diagonal scrollers, and a fully synthesized music engine—all within what the TIA (Television Interface Adaptor) chip permits. The TIA has no framebuffer. No sprite table. No PCM audio. The programmer must feed instructions cycle by cycle as the CRT electron beam sweeps across the screen, birthing the image in the very moment of its own creation. The demoscene calls this "racing the beam." Triplet won 1st place in the Oldskool Demo competition.

The TIA chip was "chiseled" in 1977: its constraints, its timing structures, its every limit are fixed in silicon. The Atari game industry of the 1980s pushed the chip toward its apparent boundaries and left behind Space Invaders, Adventure, Pitfall!—these are 已构 (already-construct), beautiful sediments of a completed chisel-construct cycle. Their aesthetics are settled, named, collectible by museums. But the chip's remainder never fully resolved. The remainder doesn't live outside the chip; it lives in the growing gap between the hardware's specific logic and human understanding of that logic.

What Otomata Labs did was drive another chisel into that gap. The TIA provides only two sprite registers, one ball, one playfield system; "Triplet" uses mid-scanline sprite resets and a Cosmic Ark-derived star trick to make a 1977 chip say something it had never said before. The demoscene community's responses reveal the true depth of the gap: "Not a single cycle is wasted. You guys really understood the hardware and wrote the demo for it." Another: "There's racing the beam, and there's wrestling it to the ground and making it your bitch." These aren't reactions to polish—they're recognition of an unexpected structural possibility: the hardware's remainder has seeped through the crack once more.

From the SAE perspective, "Triplet" is a textbook case of 余项之美 because of the mismatch between its structure and art-historical discourse. Media archaeology and platform studies already know the demoscene exists. Academic papers on retrocomputing aesthetics have been written. But "Triplet" itself—this specific technical breakthrough, this particular synthesized music engine, this exact redrawing of the TIA's boundary in April 2026—has not been fully named by any discourse. The demoscene community is astonished; the art world hasn't arrived. That is precisely the proof that the remainder is alive: the craft community has seen it, the naming community hasn't caught up.

This is why encountering it now matters more than later. "Triplet" sits inside that brief window: the moment before remainder hardens into heritage, before VCS demoscene gets formally categorized by a media arts institution. The chisel-construct cycle is still in motion—the next group of programmers is already watching this work, searching for where the next crack is. To engage with it now is to engage with a living remainder, not a fixed historical artifact.
