Marex — 二十五分钟活码：余项作为形式

Marex / Twenty-Five Minutes: Live Code as Remainder

Han Qin (秦汉)

Marex 是法国 Demoscene 艺术家，隶属 Flush、GPO 等小组。2026年4月，他在德国萨尔布吕肯举行的 Revision demoparty 的"着色器对决"（Shader Showdown）决赛中夺冠。这场比赛的规则极其简单：选手在二十五分钟内从零开始在屏幕上实时编写 GLSL 片元着色器代码，代码全程实时渲染，观众以现场投票决定胜者。Marex 的决赛作品选择以纯黑白调色，在这一约束中创造出被评论者形容为"结构上极为优雅，视觉上极为有力"的图像。决赛录像留在 YouTube，代码留在 livecode.demozoo.org，但那二十五分钟的现场——那本身——已经过去。

在 SAE 的框架下，着色器对决这一形式与余项之间存在结构性关联，而这种关联是大多数其他艺术形式所不能主张的。正常的 Demoscene 制作——四千字节的 intro、六十四千字节的 demo——经历数月乃至数年的打磨，在不断往复的凿构循环（凿构循环）中磨炼，直至抵达一个完成的视觉命题，语言足以命名它，批评足以定位它。但二十五分钟的强制截止从根本上打断了这一过程。对决中产生的代码无法完成凿构循环，它被迫以余项的状态存在。每一行代码都是在时间压力下写就的凿击；每一次编译都是一次艺术家并未完全预谋的构建。最终呈现在屏幕上的图像，是技艺与时间之间碰撞的沉积物，而非某个既定美学意志的实现。

更根本的问题是：活码着色器表演作为一种艺术形式，至今仍未被艺术批评充分命名。Demoscene 有自己的奖项系统——Revision 的官方竞赛、一年一度的 Meteoriks 奖——但这些奖项运作于一个主流艺术批评尚未触及的社群内部。"实时表演艺术"已是已构（已构）：它属于 Marina Abramović 的历史遗产，属于表演研究的制度框架。而在 Bonzomatic 软件中实时编写 GLSL，让着色器在渲染的同时被台下观众看见并投票——这一形式所产生的美学经验，在现有批评词汇中仍然没有对应的容器。批评家尚未到来，命名尚未发生。这是它作为余项的核心证据。

Marex 的黑白选择值得单独审视。颜色往往是视觉艺术中最属于"已构"的维度——最易于命名、最易于被市场消化、最迅速被符号化的部分。主动舍弃颜色，只保留结构与明暗关系，是一种刻意的余项化操作：将图像还原到语言最难以穿透的区域。这也解释了为何旁观者倾向于用"力度"来描述那件作品，而非通常适用于彩色图像的词汇——"力度"是语言在无法完成命名时留下的一个占位符，一个指向尚未成形之物的手势。

此刻，活码着色器表演作为一种形式，本身处于余项的状态。艺术世界正在缓慢地靠近它——偶尔出现涉及 Demoscene 的文章，有大学课程开始将其纳入研究——但吸收尚未发生，命名尚未完成。Marex 在 Revision 2026 的那次胜利，留下了可查阅的代码和可观看的录像，但没有任何批评语言足以完整地说明"这究竟是什么"。现在去看它，你看到的是一种仍在生长的逻辑，一种尚未固化为已构的美学。等到批评到来、给它一个完整的名字，这一时刻的结构性透明度就将永远消失。余项之美存在于尚未被说出的事物之中。

Marex is a French demoscene artist, member of groups Flush and GPO, who won the Revision 2026 Shader Showdown Final in Saarbrücken, Germany in April 2026. The format is deceptively simple: contestants write a GLSL fragment shader from scratch in twenty-five minutes while it renders live on a projected screen, with the audience voting to determine the winner. Marex built his winning piece entirely in black and white — no color palette, only structure and the interplay of light and shadow — which the crowd and commentators described afterward as "incredibly elegant and graphically powerful." The final recording exists on YouTube; the code exists on livecode.demozoo.org; the twenty-five minutes themselves are gone.

In SAE terms, the Shader Showdown format has a structural relationship to 余项 (remainder) that most other art forms cannot claim. Normal demoscene production — the four-kilobyte intro, the sixty-four-kilobyte demo — goes through months or years of iteration, of chiseling and constructing through repeated cycles (凿构循环), until it arrives as a finished statement that language can name and criticism can locate. The twenty-five-minute cutoff interrupts this process entirely. The code produced in a Shader Showdown cannot complete the chisel-construct cycle; it is forced to exist as remainder. Every line is a chisel written under pressure; every compilation is a construction the artist did not fully intend. What appears on screen at the end is the sediment of a collision between skill and time, not the realization of a predetermined aesthetic vision.

More fundamentally, live-coded shader performance as an art form has not been adequately named by art discourse. The demoscene has its own awards system — Revision's official competitions, the annual Meteoriks — but these operate entirely within a community that mainstream criticism has yet to absorb. "Live performance art" is already-construct (已构): it belongs to the inheritance of Marina Abramović, to the institutional frameworks of performance studies. Writing GLSL in Bonzomatic while a crowd watches the shader render in real time and then votes — producing something that is simultaneously sport, craft, and aesthetic experience — has no adequate container in existing critical vocabulary. The critics haven't arrived. The naming hasn't happened. This is the core evidence of its status as remainder.

Marex's black-and-white choice deserves separate attention. Color is, in a sense, the most "already-constructed" dimension of visual art — the dimension most easily named, most quickly absorbed by the market, most readily converted into symbol. Deliberately removing color and working only with structure and luminance is an act of deliberate remaindering: reducing the image to the region language finds hardest to penetrate. This is why observers reached for the word "force" to describe the piece — not a word typically applied to color imagery, but a placeholder that language uses when naming fails, a gesture toward something not yet fully formed into construct.

Right now, live-coded shader performance exists in its own remainder state. The art world is slowly approaching it — occasional essays mention the demoscene, university programs begin to study it — but the absorption hasn't happened, the naming isn't complete. Marex's win at Revision 2026 left queryable code and watchable footage, but no critical vocabulary adequate to say what it actually is. To look at it now is to see a logic still growing, an aesthetic not yet hardened into construct. When the critics arrive and give it a proper name, this moment of structural openness will be gone. The beauty of the remainder lives in what has not yet been said.
