Trpti Sanghvi：地铁闸机数据里的群飞余项

Trpti Sanghvi: The Murmuration Remainder Inside Turnstile Data

Han Qin (秦汉)

纽约地铁的闸机每天被推动数百万次。每一次金属臂的旋转都被MTA的系统记录下来——时间、站点、方向、计数。这些数据的原始目的是票务管理和运营调度，没人在设计闸机时想过它会成为艺术的原料。数据是通勤行为的沉积物，是城市交通系统运转后留下的副产品——用SAE的术语说，它是一个庞大的余项库。Trpti Sanghvi的《Turnstile Murmurations》在2026年3月的Data Through Design展览"Echo(logies)"中出现，展于布鲁克林BRIC House，做的事情是：把这个余项库接入另一个余项生成机制——椋鸟群飞（murmuration）算法。

群飞是涌现的经典案例。没有任何一只椋鸟"设计"了那个图案，那些令人屏息的空中形态是每只鸟遵循简单的局部规则（与邻居保持距离、对齐方向、趋向中心）之后的剩余产物。图案是规则的余项，不是目的。Sanghvi做的是一个双重余项叠加：闸机数据是通勤的余项，群飞图案是代理规则的余项。当数百万次闸机旋转的时间节奏被映射为虚拟鸟群的行为参数时，屏幕上出现的形态既不属于交通规划的词汇表，也不属于生物学的词汇表。它是两套系统的凿构循环碰撞后留下的东西——没有人规划过的东西。

这里有一个关键的结构性观察：Sanghvi不是一个典型意义上的"数据艺术家"，也不是"生物模拟"程序员。她的背景横跨奥迪西古典舞（Odissi）、数据分析（她的日常工作是产品分析师）、神经科学兴趣和算法艺术。她在个人网站上说，她的艺术是"数字时代的抽象表现主义，身体是画笔，互联网是画布；运动的记忆被颜色捕捉。"这意味着她从身体/舞蹈的角度看数据流动——闸机的推动本身就是一种身体动作的累积，和舞蹈的关系比和电子表格的关系更近。这个视角不是任何现有学科教出来的，它是她个人经验的余项。

现在看这件作品比以后看更重要，因为它还处在"余项"状态。数据艺术（data art）作为一个类别已经存在了二十年，但它的主流语法还是信息可视化的延伸——图表的美化、仪表盘的诗意化。群飞算法在生物模拟领域也是已构的技术。但把城市交通数据喂给群飞算法、让涌现模式去揭示人类集体行为中隐藏的节奏——这个操作还没有被命名为一个类别。它不是"数据可视化"，因为它不以信息传达为目的。它不是"bioart"，因为没有真实的生物体。它不是"城市艺术"，因为它不发生在街头。它就在这些名词之间的缝隙里——还是余项，还活着。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

New York City's subway turnstiles are pushed millions of times each day. Every rotation of the metal arm is logged by the MTA's system — timestamp, station, direction, count. The original purpose of this data is fare management and operational scheduling. Nobody designing the turnstile mechanism was thinking about art. The data is the sediment of commuting behavior, a byproduct left behind after the transit system does its job — in SAE terms, a vast remainder archive. Trpti Sanghvi's Turnstile Murmurations, shown at the Data Through Design 2026 exhibition "Echo(logies)" at Brooklyn's BRIC House in March, does something structurally precise with this archive: she feeds it into another remainder-generating mechanism — the murmuration (starling flocking) algorithm.

Murmurations are the textbook case of emergence. No individual starling "designs" the pattern. Those breathtaking aerial formations are the residue of each bird following simple local rules — maintain distance from neighbors, align direction, move toward the center. The pattern is the remainder of the rules, not their goal. What Sanghvi constructs is a double-remainder stack: turnstile data is the remainder of commuting; the murmuration pattern is the remainder of agent rules. When the temporal rhythms of millions of turnstile rotations are mapped as behavioral parameters for a virtual flock, the shapes that appear on screen belong to neither the vocabulary of transit planning nor the vocabulary of biology. They are what is left after two systems' chisel-construct cycles collide — something no one planned for.

There is a critical structural observation here: Sanghvi is not a "data artist" in any conventional sense, nor a biological simulation programmer. Her background spans Odissi classical dance, data analytics (her day job is product analyst), neuroscience interests, and algorithmic art. On her personal site, she describes her art as "abstract expressionism in the digital age, with the body acting as a paint brush on the canvas of the internet; the memory of movement is captured with color." This means she approaches data flows from the perspective of the body and of dance — the pushing of a turnstile arm is itself an accumulated bodily gesture, closer to choreography than to a spreadsheet. This perspective is not taught by any existing discipline. It is the remainder of her particular lived experience.

Seeing this work now matters more than seeing it later, because it is still in its remainder state. Data art as a category has existed for two decades, but its dominant grammar remains an extension of information visualization — beautified charts, poeticized dashboards. The flocking algorithm is already-construct in the biological simulation field. But feeding urban transit data into a flocking algorithm and letting emergent patterns reveal hidden rhythms in collective human behavior — this operation does not yet have a category name. It is not "data visualization," because information delivery is not its purpose. It is not "bioart," because no actual organisms are involved. It is not "urban art," because it does not happen on the street. It sits in the gaps between these terms — still a remainder, still alive.
