弓和弦是同一根线

The Bow and the String Are the Same Line

Han Qin (秦汉)

会唱歌的风筝是老东西：中国的弓弦、日本的唸り、越南的笛、百慕大的哨，做法各不相同，思路一致——把发声器挂上去，让风筝当那具被风吹活的身体，哨是它的喉咙。建筑出身的 Firat Erdim 从 2018 年起做的 Kite Choir 把这个位置整个搬了下来。发声器不上天，装在绞盘和整条牵引线上。2026 年 7 月 7 日，Warm Gospel 发行了黑胶《Seyðisfjörður Soundings》，两面各一整轨，录自冰岛东部峡湾小镇 Seyðisfjörður 的 HEIMA 驻地。两个月里他几乎每天早上先给乐器调音，再背上风筝和录音设备，沿峡湾往海的方向走，走到风合适的地方就在那里"探音"（sounding）。

唱片用的是他称为"电 hurdy-gurdy 绞盘"的一支——除了有个手摇轮、会发长音之外，跟真正的绞弦琴没关系。牵引线是尼龙单丝，涂了松香，略带粘性；绞盘收放的时候，这条线横着擦过四到六根定音弦，弦从绞盘向风筝方向由低到高排开。全是空弦，不按品，靠改变乐器相对于风筝的角度决定擦到几根。这是可控的那一半。另一半是：由于涡激振动，所有弦——包括牵引线自己——在风里本来就在响，不需要被擦。而牵引线的长度、张力和几何形状随着收放持续改变，所以它自己那份声音可以从轻轻的咕咕一路滑到尖利的嚎叫。唱片 A 面第一轨里能听到纯风致的那一段。

结构上最要紧的一点在这里：擦弦的弓，和被风擦响的弦，是同一根线。你没办法把控制通道和不可控通道分开来调，动一边必然动另一边。绝大多数乐器的设计目标恰好相反——钢琴是一台让意图和声音尽可能重合的机器，余项被压到最小，能压多小压多小。Kite Choir 把大气插进因果链的正中间，然后拒绝把它拿掉。Erdim 自己的定位写得很准："这些乐器处在两极之间——既不给操作者自主表达的感觉，也不放过他去当一个被动的聆听装置。"这两极都是已构：浪漫主义的作者性是一整套完备的名字，凯奇以来的机遇、田野录音、深度聆听是另一整套完备的名字，两边都被艺术界消化了几十年。他站的是两个已构之间那道缝。缝之所以没有名字，不是因为它冷门，而是因为它不可归约到任何一边。

还有一件更精确的事，容易被当成技术细节放过去：风吹出来的那部分声音不是随机的。涡激振动产生的永远是每根弦自身调音的泛音。也就是说，你选定的调音决定了不可控部分的形状。余项不是噪声，是构造自己生出来、却装不下的东西——它不在系统外面另行飘着，它由系统生成，被系统的边界排除，然后从外面反过来撑住系统。Erdim 写道，牵引线因为一直在振而"获得了一种厚度"，而这个厚度改变了它作为弓的行为：被排除的那部分回来改写了控制的那部分。这是凿构循环里最容易被跳过的一环——凿出边界的同时就生成了溢出，溢出又成为下一次凿的着力点。

谱是电声作曲家 Paula Matthusen 写的，印成一副牌（由 Kaitlin McCoy 设计制作），四组指令分别依风况、气候、时间，以及他称为"模糊"的一类性质来抽。Erdim 说，多年下来，最有洞察力的反而是那些干脆不用风筝、也不用乐器的指令——通常正是天气不允许的时候才抽到。一个形式化系统，最高产的输出落在它自己对"输出"的定义之外，这是余项最纯的形状。"weather permitting"这个词也一样：它平常出现在邀请函底下，是一句免责，是日程表的残渣；他把它翻过来当总则——"它通常表示限制，但也可以是一张许可证。"残渣升格为第一原理。

值得现在看，是因为它正在被命名，但还没被命名完。"aeolian"、"非人能动性"、"attunement"、"weathering"，这批词最近几年在艺术写作里硬化得极快，词汇已经备好，位置也已经腾出来了。一旦 Kite Choir 被完整归档进"声音艺术／生态声学／人与非人协作"，它的余项就用完了，剩下的会是一件很美的已构：可复述、可教学、可申请驻地。而现在还差一步。项目 2018 年起步，冰岛的探音 2019 年就有片段流出，唱片拖到 2026 年 7 月才发——八年里这个东西一直存在而没有一个完成的名字，这个间隙本身就是余项，黑胶一发行、评论一积累，命名就启动了。最后一句大概最难被收编：他说"地方才是 Kite Choir 乐器真正的身体"。峡湾的峭壁和从海上飘进来的雾把风梳成那个样子，乐器是故意做成不完整的，靠外面的东西才闭合。大部分形式化都在掩饰自己对外部的依赖，这一件把依赖写在正面。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

Singing kites are old. China has bowed strings, Japan has the unari, Vietnam has flutes, Bermuda has hummers — different builds, one idea: hang the noisemaker aloft and let the kite be the body the wind animates, with the hummer as its throat. Firat Erdim, trained as an architect, has spent since 2018 moving that throat back down to earth. In Kite Choir, nothing sounds in the sky; the instrument is the reel and the entire tow line. On 7 July 2026, Warm Gospel released Seyðisfjörður Soundings, a vinyl LP with one continuous track per side, recorded during a two-month residency at HEIMA in Seyðisfjörður, a fjord town on Iceland's east coast. Most mornings he tuned the instruments, packed the kites and the recording gear, and walked out along the fjord toward the sea, performing a "sounding" wherever the wind turned favorable.

The record was made with what he calls an electric "hurdy-gurdy reel," which has nothing to do with an actual hurdy-gurdy except that it involves a hand-cranked wheel and produces long drones. The tow line is nylon monofilament coated in rosin, slightly tacky. As the kite is reeled in and out, that line bows a rank of four to six tuned strings arranged low to high, reel toward sky. The strings are played open — no fingering — and how many of them sound depends on the angle you hold the instrument at relative to the kite. That is the controllable half. The other half: because of vortex-induced vibration, every string, including the tow line itself, is already singing in the wind whether or not anything bows it. And the tow line is continuously changing length, tension and geometry as it pays out, so its own voice can slide from a soft cooing to a thin, fiendish wail. The first track on Side A is largely that purely wind-driven sound.

Here is the part that matters structurally: the bow and the bowed string are the same line. There is no way to tune the controllable channel independently of the uncontrollable one; touch either and you have touched both. Nearly every instrument ever built is designed the other way around — a piano is a machine for making intention and sound coincide, and the remainder is squeezed down as far as engineering allows. Kite Choir inserts the atmosphere into the middle of the causal chain and then refuses to remove it. Erdim's own framing is exact: these instruments sit between two poles, "neither allowing the operator a sense of autonomous expression nor letting them off the hook with a passive listening device." Both poles are already-construct. Romantic authorship is a complete and finished vocabulary. So is the Cageian lineage of chance, field recording and deep listening — the art world has been digesting it for seventy years. What he occupies is the seam between two finished constructs, and the seam has no name not because it is obscure but because it does not reduce to either side.

One further detail, easy to skim past as engineering: the wind's contribution is not random. Vortex-induced vibration always produces overtones of whatever each string is tuned to. Which means the tuning you chose determines the shape of the part you cannot control. The remainder is not noise — it is what the construct generates and then cannot contain. It does not float outside the system independently; the system produces it, the system's boundary excludes it, and from outside it turns around and bears load. Erdim notes that the constantly vibrating tow line "acquires a thickness," and that thickness changes how it functions as a bow: the excluded part comes back and rewrites the controlling part. That is the step in the chisel-construct cycle most often skipped — the same cut that draws the boundary is what generates the overflow, and the overflow becomes the purchase for the next cut.

The score, by the electroacoustic composer Paula Matthusen, is printed as a deck of playing cards (designed and fabricated by Kaitlin McCoy), with four sets of instructions contingent on aeolian, climatic, temporal and what he calls "fuzzy" conditions. Erdim reports that over the years the most insightful instructions have turned out to be the ones that dispense with kites and instruments altogether — usually drawn because the weather refused. A formal system whose highest-yield output falls outside its own definition of output is the remainder in its purest form. The phrase "weather permitting" works the same way: it normally lives at the bottom of an invitation as a disclaimer, the sediment of a calendar. He turns it over and makes it the governing principle — it "usually indicates limitation, but it can also function as license." Sediment promoted to first premise.

Why see it now rather than later: the naming has started but has not closed. "Aeolian," "more-than-human agency," "attunement," "weathering" — this vocabulary has hardened fast over the last few years, the words are stocked, the slot is cleared. The moment Kite Choir is fully filed under sound art / eco-acoustics / human–nonhuman collaboration, its remainder is spent, and what is left is a very beautiful already-construct: repeatable, teachable, fundable. It is not there yet. The project began in 2018, excerpts of the Iceland soundings have been circulating since 2019, and the record only appeared in July 2026 — eight years of the thing existing without a finished name, and that gap is itself the remainder. Press a vinyl, let reviews accumulate, and the naming begins. The last line may be the hardest to absorb: "the place is the actual body of a Kite Choir instrument." The cliffs and the sea fog comb the wind into that particular shape; the instrument is deliberately built incomplete and closes its circuit only on something outside itself. Most formalizations conceal their dependence on the outside. This one puts it on the front.
