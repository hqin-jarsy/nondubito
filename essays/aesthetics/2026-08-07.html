SAE Judgment & Aesthetics · Beauty of the Remainder

瓦莱里娅·霍马依登 / 《惊奇田野笔记》

Valeria Homayden / Field Notes for Wonder

Han Qin (秦汉)

瓦莱里娅·霍马依登（Valeria Homayden）是一位现居英国的平面设计师，她的《惊奇田野笔记》由两部分构成。其一是一系列孔版印刷动画：她以显微镜头拍摄岩石、贝壳、苔藓的表面纹理，将这些图像通过孔版机（Risograph）逐色叠印，再以逐帧手法将印版合成为动态影像。其二是一本口袋大小的田野手册，收录了她搜集的纹理图样与若干"感知练习"——"走到户外，找到质地。""看看你脚下有什么，再看看你头顶有什么，写一段两者之间的对话。"项目的起点是一个简单的视觉观察：远处树木的顶冠轮廓，与近处苔藓的微观剖面，在形态上几乎无法区分——宏观与微观在图案上互为镜像。

这件作品的余项之美，藏在孔版机无法压制的地方。在商业印刷语境中，孔版机的"错误"需要被纠正：各色套版之间因纸张逐次进纸而产生的偏移（套印差），以及肉眼可见的网点结构——这些都是需要消除的缺陷。但霍马依登不纠正它们。正是这种套印偏差，使颜色从物体的识别中逃逸出来：观看者先感知到图案与形状，后才辨认出苔藓或叶脉。鲜艳的色彩将微观纹理抽象化，物理世界的可辨识性退位，留下的是纯粹的图案逻辑。这是凿构循环中最典型的余项结构：凿子（孔版机的物理限制）无法压制的那部分，成为了美的生成者，而非需要被清除的噪音。

在SAE美学的框架下，这件作品处于凿构循环的特定时刻——孔版印刷动画作为一种实践，正在被艺术界接近，但尚未被完全命名与建构。"动画"、"版画"、"艺术家书"——每一个词都部分覆盖这件事，但没有哪一个能完整容纳它。《惊奇田野笔记》同时是：自然观察练习、印刷实验、感知训练手册、逐帧运动影像。它的内在逻辑还在生长：孔版机的物理限制如何精确地产生了这种特定的美学？在多大程度上这是"错误的正当化"，在多大程度上这是一种新的媒介探索？这些问题尚无定论。正是这种未完成性，是余项之美的条件：已构（已被命名的实践）尚未将它完全收编。

还有一个更深的结构值得注意：这件作品的核心命题本身——宏观与微观在图案上的同构——是一个尚在被科学、艺术、哲学共同接近的问题，而非任何单一领域已经给出完整答案的问题。苔藓的剖面为什么像森林？分形？自相似性？涌现？每一个词都是已构的碎片，但它们加在一起，并不能穷尽霍马依登的图像所打开的那个观看方式。她的孔版机把套印偏差留下来，把图案的自相似性留下来，把观察的缓慢留下来——这些都是余项，是机器与自然界的双重剩余。

几年后，将会有展览专注于孔版印刷动画作为一种独立实践，机构将归档其早期从业者，套印偏差将作为已知的美学策略被引用。霍马依登的工作将作为这个类别的早期案例之一，被凿入已构的档案。但现在，这件作品仍是余项——艺术界正在接近它，尚未完成命名。现在接触它，意味着接触一个结构正在生长中的事物，而非一个已经结晶的名字。这是余项之美最重要的时间性：它只在某个特定的当下存在，过了这个当下，它就变成了已构，变成了下一个凿子的基础材料。

Valeria Homayden is a graphic designer based in the UK. Her project Field Notes for Wonder has two parts. First, a series of Risograph animations: she photographs the microscopic surface patterns of rocks, shells, and moss, prints these images in color layers through a stencil-based Risograph machine, then animates the resulting prints frame-by-frame. Second, a pocket-sized field guide containing her collected texture images alongside observation prompts — "Go outside and find texture." "Look at what is by your feet. Now look at what is above your head. Write a short conversation between these two things." The project began with a simple visual observation: the silhouette of distant tree canopies and the cross-section of nearby moss are, in terms of pattern, nearly indistinguishable. Macro and micro mirror each other.

The 余项之美 in this work lives in what the Risograph cannot suppress. In commercial printing, the machine's imperfections are problems to correct: misregistration — the slight offset between color layers caused by paper feeding through separately for each pass — and visible halftone dot structure. These are defects to eliminate. Homayden doesn't eliminate them. And precisely because she doesn't, the colors in her animations slip free from object recognition: you perceive the pattern and shape before you identify the moss, the leaf, the vein. The bright colors abstract the microscopic forms until the identifiable world recedes, leaving only the logic of pattern. This is the most classical remainder structure in the chisel-construct cycle: what the chisel (the Risograph's physical constraints) cannot suppress becomes the generator of beauty, not noise to be cleared away.

Within the SAE aesthetics framework, this work occupies a specific moment in the chisel-construct cycle: Risograph animation as a practice is currently being approached by the art world, but not yet fully named or construed. "Animation," "printmaking," "artist's book" — each word partially covers it, none covers it completely. Field Notes for Wonder is simultaneously: a nature observation practice, a print experiment, a sensory training manual, a frame-by-frame motion work. Its internal logic is still growing. How exactly do the Risograph's physical constraints produce this particular aesthetic? To what degree is this "legitimizing error" and to what degree is this a genuinely new medium exploration? These questions don't have settled answers. That unresolvedness is the condition for 余项 to exist: the Already-Construct hasn't yet fully absorbed it.

There's a deeper structure worth noting: the project's central proposition — that macro and micro are isomorphic at the level of pattern — is itself a question being approached simultaneously by science, art, and philosophy, without any single field having given the complete answer. Why does the cross-section of moss look like a forest? Fractals? Self-similarity? Emergence? Each word is a fragment of the Already-Construct, but taken together they don't exhaust the mode of seeing Homayden's images open up. Her Risograph retains the misregistration, retains the pattern's self-similarity, retains the slowness of observation — these are the remainder, a double residue of machine and nature.

In a few years there will be exhibitions dedicated to Risograph animation as a discrete practice; institutions will archive its early practitioners; the misregistration effect will be cited as a known aesthetic strategy. Homayden's work will be entered into that construct as an early instance. But right now the work is still remainder — the art world is approaching it, hasn't completed the name. To encounter it now is to encounter a logic still in motion, before it crystallizes into the recognized. This is the essential temporality of 余项之美: it only exists at a specific present, and once that present passes, it becomes Already-Construct — raw material for the next chisel.
