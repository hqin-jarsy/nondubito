没有档案室的记忆

A Past With No Archive

Han Qin (秦汉)

Mirna Kramar 与 Karen Alim 在 Max Planck 动力学与自组织研究所（及慕尼黑工业大学）研究黏菌 Physarum polycephalum。这是一个单细胞生物，没有神经系统，身体是一张由管道组成的网，可以铺开几厘米乃至几米。他们的论文《Encoding memory in tube diameter hierarchy of living flow network》发表于 2021 年 2 月 23 日的 PNAS。发现很具体：当黏菌碰到食物，会释放一种化学物质，沿着管网传遍全身，使管壁变软，并把整体的迁移方向重新拨向食物。而"过去的进食事件被嵌在管径的层级里，也就是粗管与细管的排布里"——粗管像高速路，优先输送那种软化物质；先前喂食留下的印记，在进食之后仍然保留在粗细的格局中，决定之后往哪里走。

这是因果之美，而且是很干净的一例。因果之美的核心是：发生过的事不能不发生，它留在后来的东西里，给后来的东西一个来历。通常我们要在身体之外找这个"留"：年轮、遗迹、实验记录。这里没有外面。没有档案室，没有一个专门存放过去的器官；过去就是现在这张网的几何，而现在这张网正用这个几何去行动。文章里有一句话几乎是结构本身："逐渐软化的过程，正是既有印记起作用的地方，也是信息被存入、被读出的地方。"存与取，是同一个动作，在同一个身体里。

判断者不能不承认什么？不能不承认这一层：拿走"过去"，现在的粗细分布就失去来历，无法解释；而保留过去，也不需要另设一个"记忆体"来装它。这是一个形式在它自己的维度里展开到了头——因果被完整地还给了它的后果。它也不是别的东西：不是"没有大脑也很聪明"的趣闻，不是对仿生学有用，不是令人愉快，也不是重要。它之所以美，和"有用"无关，和人们对黏菌的惊叹也无关；它只要求你把因果看到底。我们还不能把它归约成流体力学或化学：那样做，就丢掉了"过去留在后来"这个维度本身。这一篇属于完备之美：安静，已经完成，没有生成之美的紧张。

它有没有余项？有一个很小、也很诚实的。"记忆"这个词在这里是借来的。管径里留下的是原因的残迹，像年轮一样；而第九条射线（认知之美）要问的是另一件事：缺席的东西还算数，还没来的东西先到了。黏菌被印记推向食物的方向，是不是已经包含这样的"先到"，论文摘要式的报道没有说，我也不替它说。这个缺口指向的，是下一个维度：残迹什么时候变成"对不在场者的掌握"。

现在看它的意义，是一个很朴素的提醒：我们习惯把"记得"想成有地方存、有东西读。这里有一个单细胞的例子，记得本身就是身体的形状。看见它，也就看见了因果之美最根本的形态：过去不在别处，过去就是现在正在长的样子。原文与报道见下。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学 · Ray 5

Mirna Kramar and Karen Alim, at the Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization and the Technical University of Munich, studied the slime mold Physarum polycephalum: a single cell with no nervous system, whose body is a network of tubes that can stretch from a few centimeters to meters. Their paper, "Encoding memory in tube diameter hierarchy of living flow network," appeared in PNAS on 23 February 2021. The finding is concrete. When the slime mold meets food, it releases a chemical that travels through the whole network, softens the tube walls, and turns the organism's migration toward the food. And, in the authors' words, "past feeding events are embedded in the hierarchy of tube diameters, specifically in the arrangement of thick and thin tubes in the network." Thick tubes act as highways that carry the softening chemical first; the imprint of earlier feeding persists in the pattern of thick and thin, and steers where the organism goes next.

This is the beauty of causation, in an unusually clean case. Its core is that what happened cannot un-happen; it stays in what comes after and gives it a provenance. Usually we look for that staying outside the body: tree rings, ruins, a lab notebook. Here there is no outside. No archive, no organ set aside for holding the past. The past is the geometry of the network as it is now, and the network is acting through that very geometry. One line from the report is almost the structure itself: "The gradual softening is where the existing imprints of previous food sources come into play and where information is stored and retrieved." Storing and retrieving are one act, in one body.

What is it that a judge cannot not acknowledge? This: take the past away and the present pattern of thick and thin loses its origin and cannot be explained; keep the past, and you need no separate memory store to hold it. A form has unfolded completely in its own dimension, and causation has been handed back whole to its consequence. It is also not the things it might be mistaken for: not a fun "smart without a brain" anecdote, not useful to biomimetics, not pleasant, not important. It is beautiful independently of usefulness and of anyone's wonder at slime molds; it asks only that you follow cause all the way down. Nor can it be reduced to fluid mechanics or chemistry without losing the very dimension in question, the past remaining in what follows. This belongs to completed beauty: quiet, finished, without the tension of the generative face.

Does it have a remainder? A small and honest one. "Memory" is a borrowed word here. What the tube diameters hold is the trace of a cause, as tree rings do; ray 9, the beauty of cognition, asks something further: that the absent still counts and that what has not come arrives first. Whether the slime mold's imprint-guided drift toward food already contains such an "arriving first" is not something the report says, and I will not say it for the organism. That gap points to the next dimension: when does a trace become a grip on what is not present?

Why see it now? As a plain reminder. We tend to picture remembering as having a place to store and something to read it with. Here is a single cell in which remembering is simply the shape of the body. Seeing it, you see the root form of causation: the past is not somewhere else; it is what the present is currently growing into. The report is linked below.
