Spectrum Slit：当电磁场成为无人感知的余项

Spectrum Slit: When the Electromagnetic Field Becomes an Unperceived Remainder

Han Qin (秦汉)

一个房间看起来是安静的。灯光稳定，墙面空白，空气中没有可见的运动。但在人的感官阈值之下，这个房间从来不是安静的。2.4 GHz和5 GHz频段里，WiFi路由器每秒发射数十万个数据包，蓝牙设备持续广播发现信号，智能家居传感器在固定间隔发送心跳帧。这些射频辐射以光速穿透墙壁、穿透身体、穿透每一个物体，但人类对此毫无感知。rootkid的Spectrum Slit所做的事情，是用软件定义无线电（SDR）连续扫描这些频段，将信号强度实时映射到一排64根LED灯丝上——每根灯丝对应频谱的一个切片，亮度直接由该频段的电磁强度驱动。

为什么这是余项之美？因为电磁场本身就是无线通信的余项——是凿构循环中被凿掉的那一面。现代无线基础设施的构建过程是一个极其精密的工程行为：协议栈、频率分配、调制方式、纠错编码，所有这些都是已构（construct）。在这个构建中，工程师关心的是数据的完整传输——电磁波只是载体，传输完成后它就是废弃物。没有人设计电磁场的美。没有人在意一个房间里WiFi信号强度的实时图形长什么样。这些信号的空间分布模式——在有人浏览视频时涌起、在深夜三点衰退、在一条消息发出时短暂闪烁——是通信行为的副产物，是工程的余项，是从来没有被观看过的东西。

Spectrum Slit的关键操作，是一次凿构逆转：它把工程的已构（数据传输协议）当作不可见的背景，把工程的余项（电磁场的空间-时间模式）拉到前景。64根灯丝组成的光带变成了集体数字行为的时间肖像——不是任何人设计的图案，而是所有在场者的无线活动叠加后的电磁沉积物。这和Andrei Jay的视频反馈（帧残留的累积）有结构上的相似性，但Spectrum Slit的余项来自一个更深的层次：它不是艺术过程的残留物，而是整个技术文明的残留物——我们每天泡在里面却完全无知的物理层。

rootkid是一个使用黑客化名的匿名艺术家，只有四件作品，没有画廊代理，没有艺术界背景。其整个实践围绕一个命题：技术和工程在艺术中被低估了。另一件作品Exhibit A让观众按下按钮就真实地运行一个暗网市场，将犯罪行为本身变成艺术媒介。这种实践位于雕塑、数据可视化、基础设施批评、电磁艺术（Christina Kubisch的传统）和监控艺术之间的缝隙中——没有哪个类别能完整命名它。现在观看Spectrum Slit比将来更重要，因为随着WiFi 7、IoT设备和永远在线的连接继续增殖，电磁余项会变得更加稠密，终有一天艺术界会给这种实践命名——那时它就是已构了。此刻它还在生长。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

A room looks quiet. The lighting is steady, the walls blank, no visible motion in the air. But beneath the threshold of human perception, a room is never quiet. In the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands, WiFi routers emit hundreds of thousands of data packets per second, Bluetooth devices continuously broadcast discovery signals, smart home sensors send heartbeat frames at fixed intervals. These radio-frequency emissions pass through walls, through bodies, through every object at the speed of light — and humans perceive none of it. What rootkid's Spectrum Slit does is use a software-defined radio (SDR) to continuously scan these bands and map signal intensity in real time onto a linear array of 64 LED filaments — each filament corresponding to a slice of the measured spectrum, its brightness driven directly by local electromagnetic intensity.

Why is this the beauty of the remainder? Because the electromagnetic field is itself the remainder of wireless communication — the side that gets chiseled away in the chisel-construct cycle. The construction of modern wireless infrastructure is an extraordinarily precise engineering act: protocol stacks, frequency allocation, modulation schemes, error-correcting codes — all of this is construct. In this construction, engineers care about the integrity of data transmission. The electromagnetic wave is merely a carrier; once transmission is complete, the wave is waste. No one designs the beauty of an electromagnetic field. No one cares what the real-time graph of WiFi signal strength in a room looks like. The spatial distribution patterns of these signals — surging when someone streams video, diminishing at 3 AM, flickering briefly when a message is sent — are byproducts of communication behavior. They are engineering's remainder, something that has never been watched.

Spectrum Slit's key operation is a chisel-construct reversal: it treats engineering's construct (data transmission protocols) as the invisible background and pulls engineering's remainder (the electromagnetic field's spatiotemporal patterns) into the foreground. The light band formed by 64 filaments becomes a temporal portrait of collective digital behavior — not a pattern anyone designed, but the electromagnetic sediment of all wireless activity by everyone present, superimposed. This bears structural similarity to Andrei Jay's video feedback work (accumulation of frame residue), but Spectrum Slit's remainder comes from a deeper layer: it is not the residue of an artistic process, but the residue of an entire technological civilization — the physical layer we soak in every day in complete ignorance.

rootkid is an anonymous artist working under a hacker handle, with only four works, no gallery representation, no art-world background. Their entire practice orbits a single proposition: technology and engineering are underrepresented in art. Another work, Exhibit A, lets viewers press a button to actually operate a darknet marketplace, turning the criminal act itself into the artistic medium. This practice sits in the gaps between sculpture, data visualization, infrastructure criticism, electromagnetic art (Christina Kubisch's tradition), and surveillance art — no single category can fully name it. Seeing Spectrum Slit now matters more than later, because as WiFi 7, IoT devices, and always-on connectivity continue to proliferate, the electromagnetic remainder will grow denser. Someday the art world will give this practice a name — and then it will be construct. Right now, it is still growing.
