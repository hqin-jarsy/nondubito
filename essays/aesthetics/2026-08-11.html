纽约街道时钟：城市的意外报时

NYC Street Clock: The City's Accidental Timepiece

Han Qin (秦汉)

Morry Kolman 与 Yufeng Zhao 共同制作的《纽约街道时钟》（clock.alltext.nyc）是一只真正在走的时钟——但它不靠指针，不靠数字显示屏，而靠纽约市街头的照片。每一分钟，时钟显示一张或几张谷歌街景照片，照片里恰好藏着此刻的时间。下午一点十六分，是 M116 路公共汽车驶过的身影；晚上六点五十，是一块清真餐厅的牌子，标价$6.50；午夜刚过，是第1204号百老汇的珠宝店招牌。照片里的这些数字并非为了报时而存在，它们是公交线路、价格、门牌，是城市的日常功能符号。而 Kolman 的脚本逐帧扫描了逾两万张街景图像，从中提取出偶然与当下时刻重合的数字，将它们拼成一只运行的时钟。

要理解《纽约街道时钟》的余项之美，需要先认清两个已构。第一个已构是谷歌街景本身：这是当代最彻底的城市空间文档系统，数十亿张照片将城市的每一面墙、每一扇橱窗、每一根路牌编入索引，形成一个完整的空间构形。第二个已构是数字时钟：时钟的形式已在几个世纪里被彻底穷尽——表盘、液晶、LED、原子钟——它是纯粹的功能性已构，没有任何剩余待凿。这两个已构叠加在一起，一个是对城市空间的穷尽，另一个是对时间表达的穷尽。

然而当谷歌的摄影机在凿构循环中扫过城市表面时，它留下了一个没有被命名的余项：城市表面铺满了数字。公交线路号、商品标价、建筑门牌、广告电话、营业时间——这些数字从未被设计用来报时，却在城市空间里无处不在。每一个时刻，都有若干这样的数字与当前时间重合。街景数据库的凿构行为——以空间为目的的摄影——意外保存了城市时间的沉积层。余项不是某个具体的图像，而是空间记录中无意间积累的时间内容。《纽约街道时钟》所做的，是把这层积累命名为一只时钟。

这个命名行为之所以是余项之美而非已构，在于它尚未被完全消化。《纽约时报》的"优选清单"在2026年5月提到了它，但没有给它一个范畴名称。是数据可视化？太过功能性。是街头摄影？方向错了。是时钟设计？遗漏了概念核心。是观念艺术？又太宽泛。余项之美的标志之一，正是被多个现有范畴同时部分包含，却不被其中任何一个完整容纳。命名缝隙本身就是余项存在的证明。

《纽约街道时钟》并非孤立的灵感，而是 Kolman 整个创作逻辑的一个节点。他的《第一道光》寻找被计算机发现却从未被人类目光触及的星星——天文数据集中的未见余项；他的《MNIST 历史》追踪1989年为机器学习提供手写样本的三千五百名普通人，在最经典的训练数据库里寻找被抹去的个体余项；《交通摄像头照相亭》发现了城市监控基础设施中未被官方利用的个人表达空间。一致的方法论是：任何系统在完成其指定功能之后，总会留下某种剩余物。Kolman 的工作是收集这些剩余物，并让它们重新变得可见。

余项之美要求我们在它尚未成为已构之前看见它。《纽约街道时钟》目前仍处于最敏感的命名缝隙中：足够简洁，以至于难以被现有理论框架锁定；足够诗意，以至于任何单一的功能标签都会缩减它。此刻看它，是在它被某个艺术范畴彻底归并之前，在它的余项尚未沉淀为新的已构之前。城市一直在无意中报时。直到有人写了一段脚本、扫遍两万张照片，我们才听见了它。

NYC Street Clock (clock.alltext.nyc), made in 2026 by Morry Kolman and Yufeng Zhao, is a clock that runs on photographs. At 1:16 PM, it shows the M116 bus caught mid-route by Google Street View; at 6:50 PM, a halal food stand with a "$6.50" price sign; past midnight, the awning of the jewelry store at 1204 Broadway. None of these images were taken to show the time. The bus number is a transit designation, the price a market rate, the address a postal coordinate. Kolman and Zhao's scripts scanned over 20,000 Street View captures of New York City and extracted the numbers that—at some moment in the day—happen to match the current time, assembling them into a running clock.

To understand why this is beautiful in the SAE sense—余项之美, the beauty of the remainder—begin with the two constructs it works against. The first is Google Street View itself: the most comprehensive spatial document of the city ever assembled, billions of images that index every surface of human habitation. The chisel-construct cycle of Street View is complete. The second construct is the digital clock: a form so thoroughly optimized—analog, digital, LED, atomic—that there is nothing left to discover about it as a form. These are both fully already-construct (已构): total systems that appear to leave no remainder.

But when Street View's cameras swept the city, they photographed surfaces covered in numbers. Bus routes, price tags, building addresses, phone numbers, years, hours of operation—none of these were placed in the city to tell time. Yet a city is an ocean of numbers, and at every moment, some subset of them happens to match the current time. The spatial documentation project—with its purely geographic purpose—accidentally preserved the city's temporal remainder: not any one photograph, but the accumulated temporal content that lives, unremarked, inside the spatial archive. NYC Street Clock names this remainder as a timepiece.

The naming act is still 余项之美, not yet already-construct, because it has not been fully digested. The New York Times put it on their "Good List" in May 2026 but gave it no categorical name. It is not data visualization (too functional a framing), not street photography (the wrong direction of attention), not clock design (misses the conceptual core), not systems art (too academic a register). One signature of 余项之美 is being partly contained by several existing categories while not being fully claimed by any of them. The gap in naming is itself evidence that the remainder is still alive.

NYC Street Clock is a node in a coherent larger logic. First Light looks for stars discovered by computers but never seen by human eyes—the unwitnessed remainder of astronomical data collection. MNIST Histories is a work-in-progress recovering the identities of the 3,500 census workers and high school students who gave handwriting samples in 1989 to train the most canonical machine-learning dataset in existence—human remainder inside the most machine-processed construct. Traffic Cam Photobooth redirected a surveillance infrastructure toward personal expression, finding the individual remainder in the city's monitoring apparatus. In each case, the method is the same: a completed system, once closed, always leaves something behind. Kolman's work is the practice of collecting that surplus.

余项之美 asks us to see a remainder before it becomes construct. NYC Street Clock is still in the most interesting gap: simple enough that no existing theoretical frame can fully lock it down, poetic enough that any functional label diminishes it. See it now, before it gets absorbed into some tidy genre, before its remainder sediments into a new already-construct. The city has always been telling time by accident. It took a script and twenty thousand photographs to hear it.
