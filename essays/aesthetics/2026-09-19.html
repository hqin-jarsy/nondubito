滴答是咬住的声音

The Tick Is the Sound of Catching

Han Qin (秦汉)

九月中旬，一个叫 Many Vices 的人传了一段视频上去，标题是《复原一台 370 年前的钟——而且它不出声》。复原的对象，是 1656 年 Campani 兄弟为教皇亚历山大七世造的那台夜钟；材料是挤出来的塑料。他手上全部的依据，是意大利钟表师 Carlo G. Croce 2018 年发在《Horological Journal》上的一篇文章，外加 Croce 自己拍的一批视频——那台黄铜复制品，是 Croce 和另外两个从没见过面的论坛网友，为了一个意大利摆钟论坛的十周年一起造出来的，一人管机芯，一人管钟壳，一人管钟面上那幅画。Croce 写得很实在：没有常规夹板，也没有可用的图纸，所以每一个零件都是「一边想象它将如何与别的零件相互作用，一边做出来的」。他用了一根 9 米长的发条，目标走满 24 小时，最后「遗憾地」只走到 20 小时。而 Many Vices 做了一件 Croce 没做、370 年里也没人做过的事：他把两种擒纵都打印出来，装在同一台机器上，一前一后跑给你听。先是标准锚式擒纵，滴、答、滴、答。再是 Campani 的偏心曲柄，轮系连续转动，没有声音。然后他当场把账算给你看——安静的那台，跑不了多久就停了。

这台钟的来历本身是一串失败。最早那个想法不是 Campani 的，是威尼斯公证人 Attilio Parisio 的：一只密封的鼓，内部隔成几个「房间」，房间之间只留一个小孔，里面的液体把鼓推着转过 270°，剩下的 90° 靠重力自己落下去，一圈就换一次钟点。Parisio 把做法写成了手稿，但写得足够含混，别人抄不走。Campani 兄弟之一 Pier Tommaso 是梵蒂冈的 temperatore——负责维护宫里钟表的技工。他领的是维护的差事，不是发明的委托，而这恰恰是他得以把那台钟看得很近的原因；多年以后，他会在自己的夜钟上签下 Petrus Thomas Campanus Inventor Romae 1682。兄弟俩照着做了第一台静音夜钟，鼓用象牙，液体用水银。然后两样都坏了：象牙有孔隙，水银怕温差，钟要不停地伺候，而且走不准。静音擒纵是在这之后才出现的——它不是一个构想的实现，它是一次失败之后的替补方案。顺带一提，这套机构到底该记在哪个兄弟名下，至今也没定：Croce 写是 Giuseppe，钟表业界的说法多半给 Pier Tommaso，百科条目把「无声钟」与「发光钟面」一并记在第三个兄弟 Matteo 名下，Hackaday 索性含糊成「Campani 兄弟」。一件东西连署名都还没凝固，这本身就是它尚未被完全消化的证据。

但真正要紧的不是安静，是安静底下那件事。Hackaday 评论区有个 ID 叫 Dude 的人，用三条回帖把它说穿了，而且比正文说得准：常规擒纵的全部原理是解耦——每个周期只给摆一记短促的踢，踢完就松手，让它自己荡；决定时间的是摆自身的固有周期，动力源只负责把损耗补回去。Campani 的曲柄从不松手。轮系与摆持续咬合，它是一个受迫振子，所以周期取决于你施加多大扭矩，扭矩一变，钟就漂。也就是说，输掉的那一支根本不是「更差的钟」，它是另一套关于时间测量的理论：锚式说，时间是一个自由物体自己做出来的事，机器只负责不让它停；曲柄说，时间是一段持续耦合最终稳定下来的状态。而滴答，从来就不是时间的声音。锚式擒纵里最响的那一下，是擒纵轮的一枚齿撞上叉瓦、被咬住——滴答是「咬住」的声音，是一副轮系被停住、放走、再停住的可听签名。静音不是一台钟被调轻了音量：一个从不松手的机构也就从不咬住，所以它没有东西可响。教皇要的只是能睡个觉，他无意中选中的却是一门对立的形而上学；它输了，而赢家的声音，从此被整个世界当成时间本身的声音。

为什么这个余项到现在还活着，看两件事就够了。第一，把输掉的那一支重新做一遍，过去意味着一间黄铜作坊和一年工期，于是「我们当初放弃了什么」这个问题在经济上根本不成立——没人拦着你问，只是没人付得起答案。塑料把它变成了一个下午，而且是一个可以并排对跑、当场听差别的下午。凿削本身第一次成了可以拿在手里的实验对象。第二，这个设计空间此刻还是敞着的，而且正在有人往里填。Hackaday 那条帖子底下，一个 ID 叫 Dude 的人转述了别处有人提的苏格兰轭——用它代替滑块曲柄，至少能得到对称的正弦摆动——然后自己接着往下推：用瓦特连杆彻底消掉那道滑槽，用凸轮加从动件去定制加速度曲线。Many Vices 自己加了一套直线导轨的弹簧摆；Hackaday 的编辑在琢磨把飞轮频率调到与摆同步；还有人报告说磁铁和铜片的涡流制动把整台机器拖慢的程度出乎意料，而且很好调。这些都是没被造出来的分支，没有正典，没有教程，没有名字。一个连命名都还没有的设计空间，正是余项最完整的形态。

而已经被消化掉的那一半，恰好说明了美学世界是怎么挑食的。Campani 这台钟同时留下两样东西：静音擒纵，和一种全新的读时方式——十二枚镂空的钟点牌串成一条链，被灯火从背后照亮，一枚一枚从窗口的左边升起、横穿、落下，像太阳过境一样。后面这一样被完整地接管了。1991 年爱彼把它装回腕表，做成 Star Wheel；1997 年那家叫 URWERK 的独立品牌带着它进了市场，Baumgartner 兄弟的父亲是修古董钟的，Campani 夜钟曾经过他的工作台。今天这项机构有正式名称（漫游时）、有目录页、有报价、有三维卫星的现代演绎。静音擒纵没有跟过去。差别不在难度，在可见性：钟点牌是可以拍照的，而一台安静的机器结构上就无法被展示——它的全部内容是一件不发生的事。美学制度命名它能陈列的东西，剩下的留在原地。更彻底的反证在今天的钟表店里：一只石英挂钟压根没有擒纵，没有任何东西在被放走或被咬住，可它照样滴答——那是步进电机每秒推一次轮系的副产品；想要那种连续扫秒的机芯，你得加钱。构固化到这个地步——声音比产生它的机构活得更久，如今悬在半空，底下已经什么都没有了。

所以为什么现在看比以后看更要紧。此刻它还摆在正确的位置上：一段业余频道的视频，一篇 2018 年发在钟表协会会刊上的复制记录，三个没见过面的人和一个意大利论坛的十周年，一份诚实写着「遗憾地只走到 20 小时」的数据，一条总共 12 条的评论串，里面有一个人连发三帖，自己跟自己推演苏格兰轭和瓦特连杆。没有人宣称这是艺术，也没有人宣称这是修复——修复的前提是对象的类别已经定了，而这件事连署名和名字都还没定。等它拿到名字的那天——「静音机构」成为一档可选配置，偏心曲柄成为某个高端系列的卖点，这段历史被压缩成产品页上的一行故事——电子邮件里会写「灵感源自 1656 年的教皇夜钟」，而那个真正的问题，也就是我们为什么默认时间是一颗一颗的、而不是连成一条的，会随着那段话一起被封进玻璃展柜。此刻它还只是有人把两台机器摆在一起，让你自己听。

In mid-September someone called Many Vices posted a video titled "Recreating a 370 Year Old Clock — That Runs SILENTLY." The object being recreated is the night clock the Campani brothers built for Pope Alexander VII in 1656; the material is extruded plastic. Everything he has to work from is an article the Italian watchmaker Carlo G. Croce published in the Horological Journal in 2018, plus Croce's own videos — that brass replica having been built by Croce and two forum acquaintances he had never met in person, to mark the tenth anniversary of an Italian pendulum-clock forum, one man taking the movement, one the case, one the painting on the dial. Croce is plain about the conditions: no conventional plates, no available drawings, so every component was "developed while imagining how it would interact with the others." He used a 9-metre mainspring, aimed at a 24-hour duration, and reports that he "sadly" achieved only 20 hours. What Many Vices then does is something Croce did not do and nobody has done in 370 years: he prints both escapements, mounts them in the same machine, and runs them one after the other so you can hear the difference. First the standard anchor escapement — tick, tock, tick, tock. Then the Campani eccentric crank, the train turning continuously, silent. Then he shows you the bill: the quiet one stops rather soon.

The clock's ancestry is a chain of failures. The original idea was not Campani's but that of a Venetian notary, Attilio Parisio: a hermetically sealed drum, partitioned into "rooms" joined by a single small hole, the liquid inside driving the drum through 270° while the remaining 90° dropped under its own weight, and each revolution changing the hour. Parisio wrote up the method in a manuscript cryptic enough that nobody could copy it. One of the Campani brothers, Pier Tommaso, was temperatore to the Vatican — the technician who maintained the palace clocks. His post was maintenance, not invention, which is exactly why he got to look at the thing so closely; years later he would sign night clocks Petrus Thomas Campanus Inventor Romae 1682. The brothers built their first silent night clock on that drum, in ivory, with mercury inside. Both choices failed: ivory is porous, mercury reacts to temperature, the clock needed constant attention and kept poor time. The silent escapement arrived only after that. It is not the realisation of a design; it is the fallback after a failure. Worth noting too that it has never settled whose invention it was — Croce credits Giuseppe, the watch trade mostly credits Pier Tommaso, the encyclopedia entry assigns both the noiseless clock and the illuminated dial to a third brother, Matteo, and Hackaday hedges to "the Campani brothers." A thing whose attribution has not yet hardened is by that fact still undigested.

But the point was never the quiet. It is what the quiet is made of. In the Hackaday comments a user called Dude works it out across three replies, more precisely than the article does: the whole principle of a conventional escapement is decoupling — give the oscillator one short kick per cycle and then let go, so that what determines time is the pendulum's own natural period and the power source merely replaces losses. The Campani crank never lets go. Train and pendulum stay continuously engaged, which makes it a forced oscillator, which means the period depends on how much torque you apply, and drifts when the torque does. So the branch that lost is not a worse clock. It is a rival theory of what measuring time is. The anchor says time is what a free body does by itself and the machine's job is to stop it dying. The crank says time is the equilibrium a continuously coupled system settles into. And the tick was never the sound of time. The loudest event in an anchor escapement is a tooth of the escape wheel landing on a pallet and locking — the tick is the sound of catching, the audible signature of a train that is stopped, released, and stopped again. Silence is not a clock with the volume turned down: a mechanism that never lets go never catches either, so it has nothing to sound with. The Pope wanted to sleep, and what he inadvertently commissioned was an opposing metaphysics; it lost, and the winner's noise has been mistaken for the sound of time ever since.

Two things show why this remainder is still alive. First, rebuilding the losing branch used to mean a brass workshop and a year, which made the question "what exactly did we give up?" not forbidden but unaffordable — nobody was stopping you from asking; nobody could pay for the answer. Plastic turns it into an afternoon, and specifically into an afternoon where both branches can be run back to back and the difference simply heard. The act of severing has itself become an object you can hold. Second, the design space is still open and people are visibly filling it right now. Under the Hackaday post, Dude relays someone else's suggestion of a Scotch yoke in place of the slider crank — at least it swings symmetrically — and then pushes on alone: a Watt's linkage to eliminate the slot entirely, a cam and follower to tune the acceleration profile to order. Many Vices himself has added a linear-rail spring pendulum; the editor wonders aloud about matching the flywheel frequency to the pendulum; another commenter reports being surprised by how much the magnet-and-copper eddy-current braking slowed the whole thing down, and how easily it tuned. These are unbuilt branches. There is no canon, no tutorial, no name. A design space that has not been named yet is a remainder in its most complete form.

The half that has been digested shows exactly how the aesthetic establishment eats. The Campani clock left two things behind: the silent escapement, and a new way of reading time — twelve fret-worked hour cartouches on a chain, backlit by a lamp, each rising at the left of a window, crossing it, and setting at the right, like the sun in transit. That second thing was taken over whole. Audemars Piguet put it back on the wrist as the Star Wheel in 1991; in 1997 an independent brand called URWERK brought it to market, the Baumgartner brothers' father having restored antique clocks with Campani night clocks passing across his bench. Today the mechanism has a formal name (the wandering hour), a catalogue page, a price, and a three-dimensional satellite update. The silent escapement did not come along. The difference is not difficulty but visibility: the cartouches photograph, and a silent machine is structurally undisplayable — its entire content is something that fails to happen. The institution names what it can exhibit and leaves the rest where it lies. The sharper proof is in any clock shop today: a quartz wall clock has no escapement at all, nothing being released and nothing being caught, and it ticks anyway — that noise is a byproduct of a stepping motor shoving the train forward once a second. If you want the continuous sweep, you pay more. The construct has set that hard — the sound has outlived the mechanism that made it, and now hangs there with nothing underneath.

Which is why seeing it now matters more than seeing it later. At the moment it sits in exactly the right place: an amateur channel's video, a replication note in a horological society journal, three strangers and a forum's tenth birthday, a figure honestly reported as 20 hours instead of 24, and a thread of 12 comments in which one man works through Scotch yokes and Watt's linkages in three consecutive replies to himself. Nobody has claimed this as art, and nobody has claimed it as restoration either — restoration presumes the category of the object is settled, and here neither the name nor the attribution has settled. The day it gets a name — "silent movement" as a configurable option, the eccentric crank as a flagship talking point, this whole history compressed into one line of copy — the email will say inspired by a papal night clock of 1656, and the actual question, which is why we assume time comes in grains rather than in a continuous line, will go into the vitrine along with it. Right now it is still someone putting two machines side by side and letting you listen.
