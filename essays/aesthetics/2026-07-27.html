参数的余项：达伦·巴斯坦与BEAST Repeat里的无名声响

Parameters of the Remainder: Dillon Bastan's BEAST Repeat and the Sounds With No Name Yet

Han Qin (秦汉)

BEAST Repeat是Dillon Bastan今年七月发布的一个免费Max for Live设备。它的起点是Ableton Live内置的Beat Repeat——一个用于制造节奏性重复、切割和颤抖效果的经典插件。Bastan将这个设备的参数空间扩展到了一个惊人的程度：他不只是增加了几个旋钮，而是重新设计了重复的时间域行为，加入了步进衰减（stepped decay）、飞溅（spray）、回旋（boomerang）曲线、FFT音高变换、共振峰位移（formant shifting）、快照系统（snapshots）……每一项参数都可以在每一次重复上施加独立的衰变轨迹，向上或向下，在固定的三角分布中回弹，与门控宽度交织。他把一个已知的工具凿开，让它变成了一个远远超越自身原始功能的机器。

Beat Repeat是已构（already-construct）。它的声音被命名过了：节奏毛刺、IDM切片、stutter效果、鼓组织体里的不稳定性。二十年来，无数电子音乐教程教人如何使用Beat Repeat，赋予它一套稳定的使用语法。那些语法本身就是凿构循环（chisel-construct cycle）已经完成的标志——余项（remainder）曾经在那里，当有人第一次把音频送入Beat Repeat时，它存在于「按照正弦曲线重复一段鼓」与「你根本无法预料的切割声」之间的缝隙里；而现在那条缝隙已经被命名、被收编进了风格标签，成为了构（construct）的一部分。Bastan的凿是重新打开那道缝的动作。

他打开的东西是参数空间的余项。当共振峰位移以步进衰减的方式在八次重复中向上走，同时音高包络在回旋模式下在三角分布里往返，同时飞溅参数给每一次重复注入随机的栅格宽度——这时产生的声音是什么？它不是IDM，不是毛刺，不是节奏性重复，也不是任何现有风格词汇能描述的东西。CDM的Peter Kirn开玩笑地造了一个词：「beastiful」，这本身就是命名危机的症状。一个正经的名字还没来，但声音已经存在了。这个缝隙——这个参数空间里人类耳朵已经抵达但语言还没抵达的领域——就是余项所在之处。

为什么Bastan的这件工作值得单独讨论，而不只是把它视作一件功能性工具？因为他的整个实践有一种系统性的特征：他一直在针对音乐软件里的已构边界做有准备的凿击。Swarmalators把群体智能数学（swarm intelligence）引入合成器，产生的声音没有流派名称；Superposition把马尔可夫链（Markov chain）引入多重采样器，产生的声音没有流派名称；Spectral Attractors把奇异吸引子（strange attractors）引入频谱合成，产生的声音没有流派名称。每一个设备都是一次凿：把一个稳定的音乐构（sampler、sequencer、effect）的参数空间扩张到它的边界之外，然后在那个边界之外留下一片未命名的领土。BEAST Repeat是他迄今为止最直接的案例，因为它的原型（Beat Repeat）是如此彻底地已构，让余项的位移对比变得极为清晰。

现在是看这件事的时刻，因为命名压力已经开始积累。音乐技术媒体已经报道了它；YouTube教程正在制作中；制作人会慢慢把BEAST Repeat里某些参数组合固化成预设、固化成个人风格、固化成可识别的声音美学。这一过程是不可避免的——余项终将被新一轮凿构循环收编进构。但就在此刻，那个被打开的空间还是开放的，还是活的，还在等待名字的到来。现在听它，是听到了那个等待本身。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

BEAST Repeat is a free Max for Live device released by Dillon Bastan in July 2026. It begins with Ableton's built-in Beat Repeat — the classic plugin for rhythmic stutters, cuts, and repeats — and expands its parameter space to a startling degree. Not just more knobs, but a complete redesign of how repetition unfolds in time: stepped decay that can travel upward or downward across each successive repeat, spray that injects randomness into every grid setting, boomerang curves that make parameters arc back through a triangular distribution, FFT pitch shifting that preserves tempo while altering pitch, formant shifting with its own decay trajectory, an eight-slot snapshot system for morphing between states. Every parameter can decay independently, per-repeat. He took a known machine, carved into it, and left something that vastly exceeds the original's functional footprint.

Beat Repeat is already-construct. Its sounds have been named: beat stutter, IDM chop, rhythmic glitch, drum instability. Two decades of tutorials have built a stable grammar around it. That grammar is precisely the sign that the chisel-construct cycle has completed — the remainder existed once, in the gap between "loop a drum segment cleanly" and "something you couldn't predict"; now that gap is named, absorbed into style tags, and has become construct. Bastan's act is to reopen the gap. He does this not by rejecting Beat Repeat but by chiseling directly into its boundary — pulling its parameter space past the point where existing categories can follow.

What he opens is the remainder of the parameter space. When formant shifting steps upward across eight repeats while pitch boomerangs through a triangular distribution and spray randomizes the gate width per repeat — what is the sound that results? It isn't IDM. It isn't glitch. It isn't rhythmic repetition in any genre-nameable sense. Peter Kirn at CDM half-jokingly coined "beastiful," which is itself a symptom of the naming crisis. A real name hasn't arrived, but the sounds are already here. That gap — where the ear has reached but language has not — is precisely where the remainder lives.

Why discuss BEAST Repeat as something more than a functional tool? Because Bastan's broader practice has a systematic quality: he is consistently attacking the already-construct boundaries of music software. Swarmalators brought swarm-intelligence mathematics into synthesis — the resulting sounds have no genre name. Superposition brought Markov chains into multi-sampling — no genre name. Spectral Attractors brought strange attractors into spectral synthesis — no genre name. Each device is a chisel: it extends a stable musical construct (sampler, sequencer, effect) past its edge, and what lies beyond that edge is unnamed territory. BEAST Repeat is his clearest case yet, because its source material — Beat Repeat — is so thoroughly construct that the displacement into the remainder is especially legible by contrast.

Now is the time to look at this, because the naming pressure has already begun. Music tech media has covered it. Tutorial videos are being made. Producers will crystallize certain parameter combinations into presets, into personal aesthetics, into recognizable sounds. The remainder will eventually be absorbed into construct by the next turn of the chisel-construct cycle — this is inevitable. But at this moment the opened space is still open, still alive, still waiting for its names to arrive. To listen now is to hear the waiting itself.
