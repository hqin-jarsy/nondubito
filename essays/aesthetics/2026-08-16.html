一个词刚刚离开它的作者

A Word Just Left Its Author

Han Qin (秦汉)

今年五月，Nathalie Lawhead 在自己的网站上放出了一件浏览器作品，名叫 Meanderware: Things I loved about cyberspace。它只在桌面浏览器里跑，用的是最朴素的 JavaScript 和 CSS。你在几个空间里走动，空间里立着巨大的骨架，你从骨架身上收集"想法"；空间里另有一些门，门是超链接，推开就把你送到互联网别的地方去——都是些小站、个人页、手作的网络角落。走到球池就算走完了。

她把这类东西叫做"可玩的随笔"（playable essay），去年那件《个人主义在死互联网时代》是同一类。而"meanderware"这个词，是九十年代末别人用来形容她第一个网络艺术项目的说法，她一直很喜欢，这次拿回来当作品名。

值得注意的不是作品本身有多完成，而是接下来三个星期里发生的事。六月初，一篇写另一件作品（Texturetown，一个用 Club Penguin、加勒比海盗网游等已死 MMO 的残骸拼起来的怪物）的文章里出现了这样一句：也可以把它归类为 Lawhead 最近命名的那种"meanderware"，即献给朴素超链接之诗学的游戏设计。一个作者的自称，在三个星期里变成了别人手里的类名。

这正是凿构循环被抓了个现行的时刻。在 SAE 的语言里，一个已构（construct）之所以是已构，是因为它已经有了完整的名字，因而可以被引用、被归类、被安全地转述。"net art"是已构，"walking sim"是已构，"生成艺术"更是已构得彻底——它们都还美，但那种美是上一轮凿击留下的沉积，不再是活的余项。而"meanderware"此刻还不是。它有一个词，但这个词还没有边界：它到底指"以超链接为诗学的作品"，还是"某种漫游式的浏览器软件"，还是"Lawhead 这一路人马做的东西"？三个月里没有任何人给出定义，而已经有人在用它归类别人的作品。名字先于定义存在的这段空档，就是余项本身。

这里有一个需要正面处理的张力：Lawhead 本人并不是无名者。她有维基词条，有 Rhizome 的艺术家专访，得过 IGF。按"非艺术界建制"这条标准，她似乎不该入选。但要分清楚被命名的是什么。被艺术界消化掉的是"Nathalie Lawhead 这位网络艺术家"——那是已构，是二十多年凿击的沉积。没有被消化的是她这三个月做的这件事：把超链接重新当作可玩的材料，并且顺手给它起了个词，而这个词已经脱手了。作者的知名度和作品的未命名度，是两条独立的曲线。SAE 关心的从来是后者。

她自己在文里说了一句很实在的话：这类东西"太怪了，没法被简化成可解析的内容"，所以它对 AI 内容磨坊没用。这话可以读得更结构一点——可解析性正是已构的技术指标。一样东西能被稳定地抽取、归类、再生产，说明它的名字已经完整。Meanderware 现在还抽不动，不是因为它高深，而是因为写作、声音、代码、图形被搅在了一起，谁也不是主导项。这种"搅在一起"的状态不会持续太久。

为什么现在看比以后看要紧？因为这个词正在被填。每多一篇文章用"meanderware"归类一件作品，它的边界就清晰一分；等到有人写出第一段像样的定义，或者第一份策展说明把它排进谱系，它就完成了从余项到已构的迁移。到那时它仍然会是好看的——但那是历史的好看。现在还能看到的，是一个类名尚未合拢时的样子：一件作品，一个刚被造出来的词，和一个已经在用这个词、但还说不清它到底指什么的读者群。这个空档窄得很。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

In May, Nathalie Lawhead put a browser piece on her own site called Meanderware: Things I loved about cyberspace. It runs on desktop browsers only, built out of plain JavaScript and unruly CSS. You wander through spaces populated by enormous skeletons; you collect thoughts from the skeletons. There are also doors, and the doors are hyperlinks — push one and you are somewhere else on the internet entirely, usually a small site, a personal page, some handmade corner. You've reached the end when you find the ball pit.

She calls this kind of thing a playable essay; last year's individualism in the dead-internet age was the same form. The word "meanderware" is older: it's what people called her first net art project in the late nineties. She always liked it, and this time she took it back as a title.

What matters here isn't how finished the piece is. It's what happened over the following three weeks. In early June, a write-up of a completely different work — Texturetown, a Frankenstein MMO assembled from the corpses of Club Penguin, Pirates of the Caribbean Online, and other dead worlds — contained this sentence: you could also categorise it as an expression of what Nathalie Lawhead has recently termed "meanderware," game design devoted to the poetics of the humble hyperlink. An artist's name for her own work had become, in three weeks, somebody else's category.

That is the chisel-construct cycle caught mid-stroke. In SAE terms, a construct is a construct because it has a complete name — which is what lets it be cited, sorted, safely paraphrased. "Net art" is construct. "Walking sim" is construct. "Generative art" is construct all the way down. They are all still beautiful, but that beauty is sediment left by an earlier strike, not a living remainder. "Meanderware" isn't there yet. It has a word but no boundary: does it mean work whose poetics are hyperlinks, or a genre of wandering browser software, or simply the kind of thing Lawhead and her lineage make? Nobody has defined it in three months, and people are already using it to file other artists' work. That gap — where the name exists before the definition does — is the remainder itself.

There's a tension worth meeting head-on. Lawhead is not obscure. She has a Wikipedia entry, a Rhizome artist profile, an IGF award. By the rule that says not art-world establishment, she looks disqualified. But be precise about what has been named. What the art world digested is "Nathalie Lawhead, net artist" — construct, the sediment of twenty-five years of chiselling. What it has not digested is the thing she did three months ago: treat the hyperlink as playable material again, and coin a word for it that has since slipped out of her hands. An artist's recognition and a work's namelessness are two independent curves. SAE has only ever cared about the second.

She says something usefully blunt in her own write-up: this sort of thing is "too weird to be of use to the AI content mill," because it can't be reduced to parseable content. Read that structurally and parseability turns out to be the technical signature of a construct. A thing that can be reliably extracted, sorted, and reproduced is a thing whose name is finished. Meanderware resists extraction not because it's difficult but because the writing, the sound, the code, and the drawing are tangled and none of them dominates. That tangle has a shelf life.

Why does seeing it now matter more than later? Because the word is being filled in as we watch. Every article that files a work under "meanderware" sharpens its edge by a degree. The moment somebody writes a serviceable definition, or the first curatorial note slots it into a lineage, it completes the migration from remainder to construct. It will still be lovely then — historically lovely. What is visible only now is the shape of a category before it closes: one piece of work, a word freshly minted, and a readership already using that word without being able to say what it means. The window is narrow.
