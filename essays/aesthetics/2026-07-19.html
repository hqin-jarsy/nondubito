SIGN/e：DAW 时间轴第一次向外说话

SIGN/e: The First Time the DAW Timeline Spoke Outward

Han Qin (秦汉)

Evan Montpellier 为蒙特利尔大学 Laboratoire formes • ondes 编写了一组免费的 Max for Live 插件，名为 SIGN/e——Symbol-based Interface for Graphic Notation/Edition，图形记谱符号界面。它直接嵌入 Ableton Live 的时间轴：作曲家将 PNG 图像放置在会话中，然后对它们进行动画处理。圆圈可以旋转，以指示演奏者应当执行一个滚动动作；线条可以滑动，以引导滑奏。演奏者在演出中实时读取这些移动符号，而不是纸张上的静态音符。2026年5月12日，v1.0.0 正式发布，配套学术论文由 Pierre-Luc Lecours 与 Nicolas Bernier 联合撰写，于同年2月发表于剑桥大学学术期刊《Organised Sound》。工具免费，代码开源，可从 GitHub（LFO-lab/SIGNe）下载。

理解 SIGN/e 的结构地位，需要识别它同时凿开了哪些已构。第一个已构：西方音符记谱法（CWN），五百年来作为连接作曲家与演奏者的普遍语法，由 Sibelius、Finale、Dorico 在数字时代延伸，但始终是独立于演奏软件的文档。第二个已构：DAW 会话作为封闭的电子演奏媒介——Ableton Live 的时间轴向内播放，指向扬声器；演奏者是产品制作人，不是乐谱的读者。第三个已构：20世纪50至60年代的前卫图形乐谱传统（Cage、Feldman、Cardew），如今是美术馆里的历史文献，已被艺术界完整命名，进入教科书。SIGN/e 凿开的，正是这三个已构的交点：乐谱活在 DAW 会话内部，是动态的而非静态的；DAW 时间轴第一次向外——指向活人演奏者，而不只是指向扬声器。

凿开之后，剩下来的是什么？余项有两层。第一层：动态图形符号作为真正意义上的新记谱原语——旋转的圆圈、滑动的线段。这些是在任何现有记谱语法中都不存在的符号，而它们恰好适合记录电子音乐所特有的"受控意外"（Nicolas Bernier 的措辞）：旋转旋钮、扫过合成器、从复杂处理链中涌现出来的声音——CWN 在结构上根本无法记录这类动作。第二层：DAW 时间轴作为电子音乐与活人演奏者之间的共享时间媒介。指挥者、记谱者与演奏机器第一次共用同一条时间轴。这个潜力自从90年代起就潜伏在每一台有时间轴的 DAW 里，从未被激活，直到 SIGN/e 出现。

SIGN/e 所对抗的已构正在快速关闭。人工智能音乐生成（Suno、Udio、Stable Audio）正在固化"电子音乐=封闭的AI产品=无需活人演奏者"的等式——DAW 向内播放的模型即将完全密封。TENOR 国际记谱技术会议（2026年5月，维也纳）与《Organised Sound》学术论文是第一批靠近这片领地的命名尝试，但命名尚未完成。Nicolas Bernier 与吉他手 Simon Trottier 的夏季合作正在进行，说明这个逻辑还在生长，尚未凝固成已构。在凿构循环里，此刻 SIGN/e 处于凿开阶段：被激活，但还没有被完整地给出名字。艺术界的命名从来都是滞后的——当"嵌入 DAW 的动态图形记谱"变成一个现成类别的名字，今天看见的这片领地就变成了已构的沉淀。

现在看，它还是活的。

Programmer Evan Montpellier, working with Nicolas Bernier's Laboratoire formes • ondes at Université de Montréal, built a suite of free Max for Live devices called SIGN/e — Symbol-based Interface for Graphic Notation/Edition. Rather than drawing graphic symbols in Illustrator, exporting an image, and then struggling to align it against a DAW timeline pixel by pixel, SIGN/e places those symbols directly inside an Ableton Live session. A PNG is placed on the timeline and animated in real time: a circle can rotate to indicate a roll; a line can scroll to guide a glissando. Live performers read these moving symbols during performance — not static notes on paper. Version 1.0.0 was released May 12, 2026. An accompanying paper by Pierre-Luc Lecours and Nicolas Bernier appeared in the Cambridge journal Organised Sound in February 2026. The tool is free and open source, available at GitHub (LFO-lab/SIGNe).

SIGN/e's structural position becomes clear when you identify the three already-constructs it chisels simultaneously. First: Common Western Notation as the universal grammar connecting composers to performers — 500 years of staff, clef, and note, extended digitally by Sibelius, Finale, and Dorico, always as a separate document disconnected from performance software. Second: the DAW session as closed electronic performance — Ableton's timeline aims inward at speakers; performers are producers, not score-readers. Third: the avant-garde graphic-score tradition (Cage, Feldman, Cardew, 1950s–70s) — now fully art-world-named historical artifacts, canonical, museumified. SIGN/e chisels the intersection of all three: the score lives inside the DAW session, animated not static; the DAW timeline points outward toward live performers, not only toward speakers, for the first time.

What the chisel opens is the remainder. It has two layers. The first is the animated graphic symbol as a genuinely new notational primitive — the rotating circle, the scrolling line — formal elements that exist in no prior notation grammar. These primitives are suited precisely to what Nicolas Bernier calls "controlled accidents": the knob turn, the hand sweep across a synthesizer, the sonic surprise that emerges from complex processing chains. This is the kind of performative act that Common Western Notation has always been constitutionally unable to notate — not because no one tried, but because the logic of CWN requires a predictable link between symbol and sound, and electronic music's characteristic unpredictability destroys that link. The second layer is the DAW timeline as a shared temporal medium between electronic music and live performers — conductor, score, and performance machine sharing a single timeline. This was latent in every DAW with a timeline since the 1990s. No one had actualized it until SIGN/e.

The constructs SIGN/e works against are closing rapidly. AI music generation (Suno, Udio, Stable Audio) is cementing the equation: electronic music = closed AI product = no live performers needed. The inward-pointing DAW model is approaching full seal. TENOR 2026 (Vienna, May) and the Organised Sound paper are the first institutional approaches to naming what SIGN/e opens — but naming is not yet complete. Bernier's ongoing summer 2026 collaboration with guitarist Simon Trottier means the logic is still growing, still unresolved. In the chisel-construct cycle, SIGN/e is at the chisel moment: actualized, but not yet fully named. When "animated graphic notation embedded in a DAW" becomes a standard feature category — when Dorico for electronic performers is a thing — what is visible today settles into the sediment of the past construct. Right now, it is still alive.
