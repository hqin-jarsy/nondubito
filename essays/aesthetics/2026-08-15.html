历书没有命名的那些天

The Days the Almanac Never Named

Han Qin (秦汉)

Ivan Zhao 写诗、做游戏，也一直在为一个多语言的互联网留出空间。他今年做了 The Infinite Almanac：一首三百六十五句的诗，一天一句，双语，铺在一张会随季节换色的浏览器页面上。背景是他为了学 shader 硬写出来的天空与云影——他自己承认到现在也没写顺。声音不是装饰：诗里提到虫、雷、雨、河，页面滚到那一句，对应的那层声音就开或关。整首诗仿连歌（renga）写成，并且明确说明可以按任意顺序读。

它的来处是黄历。中国的阴阳合历把一年切成二十四节气，每个节气再分三候，共七十二候，每一候都有名字：桃始华、鹰化为鸠、腐草为萤。这套系统是一个已构——一个早已凿好、封口、可以流通的构造物。它当然有它的美，但那种美是上一轮凿构循环留下的沉积物：命名一年之变化的那次开凿，一千年前就结束了。

Zhao 做的事，恰好落在这个已构自己剩下的余数上。七十二候意味着一年被量化成七十二个块，块内的日子不被单独命名，只是继承所在那一块的名字。也就是说，历法的分辨率本身产生了一个余项：那些没有资格被单独看见的日子。把七十二扩写成三百六十五，不是把旧系统翻译一遍，是往它舍去的那个区间里写字。每一天重新领回属于自己的一句话。

第二层余项在时间的形状上。线性发展的时间——长大、成家、留下点什么——同样是一个已构，而不合这套顺序的人，就是它的余数。Zhao 谈到酷儿时间性与离散经验时说得很直接：他同时被拉向推想的未来和推想的过去。要紧的是他没有拿另一套顺序去覆盖旧顺序。他的做法是让作品失去顺序：不标年份，任意读，随时回来。余项没有被解决成一个新构造，而是被给了一个能一直敞着的形状。

所以现在看它，比以后看重要。这件东西目前无处可归：它不是日历（它没有年份），不是诗集（声音绑在滚动上），不是网络艺术（没有展场），不是冥想应用（没有目标），也不是屏保——虽然他自己用了「几乎像屏保」这个说法，可见连他也在借词。所有能吞下它的类别都在旁边，没有一个真的合身。连那片他还没写顺的 shader 都是活的证据：技艺尚未闭合，作品就还在长。等到「季节性网页诗」或者「环境日历站」这类词被造出来并且好用起来，它就沉下去，成为又一层已构。趁它还没有名字，去看一遍。

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

Ivan Zhao writes poetry, makes games, and keeps making room for a multilingual internet. This year he built The Infinite Almanac: a poem of 365 lines, one per day of the year, bilingual, laid across a browser page whose colors shift with the seasons. The background is a sky-and-cloud shader he wrote mainly to teach himself shaders — he admits he still struggles with them. The sound isn't decoration: the poem names insects, thunder, rain, rivers, and as you scroll to those lines the corresponding layer switches on or off. The whole thing is written after the renga form, and he says plainly that it can be read in any order.

It comes out of the Chinese lunisolar almanac. That calendar cuts the year into 24 solar terms, each divided into three pentads — 72 in all, and every one of them carries a name: peach trees begin to blossom, hawks turn into doves, rotting grass becomes fireflies. That system is an already-construct: chiseled, sealed, in circulation for a millennium. It has its beauty, but that beauty is sediment from a finished chisel-construct cycle. The cut that named the year's changes closed a thousand years ago.

What Zhao does lands exactly on the remainder that this construct left behind in itself. Seventy-two pentads means the year is quantized into 72 blocks, and the days inside a block are never named individually — they inherit the name of the block they fall in. The calendar's own resolution generates a residue: the days that don't qualify to be seen on their own. Expanding 72 into 365 isn't a translation of the old system. It is writing into the interval the system rounded away. Each day gets its own sentence back.

The second remainder is in the shape of time itself. Linear developmental time — grow up, settle down, leave something behind — is also an already-construct, and whoever doesn't fit its sequence is its remainder. Zhao is direct about this when he talks about queer temporality and diaspora: he feels pulled toward speculative futures and speculative pasts at once. What matters is that he doesn't lay a counter-sequence over the old one. He takes the sequence out: no year is marked, the poem is read in any order, you come back whenever. The remainder isn't resolved into a new construct. It's given a form that can stay open.

Which is why seeing it now matters more than seeing it later. Right now the work has nowhere to be filed. It isn't a calendar (it has no year), isn't a poetry collection (the sound is bound to scroll position), isn't net art (there's no gallery), isn't a meditation app (there's no goal), and isn't a screensaver — though he reaches for "almost like a screensaver" himself, which shows even the maker is borrowing words. Every category that could digest it sits nearby and none of them fits. Even the shader he hasn't gotten right is live evidence: the craft hasn't closed, so the work is still growing. Once someone coins "seasonal web poem" or "ambient calendar site" and the term starts working, it settles into another layer of already-construct. Go look while it still has no name.
