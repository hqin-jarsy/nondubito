他把电源摇成了音色

He Turned the Power Supply Into Timbre

Han Qin (秦汉)

弗吉尼亚州里士满有一间叫 Molasses Industries 的工作室，主理人只有 Will Mullany 一个。他做电子乐器，但做法和这个行当的常规反着来。最说明问题的是 Generator Organ：一台离网的模拟风琴，侧面嵌着一根手摇曲柄，演奏它所需的全部电力由那根曲柄实时发出，机身自带放大，不接音箱、不插插座就能响。音量和音色不靠旋钮，靠摇——摇快摇慢，声音跟着走。电路全模拟，用顶八度分频取得复音。这台琴目前停产，官网上"停产"那行字后面跟了一句"暂时"。另一组作品叫 Embodied Electronics，做法是把人体直接接成电路里的一个元件：不是用身体去操作设备，而是让身体的电导、阻抗、接地变化直接扰动电路，系统因此偶然、不稳定、拒绝被精确标定。

先把一件事挡在前面，否则底下全是空话：手作合成器这个观感本身早就是已构。木壳、旋钮、丝印、Lo-Fi、circuit bending、Bandcamp 上的噪音专辑、Instagram 上的工作台照片——这一整套已经被彻底消化，有市场、有受众、有可复制的视觉语法，是上一轮凿构循环留下的沉积物。它的美是真的，但那是构的美，不再是活着的余项。如果 Mullany 的价值只在于"手工做的、听起来很脏的合成器"，这一则不该写。

真正的差别不在外观，在那一凿落在哪一层。电子乐器的设计传统本身就是一次极长的凿：先划定什么算"信号"，然后把所有不算信号的东西——电源纹波、元件公差、温漂、阻抗变化、人手带来的杂讯——认定为余项，逐条清除。circuit bending 是对这个传统的反抗，但它的凿落在成品表面：改别人已经做完的东西，产出一类已知的惊喜。Mullany 的凿落得更深。Generator Organ 动的根本不是音色层，是电源层。电源在任何电子乐器的定义里都是最彻底的"非音乐"：它应该稳定、沉默、不被听见，是演奏得以发生的前提，从不算作乐器的一部分。而每一台合成器背后其实站着一整套没被计入的基础设施——市电、功放、房间。那套基础设施才是这个定义留下的、从来没人结算过的余项。他把它结算了：接进琴里，接到手上。一旦结算，它立刻变成可演奏的。

结算的后果很具体：演奏者的手臂成了发电厂，于是疲劳进了声音里。一个乐句能维持多久，取决于胳膊能摇多久；想要更响就得更用力，而更用力必然更短。这是一条真实的作曲约束，现有的任何门类都没给它名字。Embodied Electronics 是同一个操作往上挪一层：常规界面设计把身体当作外部的东西，通过按键发出离散指令，身体自身的电学性质属于要隔离掉的干扰；他把这层干扰接成电路本身，于是"标定不准"不再是缺陷，而是这件器物的全部内容。他自己对这套方法的描述是自下而上的——不预设一个想要的结果再去驯服电路，而是跟着电路走，让它的怪癖变成使用者不得不应付的东西。

余项还活着，因为还没有一个名字接得住它。叫 circuit bending 不对，他自己画 PCB、送厂打样，改的不是别人的成品。叫声音艺术不对，那预设画廊是终点，而他的主要产出是限量做完、装箱寄给陌生人，之后再没消息——他说他其实并不知道谁在买、为什么买。叫乐器制作也不对，那预设乐器为已经存在的音乐服务，而他的东西按他自己的描述是可以独立成立的、供玩与长期互动的系统。叫噪音也不对，噪音是一种听感风格，而这里发生的是一层结构性的重新划界。四个名字，没有一个覆盖得住"基础设施就是界面"这一句。覆盖不住的这个缺口，正是余项尚未变成构的证据。

为什么现在看比以后看重要：命名的压力已经开始，而且有日期可查。2024 年 10 月耶鲁 CCAM 请他去做过一场以他的设计与制造流程为题的工作坊；2025 年他进了 APOSSIBLE 的 Applied Experiment 名单；柏林 Dystopia 2026 的艺术家名单里现在有他。更危险的是那句话他自己已经写好了——"受控的技术失败"是他本人的措辞。一旦这个措辞开始作为门类标签流通，作品就成了构：你可以买到那种美学，买不到那份风险。此刻风险还是真的：Generator Organ 停产，乐器一批一批地做，失败还是失败。还有一处他自己也没解决的矛盾——他做的是极端单件的物，却说自己真正怀念的是阿巴拉契亚老派曲调那样的传统：一套人人可以进出的规则，个人表达自然落进集体的作品里，而不是今天这种以个体与创新为轴心的技术文化。一个把单件做到极致的人，想要的是共通的代码。这个矛盾在他嘴里没有答案。没有答案，就说明它还在长。

Molasses Industries is a studio in Richmond, Virginia, and it is one person: Will Mullany. He builds electronic instruments, but against the grain of how that trade normally works. The clearest case is the Generator Organ, an off-grid analog organ with a hand crank set into its side. Every watt it needs to sound is produced in real time by that crank; the body amplifies itself, so it needs no PA and no wall socket. Volume and timbre are not set with knobs, they are cranked — turn faster or slower and the sound follows. The circuitry is entirely analog, with polyphony from a top-octave division scheme. The organ is currently out of production; the website follows that word with "for now." A second body of work, Embodied Electronics, wires the human body in as a circuit element rather than an operator of one: variations in conductivity, impedance and grounding perturb the circuit directly, so the system behaves contingently, unstably, and resists precise calibration.

One thing has to be cleared out of the way first, or none of the rest holds. The handmade-synth look is already construct. Wooden enclosures, knobs, silkscreen, lo-fi, circuit bending, noise albums on Bandcamp, workbench photos on Instagram — all of it has been thoroughly digested, with a market, an audience and a reproducible visual grammar. It is sediment left by a chisel-construct cycle that closed some time ago. Its beauty is real, but it is the beauty of construct, not a living remainder. If Mullany's value were just "handmade synths that sound dirty," this entry should not exist.

The difference is not in appearance. It is in which layer the chisel lands on. The design tradition of electronic instruments is itself an extremely long chisel: first define what counts as signal, then identify everything that does not — supply ripple, component tolerance, thermal drift, impedance variation, the noise a human hand introduces — as remainder, and eliminate it item by item. Circuit bending rebels against that tradition, but its chisel lands on the finished surface: it modifies something somebody else already completed, and it yields a known kind of surprise. Mullany's chisel goes lower. The Generator Organ does not operate on the timbre layer at all. It operates on power. In any definition of an electronic instrument, power is the most complete form of "not music": it is supposed to be stable, silent, unheard, the precondition for playing rather than any part of what is played. And behind every synthesizer stands an entire uncounted infrastructure — the grid, an amplifier, a room. That infrastructure is the remainder this definition leaves behind, and nobody ever settles the account. He settles it: the power goes into the instrument, and the instrument goes into a hand. Once counted, it becomes playable.

The consequence is concrete. The player's arm becomes the power plant, so fatigue enters the music. How long a phrase can be held depends on how long an arm can keep turning; louder requires more force, and more force means shorter. That is a real compositional constraint, and no existing category has a name for it. Embodied Electronics performs the same operation one layer up. Conventional interface design treats the body as external, issuing discrete control states through switches, with the body's own electrical properties classified as interference to be isolated. He wires that interference in as the circuit itself, at which point "won't calibrate" stops being a defect and becomes the entire content of the object. His own account of the method is bottom-up: rather than starting from a desired outcome and taming the circuit into producing it, he follows the circuit and lets its quirks become something the user has to contend with.

The remainder is still alive because no name catches it. Circuit bending is wrong: he draws his own PCBs and has them fabricated; he is not modifying somebody's finished product. Sound art is wrong: it presumes the gallery is the destination, whereas his main output is built in limited runs, boxed and shipped to strangers, after which he hears nothing — he says he has no real idea who buys these things or why. Instrument making is wrong: it presumes instruments serve music that already exists, whereas his objects are, in his own description, systems that stand on their own for play and sustained interaction. Noise is wrong too: noise is a style of listening, and what is happening here is a structural redrawing of a boundary. Four names, and not one of them covers the sentence "the infrastructure is the interface." That gap in coverage is the evidence that the remainder has not yet become construct.

Why seeing it now matters more than seeing it later: the naming pressure has already started, and it is dated. In October 2024 Yale's CCAM ran a workshop with him on his design and manufacturing process. In 2025 he was in APOSSIBLE's Applied Experiment cohort. His name is on the artist roster for Dystopia Berlin 2026. More dangerous still, he has already written the sentence himself — "controlled technological failure" is his own phrase. The moment that phrase begins circulating as a genre label, the work becomes construct: you will be able to buy the aesthetic without buying the risk. Right now the risk is real. The Generator Organ is out of production, the instruments are made in small runs, and the failures are still failures. There is also a contradiction he has not resolved. He makes radically singular objects, yet says what he actually longs for is a tradition like Appalachian old-time music — a set of rules people can step into and out of, where individual expression slots naturally into collective work, rather than a technoculture organized around individuality and innovation. A man who has taken one-off making to its limit wants a common code. He offers no resolution. No resolution means it is still growing.
