眨眼之间：一份被感知丢弃的清单

In the Blink: An Inventory of What Perception Discards

Han Qin (秦汉)

Jānis K 住在拉脱维亚，六年来每月在 absurd.website 上发布一件「荒谬的创业项目」——有编号、有落地页、有邮件订阅、有付费会员，只是从来不打算真的卖出什么。2026 年八月的第 56 号叫 BLINK，副题是「一次人类正常运行时间测试」（A Human Uptime Test）。它调用摄像头做面部追踪，唯一的玩法是睁着眼，看你能把下一次眨眼推迟多久。结束时它给你一个秒数，并按页面上写明的一套假设——八十年寿命、每天十六小时清醒、每分钟十五次眨眼、每次闭眼零点一五秒——算出你一生中有多少清醒时间消失在这些缝隙里：每天约三十六分钟，八十年累计约七百三十天，正好两年。页面自己标注：这是艺术估算，不是医学测量。旁边还挂着一个全球前三十名的排行榜。

眨眼是感知构造的余项，而且是最干净的那一种。我们之所以觉得世界是连续的，恰恰因为神经系统主动抹掉了这些中断——连续性不是被看见的，是被造出来的，代价就是把凿痕擦干净。BLINK 没有添加任何东西，它只是把已经被丢弃的那部分重新计数。这在凿构循环里是一个很少有人站的位置：它不去凿一个新构造，而是把一个早已完成、并且完成得极其成功的构造（「我一直在场」）翻过来，让它露出底下的账。它的宣言比它的代码更狠：眨眼是藏在公开在场里的私人出口，从外面看你还在这里，从里面看世界刚刚消失过一次；睡眠只是最长的那一次眨眼，一次我们已经排进日程、已经接受的消失。

但更值得看的是第二层。BLINK 的载体是一张标准的创业公司落地页：hero 区、个人最佳、全球榜、宣言、订阅按钮、会员专享。这套形式已经被资本彻底消化过了，它是典型的「已构」——它当然有它自己的美，但那是上一轮凿构循环留下的沉积物，早就不再作为余项活着。Jānis 做的事，是把这个耗尽的形式原封不动地跑起来，只抽掉它唯一的目的：没有东西要卖。于是形式自身的剩余部分浮了出来。他自己的表述是「死掉的创业项目就直接变成作品」，以及「真实与荒谬的分界只由参与度决定」——只要有足够多的人使用它、付钱、习惯它，它就变成真的。这不是讽刺；讽刺是有立场的，是站在外面指着。他做的是把构造的生成条件本身当材料用。

这件事现在还活着，有一个结构性的原因，而不只是运气。Jānis 没有艺术教育背景，他自己写下：我很少觉得自己是个传统意义上的艺术家，因为我来自艺术界之外。他的作品发布在 dev.to、Hacker News、Reddit，不在任何机构里。艺术界手上已经备好了几个名字——后网络时代的机构批判、创业讽刺、概念性网络艺术——这些名字随便哪一个落下来，这套东西就完成了它的凿构循环，变成一个可展出、可收藏、可写进综述的构造。他还给自己留了一道更硬的保险：每月两件，其中一件永远只对付费会员开放。这意味着这个档案在原则上不可能被完整归档，永远缺一半。这是主动保留的余项。

最后一点我认为最该记下来。他明确写过：有时候感觉像在沙漠里盖一座没人注意的大教堂；奇怪的是，我有一部分希望它被发现得越晚越好。大多数人做完东西就等着被命名，他在护着未命名的状态。这本身就是一种判断力——他知道被看见的那一刻，也就是余项开始固化的那一刻。所以现在看 BLINK，和三年后在某个双年展的墙上看它，看到的不会是同一个东西。计数器目前停在 56，还在往上走，还没有人替它写下定论。趁着账还没结清的时候去看一眼。

absurd.website ↗

Han Qin (秦汉) · @hqinjarsy · SAE 判断力与美学

Jānis K lives in Latvia, and for six years he has shipped one absurd startup a month at absurd.website — numbered, with a landing page, a mailing list, and a paid membership tier, none of it ever meant to actually sell anything. Number 56, from August 2026, is called BLINK, subtitled "A Human Uptime Test." It opens your webcam, tracks your face, and gives you exactly one thing to do: keep your eyes open and see how long you can postpone the next blink. Then it hands you a number, and using the assumptions printed on the page — an eighty-year life, sixteen waking hours a day, fifteen blinks a minute, 0.15 seconds of closed eye per blink — it estimates how much of your waking life vanishes into those gaps: about thirty-six minutes a day, roughly 730 days over eighty years, which is to say two years exactly. The page labels this an artistic estimate, not a medical measurement. Next to it sits a global top-thirty leaderboard.

The blink is a remainder of perception, and the cleanest kind there is. The world feels continuous to us precisely because the nervous system deletes these interruptions — continuity is not something we see, it is something built, and the price of building it is wiping the chisel marks clean. BLINK adds nothing. It simply re-counts what was already thrown away. That is an unusual place to stand in the chisel-construct cycle: rather than carving a new construct, it turns over one that was finished long ago and finished extremely well — "I have been here the whole time" — and shows you the ledger underneath. The manifesto is harder than the code: a blink is a private exit hidden inside public presence; from the outside you are still here, from the inside the world just briefly disappeared. Sleep is only the longest blink, the one disappearance we have already scheduled and accepted.

The second layer is the more interesting one. BLINK's vehicle is a startup landing page: hero section, personal best, global ranking, manifesto, subscribe button, members-only tier. That form has been thoroughly digested by capital. It is already-construct in the most complete sense — it has its own beauty, but that beauty is sediment from a chisel-construct cycle that closed a long time ago, and it is no longer alive as a remainder. What Jānis does is run the exhausted form exactly as-is while removing its one purpose: there is nothing for sale. What surfaces is the form's own surplus, everything it carries beyond its function. His words for it are that dead startups simply become artworks, and that the line between real and absurd is set only by participation — if enough people use it, pay for it, normalize it, it becomes real. This is not satire. Satire has a position; it stands outside and points. He is using the conditions under which constructs come into being as his material.

There is a structural reason this is still alive, not just luck. Jānis has no art training and says so plainly: he rarely feels like a traditional artist, because he comes from outside the art world. The work ships to dev.to, Hacker News, Reddit — not to institutions. The art world already has names waiting for it: post-internet institutional critique, startup satire, conceptual net art. The moment any one of those lands, the cycle closes and the whole thing becomes a construct that can be exhibited, collected, and written into a survey. He has also built himself a harder insurance policy: two works a month, one of which is permanently members-only. The archive is therefore impossible to complete in principle. Half of it is always missing. That is a remainder held open on purpose.

The last thing is the one worth writing down. He has said that it sometimes feels like building a cathedral in a desert that nobody has noticed, and that strangely, part of him hopes it gets discovered as late as possible. Most people finish something and wait to be named; he is guarding the unnamed state. That is a form of judgment in itself — he knows the moment of being seen is the moment the remainder starts to set. So looking at BLINK now and looking at it three years from now on a biennial wall are not the same act. The counter is at 56 and still climbing, and nobody has written the verdict yet. Go while the ledger is still open.
