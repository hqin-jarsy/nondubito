德文·卢·林维加 / Lø：佩亚诺的剩余语法

Devine Lu Linvega / Lø: Peano's Remaining Grammar

Han Qin (秦汉)

一九○三年，意大利数学家朱塞佩·佩亚诺发表了"无屈折拉丁语"（Latino Sine Flexione）。他的目标简单而激进：将古典拉丁语彻底剥去词尾变化，仅保留词根与语序，造出一种任何受过基础教育的欧洲人都能立刻读懂数学命题的国际书面语。他用这门语言写了自己晚年的数学论文，在少数学者之间流传了一阵，然后这个实验沉没了。没有人接手，没有人给它一个完整的语法。它带着自身内在的逻辑，悬在已构与废弃之间。

德文·卢·林维加（Devine Lu Linvega）在帆船上——没有稳定网络，只有本地的Uxn/Varvara系统——着手接手了这个实验。他们在自己的XXIIVV维基上，将佩亚诺遗留的片段用BNF（巴科斯-诺尔范式）书写成一部真正的形式语法。这门他们称为Lø的变体，在佩亚诺的基础上更进一步：删去词尾元音，消除重复辅音，将所有词汇压缩至最短可辨识形式。Lø是一门可以用代码检验的语言——每一条BNF规则都是一个可执行的断言，可以被计算机解析，也可以被人用几小时学会。

这是SAE意义上最纯粹的余项之美的例子之一。已构（已构）是什么？是世界语（Esperanto）、国际语（Interlingua）等那些已被命名、已有社区、已有语法学校的辅助语言——它们的位置稳固，它们的美是过去沉淀的产物。余项是：一个数学家在一九○三年动手、没人接住的实验，以及一个程序员兼艺术家在一二○年后把它重新拾起、用计算机语言书写成草稿的过程。凿构循环（chisel-construct cycle）在这里极缓慢地运转：每一次BNF规则的修订，每一个被解决的同音异义词（homophone），都是一次凿；而新的歧义随即在边缘浮现。语法本身是活的，未完成的，这个未完成不是缺陷，而是余项存活的标志。

二○二六年七月，《千室之书》（Thousand Rooms）的Lø版本"Mil Camer"发布——这是第一部用这门语言生成的完整文学作品。这本由德文与插画家Rekka Bellum合作的视觉小说，现在有了一个以无屈折拉丁语写成的版本。它的出现不是翻译的机械练习，而是一个语言到达临界点的证明：Lø现在足够连贯，可以承载故事，可以成为真正的阅读对象。但这个"足够"并不意味着完成。词典仍在扩充，边缘案例仍在讨论，开发日志（devlog）显示着持续的修订。这是一门在生产中的语言，而非在档案里的语言。

艺术世界还没有一个词汇来命名"用一二三年前的数学草稿改造而成的形式语言写就的生成性视觉小说"。这种命名的缺席，正是它仍是余项的结构性证据。它不完全是语言学，不完全是计算，不完全是文学，它悬在三者之间，而这个悬置构成了它的结构生命。现在看Lø，是在它的名字尚未到来之前看它——一旦某个学术标签将它纳入版图，余项的新鲜感便会消失，取而代之的是一种更安全的位置感。今天它仍在中间，仍是活的。

In 1903, the Italian mathematician Giuseppe Peano published what he called "Latino Sine Flexione" — Latin Without Inflections. His goal was precise and strange: strip Classical Latin of all case endings and verb conjugations, keep the roots and word order, and produce an international written language that any educated European could use to read mathematical propositions within hours. He wrote his later papers in it, it circulated briefly among a small circle of scholars, and then it sank. Nobody picked it up. Nobody completed the grammar. It hung there, suspended between construct and abandonment, carrying its own internal logic but belonging to no established place.

Devine Lu Linvega, living on a sailboat without reliable internet and building everything on a local Uxn/Varvara stack, decided to pick it up. On the XXIIVV wiki, they have been formalizing Peano's fragments into a proper BNF (Backus-Naur Form) grammar — a variant they call Lø, which takes Peano's language further toward compression: trailing vowels removed, repeated consonants eliminated, every word reduced to its shortest recognizable form. Lø is a language that can be checked by code — each BNF rule is a computable assertion about grammaticality, machine-parseable and humanly learnable in hours at once.

This is 余项之美 in a structurally precise sense. The "already-construct" (已构) here is the family of finished auxiliary languages — Esperanto, Interlingua, Ido — languages with names, communities, textbooks, and stable grammars. Their beauty is the sediment of a past moment; their position is fixed. The "remainder" (余项) is: a mathematician's 1903 experiment that was never adopted, and a programmer-artist's attempt to give it computational structure 120 years later. The chisel-construct cycle (凿构循环) runs very slowly here: each time a BNF rule is refined or a homophone collision is resolved, that's the chisel stroke; and new ambiguities surface at the edges immediately. The grammar itself is alive and unfinished, and that unfinishedness is not a defect — it is the mark of the remainder still living.

In July 2026, Thousand Rooms — a short visual novel created with illustrator Rekka Bellum — was released in its Lø version, titled Mil Camer. This is the first complete literary work generated in this language. The release is not a mechanical translation exercise; it is a threshold being crossed. Lø has become coherent enough to carry a story, to sustain a reader across a narrative. But "coherent enough" is not "finished." The dictionary is still expanding, edge cases in the grammar are still under debate, and the devlog shows active revision. This is a language in production, not in archive.

The art world has no word yet for "a generative visual novel written in a computationally formalized reconstruction of an unfinished 1903 mathematical auxiliary language." That absence of naming is the structural evidence that this remains a remainder. It is not fully linguistics, not fully computing, not fully literature — it lives suspended between all three, and that suspension is where its structural aliveness resides. Seeing Lø now, before the name arrives, is seeing it while it still breathes as remainder. Once a category claims it, the freshness of the remainder will fade and something safer will take its place. Today it remains in between, still alive.
