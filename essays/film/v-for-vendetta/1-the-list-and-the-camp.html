The List and the Nameless Camp

一份名单，和一间没有名字的营地

Sep 4, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)Independent English edition中文原作 · 简 / 繁

Edition note: An independent English rewriting for readers of V for Vendetta. It preserves the Chinese argument while reorganizing its sequence for an English-language essay. Full-film spoilers.

The first image of power in V for Vendetta is not a rally or a firing squad. It is a meeting.

Britain's senior officials sit around a table while Adam Sutler fills an entire wall. Each branch reports in turn: what has been seen in the streets, heard over the wires, said on television, and done by the police. Sutler answers. Nobody deliberates.

The scene matters because dictatorship is rarely sustained by one furious man alone. It survives by becoming a system in which looking, listening, speaking, and taking people away have already been connected.

A Government That Occupies the Day

The regime gives its agencies anatomical names. The Eye watches cameras. The Ear monitors communications. The Mouth controls the broadcast network. The Finger reaches into homes and streets.

When Evey Hammond breaks curfew at the beginning of the film, Fingermen stop her. The men charged with enforcing order immediately turn their authority into sexual menace. Yet Evey's first instinct is not outrage. She explains herself. She apologises.

Even the public announcement is gentle: for your protection, return home. Norsefire no longer has to make every sentence sound like a threat. Its citizens have learned to carry the threat inside themselves. I should not be here. I should not ask. If I obey, perhaps I will be safe.

That is fear at its most useful. It no longer needs to shout.

The Founding Instrument

Inspector Finch's pursuit of V leads him back to events that made the present government possible. A virus struck St Mary's school, a Tube station, and the Three Waters treatment plant. Almost one hundred thousand people died. Panic fractured the country. Norsefire won the election, and a pharmaceutical company controlled by party insiders later supplied the cure and made them rich.

The film gives the fullest account through “William Rookwood,” who is actually V in disguise. He tells Finch that the attack was not foreign terror at all. It was an operation conceived inside the emerging regime: create the catastrophe, sell the rescue, and let frightened citizens confer powers they would otherwise have resisted.

Finch is being offered a story, not handed a judicial verdict. But the erased records, Dr Delia Surridge's diary, Larkhill, and the money behind the cure have already made the official version impossible to accept at face value.

Fear, on this account, is not an unfortunate consequence of Norsefire's rise. Fear is the instrument with which it builds the state. First persuade people that ordinary life may collapse at any moment; then present obedience as the only shelter left.

What Happened Before the Experiments

The virus was developed at Larkhill, a detention facility for those the regime called undesirable: gay people, Muslims, political prisoners, and anyone else it could place outside the protected public.

The inmates lost their names, received numbers, and were injected with experimental compounds. Most died. The medical cruelty is obvious. Less spectacular, but no less important, is the decision that had to come first.

Before a human being could be used as laboratory material, that person had to be rewritten as a category.

Once someone is only a homosexual, a religious deviant, a terrorist, or an undesirable, the particular life becomes administratively irrelevant. Whom she loved, what she hoped for, how she understood herself—none of it fits the column that determines her fate. What may be done to her begins to look like a technical question.

Violence does not always begin with a blow. It often begins with a sentence that sounds tidy: those people are all the same.

Valerie Writes Her Name Back In

The film spends an unusual amount of time restoring one person whom Larkhill reduced to a category.

Her name is Valerie. She is an actor and a lesbian. On scraps of toilet paper, she writes about sunlight in her childhood, her first girlfriend, rejection by her parents, her career, and the years of happiness she shared with Ruth. After Norsefire came to power, Ruth was taken. Valerie eventually arrived at Larkhill.

Her account is not a manifesto. She asks the unknown reader to perform no mission. She simply insists on the shape of a life: before your number, I had a name; outside your category, I loved and was loved.

At the end she describes a final inch of integrity—small, fragile, and hers. The state can imprison her body and dictate the public words attached to it. It cannot make that last inward claim on her behalf.

The Person the List Could Not Finish

The prisoner in the next cell is known only by the Roman numeral V. Valerie's pages reach him, and he survives the fire that destroys Larkhill.

This makes her letter the film's quietest form of resistance. Valerie does not know who will read it, whether it will escape the camp, or what it will cause. She does not control its result. She leaves another person the chance to encounter someone the regime's records failed to contain.

Norsefire believes that if it owns the files, cameras, names, and broadcasts, it can define a person completely. Valerie does not destroy that machinery. From inside it, she leaves one piece of testimony: the person you recorded was never all of me.

That is enough to open a crack in a list designed to admit no remainder.

一间会议室，长桌旁坐着这个国家最有权力的几个人。整面墙是一块巨大的屏幕，屏幕上只有一张脸。

最高领袖不必亲自到场。他逐个点名，各部门逐项汇报：街上的动静、电话里的声音、电视里的说法、需要抓的人。每个人说完，他给出命令。没有讨论，也没有谁问一句：我们有没有可能错了？

这就是《V字仇杀队》让我们看见的第一个事实：独裁并不只是一张愤怒的脸。它是一套已经接好的系统。

一、它接管了一个人的一天

“眼睛”看摄像头，“耳朵”听通信，“声音”决定电视播什么，“手指”负责把人带走。

电影开场，艾薇违反宵禁，遭几名便衣执法者拦下。他们本该维护秩序，却立即把手里的权力变成了对她的威胁。更值得留意的，是艾薇最初的反应：她不是质问，而是解释，是道歉。街上的广播甚至语气温和，提醒市民为了自身安全尽快回家。

恐惧最稳固的时候，往往不需要一直大喊。人已经学会了替它说话：我不该出来，我最好别问，我只要服从就会安全。

二、恐惧不是副产品

芬奇探长追查 V 的过程中，逐渐把多年前的几件事连了起来。圣玛丽学校、地铁站和“三水”水处理厂遭到病毒袭击，近十万人死亡。全国陷入恐慌，诺斯费尔党借机赢得选举；不久之后，一家由党内人士控制的制药公司拿出了救命药，也让相关人物暴富。

电影借 V 假扮的“鲁克伍德”讲出完整版本：病毒并非外部敌人投放，而是权力集团自己制造，再用自己造成的灾难证明强权的必要。芬奇听到的是一则尚待判断的故事，但此前的档案、拉克希尔医生的日记和利益链，已经让它不再只是无凭无据的阴谋论。

这个政权并不是在恐惧中偶然变坏。恐惧本身就是它的建国工具。先让人觉得世界随时会毁灭，再把服从包装成唯一的避难所。

三、拉克希尔先做了一件事

病毒实验发生在拉克希尔拘留营。被送进去的，是同性恋者、穆斯林、政治异议者，以及一切被政权归入“不受欢迎者”的人。他们失去名字，得到编号，被注射药物；大多数人死在那里。

实验当然残酷。但在实验开始以前，拉克希尔已经完成了更基础的一步：它先把具体的人改写成类别。

一旦一个人只剩“同性恋者”“异教徒”“危险分子”或“不合格者”这个标签，他的经历、爱过的人、想过的生活，都可以从表格上消失。接下来，对他做什么似乎只剩技术问题。

暴力并不总从拳头开始。它常常从一句看似整齐的话开始：这些人都是一样的。

四、瓦莱丽把名字写了回来

电影用了很长一段时间，让其中一个被归类的人重新成为一个人。

她叫瓦莱丽，是演员，也是女同性恋。她写童年的阳光，写十几岁时爱上的第一个女孩，写被家人赶走，写后来遇见露丝，写她们共同拥有的那些好日子。诺斯费尔上台后，露丝先被带走，她随后也被投入拉克希尔。

在牢房里，瓦莱丽把自己的一生写在厕纸上，从墙缝递给隔壁的人。她没有列举理论，也没有要求读者替她完成某项使命。她只是坚持说：在你们给我的编号之前，我有一个名字；在你们给我的类别之外，我活过这样的一生。

她把那最后一寸称为很小、很脆弱，却是世上唯一值得拥有的东西。政权可以囚禁身体，可以规定别人如何称呼她，却不能替她决定那一寸是什么。

五、名单遗漏了什么

隔壁牢房住着编号为 V 的囚犯。他读到了这些纸，并在拉克希尔的大火中活了下来。

这使瓦莱丽的信成为全片最安静、也最顽强的反抗。她不知道读者是谁，不知道纸片能否离开营地，更不知道它会改变谁。她没有控制结果，只留下一个机会，让另一个人遇见一个没有被名单写尽的她。

诺斯费尔相信，只要掌握名字、档案、镜头和广播，就能把一个人完整地规定下来。瓦莱丽没有摧毁这套机器。她只是从机器内部留下了一条证词：你们记录的那个人，不是我的全部。

而这句话，已经足以让一份看似封闭的名单出现裂缝。
