Whose Hand Is on the Lever?

那根操作杆是谁的决定

Sep 4, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)Independent English edition中文原作 · 简 / 繁

Edition note: An independent English rewriting for readers of V for Vendetta. It preserves the Chinese argument while reorganizing its sequence for an English-language essay. Full-film spoilers.

An abandoned Underground tunnel contains a train packed with explosives. V is dead, and Evey has laid his body among the flowers inside it. The track leads beneath Parliament. Sending the train forward requires only the movement of a lever.

Inspector Finch arrives, points his gun at Evey, and tells her to take her hand away.

She says no.

The Final Step V Does Not Take

V has already completed his revenge. He surrendered himself on the condition that Peter Creedy first deliver High Chancellor Sutler. Creedy executed Sutler; V then killed Creedy and his men, sustaining the wounds from which he dies.

For years V had prepared to destroy Parliament. At the end, however, he tells Evey that the choice cannot be his. The world affected by the act is not his alone, so he is not entitled to make its final decision alone.

This is a genuine retreat from control. V has recognised a boundary that his treatment of Evey ignored. He can prepare the train, the explosives, and the symbolic target. None of that automatically authorises him to decide for everyone else.

The retreat comes very late. He has arranged the options, the hour, and, to a considerable extent, the person standing beside the lever.

Evey Was Never Blank Material

It is tempting to conclude that Evey is merely V's finished instrument. The film supplies evidence against that conclusion. Her brother died in the St Mary's attack. Her parents disappeared after becoming political activists. She chooses to help V escape the television station, and in the cell she chooses not to inform even when she believes that refusal will kill her.

V changes her profoundly. He does not manufacture her from nothing.

Her reason to Finch is simple: the country needs more than a building; it needs hope. The language clearly continues V's project. Yet no human judgment originates in a vacuum. Her parents, Valerie, V, her own fear, and her own refusal all meet in the decision.

The better test is not whether Evey invented every reason unaided. It is whether, at this moment, she can make those reasons answerable to herself and accept responsibility for what follows.

V built the lever. The hand that moves it may still be Evey's.

Finch's Gun and the Missing Order

Finch has traced Larkhill and assembled the connections among the virus, the pharmaceutical company, and Norsefire's rise. He may not possess a complete prosecutable case, but he knows the official history can no longer be trusted.

In the tunnel he first tries to stop Evey and then lowers his gun. Above ground, people wearing the masks V distributed are moving toward Parliament. Soldiers wait for commands from Sutler and Creedy that will never come. The officer on the scene finally orders them to stand down.

The night therefore does not happen because one heroic man chooses for an entire country. It depends on V's preparation, Evey's decision, Finch's restraint, a commander's refusal to fire without authority, and ordinary people actually leaving their homes. Remove any one of these acts and the ending may be different.

At the climax, agency has begun to leave V's hands and divide among people who can still say no.

The Two Meanings of One Mask

Thousands of people in one face make the film's most famous image, and its most ambiguous.

The masks can look like another uniformity. One man sets the date, supplies the object, and calls a crowd to the destination he selected. Has a regime of one official face merely been answered by another?

But anonymity can also work in the opposite direction. Norsefire uses cameras, files, and classifications to identify isolated people and dispose of them one by one. The mask interrupts that identification. It allows an ordinary person to enter public space without first having to become a recognised hero.

At the end, the marchers remove their masks. We see many different faces, including impossible appearances by people who have already died. The gesture qualifies the spectacle that came before it. A shared symbol is defensible only while it protects particular faces. If everyone must finally become V, his victory will have reproduced the logic he fought.

An Explosion Creates an Empty Place

Parliament falls to music, and the film ends. It does not show who governs the next morning, how institutions are rebuilt, or how citizens with incompatible convictions will live together.

That omission need not mean that the filmmakers have no answer. It marks the limit of what revolutionary spectacle can do. A symbol can be destroyed in one night. A society in which concrete people are no longer consumed by political abstractions requires the long morning after.

These are different kinds of work. The first can converge on a date, a train, and an explosion. The second cannot be performed by V, or by any single author of history. It has to remain open to people whose purposes he cannot prescribe.

V creates a vacancy. He does not, and cannot, fill it with a complete future.

Valerie's Other Kind of Power

The trilogy ends where it began, with Valerie's letter.

She does not know who will receive the pages. She tells the stranger to destroy no building and to become no version of herself. She records the inch she did not surrender, then hopes that an unknown person will escape and that there will be roses again.

V leaves Evey a lever connected to a destination already chosen. Valerie leaves a stranger a little strength that may be used freely—or not used at all.

The first gift produces a historic night. The second suggests the relation the next morning will require: not deciding the shape of another person's freedom, but helping preserve the chance for that person to become herself.

废弃的地铁隧道里停着一列装满炸药的列车。V 已经死了，艾薇把他的遗体安放在车厢中。列车前方的轨道通向议会大厦，启动它，只需要推下一根操作杆。

芬奇探长赶到，用枪指着艾薇，命令她把手拿开。

她回答：不。

一、V 把最后一步留了下来

在此之前，V 已经完成了自己的复仇。他以交出自己为条件，迫使克里迪先处决最高领袖萨特勒；随后，他杀死克里迪和他的手下，也因枪伤走到生命尽头。

他本来为炸毁议会准备了多年，却在最后告诉艾薇：拉动操作杆的选择不该由他来做。这个世界不仅属于他，决定也就不能只是他的。

这是一个真实的退让。V 至少看见了自己过去没有看见的边界：他可以准备列车、炸药和轨道，可以提出一个象征，却不能因此天然获得替所有人作决定的资格。

问题在于，他退让得很晚。选项、时刻和站在杆前的人，几乎都由他安排好了。

二、艾薇并不是一张白纸

如果说艾薇只是 V 制造出来的执行者，就会漏掉电影早已给出的另一半。她的弟弟死于圣玛丽学校的病毒袭击；父母因参加抗议被带走；她冒险帮助 V 从电视台脱身，也在牢房里独自选择不告密。V 深刻地改变了她，却没有从零创造她。

她面对芬奇时给出的理由也很简短：这个国家此刻需要的不只是一座建筑，它需要希望。这当然延续了 V 的语言，但人的判断从来不是在真空里长出来的。父母、瓦莱丽、V、自己的恐惧与拒绝，都进入了她此刻的决定。

所以更准确的问题不是“这个念头是不是完全原创”，而是：她有没有在此刻把理由接到自己身上，并愿意承担它的后果？

操作杆是 V 留下的。推下它的人，仍然可能是艾薇。

三、芬奇放下枪，不等于所有问题都解决了

芬奇查到了拉克希尔，也把病毒、制药公司与政权兴起之间的线索拼在了一起。他未必拥有一份能在法庭上结案的完整证据，却已经知道官方故事无法再相信。

隧道里，他先阻止艾薇，随后把枪放下。与此同时，地面上的人群正戴着 V 寄出的面具走向议会。军队等不到萨特勒或克里迪的命令，现场指挥官最终让士兵退下。

这一夜能够发生，不是因为一个英雄独自替全国完成了选择。V 的计划、艾薇的决定、芬奇的克制、士兵没有开枪，以及普通人真的走出家门，缺少任何一环，结局都可能不同。

电影在这里把行动从一个人手里拆开，分给了许多仍能说“不”的人。

四、同一张面具的两种读法

成千上万的人戴着同一张脸，这是全片最著名的画面，也最值得保持一点警惕。

同一张面具可以意味着又一次整齐划一：一个人设定日期、发出道具，所有人按他的节奏出现。推翻统一面孔的政权，是否只是换上了另一张统一面孔？

但面具也可以做相反的事。诺斯费尔依靠摄像头、档案和类别辨认每一个人，再把孤立的人逐个处理。面具暂时切断了这种辨认，让任何一个普通人都能走进人群，不必先成为英雄。

电影最后让人们摘下面具，露出不同的脸，甚至让已经死去的人也出现在其中。这个选择像是在提醒我们：共同的象征只有在保护每张具体面孔时才有意义。如果所有人最终只能成为 V，那么 V 的胜利也会变成另一种失败。

五、爆炸不是答案，只是空位

议会在音乐中炸毁，电影随即结束。它没有告诉我们第二天由谁治理，旧机构如何重建，彼此意见不同的人怎样生活在一起。

这并不证明电影“给不出”答案；更像是它有意停在革命最擅长处理、也最容易被误认成终点的地方：让一个象征倒下。

推翻一套靠恐惧维持的秩序，与建立一个不再吞掉具体之人的社会，是两种不同的工作。前者可以集中在一个日期、一列火车和一场爆炸；后者只能由许多人在漫长的第二天里共同完成。

V 打开了一个空位。他没有、也不可能替生活在其中的人写好未来。

六、瓦莱丽留下的是另一种力量

三篇读到最后，最值得带走的或许仍是瓦莱丽的信。

她不知道纸片会落到谁手里，没有规定陌生人应该炸掉什么，也没有要求对方变成自己。她只是把自己没有交出去的那一寸写下来，然后祝愿一个素未谋面的人能逃出去，能再次看见玫瑰。

V 给艾薇留下一根通向既定目标的操作杆；瓦莱丽给陌生人留下的，却是一点可以自行使用、也可以不用的力量。

前者制造了一个历史性的夜晚。后者提示了第二天可能需要的关系：不是替别人规定自由的形状，而是让他还有机会，成为他自己。
