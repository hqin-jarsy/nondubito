The Prison He Called Freedom

他用了同一套办法

Sep 4, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)Independent English edition中文原作 · 简 / 繁

Edition note: An independent English rewriting for readers of V for Vendetta. It preserves the Chinese argument while reorganizing its sequence for an English-language essay. Full-film spoilers.

Evey wakes with a hood over her head. Her hair is shaved. She is dressed as a prisoner and locked in a wet cell. Interrogators demand V's location; when she refuses, they use water and the prospect of execution to force an answer.

At the final interrogation, the choice is made brutally simple: inform, or die.

Evey chooses death. The door opens.

The Cell Is False; the Terror Is Not

This is not a Norsefire prison. V built it. The seizure, the shaving, the questioning, the immersion in water, and the death threat were all staged by him.

The set is false. Evey's experience is not. She does not know that the execution will never occur, who is outside the door, or whether she will leave at all. For the person inside it, V's construction is a prison.

The film deliberately makes her ordeal echo Larkhill. V was numbered, confined, and experimented upon. Years later he reconstructs the grammar of the terror he survived and makes someone else pass through it.

A victim of violence may learn to oppose violence. A victim may also learn violence's most seductive argument: if I know the destination is good, perhaps I am entitled to choose what another person must endure on the way there.

V's Definition of Freedom

When the deception is revealed, Evey asks the right question: how could he do this to her? V does not say that he needed an operative. His claim is more intimate. He believes that fear has imprisoned Evey all her life and that he has shown her the bars. He wishes there had been another way, but says he could not find one.

The admission does not absolve him. It identifies the problem. V has already defined Evey's condition and prescribed her cure without her participation.

His purpose is not Norsefire's purpose. The regime wants a frightened population; V wants Evey to cease being ruled by fear. Motive and direction matter, and collapsing him into the state would erase a real moral difference. But a liberating intention cannot make every method legitimate.

If freedom must first be delivered through abduction, torture, and psychological destruction, what has been given to the captive—freedom, or someone else's preferred shape of it?

Valerie Inside the Experiment

Evey survives the cell by reading a letter hidden in the wall. Valerie is real. The words are real. They were written at Larkhill and passed to the neighbouring prisoner. V later gives Evey the letter as it was given to him.

Yet this genuine testimony has been placed at a moment of V's design. He understands what Valerie's words may do to someone awaiting execution. He does not forge her story; he makes it part of an ordeal whose meaning and timing he controls.

That is why the sequence resists an easy verdict. Valerie's letter truly gives strength to a stranger. V also borrows that strength for an involuntary transformation.

The same words can be a gift in one relation and become an instrument in another. The difference lies partly in whether the recipient remains able to refuse the arrangement surrounding them.

A Good Outcome Cannot Supply Earlier Consent

Evey refuses to betray V. When the door opens, she reaches the roof and discovers, in the rain, that death no longer governs her. After her rage, she recognises something liberating in the change. Before leaving, she even says that perhaps she should thank him.

The film plainly invites us to experience this as rebirth. There is no need to deny Evey that meaning. People can draw strength from what harmed them. They can take possession of an ordeal and make it a turning point in a life that remains their own.

But the value later drawn from an injury cannot travel backward in time and become permission to inflict it. Evey may thank V and still have been violated by him. She may claim the transformation and still deny that it was his to impose. The meaning of her experience belongs, finally, to her.

V's deepest error is not wanting Evey to live without fear. It is briefly believing that because he can see a better destination, he may choose the whole route for her.

The Limits of Liberation

Norsefire confines people in order to break and use them. V confines Evey in order to make her unbreakable. Their purposes and consequences are not equivalent.

The film nevertheless leaves a narrower and more disturbing resemblance. Both place “I know what you must become” ahead of “Do you consent?”

A just cause does not turn a particular person into material for that cause. Help may accompany someone toward what frightens her. It may reveal a path she could not previously see. But it must preserve room to refuse, to leave, and to change in a way the helper did not plan.

Otherwise the liberator may tear down the public prison while reproducing a private one inside another life.

In the final essay, Evey will stand before a lever. V will already be dead, and the decision will at last appear to be hers. But after everything he has done, the most difficult question remains: in what sense can it be her decision?

艾薇醒来时，头上套着袋子。她被剃光头发，换上囚服，关进一间潮湿的牢房。审讯者反复追问 V 在哪里；她不说，他们就用水和死亡来逼她开口。

最后一次审讯，她得到两个选择：交代，或者被枪决。

她选择死。牢门却打开了。

一、牢房是假的，经历不是

那不是诺斯费尔的监狱，而是 V 搭出来的布景。抓捕、剃头、审讯、浸水和处决威胁，全部由他安排。

布景是假的，艾薇经历的恐惧却没有因此变成假的。她不知道行刑不会发生，不知道门外站的是谁，也不知道自己什么时候能出去。对身处其中的人来说，那就是一座监狱。

电影故意让这段经历与拉克希尔互相映照。V 曾被编号、囚禁和实验；多年以后，他把自己熟悉的恐惧重新搭建起来，让另一个人走了一遍。

受过暴力的人当然可能反抗暴力。但他也可能学会暴力的语法：如果我确信终点是好的，是否就可以替另一个人决定她必须经历什么？

二、V 所说的自由

真相揭开后，艾薇愤怒地问：你怎么能这样做？V 的回答不是自己需要一个帮手。更准确地说，他相信艾薇一生都被恐惧囚禁，而自己只是让她看见牢笼。他说，他希望有别的办法，却想不到别的办法。

这句话没有替他开脱，反而点出了问题：他已经先替艾薇定义了她的困境，也先替她规定了治疗方式。

V 想给她的不是服从，而是不再畏惧；诺斯费尔想让所有人害怕，他想让一个人从害怕里出来。两者的方向不能混为一谈。可方向不同，并不会自动让一切手段都变得正当。

如果“自由”必须由别人绑架你、折磨你、击碎你之后才能交到你手里，那么被交付的究竟是自由，还是他心目中自由的形状？

三、瓦莱丽的信也被放进了布景

牢房里唯一支撑艾薇的，是从墙缝里找到的瓦莱丽来信。信是真的。瓦莱丽是真的。她在拉克希尔写下那些字，再把它们递到隔壁；V 后来只是照着自己收到它的方式，把信递给艾薇。

然而，这封真实的信被放进了一个人为设计的时刻。V 知道一个等待处决的人读到它，会受到怎样的触动。他没有伪造瓦莱丽，却把她的见证编进了自己的计划。

这让场景变得尤其难判。瓦莱丽的文字确实把力量给了一个陌生人；V 也确实借用了这份力量，去完成一次未经同意的改造。

同一样东西，可以是一份赠予，也可以在另一个人的安排中变成工具。区别不在文字本身，而在接收者有没有拒绝整个安排的可能。

四、结果不能倒推许可

艾薇最终拒绝出卖 V。门打开后，她走上屋顶，在雨里发现自己不再害怕。愤怒之后，她也承认，这段经历给了她某种从前没有的自由，甚至在离开前向 V 道谢。

电影明显邀请观众把这一刻当作重生。这个感受不必被否认：一个人可能从伤害中获得力量，也可能主动把一段遭遇重新解释成自己的转折。

但后来得到的力量，不能倒过来成为先前伤害的许可。艾薇有权感谢 V，也有权恨他；有权认领这次改变，也有权说那不是他可以替她做的事。她怎样理解自己的经历，应该由她决定。

V 最大的问题，不是他希望艾薇摆脱恐惧，而是他一度相信：因为自己看见了更好的终点，所以可以替她走完通向终点的全部选择。

五、解放者也需要边界

诺斯费尔把人抓起来，是为了压服和利用他们。V 囚禁艾薇，是为了让她不再被压服。目的不同，后果也不同；把两者说成完全相同，会抹掉重要的道德区别。

可是这部电影留下了另一条更窄、也更锋利的相似：双方都曾把“我知道你应该成为什么”置于“你愿不愿意”之前。

一个目标再正当，也不能把具体的人变成实现目标的材料。真正的帮助可以陪一个人走近恐惧，可以让她看见原来没有的路，却必须为她保留说“不”、离开和按自己的方式改变的空间。

否则，解放者推倒了外面的牢门，也可能在另一个人心里复制一间自己的牢房。

下一篇，艾薇会站在一根操作杆前。那时 V 已经死了，他终于把决定留给了她。但一个更难的问题才刚刚开始：经历过这场改造以后，那还是不是她自己的决定？
