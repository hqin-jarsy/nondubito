What This College Produces

这所学校在生产什么

Aug 28, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)Independent English edition中文原作 · 简 / 繁

Edition note: An independent English rewriting of the Chinese argument, checked against the released film and its published screenplay.

On the first day at the Imperial College of Engineering, Director Viru Sahastrabuddhe presents the new class with a cuckoo’s nest. The bird lays in another’s nest; once hatched, its young eject the other eggs. ICE receives hundreds of thousands of applications and selects only a few hundred. The students before him, he says, are the chicks that survived.

Before anyone learns engineering, everyone learns the governing metaphor: life is a race, another person occupies your place, and falling behind means becoming a broken egg.

I. Sincerity is part of the problem

“Virus” is easy to flatten into a villain. Yet he is disciplined, hardworking, and institutionally successful. He has run ICE for thirty-two years and moved it, by his own account, from twenty-eighth place to first. His schedule turns eating, shaving, and a short nap into optimized units.

He does not think competition is cruelty. He thinks the world is cruel, and a college that simulates its pressure prepares students to survive it. If one begins from that premise, strict deadlines, public ranking, and refusal to make exceptions appear responsible.

This is why good intention cannot settle the film’s question. A sincere administrator may construct a system whose ordinary, rule-following operation destroys the very capacities education should cultivate.

II. The answer and its authorized wording

Asked to define a machine, Rancho explains in ordinary language: anything that reduces human effort; a fan, a telephone, even a zipper. The professor rejects the answer. Another student recites the textbook’s long formulation and is praised.

Rancho returns for his forgotten “instruments that record, analyze, summarize, organize, and explain information”—then translates: books. The class cannot understand the elaborate formulation outside the ritual that gives it value.

The scene does not prove that technical definitions are useless. Precision matters in engineering. It shows something narrower and more damaging: the institution cannot distinguish precision used to clarify a concept from sanctioned language reproduced to display obedience.

Once the examination rewards identical surfaces, understanding becomes an expensive route to the same mark that memorization can obtain more safely.

III. Chatur is not a failed product

Chatur Ramalingam memorizes, studies obsessively, uses medication to improve recall, and distracts competitors before exams. The film makes him comic, but not unsuccessful. He ranks highly, enters a major corporation, becomes a vice president, and accumulates the objects by which his old school taught him to count success.

That matters. If rote learning merely produced failure, the criticism would be trivial. ICE works on its own terms. Chatur learns how to deliver the approved answer, outperform peers, and convert rank into institutional position.

The question is not whether the system can fulfill its promise. It is what must disappear from a person for the promise to become the organizing purpose of education.

IV. Joy’s unauthorized work

Joy Lobo builds a small helicopter carrying a wireless camera. It is not a routine assignment; it is an invention he has pursued beyond the approved timetable. The Director calls the design impractical and denies the extension Joy needs to graduate. He then telephones Joy’s father and says his son will not finish that year.

Each act can be described administratively: a deadline existed, the project was late, standards require consistency, families should be informed. No single step requires personal malice.

Joy dies by suicide. Rancho, Farhan, and Raju have secretly completed the camera helicopter as a surprise. When it flies to Joy’s window, its first successful images reveal his body.

The invention works. Recognition arrives after it can no longer reach its maker.

V. Distributed causes do not fit the form

Rancho calls the death murder, then clarifies that he blames not one man but the system. This is an accusation, not a legal verdict. Its force lies in distributed causation: no rule says “kill a student,” yet deadlines, humiliation, family pressure, ranking, and the refusal to recognize work outside the metric can converge on one vulnerable life.

The Director answers with pressure as a universal fact and institutional rank as proof of success. Both responses translate the singular event into categories the college already knows: an individual failed to cope; the college remains number one.

A system designed around pass and fail has no stable field for “a young person making something important whose capacity to continue is collapsing.” What it cannot record, it treats as external to its responsibility.

VI. The son already inside the metaphor

The opening speech contains a fact whose meaning Virus cannot yet face: his own son tried for three years to enter engineering and was rejected each time. Later Pia reveals that he wanted to study literature and become a writer, and that the “train accident” was suicide.

The father had used the same standard on the person he loved. Even after that death, he could narrate his son as one of the broken eggs and offer the story as instruction to another class.

This does not make him uniquely monstrous. It shows the depth of institutional belief. When a metric has become one’s account of reality, evidence of its damage can be reclassified as evidence that the victim was unfit.

ICE therefore produces more than engineers. It produces a grammar in which rank looks like merit, compliance looks like knowledge, and suffering looks like private inadequacy. The people inside may be decent. The grammar still governs what they are able to see.

This essay reads Rajkumar Hirani’s 2009 film 3 Idiots. The film credits Chetan Bhagat’s novel Five Point Someone as a source; plot analysis here follows the film and its published screenplay.

帝国工程学院的新生第一天，校长维鲁·萨哈斯特布德拿出一个杜鹃巢。杜鹃把蛋下进别的鸟窝；幼鸟孵出以后，会把其他蛋推出去。ICE 每年收到数十万份申请，只留下几百人。面前这些学生，就是活下来的幼鸟。

工程学还没有开始，每个人先学会了支配这里的比喻：生活是一场竞赛，别人占着你的位置，掉队就会成为摔碎的蛋。

一、真诚也是问题的一部分

把“病毒”当作反派很容易，但他自律、勤奋，而且在制度意义上成功。他管理 ICE 三十二年，按自己的说法，把学校从第二十八名带到第一名。他的时间表把吃饭、刮胡子和短暂午睡都切成经过优化的单位。

他不认为竞争是残忍，只认为世界本来残忍，而一所提前模拟压力的学校能让学生学会生存。从这个前提出发，严格期限、公开排名和拒绝例外，都会显得负责任。

所以，善意不能回答电影的问题。一个真诚的管理者，完全可能建成一套在正常、合规运转时，恰好摧毁教育本该培养之能力的制度。

二、答案与被批准的说法

老师问什么是机器，兰彻用日常语言回答：凡是减少人力的东西，风扇、电话，甚至裤子拉链都是机器。老师不接受。另一个学生背出课本上冗长的定义，得到表扬。

兰彻走到门口，又回来寻找自己忘拿的“记录、分析、概括、组织并解释信息的工具”，然后把这串话翻译成两个字：书本。离开赋予它价值的考试仪式，课堂反而听不懂那套精密表述。

这场戏不是证明技术定义无用。工程需要精确。它指出的是一个更窄也更危险的问题：学校无法区分“为了澄清概念而精确”，和“为了表演服从而复述规定语言”。

当考试只奖励相同表面，理解便成了抵达同一分数更昂贵、更危险的路径；背诵反而更稳妥。

三、查图尔不是失败产品

查图尔·拉马林加姆背书、熬夜、服用增强记忆的药，也会在考试前分散竞争者的注意。电影让他显得可笑，却没有让他失败。他名列前茅，进入大公司，当上副总裁，并取得母校教他用来计算成功的种种东西。

这一点很重要。如果死记硬背只会制造失败，批判便太容易了。ICE 按照自己的标准确实有效：查图尔学会交出规定答案、超过同伴，再把排名兑换成组织位置。

真正的问题不是制度能否兑现承诺，而是当这份承诺成为教育的组织目的时，一个人身上还有什么必须消失。

四、乔伊没有被批准的作品

乔伊·罗伯在制作一架带无线摄像头的小型直升机。它不是普通作业，而是一项超出规定时间表的发明。校长认定设计不实用，拒绝乔伊为毕业所请求的延期，随后给乔伊父亲打电话，告诉他儿子当年无法毕业。

每一步都能用行政语言说明：期限已经公布，项目确实迟交，标准需要一致，家庭有权知道。没有一步必须出于个人恶意。

乔伊自杀了。兰彻、法尔汉与拉朱原本想给他惊喜，暗中完成了摄像直升机。它飞到乔伊窗口，第一次成功传回的画面，是制造者吊在房间里的身体。

发明确实成功了，识认却来得太晚，再也无法抵达它的作者。

五、分散的原因无法填进表格

兰彻把这场死亡称为谋杀，随后说明自己责备的不是某一个人，而是制度。这是愤怒的控诉，不是法律判决。它的力量来自分散因果：没有一条规则写着“杀死学生”，但期限、羞辱、家庭压力、排名，以及对指标之外作品的拒绝，可以共同落在一个已经脆弱的生命上。

校长用“人生本来有压力”与学校排名回应。两种回答都把一件单数事件翻译成学院已经熟悉的类别：某个学生承受失败；学院仍是第一。

一套围绕及格与不及格设计的系统，没有稳定位置记录“一个年轻人正在做重要的东西，而继续生活的能力正在崩塌”。无法记录的东西，也就容易被当成责任之外。

六、早已藏在比喻里的儿子

开学演讲里已经放着一个校长尚不能面对的事实：他自己的儿子连续三年报考工程都被拒绝。后来皮娅揭开，哥哥真正想读文学、成为作家，所谓“火车事故”其实是自杀。

这个父亲把同一标准用在最爱的人身上。即使儿子已经死去，他仍能把那段失败说成一枚摔碎的蛋，再拿来教育新一届学生。

这不说明他特别邪恶，而说明制度信念有多深。当一个指标已经成为现实本身的解释，损害它的证据也能被重新分类成：受害者原本就不够合格。

因此，ICE 生产的不只是工程师。它还生产一套语法：排名看起来像功绩，服从看起来像知识，痛苦则看起来像个人能力不足。里面的人可以善良，这套语法仍会决定他们能够看见什么。
