SAE Theory of Government

To Whom a Government Answers

政府论系列

In Apple City’s old quarter there is no parking after ten at night; on Levee Street a woman kept a stall for twenty years; downstream lies Willow Ferry. A government judges every day: it writes a ticket, decides on an application, pins a man down, freezes an account, renews an emergency order. To whom, and for what, must a government’s judgments in particular situations answer? The series writes no handbook of judgment, since rules cannot judge in anyone’s place; it writes where judgments fall, in how many layers they answer, and whether, once a question has been answered, anything can actually move.

Six questions follow one another: who makes a government’s judgments, and in whose name they take effect; on what grounds it compels, and where it stops; how far it must guarantee what it has embedded; how levels of government divide the work, and who checks whom; what holds in an emergency, and what may go faster; who calls power to account, and whether calling to account has a final bearer. Together they make one main line: toward its internal subjects, a government holds to anti-colonization and strives for cultivation; its judgments cross no line, must be explained, and remain open to questioning.

Seven essays from the seven papers of SAE Theory of Government: a prequel and six essays. They all take place in one invented city, Apple City: a permit examiner and a review officer, a patrol officer and a social worker, an empty assessment post at the Housing Department, the water works downstream, a mayor on the night of a flood, and a parent who went to ask.

Three reading modes: English, Simplified and Traditional Chinese.

Prequel
A Parking Ticket

In Apple City’s old quarter there is no parking after ten at night; a resident who disagrees parks there anyway and gets a ticket. This series asks how a government judges in particular situations, and to whom, and for what, its judgments must answer. First it clears away what a government is not: not an end, not a subject, not a duty-bearer, not a “we”; holding office and carrying out are not legitimacy; nor is it the source of basic rights.

Essay 01
Fortieth in Line

A woman kept a stall on Levee Street for twenty years; she has died, and her daughter wants to carry it on. The rules say nothing of succession, so as a new application she is fortieth in line. Who makes a government’s judgments, in whose name they take effect, to whom and for what they answer; why rules cannot judge in anyone’s place and “I was only following the rules” shields no one; and what is left to ask when a question is heard but moves nothing.

Essay 02
A Night and a Day

In one night and one day Apple City laid hands on people three times: a patrol officer pinned down a man with a bottle, a social worker took a child away, the Tax Office froze a shopkeeper’s account. None waited for consent, and they did not rest on the same thing. On what grounds a government compels and where it stops: stopping, enforcement and substitute exercise each go along a chain of their own; compulsion must itself be a limit, and may protect channels but not maintain a direction.

Essay 03
Twelve Hundred Applications

In the winter of Year 7 the application counter of Apple City’s Housing Department is open as usual, but the assessment post has been empty for three years, and twelve hundred applications for a heating subsidy lie unprocessed. On paper the subsidy lacks nothing; it simply does not operate. What a government guarantees: for basic rights it gives backing and enforcement, not the core; for embedded recognition-based rights it keeps three things, no de-righting, no empty shell, withdrawal or amendment made in public and open to questioning; a guarantee kept does not make power legitimate by itself, and taxes are not the price of one.

Essay 04
Downstream

Willow Ferry lies downstream of Apple City, and its intake shows a substance above the standard; Apple City answers in two sentences: “Our releases comply with the permit. The residents of Willow Ferry are not residents of Apple City.” One matter, several levels of government: every level is a construct, and there is an interior beyond the jurisdiction; who checks Apple City, and why the separation of powers follows conditions and is required at a threshold; the upper level does not swallow the lower, and the lower does not seal off exit.

Essay 05
The River Is Rising

On the night of the flood, Apple City’s mayor declared a state of emergency: a curfew, a high school requisitioned, residents outside the levee told to leave. The water went down in two weeks; three months later the renewal order still gave as its reason “The river is rising.” What holds in an emergency and what may go faster; how emergency powers are re-examined when their term runs out; who determines an emergency and who can bring it to an end; and, with three hundred beds for five hundred people, how a transition is made and responsibility attributed when something cannot be achieved.

Essay 06
Place by Place

In the autumn of Year 5 a parent asks: the water went down long ago, so why has the school not been returned? The hotline says it is outside its remit; the state ombudsman takes the complaint and looks into it, and a month after its report the emergency order ends. Who calls power to account, whether calling to account has a final bearer, and whether, once the question is answered, anything can actually move: the four functions of control, what follows a closed case, how a grant is taken back, capture, the cost of exit, the voting-position. The series closes here: a government can never be perfect, so the close lies not in completion but in being open to questioning.

SAE 政府论

政府向谁作答

SAE Theory of Government

苹果城的老城区，夜里十点以后禁停；河堤街上，一位妇人摆了二十年的摊；河的下游是柳渡镇。政府每天都在判断：开一张罚单，批一份申请，按倒一个人，冻结一个账户，续一道紧急令。政府在具体处境里的判断，要向谁、就什么作答？这个系列不写判断手册，规则代不了判断；它写的是判断落在哪些位置上，作答分几层，问到了以后，能不能真的动得了什么。

六件事一路问下去：判断由谁完成、以谁的名义生效；强制凭什么、到哪里为止；载入的东西要保证到什么程度；几层政府怎样分工，谁来查谁；紧急时什么照旧，什么可以快；谁追问权力，追问有没有最终的承担者。合起来是一句主线：对内部主体，政府坚持反殖民，争取涵育；它的判断不越线，要说明，可被追问。

七篇散文，改写自《SAE 政府论》七篇论文：一篇前置篇，六篇正文。它们都发生在一座虚构的苹果城：许可处的审批员与复核员，巡警与社工，住房局空着的审核岗，下游的水务所，洪水那一夜的市长，和一位去问的家长。

三种阅读模式：英文、简体、繁體。
秦汉 · 2026 · 七篇 · 英文／中文／繁體
前置篇
一张罚单

苹果城的老城区，夜里十点以后禁停；一位住户不同意，照旧停，收到了罚单。这个系列问政府在具体处境里怎样判断，判断要向谁、就什么作答。动笔之前，先清开几样政府不是的东西：不是目的，不是主体，不是义务人，不是“我们”；在位与执行不等于正当；它也不是基础权利的来源。

第一篇
排在第四十位

一位妇人在河堤街摆了二十年摊，她去世了，女儿想接着摆。规则没写接续，按新申请办，她排在第四十位。政府里的判断由谁完成，以谁的名义生效，向谁、就什么作答；规则代不了判断，“我只是执行规定”也挡不住它；问得到、却动不了的时候，还要问什么。

第二篇
一夜一日

一夜一日里，苹果城动了三次手：巡警按倒了持瓶的人，社工带走了一个孩子，税务处冻结了一位店主的账户。三件都没有等被作用的人同意，凭的却不是一样东西。政府的强制凭什么、到哪里为止：阻止、执行、代行各走一条链；强制本身须是限，可以保护通道，不可以维持方向。

第三篇
一千二百份

第七年冬天，苹果城住房局的申请窗口照常开着，审核的岗位却空了三年，一千二百份供暖补助的申请没人处理。纸面上这项补助什么都不缺，它只是不运行。政府保证什么：基础权利那一半，它给背靠与执行，不给核；已载入的承认性权利，它守三件，不脱权，不留空壳，撤改公开受问；保证了也不因此正当，交税也不是买保证的价钱。

第四篇
下游

柳渡镇在苹果城的下游，取水口测出了超标的物质；苹果城答了两句：“我们的排放合乎许可。柳渡镇的居民不是苹果城的居民。”同一件事，几层政府都管：每一级都是一个构，辖区之外也有内部；谁来查苹果城，分权为什么随条件、到了门槛就要分立；上一层不吞下一层，下一层不封死退出。

第五篇
河水上涨

洪水那一夜，苹果城的市长宣布紧急状态：宵禁，征用一所中学，堤外的人家限期撤离。水两周就退了，三个月后，续期令的理由栏里还写着“河水上涨”。紧急时什么照旧，什么可以快；紧急权力怎样到期重审；谁认定紧急，谁能叫它结束；三百张床对五百个人，做不到时怎样过渡、怎样归责。

第六篇
一处一处去问

第五年秋，一位家长问：水早退了，学校为什么还不还？热线说不在受理范围，州监察专员受理了，查了，报告发布一个月后，紧急令终止。谁追问权力，追问有没有最终的承担者，问到了以后能不能真的动得了什么：控制的四项功能，结了案以后，授权怎样收回，捕获，退出的代价，投票位。系列在这里收束：政府永远做不到完美，所以收束不在做成，在可被追问。
