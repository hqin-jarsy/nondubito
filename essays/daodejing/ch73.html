Chapter 73 of 81

还有一种勇，是敢于不做

The Courage Not to Do It

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

勇于敢者则杀，勇于不敢者则活，此两者或利或害。天之所恶，孰知其故？天之道，不战而善胜，不言而善应，不召而自来，繟然而善谋，天网恢恢，疏而不失。

一、那条没有发出去的消息

消息已经打好了。你读一遍，觉得每一句都有道理，最后那一句尤其痛快。只要发出去，对方就会知道，你并不是没有话可以回。

手指却停了一下。删掉它，不会有谁知道你做过什么。对方看不见那场已经在你心里打完的仗，旁人也不会赞扬你承担了一次克制。那一刻，你甚至担心，沉默会不会被当成认输。

很多“没有做”的事，都带着这种不容易被看见的重量。别人都在追一个发烫的项目，你决定暂不加入；机会已经谈到最后，你发现条件不合适，仍然说了不；周围的人都急着表态，你还想再弄清楚一点。

这当然不能证明不做总是更好。有时迟疑会错过机会，沉默会让别人独自承受，而删掉的消息也可能正是一句该说的拒绝。第七十三章有意思的地方，是它让两种勇气同时坐到了桌边：敢于做，也敢于不做。

二、两个方向，都不是免费的

勇于敢者则杀，勇于不敢者则活，此两者或利或害。

“敢”有大胆向前、敢于行动的意味，在不同语境中也可能带有逞强冒进的色彩。不必先证明这个字完全中性，才看得见句子里那个重要的对照：勇气不只发生在向前冲的时候。

杀与活，把风险说得很重。但我们也不能把它变成一张预测表：敢做的人会死，退下来的人会活。紧接着的“或利或害”，已经不让人舒服地把判断交给一个方向。

那个押上全部的人，可能做成一件别人不敢做的事，也可能让自己和同伴失去退路。那个没有加入的人，可能避免了损失，也可能多年以后仍然想起，当初若试一次会怎样。

两种选择还有一种不对称。行动做成了，有成果可看；失败了，也有一段过程可追问。不行动避免的损失却很难展示：没有发生的事，不会自己站出来证明你的判断。你只能说明当时看到了什么，而不能拿一个平行世界替自己作证。

所以，敢不做不是把退缩改一个好听的名字。它仍然需要承担：说清楚理由，接受可能错过，也在条件变化后愿意重新考虑。对风险认真，不等于对一切机会关门。

三、留住那个“谁知道”

“天之所恶，孰知其故？”读到这里，语气忽然有了一点停顿。为什么有些做法会遭遇那样的结局？人是否真能把利害背后的理由全部看透？

关于这里的“天”，可以有古代宗教、天道秩序等不同层面的理解。本系列更愿意沿着人与其所处世界的关系来读，但这是一条解释的路，不是宣布古文里所有关于天的语言都已经等同于现代自然规律。

同样，“孰知其故”也不必被抹成一句假问。我们确实常在事后编出完整的原因：他太敢了，所以如此；他太谨慎，所以如此。可相近的选择在另一种条件下，可能走向不同的结果。解释得流畅，不等于解释得充分。

保留这个问题，并不会让人什么都不能决定。它只是给决定加上一点分寸：我看到了这些条件，愿意为这次判断负责，但不把自己的选择说成世界唯一允许的道路。

面对生死、冲突和别人的生活时，这份分寸尤其重要。不能因为自己已经想出一个道理，就认为别人应该毫无余地地承担它的后果。

四、不必把每件事，都逼出来

后面有四个短句：“不战而善胜，不言而善应，不召而自来，繟然而善谋。”前三句用“不”，最后一句说从容而善于谋划，并不是“不谋”。

本站采用“不战”，通行本常作“不争”。沿着这里的文字，我们可以先想一想那些不必发生的冲突：有些争执是问题本身需要解决，有些却是为了证明谁不能退。少发动后一种冲突，并不保证别人不来攻击，也不取消必要的防卫和保护。

“不言而善应”，首先可以读作不靠言说而能恰当地回应。放进人与人的交谈里，它提醒我们的也许不是少说几句便能得到拥护，而是：有没有认真接住眼前这件事？一句少而准确的话，可能比一长段早已准备好的道理更贴近对方的困难。

“不召而自来”给人的想象，是无需靠反复命令维系的靠近。像前面读过的江海，可以成为关系的比喻，却不能保证你只要做好自己，机会和同伴就必然出现。让人愿意来，有时仍需要邀请、说明和耐心建立联系。

“繟然而善谋”则不是赞美没有计划。安排得太满，常会把现实只许走的一条路提前画死；留下余地，反而可以在意外出现时调整。余地也需要认真准备：备用的资源、可以商量的时间，以及发现不对时能够停下来的办法。

四句话放在一起，指向一种不以强逼为唯一办法的行动。你仍然回应，仍然筹划，仍然做事，只是不把事情是否肯服从自己的姿态，当成衡量一切的尺度。

五、那张疏疏的网

天网恢恢，疏而不失。

这幅图像很有力量：网如此广，又如此疏，却没有什么能被轻易当作与整体无关。“失”在这里可以理解为遗漏、漏失，不只是我们今天说的失败。

历来有人从报应、天道秩序来理解它。本文侧重的，是人的行动并不只留在行动那一刻。话说完了，信任可能已经变了一点；一个为了赶工绕过去的缺口，可能被后来的人接着承担。

手伸进火里会被烫到，并不需要火先对谁怀有恶意。这能帮助我们理解“后果不以愿望为转移”，却不能进一步证明，复杂的人世间也有一套同样清楚、永不漏账的奖惩程序。

现实并不总把代价还给制造代价的人。有人逃过了责任，别人却承受了损伤；有些用心没有得到回报，有些失误也能够修补。把“天网”说成每一笔好坏迟早都会结清，听起来像安慰，却可能让正在受伤的人只能等待。

这句话仍然可以让我们认真看待那些尚未显形的东西。团队不再愿意报告问题，不会因为报表上没有这一栏就不存在；一次诚实的解释，也不必等到变成业绩，才算曾经帮助过人。

好意有时没有换回信任，但那不表示它从未给过支持。默默做的事不需要被许诺未来的奖赏，才值得做。我们可以在没有总账可查的世界里，继续对已经知道的后果负责。

六、下一次，先分清自己在怕什么

准备冲上去时，可以问一句：我是真的认为这件事值得做，还是害怕别人觉得我不敢？两种动机可以混在一起，不必因为混杂就自责，但至少应当看见。

准备退下来时，也可以反过来问：这是对条件的判断，还是只想避开一场必要的谈话？勇于不做，并不替逃避责任自动发放证明。

在争执里，把下一句话晚发几分钟，有时足够让它从伤人变成说明问题。也有时候，你需要尽快联系能够提供保护的人，而不是继续独自练习平静。分寸要服从处境，不能要求危险中的人先表现得从容。

做计划时，留一块现实可以进入的空地。不知道的条件写出来，不能承担的损失说清楚，约定什么时候重新看一遍。没有把每个格子填满，并不等于没有认真准备。

最后，承认那些没有掌声的判断。一次拒绝、一次暂停、一次不再加重伤害，未必会成为故事的高潮。它们仍然可以是你认真过日子的部分，和那些向前走的时刻一样。

七、沿着最后九篇，继续往下走

从这里开始，本系列进入按通行章次安排的最后九篇。这个阅读次序帮助我们回望前文，却不意味着古代各本都有同样的编排，更不能据此断言作者在此设计了一套唯一的收尾程序。

我们先遇到两种勇气，再遇到一张并不由个人掌握的网。第七十九章还会谈“天道无亲”；到那里，同样不能把方向上的思考，换成善人必定顺遂的保证。

下一章会把问题落得更重：当威吓已经不能解决事情，人是否还要不断加码？以及，一个人拿过本不该由自己挥动的斧子时，究竟接过了什么？

一路读到最后的“人之道，为而弗争”，我们不必先成为那个永远知道何时进退的人。今天能多认出一场不必发动的争执，或者终于承担一次该采取的行动，就已经是在读书之外，回答这本书了。

I. The message you did not send

The message is ready. You read it again. Every sentence seems justified; the final one is particularly satisfying. Send it, and the other person will discover that you are not short of things to say.

Then your finger stops. If you delete it, nobody will know what you have done. The recipient cannot see the battle already fought in your head. Nobody else will praise the restraint. You may even worry that silence will look like defeat.

Many things we do not do carry this invisible weight. Everyone is joining a fashionable project and you hold back. An offer has reached its final stage and you still decline because the terms do not fit. People around you are declaring positions while you want to understand a little more.

None of this proves that declining is always wiser. Delay can lose an opportunity; silence can leave somebody else exposed; the deleted message might have contained a boundary that needed stating. Chapter 73 is interesting because it brings two forms of courage to the table: the courage to act and the courage not to act.

II. Neither direction is free

The chapter contrasts bold daring that leads toward death with a courage in not daring that leads toward life, then immediately adds that the two may bring benefit or harm. The stakes are severe, but the passage should not become a simple prediction that those who advance die and those who retreat survive.

The Chinese gan can suggest daring or bold action and, in some contexts, rashness. We do not have to prove that the word is perfectly neutral to notice something important: courage does not occur only in forward motion.

Someone who commits everything may accomplish what nobody else attempted, or may leave companions without a way back. Someone who refuses may avoid a serious loss, or may spend years wondering what would have happened if they had tried. Neither decision escapes consequence merely by belonging to the right category.

There is also an asymmetry in what becomes visible. A successful action produces something to show. Even a failed action leaves a sequence that can be examined. A disaster avoided by declining does not obligingly appear afterward to vindicate you. You can explain the conditions you saw; you cannot produce a parallel world as proof.

So the courage not to act is not a flattering new name for retreat. It includes giving reasons, accepting what may be missed, and remaining willing to reconsider if conditions change. Taking risk seriously is different from closing every door. Sometimes the harder responsibility is to go forward; sometimes it is to disappoint the people urging you on.

III. Keep the question in the sentence

“What Heaven abhors—who knows its reason?” The chapter pauses here. Why do some actions meet a particular outcome? Can we really see through every cause behind benefit and harm?

Ancient language about Heaven can have religious and cosmological dimensions. This series often reads it through the relation between people and the world they inhabit. That is an interpretive choice, not a demonstration that every reference to Heaven was already a statement of modern natural science.

Likewise, “who knows?” need not be emptied into a rhetorical flourish whose answer we confidently possess. After an event, we can often tell a smooth story: he was too daring, or she was too cautious. A similar choice under different conditions might have ended differently. A fluent explanation is not necessarily an adequate one.

Keeping the question alive does not prevent decision. It changes the terms on which we decide: these are the conditions I can see; this is the judgment I am prepared to make; I will not pretend that the world permits only my route.

That restraint matters especially when decisions concern other people's lives. Finding a persuasive argument does not entitle me to demand that they absorb all its consequences. Uncertainty should not become paralysis, but neither should my need for certainty become their obligation to comply.

IV. Not everything has to be forced

Four clauses follow: prevailing without battle, responding well without speech, coming without being summoned, and planning well in an unhurried manner. The first three contain a “not.” The fourth does not say “without planning.” Its combination of calmness and good planning is worth keeping intact.

Our adopted wording has “without battle,” where the received text commonly has “without contention.” The difference need not be narrated as a deliberate weakening of an original message. It offers us a way to distinguish conflicts that have to be addressed from confrontations launched chiefly to prove that we cannot back down.

Declining to launch the second kind does not guarantee that nobody will attack. Defence and protection can still be necessary. The point is not to remove every forceful option but to stop treating force as the only evidence that we are taking something seriously.

“Responding well without speech” is first about an apt response, not about securing other people's endorsement. In conversation, it may remind us to receive what is actually being said. One accurate sentence can address a difficulty more closely than a prepared speech designed to establish how well we understand difficulties in general.

“Coming without being summoned” suggests an attraction that does not depend on repeated commands. The image of rivers and seas offers a related metaphor. But neither image promises that opportunities and companions will arrive if we merely improve ourselves. Invitations, explanations and patient efforts to make contact may still be needed.

Finally, good planning can leave room for what the planner does not know. An overfilled schedule sometimes draws the only route reality is allowed to take. A more responsive plan includes spare resources, time for discussion and a way to stop when the signs change. Leaving room is itself work, not the absence of preparation.

Taken together, the clauses describe action that has more than compulsion available to it. You still respond, prepare and do things. You simply stop making conformity to your wishes the measure of everything that happens.

V. The wide-meshed net

“Heaven's net is vast; its mesh is loose, yet it misses nothing.” The image invites us to imagine a whole from which apparently small actions cannot simply be declared irrelevant. The word rendered as missing concerns omission or escape, not merely failure in the modern sense of an unsuccessful project.

The image has been understood in terms of retribution and cosmic order. The emphasis here is on something closer to experience: an action does not remain confined to the moment in which it occurs. A remark ends, but trust may have shifted. A safeguard bypassed to meet a deadline may leave a risk that someone else later has to manage.

Fire does not need to resent a hand in order to burn it. That simple example helps us see that consequences need not follow our wishes. It does not establish that complex human affairs contain an equally transparent and perfectly reliable system of moral rewards and penalties.

Costs do not always return to the people who create them. Some evade responsibility while others suffer. Some good efforts go unrewarded; some mistakes can be repaired. To promise that every account will eventually be settled can sound comforting while leaving those being harmed with nothing to do but wait.

The net can still draw attention to what has not yet become visible. A team no longer willing to report problems has changed even if the dashboard contains no column for trust. An honest explanation can have supported someone without later appearing as measurable success.

Quiet work need not be granted a future prize before it counts as worthwhile. We can take known consequences seriously without claiming access to a universal ledger. Indeed, the absence of a guaranteed reckoning may make present responsibility more urgent: if people are being hurt now, a cosmic promise is no substitute for help.

VI. What, exactly, are you afraid of?

Before rushing forward, ask whether the action itself seems worth taking, or whether you fear being thought afraid. Motives can be mixed. Recognising the mixture is more useful than condemning yourself for having one.

Before withdrawing, reverse the question. Are you responding to the conditions, or avoiding a conversation you owe somebody? The courage not to act does not automatically certify every act of avoidance as wise.

In an argument, waiting a few minutes before sending the next sentence can sometimes turn an injury into an explanation. At other times, the important action is to contact someone who can help keep you safe. A person in danger should not have to demonstrate philosophical calm before seeking protection.

In planning, leave a place where reality can enter. Write down the unknowns, name the losses you cannot bear, and agree when the decision will be reviewed. A plan is not careless merely because some spaces have deliberately not been filled.

And give some recognition to judgments that receive no applause. A refusal, a pause, a decision not to add another injury may never become the climax of a story. They can still belong to a seriously lived life, alongside its more visible moments of advance.

VII. The last nine essays begin here

With this chapter, our series enters its final nine essays in the received numbering. The route helps us look back across earlier themes; it does not establish that every ancient version shared this sequence, or that an author designed one definitive closing programme.

We begin with two kinds of courage and a net no individual controls. Chapter 79 will return to a Heaven without favourites. There too, a direction for thought must not be exchanged for a promise that good people always prosper.

The next chapter makes the stakes heavier: if threats are not solving a problem, should those in power simply intensify them? And what does a person take on when reaching for an axe they are not equipped or entitled to wield?

As we move toward the adopted closing line about acting without contending, we need not first become people who always know when to advance or retreat. Recognising one needless conflict, or finally taking one necessary action, is already a way of answering the book outside its pages.
