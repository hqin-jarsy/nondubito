第十九章（共八十一章）
Chapter 19 of 81

有而不立

To Have Without Declaring

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

一、紧接上一章

第十八章老子怼立招牌的上位者，你们标榜的美德是你们坏了的证据。

第十九章紧接着讲解

那不立招牌的人长什么样？

老子接着画了一张画像。

画像里这个人是这样的：

有圣，但不立"圣"。
有知，但不立"知"。
行仁义，但不立"仁义"。
会巧，但不立"巧"。
知利，但不立"利"。

他全都有。

但他全都不立。

不是没有这些东西。是有这些东西而不立作默认招牌。

第十九章是 ch15 那张善为道者画像的延续，继续画这个不立招牌的人此刻怎么运作。

二、绝不是反，绝是独绝

第十九章一上来三句：

"绝圣弃知，民利百倍。
绝仁弃义，民复孝慈。
绝巧弃利，盗贼无有。"

这三句历代最常见的读法是把"绝"读成"反对、卸除"。

绝圣 = 反对圣人。
弃知 = 抛弃知识。

按这个读法，老子成了反智主义者、反对一切美德的虚无主义者。

读偏了。

"绝"在这里不是"反对"那一支字义。

"绝"在汉语里有另一支重要的字义，是独绝、独一无二、没有人能赶上。绝技、绝唱、绝代佳人都是这个用法。

按这个字义读：

"绝圣"不是反对圣，是圣到了独绝的位置。

到了独绝位的人是什么样？

绝代佳人不需要标榜自己美。
绝技之人不需要标榜自己会。
绝圣之人不需要标榜自己有圣。

绝是程度的极致。到了那个程度，标签自己掉下来。

不是不能立标签，是到了那个位置上立标签反而是降级。

要读对老子在这里在做什么，得把有和立这两个动作分开。

有 = 自己确实有这个东西，自己确实在做这件事。
立 = 把这个东西举起来作为招牌，告诉别人"我有这个"、"这是最高的"、"你们必须按这个来"。

绝圣的人圣到独绝处，依然有圣，但不立"圣"为招牌。
绝仁的人仁到独绝处，依然行仁义，但不立"仁义"为招牌。
绝巧的人巧到独绝处，依然会巧，但不立"巧利"为招牌。

有而不立。

不是没有那些东西。是有那些东西到了独绝处，自然不需要立招牌。

老子整本书都在画"善为道者"。

ch15 画外貌：与啊冬涉水，犹啊畏四邻，深不可识。
ch17 画治理姿态：太上下知有之，百姓只是知道有他在。
ch10 立工夫：六问加玄德。

如果善为道者自己没有圣、没有知、没有仁义，前面这些章在画谁？

善为道者自己是圣，能看见结构运作。
善为道者自己有知，能善用余项。
善为道者自己行仁义，人与人的关系真实在他身上运作。
善为道者自己会巧，能动手做事。
善为道者自己知利，看得见东西怎么用。

他都有。

只是他不举招牌。

三、三个方向的有而不立

第一组：绝圣弃知

善为道者圣到独绝处，不以圣立招牌；自己有知，不以自己的知为民的默认尺子。

为什么不立？

因为一立"圣"为招牌，下面的人要么崇拜要么模仿要么反对，注意力全被招牌锁死。
一立"知"为默认尺子，民只能以这个知为最高，民长不出自己的知。

这件事在 2026 年的硅谷处处可见。

立招牌的版本：

LinkedIn 上挂满 buzzwords 的 profile，"strategic thinker, visionary leader, AI disruptor"。
Twitter 上反复 emphasize 自己是 thought leader、建过几家公司、上过 Forbes。
Podcast 上反复强调"how I see the world"。

不立招牌的版本：

profile 上写"engineer"。
默默写代码、做产品、读论文。
做完事就发一个 quiet post，没有任何"how I see the world"的总结。
不上 podcast。

立招牌的人不一定真没有那个东西，他可能真有 vision、真懂 AI、真会做产品。但他选择把这些立成招牌。

招牌一立，他周围的人就被招牌锁死。要么崇拜（"哇这个人好厉害"），要么模仿（"我要像他一样"），要么反对（"他不过如此"）。所有注意力都给了他这个人，而不是给了下面要做的事。

不立招牌的人不一定比立招牌的人弱。但他周围的人有自己的空间，不被他的招牌锁死，可以自己想、自己做、自己长出自己的方向。

民利百倍，民的可用空间翻百倍。

"利"这个字接 ch11，是形态可用的意思，不是经济利益。民的可用空间多了百倍，可以在这个空间里自己运作、自己长出自己的位。

这是涵育姿态的一次结构后果：不立，留出空间，民的余项自己涌。

第二组：绝仁弃义

善为道者仁到独绝处，自己做人与人之间的事，不立"仁义"为招牌。

ch18 讲过——"大道废，案有仁义"。大道运作时没有人需要谈仁义。

第十九章是 ch18 的正面接续。不立仁义这个招牌，民的自然关系就自己涌出。

"民复孝慈"的"复"字是回到。不是新立一个"孝慈"招牌（那就又立了），是民本来就有的家庭情感、自然的关心，这些本来就在。一旦"孝慈"招牌不压住、"仁义"招牌不扭曲，自然的孝慈自己显出来。

放到今天看，立"仁义"招牌的版本：

父母在所有亲戚朋友面前反复说"我对孩子多好"。
公司大谈"we are family"。
朋友圈反复发"和家人和谐相处"的照片。

不立招牌的版本：

父母就在做对孩子真正好的事，没说几句话。
公司就在做让员工真正好的事，文化是自己长出来的。
真实的家庭关系，不一定每张照片都和谐，但在那里运作。

立招牌的不一定是假的，但招牌一立，里面真实的关系就开始被招牌反向定义。做什么事要先看符不符合"仁义"招牌、做什么事要先看在朋友圈拍出来好不好看。

招牌反向殖民里面的真实。

不立招牌，真实的关系自己运作。

第三组：绝巧弃利

这一组里的"利"和第一组的"民利百倍"不是同一个意思。

第一组的"利"是形态可用（接 ch11）。
这一组的"利"是被立作稀缺竞争的利益。

善为道者巧到独绝处，自己会做事、自己见物，不立"巧"为身份招牌、不立"物"为稀缺。

放到今天，立招牌的版本：

营销团队人为制造稀缺性，限量发售、限时优惠、网红同款。
把某项技能立作高级身份，"会 X 才是真正的工程师"。

不立招牌的版本：

东西就是东西，能用就用。
会的就用，不会的就学，不把技能立作身份。

招牌一立，稀缺被制造出来，盗贼现象就涌出。
不立，稀缺不被制造，盗贼现象自然不发生。

这跟 ch3 一脉，"不贵难得之货，使民不为盗"。

四、自觉防成新招牌

讲完三组"绝"，老子有一段很关键的过渡：

"此三言也，以为文未足，故令之有所属。"

帛书"三言"是三句话。老子明说他在说三句话。
通行本"三者"是这三件事。

一字之差。

"三言"读起来，老子自觉地说"我刚才说的是三句话"，从言到做有个明确转换。
"三者"读起来，这三件事，模糊的指代。

为什么这字差重要？

因为这一段是老子自觉防自己的话被立作新招牌的关键过渡。

老子刚说完"绝圣弃知、绝仁弃义、绝巧弃利"，他立刻意识到一个陷阱。

读者可能这样读：

"我要做到绝圣弃知！"
"我要做到绝仁弃义！"
"我要绝巧弃利！"

一旦读者这么读，三言就被立成了新招牌、新尺子、新目标。

而老子明明在说不立招牌，他的教法本身不能被立作新招牌。

所以老子自觉地落到当下工夫，"见素抱朴，少思寡欲，绝学无忧"。

从描述性的"绝"，落到此刻的可做姿态。

这一层老子的自觉很重要。

老子在做一件涵育式教师最难的事：

明明白白指着方向给读者看，但主动防自己指的方向被读者立作必须达到的终点。

我看见了什么我说了。
但我不让我说的东西成为压你的招牌。

这是涵育的精细之处。

五、当下三条工夫

后面三条不是"达到才算合格"的终点。是善为道者当下就这样在做的姿态。

见素抱朴。

素是未染色的丝，朴是未加工的原木。

见素，看见本来未染色的样子。
抱朴，守着未加工的本然。

不是要复古、不是要退回原始状态。是守着未立构之前的位。

善为道者当下姿态：不去自己染色、不去自己加工，让看见的、抱着的，保持本来未立构的样子。

放到今天：

看见同事汇报工作时，不抢着在心里给他贴标签（"这是个 X 型的人"）。
看见一个新现象时，不抢着把它分类到已有的概念框架里。
看见自己的情绪时，不抢着给它贴一个名号（"这是焦虑"）。

让它保持未染色的样子。

少思寡欲。

帛书"少思"，通行本改作"少私"。一字之差。

少思 = 少立念头的构。
少私 = 少自私。

按帛书读，少思是结构操作（少立念头的构）。
按通行本读，少私是道德规范（不自私）。

按帛书读，前面三句"绝圣弃知/绝仁弃义/绝巧弃利"都是结构操作，这一句也是结构操作，下一句"绝学无忧"也是结构操作。整章一贯。

按通行本"少私"，中间突然插入一个道德规范层，脱了。

少思，念头涌起时不抢着立构、不抢着下判断、不抢着给事情一个名号。
寡欲，欲动涌起时看见它、不跟着它抢先立构。

两个结构操作。

绝学无忧。

学的字形是双手、爻（绳结/构的象）、冖（顶盖）、子。本义是把已立的结构传给下一代。

学的本义是承接前人立的构。

绝学不是拒绝学习，是卸"入已立的构"作为默认。

善为道者当下，遇事不先问"前人怎么做"、不先问"体系里怎么规定"、不先去套已立的构。该学的时候学，但不以学为默认入口。

为什么这样做？

因为一旦以学为默认入口，自己的当下判断就被已立的构锁死。

放到今天：

遇到一个新问题，不先去 Google"业界 best practice 是什么"。先自己想。
做产品决定，不先去看"竞品怎么做的"。先自己看用户。
写代码，不先去找"标准设计模式"。先自己想问题。

不是不学。是学不作默认入口。

无忧，真的没有忧。

忧是立构的反应。立了构就担心构能不能立住。

不以学为默认、不承接已立的构作为自己的判断依据，立构的担心自然不发生。

这是 ch8 立过的"不 X → 无 X"算子样本：姿态让结果自然发生。

六、绝学无忧归这一章

这里要补一个关键字差。

帛书把"绝学无忧"放在第十九章末，作为三条工夫的第三条。
通行本把"绝学无忧"切到第二十章首句，孤悬。

按帛书读，第十九章是完整的"三破 + 三收"画像：

三破：绝圣弃知、绝仁弃义、绝巧弃利。三个方向的"有而不立"示范。
三收：见素抱朴、少思寡欲、绝学无忧。三条当下工夫。

四个"绝"字（前三破 + 第四绝学）形成完整的字法谱。
"不学 → 无忧"是干净的"不 X → 无 X"算子。
教学结构完整，从言到做，最后落到当下可做的工夫。

按通行本读，第十九章到"少私寡欲"就断了，没有工夫收束。第二十章首句"绝学无忧"孤悬，跟第二十章正文"唯之与阿"不接续。

两个本子之间的差距是一个完整的画像被切成两半。

按帛书读，第十九章是一张完整画像：有而不立 + 当下三工夫。

七、画像不是目标

读完这一章，如果你想"我要做到绝圣弃知！我要做到见素抱朴！"——

那就掉进老子在过渡句里专门防的那个陷阱了。

把"绝圣弃知、见素抱朴"立成要达到的目标，就变成新的招牌。

画像不是目标。

画像是给走在那条路上的人看的此刻样貌。

走那条路本身就自然这样运作，不是为了符合画像去做这些。

如果你看着画像觉得"对，我这样的"，画像就到位了。
如果你看着画像觉得"我离这个很远"，画像也到位了，因为你看见了方向。
如果你看完什么感觉都没有，画像也到位了，不是每张画像每个人都要立刻看见。

画像在这里。

每一个有志者走自己的路。

某一刻你不抢着立招牌，你那一刻就在画像上。
某一刻你不抢着学已有的构，你那一刻就在画像上。
某一刻你让情绪保持未染色的样子，你那一刻就在画像上。

不是达到画像才算数。

是每一个"那一刻"都是一次到位。

One · Following Directly from the Previous Chapter

Chapter 18 confronted rulers who raise signboards: the virtues you proclaim are evidence that you've corrupted what they name.

Chapter 19 follows directly.

What does the person who raises no signboards look like?

Laozi draws a portrait.

The person in the portrait is like this:

Has sagacity, but does not declare "sagacity."
Has knowledge, but does not declare "knowledge."
Practices benevolence and righteousness, but does not declare "benevolence and righteousness."
Possesses skill, but does not declare "skill."
Understands advantage, but does not declare "advantage."

He has all of it.

But he declares none of it.

Not that he lacks these things. He has them — and does not raise them as default signboards.

Chapter 19 continues the portrait from (ch. 15) of the one adept in the Dao, showing how this person who raises no signboards operates in the present moment.

Two · Jué Is Not Opposition — Jué Means Unmatched

The chapter opens with three lines:

"Jué-shèng qì-zhī, the people's benefit multiplies a hundredfold. Jué-rén qì-yì, the people return to filial care. Jué-qiǎo qì-lì, theft and banditry cease."

The most common reading throughout history renders jué as "reject" or "abolish."

Jué-shèng = oppose the sage. Qì-zhī = discard knowledge.

Under this reading, Laozi becomes an anti-intellectualist — a nihilist who opposes all virtue.

A misread.

Jué here is not from the "oppose" branch of its meaning.

In Chinese, jué carries another important semantic lineage: unmatched, without equal, peerless. Juéjì (supreme skill), juéchàng (peerless performance), juédài jiārén (a beauty without compare) — all use it this way.

Read this way: "Jué-shèng" doesn't mean opposing sagacity. It means being sagacious to an unmatched degree.

What is the person at the unmatched position like?

A peerless beauty doesn't need to proclaim she's beautiful.
A person of supreme skill doesn't need to proclaim she's capable.
A person of unmatched sagacity doesn't need to proclaim she's sagacious.

Jué is the extreme of degree. At that degree, the label falls off on its own.

Not that she can't have a label — it's that at that position, raising a label would actually be a demotion.

To read Laozi correctly here, we must separate two actions: having and declaring.

Having = actually possessing the thing, actually doing it.
Declaring = raising it as a signboard, telling others "I have this," "this is highest," "you must follow this."

The person of unmatched sagacity still has sagacity, but does not declare "sagacity" as a signboard.
The person of unmatched benevolence still practices benevolence, but does not declare "benevolence and righteousness" as a signboard.
The person of unmatched skill still exercises skill, but does not declare "skill and advantage" as a signboard.

To have without declaring.

Not absent of these things. Having them to an unmatched degree — and therefore naturally needing no signboard.

The whole book draws portraits of "the one adept in the Dao."

(Ch. 15) draws the outward form: wary as one wading winter rivers, alert as one surrounded on all sides, deep and unknowable.
(Ch. 17) draws the governing posture: the greatest is the one of whom the people only know he exists.
(Ch. 10) establishes the practice: six questions and the virtue of xuan.

If the one adept in the Dao had no sagacity, no knowledge, no benevolence — who were those earlier chapters portraying?

The one adept in the Dao is sagacious — can see how structures operate.
The one adept in the Dao has knowledge — can work skillfully with the remainder.
The one adept in the Dao practices benevolence and righteousness — genuine human connection operates through him.
The one adept in the Dao has skill — can act and make things.
The one adept in the Dao understands advantage — can see how things are usable.

He has all of it. He simply raises no signboards.

Three · Three Directions of Having Without Declaring

First pair: Jué-shèng qì-zhī

The one adept in the Dao is sagacious to an unmatched degree and raises no "sagacity" signboard; he has knowledge, but doesn't make his knowledge the default measuring stick for the people.

Why not declare?

Because once "sagacity" is raised as a signboard, those below either worship, imitate, or oppose — all attention locks onto the signboard. Once "knowledge" becomes the default measuring stick, the people can only take this knowledge as their ceiling; they can't grow their own.

This is visible everywhere in Silicon Valley in 2026.

The declaring version: LinkedIn profiles draped in buzzwords — "strategic thinker, visionary leader, AI disruptor." Twitter feeds repeatedly emphasizing: thought leader, serial founder, Forbes-listed. Podcast after podcast delivering "how I see the world."

The non-declaring version: profile says "engineer." Quietly writing code, building product, reading papers. Posting a quiet note when something's done, with no "how I see the world" summary.

The declaring person isn't necessarily fake. But once the signboard goes up, those around them are locked — worship, imitate, or oppose. All attention goes to the signboard, not the actual work.

The non-declaring person isn't necessarily weaker. But those around them have room. Not locked by a signboard, they can think for themselves, act for themselves, grow their own direction.

The people's benefit multiplies a hundredfold — their usable space expands a hundredfold. In that space, the people can operate and grow into their own positions. This is one structural consequence of the nurturing posture: not declaring, leaving space, letting the people's remainder surge forth on its own.

Second pair: Jué-rén qì-yì

The one adept in the Dao practices benevolence and righteousness to an unmatched degree, conducts his own human relationships genuinely, and raises no "benevolence and righteousness" signboard.

(Ch. 18) already established this: "When the great Dao is abandoned, benevolence and righteousness appear." When the Dao operates, no one needs to talk about benevolence and righteousness.

Chapter 19 is the positive continuation of ch. 18. When the signboard isn't raised, the people's natural relationships surge forth on their own.

The character fù (return) in "the people return to filial care" means going back. Not raising a new "filial care" signboard — that would again be declaring — but the familial feeling, the natural care that was already there. Remove the pressure of the signboard and it shows itself.

The declaring version: parents repeatedly telling relatives how well they treat their child. Companies proclaiming "we are family." Social media filled with photos of harmonious family life.

The non-declaring version: parents just doing what's genuinely good for the child, without much talk. Company culture growing on its own. Actual family relationships — not every moment perfect, but real and operating.

Once the signboard is raised, the real relationship inside gets reverse-defined by it. Every action gets checked against whether it fits "benevolence," whether it looks good posted online. The signboard reverse-colonizes the genuine interior. Without it, real relationships operate on their own.

Third pair: Jué-qiǎo qì-lì

The lì in this pair differs from "the people's benefit" in the first pair.

The first pair's lì means usable form (following ch. 11). This pair's lì is advantage raised as manufactured scarcity.

The one adept in the Dao is skilled to an unmatched degree and raises neither "skill" as an identity signboard nor things as manufactured scarcity.

The declaring version: marketing teams artificially creating scarcity — limited editions, flash sales, influencer endorsements. Raising certain skills as elite markers — "You're not a real engineer unless you know X."

The non-declaring version: things are things. Use them if they work. Know what you know, learn what you don't — without making skills into identity.

Once the signboard goes up and scarcity is manufactured, theft and banditry emerge. Don't raise it, don't manufacture scarcity — the banditry phenomenon naturally doesn't arise.

This runs through from ch. 3: "Not prizing rare goods keeps the people from stealing."

Four · Guarding Against Turning It Into a New Signboard

After the three jué, Laozi has a pivotal transition:

"These three sayings, as text alone, are insufficient — so give them something to belong to."

The silk manuscript reads "three sayings" — three sentences. Laozi explicitly says he's given three sentences, marking a clear turn from words to doing. The standard text reads "three things" — a vague referent. One character's difference.

Why does this matter?

Because this is the key transition where Laozi consciously guards against his own words becoming a new signboard.

He has just said jué-shèng-qì-zhī, jué-rén-qì-yì, jué-qiǎo-qì-lì — and immediately recognizes a trap. Readers might respond: "I must achieve jué-shèng-qì-zhī!" Once they do, the three sayings become new signboards, new measuring sticks.

But Laozi is saying don't declare signboards. His own teaching cannot itself be raised as a new signboard.

So Laozi consciously grounds into present-moment practice: "See the unhewn (sù), hold the uncarved (pǔ), have few thoughts, few desires, release learning, be free of worry."

From the descriptive jué, dropping into a posture doable right now.

This layer of Laozi's self-awareness is important.

He's doing the hardest thing a nurturing teacher does: pointing clearly in a direction so the reader can see — while actively preventing that direction from being raised as a required endpoint.

I see what I see and I say it. But I won't let what I say become a signboard pressing down on you.

This is the refinement of nurturing.

Five · Three Present-Moment Practices

The following three are not endpoints requiring achievement before one "qualifies." They are postures the one adept in the Dao is doing right now.

See the unhewn (jiàn sù), hold the uncarved (bào pǔ).

Sù is undyed silk. Pǔ is unworked timber.

Jiàn sù — see the as-yet-undyed form. Bào pǔ — hold the as-yet-unworked natural state.

Not a call for primitivism. It's holding the position before constructs are erected.

In contemporary terms: when watching a colleague present, not rushing to label them internally. When encountering a new phenomenon, not rushing to categorize it into existing frameworks. When noticing one's own emotion, not rushing to name it ("this is anxiety"). Letting it remain undyed.

Have few thoughts (shǎo sī), few desires (guǎ yù).

The silk manuscript reads "few thoughts (sī)" — the standard text changes it to "few private desires." One character's difference.

Read as the silk manuscript, the whole chapter is consistent: the three jué are structural operations, these two are structural operations, and "release learning, be free of worry" is also a structural operation. The standard text's "few private desires" suddenly inserts a moral-normative layer, breaking the flow.

Few thoughts: when thoughts surge up, don't rush to erect constructs, rush to judge, rush to name. Few desires: when desire stirs, see it — don't follow it into rushing to erect constructs. Two structural operations.

Release learning (jué xué), be free of worry (wú yōu).

The character xué (learning) shows: two hands, a knotted-cord symbol (pattern of constructs), a cover, a child. Its original meaning is passing already-erected constructs to the next generation.

Releasing learning is not refusing to learn — it's uninstalling "enter already-erected constructs" as the default.

The one adept in the Dao, meeting a situation, doesn't first ask "how did predecessors do this," doesn't first reach for already-erected constructs. He learns when learning is called for — but doesn't make learning the default entry point.

In contemporary terms: facing a new problem, don't first Google "what's the industry best practice" — think first. Making a product decision, don't first look at "what are competitors doing" — look at users first. Writing code, don't first find "the standard design pattern" — think through the problem first.

Not that one doesn't learn. Learning is not the default entry point.

Free of worry — genuinely free of worry. Worry is the reaction to erecting constructs. Not making learning the default, not inheriting already-erected constructs as your judgment — the worry about construct-stability naturally doesn't arise.

This is a sample of the "not X → no X" operator from (ch. 8): the posture lets the result naturally occur.

Six · "Release Learning" Belongs to This Chapter

The silk manuscript places "release learning, be free of worry" at the end of Chapter 19 — as the third present-moment practice.
The standard text cuts it to the opening of Chapter 20, where it hangs unattached.

Read as the silk manuscript, Chapter 19 is a complete "three-break, three-collect" portrait:

Three breaks: jué-shèng-qì-zhī, jué-rén-qì-yì, jué-qiǎo-qì-lì. Three directions of having without declaring.
Three collects: jiàn-sù-bào-pǔ, shǎo-sī-guǎ-yù, jué-xué-wú-yōu. Three present-moment practices.

Four jué characters form a complete verbal register. The "not-learning → no-worry" is a clean "not X → no X" operator. The teaching structure is complete — from words to doing, landing on present-moment doable practice.

Read as the standard text, Chapter 19 ends abruptly after "few private desires" with no practice conclusion. The opening "release learning, be free of worry" of Chapter 20 hangs disconnected — it doesn't follow from Chapter 20's body text. The gap between the two versions: a complete portrait split in two.

Seven · The Portrait Is Not a Goal

Having read this chapter — if you now think "I must achieve jué-shèng-qì-zhī! I must achieve jiàn-sù-bào-pǔ!" — you've fallen into exactly the trap Laozi guarded against in the transition.

Raising them as targets to achieve turns them into new signboards.

Portraits are not goals.

Portraits are the present appearance of someone walking that path.

Walking the path itself naturally operates this way — not doing these things in order to match the portrait.

If you read the portrait and think "yes, that's me" — the portrait has landed.
If you read the portrait and think "I'm far from this" — the portrait has also landed, because you've seen the direction.
If you read it and feel nothing — the portrait has also landed. Not every portrait must immediately reach every reader.

The portrait is here.

The moment you don't rush to raise a signboard — you're in the portrait.
The moment you don't rush to inherit already-erected constructs — you're in the portrait.
The moment you let an emotion remain undyed — you're in the portrait.

Not that achieving the portrait is what counts.

Each such moment is an arrival.
