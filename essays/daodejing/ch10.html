第十章
Chapter 10 of 81

营魄

Guarding the Soul

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

一、我们可能在做这两件事

我们可能都在做这两件事——

一件是养身体。方法很多，包括但不限于，健身房，蛋白粉，Whoop 或 Oura 监测心率睡眠 HRV，营养品，间歇性禁食，可能还有冷水浴。

另一件是修心。方法也很多，比如，冥想 app，正念课，心理咨询，阅读哲学，甚至还有人尝试微剂量迷幻药。

两件事并行做。

但很少有人意识到——它们其实必须是同一件事。

不是说必须同时做。是说在结构上是同一件事的两面，有点像武侠小说里说的内功和外功需要并进。

修心修着修着会虚脱。意识上"清明"，但身体疲惫，作息乱，体力差——修心修成了脱离身体的浮空意识。这种情况在禅修圈、冥想圈、心灵成长圈里非常常见。

养身养着养着会丢主体。身体指标全好，但人是一个"指标完美但空白"的状态——没有人在那个身体里运作。这种情况在硬核生物黑客和量化自我圈里非常常见。

老子第十章一上来就讲这件事——为什么这两个偏路都不行，正确的做法是什么。

二、营和魄

第十章第一句：

"营魄抱一，能毋离乎？"

营是什么？是血气的运行层——身体里血液、呼吸、体力、饮食、精力。是一个人最基础的物理底子。

魄是什么？是神志的安住层——意识、品德、念头、觉知、主体位上的自我。是基于营涌现出来的那个意识主体。

营是底。魄是面。

更精确地说：营是基础层，魄是涌现层。

这是这本书第一次把"基础层 / 涌现层"这对结构收到具体的个体人身上。第一章立了这对结构，第二章用六对（有无、难易、长短……）演示了它在其他尺度上的运作。但六对里没有一对直接就是个体人身上那一对。

营 / 魄就是。

身体是基础，意识是涌现。

涌现层没有基础层承接就浮空——这就是"只修心不养身"的虚脱。
基础层没有涌现层就是一块躯壳——这就是"只养身不修心"的空白。

抱一是什么？字面是"抱着一"——把两层合住，维持它们是一个整体。

但抱一不是把两层硬捆在一起。是顺着它们本来就连续不分离的那个实际。

营和魄本来就不是两个分开的东西——一个活着的人，血气在运行、意识在涌现，这是一件事，不是两件。抱一是维持这件事本来就在的样子，不让它被人为地分裂。

"能毋离乎"——问自己，能不能不让这两层分离？

三、六问的结构

这一问是这一章六问的根骨。

老子接下来又连着问了五个"能…乎"——

"抟气致柔，能婴儿乎？"
"修除玄览，能无疵乎？"
"爱民治国，能毋以为乎？"
"天门启阖，能为雌乎？"
"明白四达，能毋以知乎？"

全部六问加起来是一个结构。

第一问立两层框。第二问、第三问分别校准两层本身。第四问、第五问、第六问校两层合起来在外面的三个方向上的运作。

我们来一个一个看。

抟气致柔，能婴儿乎——校营这一层。能不能让血气回到婴儿那个柔软、顺畅、没有积累的紧张和硬僵的位上。这是基础层的工夫。婴儿不是比喻——是基础层的可返之位。

修除玄览，能无疵乎——校魄这一层。"玄览"是观照凿构循环本身。能不能在这个观照上不挂构？一想"我在观照"——挂了一层；一想"我观照得好不好"——又挂一层；一想"我观照到了什么"——再挂一层。每挂一层就是一个疵。修除是每挂一层就清一层。这是涌现层的工夫。

两层各自校到位了——然后看两层合起来在外面运作。

爱民治国，能毋以为乎——治层。做爱民、治国的事，但不挂"我在做这件事"的位。第三章讲的"无为"是从外面看的：动作不殖民。这一问从里面讲：内在不挂自己。

天门启阖，能为雌乎——感层。"天门"是身上自然在发生的开合（眼睁眼闭，吸进呼出，起念落念）。这些开合自己在发生，不由意志决定。"为雌"是承接位——不抢着做"我来开"、"我来阖"的主体。

明白四达，能毋以知乎——知层。知到顶配（明白通达四方），但不用知做殖民动作——不以"我知道"作为去覆盖别人的理由。

六问合起来——

营魄抱一——立两层框。
抟气致柔——校基础层（身体）。
修除玄览——校涌现层（意识）。
爱民治国能毋以为——两层合运作于治。
天门启阖能为雌——两层合运作于感。
明白四达能毋以知——两层合运作于知。

其实这是一份我们可以直接用的自检清单。

老子在这一章把工夫论从前九章每一章拎一个面的拆讲，第一次合到一个具体的主体身上。

六问都走通——整个人从基础到涌现到外在三个方向都没有被构收死——这种状态老子给了一个名字。

四、玄德

"生之，畜之，生而弗有，长而弗宰也，是谓玄德。"

走通六问的主体，他的运作在继续——该生的在生，该畜的在畜。不是工夫走通就停了。

但生而弗有——生出一个层、一个构、一个作品、一个事业、一个关系——不把这个生出来的东西立成"我的"。

长而弗宰——长出来的东西有它自己的运行，不由你来主宰它的方向。

走通六问的主体，他的运作就是这个样子——做事在继续，但主体没有挂在"我做了"、"这是我的"上。

这就叫——

玄德。

玄是凿构循环本身（第一章立）。德是个体余项（第一章立）。

玄 + 德合起来——凿构循环贯穿过的个体余项——一凿又一凿循环下来，始终没被任何一轮收死，反而每一轮都涌到新位置的那个状态。

这是个体余项在凿构循环里能到的最高位。

也就是说——

玄德是个体主体能到的最高位。

它不是一个抽象的美德概念。它是六问走通之后这个具体主体身上发生的事——他的余项每一轮都涌到新位置，没有一处被构收死。

五、通行本加了一句

这一章字差不少。最戏剧性的一处，是这一段命名的字差。

帛书的玄德命名是两句：

生而弗有，长而弗宰。

通行本的玄德命名是三句：

生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰。

通行本多了一句——"为而不恃"。

这一句从哪里来？

来源很清楚——第二章。

第二章讲涵育三面的时候，最后那一段是"万物作而弗始也，生而不有，为而不恃，功成而弗居"——四句。

通行本注者读到第十章，发现第十章只有两句"生而弗有，长而弗宰"，在他的框架里觉得不完整——为什么缺"为而不恃"？

于是他把第二章的"为而不恃"搬到第十章，让玄德命名"齐"起来。

这一补的意图并不是恶意的。注者想让两章对齐——让"涵育三面"在玄德章也显出完整。

但这正是历代注家最典型的动作——

用自己读前面几章整理出来的框架，回过头来修改后面的原文，让后面显得"齐"。

这一动作的性质是：把自己的框架塞进原文。

而老子第十章本来不在讲涵育三面。

老子在讲六问加玄德命名——玄德的命名从工夫论走过来，本来就不需要凑齐涵育三面。

第十章的玄德是个体余项走通凿构循环之后的品质——比涵育更宽的位。涵育者品质是它在"对他者方向"上的一个显现，不是它的全部。

通行本把"为而不恃"搬进来之后——玄德被窄化成了涵育的同义词。玄德更宽的那个位被这一补吞了。

读者把帛书和通行本的这一处摆在一起看——就能直接看到一次"patch 在做什么"。不是改字义，是改结构的范围。

这是 Paper 2 引出的一个总观察——通行本的关键字差，几乎都在做同一件事：把老子具体的教导磨成抽象的描述，把宽的位收窄到熟悉的框架里。

不是个别字的润色。是一个系统性方向。

这种 patch 每一处都不是恶意的——动手的注者都有自己的好意。但每一处合起来，老子原本立的位都被往传统已有的框架里收了一档。

六、玄德比涵育宽

玄德比涵育宽。

涵育是什么？是对他者方向上的工夫——让被育者自己长出来，让出位置，不殖民。

玄德是什么？是六问走通的整体——基础层、涌现层、治、感、知，五个面合起来都不被构收死。

涵育只是玄德在第四问那一面（爱民治国能毋以为）的显现。

一个有玄德的人，他可以是涵育者——但他不必只是涵育者。他可以做任何事——做事、感知、知识——只要这些动作里他都不挂"我"在动作上。

这是个体主体能到的最高位。

读完第十章再回头看前九章——

前九章每一章都是在拆讲玄德的一个面。

第二章讲涵育——玄德的对他者方向。
第三章讲治理——玄德在治国上的应用。
第五章讲卸默认——玄德的语义工夫。
第七章讲不自生——玄德对自己的不立构。
第八章讲水——玄德的物理类比。

九章拆讲的所有工夫——到第十章合成一个完整的主体品质。

接下来 Paper 2 的八章——从第十一章到第十八章——都是从这个主体品质往外展开。

第十一章讲虚有。第十二章讲为腹。第十三章讲贵身。第十四章讲执道。第十五章给出走通六问的主体的画像。第十六章讲两条 path。第十七章讲涵育者的四档。第十八章讲什么时候这个主体崩了。

第十章是这本书的脊梁中的一节——

把所有工夫合到一个具体的人身上。

问六遍"你能…乎"。

这个人走通了，就叫玄德。

一、We Are Probably Doing Two Things

We are probably all doing two things.

One is cultivating the body. The methods are many — the gym, protein powder, a Whoop or Oura tracking heart rate, sleep, and HRV, nutritional supplements, intermittent fasting, maybe cold showers too.

The other is cultivating the mind. The methods are also many — meditation apps, mindfulness courses, therapy, reading philosophy, some even trying microdosing psychedelics.

Both run in parallel.

But very few people realize — these must actually be the same thing.

Not that you have to do them simultaneously. The point is that structurally they are two faces of the same thing — something like how martial arts fiction speaks of inner gong and outer gong needing to advance together.

Cultivate the mind long enough and it floats loose. Consciousness becomes "clear," but the body is exhausted, the routine disordered, physical energy depleted — mind cultivation has produced a disembodied floating awareness. This is extremely common in Zen, meditation, and spiritual growth circles.

Cultivate the body long enough and you lose the subject. Every metric looks perfect, but the person is in a state of "perfect metrics, empty inside" — no one operating that body. This is extremely common in hardcore biohacker and quantified-self circles.

Chapter 10 opens with exactly this — why both detours fail, and what the correct approach is.

二、Ying and Po

The first line of Chapter 10:

"营魄抱一，能毋离乎？"

"Can ying and po embrace as one, and never separate?"

What is ying? The circulatory layer — the body's blood, breath, physical strength, food, and vital energy. The most basic physical substrate of a person.

What is po? The layer where awareness settles — consciousness, moral character, thoughts, perception, the self as subject. The conscious subject that emerges from ying.

Ying is the ground. Po is the surface.

More precisely: ying is the foundation layer, po is the emergent layer.

This is the first time the book brings the "foundation layer / emergent layer" structure down to the concrete individual. Chapter 1 established this pair; Chapter 2 demonstrated it across other scales through six pairs (being and non-being, difficulty and ease, long and short...). None of those six pairs was directly the pair at work within the individual person.

Ying / po is.

Body is foundation. Consciousness is emergence.

The emergent layer floats loose without the foundation layer to receive it — this is the exhaustion of "cultivating only the mind without tending the body."

The foundation layer without the emergent layer is just a shell — this is the emptiness of "tending only the body without cultivating the mind."

What is embracing as one (抱一)? Literally, "holding the one" — holding the two layers together, maintaining them as a whole.

But embracing as one is not forcing the two layers into a hard bundle. It is following the actual continuity that was already there — never separated.

Ying and po are not two separate things to begin with. In a living person, blood circulates and awareness emerges — this is one event, not two. Embracing as one means maintaining this event in its natural state, not letting it be artificially split.

"Can you not let the two layers separate?" — ask yourself: can you keep them from parting?

三、The Structure of Six Questions

This question is the backbone of the chapter's six questions.

Laozi follows with five more "can you...?" questions:

"抟气致柔，能婴儿乎？"
"修除玄览，能无疵乎？"
"爱民治国，能毋以为乎？"
"天门启阖，能为雌乎？"
"明白四达，能毋以知乎？"

All six together form a structure.

The first question establishes the two-layer frame. Questions two and three calibrate each layer in itself. Questions four, five, and six calibrate how the two layers together operate in three outward directions.

Zhuan qi zhi rou, neng ying er hu — calibrating the ying layer. Can you bring vital energy back to the infant's softness, fluidity, free of accumulated tension and rigidity? This is the practice of the foundation layer. Infant is not a metaphor — it is the returnable position of the foundation layer.

Xiu chu xuan lan, neng wu ci hu — calibrating the po layer. "Xuan lan" is observing the chisel-construct cycle itself. Can you keep this observation free of clinging? Think "I am observing" — one layer of clinging. Think "How well am I observing?" — another layer. Think "What am I observing?" — yet another. Each layer of clinging is a flaw (疵). Clearing means clearing each layer as it appears. This is the practice of the emergent layer.

Once both layers are individually calibrated — then look at how they operate outward together.

Ai min zhi guo, neng wu yi wei hu — the governance direction. Do the work of loving the people and governing the state, without carrying "I am doing this" as a position. Chapter 3 spoke of wu wei from the outside: action does not colonize. This question speaks from the inside: no inner clinging to the act.

Tian men qi he, neng wei ci hu — the reception direction. "Heaven's gate" is the natural opening and closing happening in the body — eyes opening and closing, breathing in and out, thoughts arising and falling. These happen by themselves, not by the will. "Being the female" is the receiving position — not rushing to be the agent of "I open this," "I close this."

Ming bai si da, neng wu yi zhi hu — the knowing direction. Knowing in full configuration — bright and reaching in all four directions — but not using knowing to colonize. Not using "I know" as justification for overriding others.

All six questions together:

Ying po bao yi — establish the two-layer frame.
Zhuan qi zhi rou — calibrate the foundation layer (body).
Xiu chu xuan lan — calibrate the emergent layer (mind).
Ai min zhi guo neng wu yi wei — two layers operating in governance.
Tian men qi he neng wei ci — two layers operating in reception.
Ming bai si da neng wu yi zhi — two layers operating in knowing.

This is in fact a self-check list we can use directly.

In this chapter, Laozi for the first time brings the practice instruction from the previous nine chapters — each contributing one face — and unites it in a single concrete subject.

Walk through all six questions and the whole person — from foundation to emergence to three outward directions — has not been closed off by any construct. Laozi gives this state a name.

四、Xuan De

"生之，畜之，生而弗有，长而弗宰也，是谓玄德。"

"To bring forth and nurture, to bring forth without possessing, to raise without ruling — this is called xuan de."

The subject who has walked through all six questions continues operating — what needs to be brought forth is still being brought forth, what needs to be nurtured is still being nurtured. Walking through the questions does not bring things to a halt.

But bring forth without possessing — bring forth a layer, a construct, a work, a career, a relationship — without staking that thing as "mine."

Raise without ruling — what grows has its own course; you do not master its direction.

The subject who has walked through all six questions operates this way — action continues, but the subject does not cling to "I did this," "this is mine."

This is called —

Xuan de.

Xuan is the chisel-construct cycle itself (established in ch. 1). De is the individual remainder (established in ch. 1).

Xuan + de together — the individual remainder that the chisel-construct cycle runs through — one chisel after another cycling, never closed off by any single round, always emerging into a new position.

This is the highest position the individual remainder can reach in the chisel-construct cycle.

Which is to say —

Xuan de is the highest position the individual subject can reach.

It is not an abstract moral concept. It is what happens in this concrete subject after all six questions are walked through — the remainder emerges into a new position with each round, not a single point closed off by any construct.

五、The Standard Text Added a Line

There are considerable textual differences in this chapter. The most dramatic falls in the naming passage.

The silk manuscript's naming of xuan de has two lines:

生而弗有，长而弗宰。

The standard text's naming has three:

生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰。

The standard text added a line — "为而不恃" ("acting without relying on it").

Where does this line come from? The source is clear — Chapter 2.

Chapter 2, speaking of the three faces of nurturing, ends with: "万物作而弗始也，生而不有，为而不恃，功成而弗居" — four lines.

A standard-text annotator, reading Chapter 10, found only two lines in the silk-text xuan de naming and felt — within his framework — that something was missing. Why was "为而不恃" absent?

So he transplanted it from Chapter 2 into Chapter 10, making the xuan de naming feel "complete."

The intent was not malicious. The annotator wanted the two chapters to align — to show the "three faces of nurturing" appearing fully in the xuan de chapter.

But this is the most typical move made by commentators through the ages —

using the framework assembled from earlier chapters to go back and revise later original text, making it seem "complete."

The nature of this move: inserting one's own framework into the source text.

But Chapter 10 was never about the three faces of nurturing.

Laozi is discussing the six questions plus the naming of xuan de — the naming carries through the thread of practice instruction, and has no need to sum up the three faces of nurturing.

The xuan de of Chapter 10 is the quality of the individual remainder after walking through the chisel-construct cycle — a broader position than nurturing. The nurturing subject's quality is only one manifestation of xuan de in the "outward toward others" direction, not the whole of it.

Once the standard text transplanted "为而不恃," xuan de was narrowed into a synonym for nurturing. The broader position was swallowed by this addition.

Placing the silk manuscript and the standard text side by side at this point — you can directly see what a patch does. Not changing word meanings. Changing the scope of a structure.

This is a general observation drawn out by Paper 2 — the critical textual variants in the standard text are nearly all doing the same thing: sanding down Laozi's concrete instruction into abstract description, narrowing the broader position into familiar frameworks.

Not individual stylistic touching-up. A systemic direction.

Every one of these patches was not malicious — every annotator who made a change had good intentions. But all of them together drew Laozi's originally established positions one notch inward, toward frameworks that tradition already had.

六、Xuan De Is Broader Than Nurturing

Xuan de is broader than nurturing.

What is nurturing? Practice directed outward toward others — letting the nurtured one grow on their own, making room, not colonizing.

What is xuan de? The whole of walking through the six questions — foundation layer, emergent layer, governance, reception, knowing: all five faces without being closed off by any construct.

Nurturing is only one face of xuan de — its manifestation in the direction of the fourth question (love the people and govern the state without clinging to the doing).

A person with xuan de can be a nurturer — but need not be only a nurturer. Such a person can do anything — act, perceive, know — as long as none of these acts carry "I" clinging to the act.

This is the highest position the individual subject can reach.

Reading Chapter 10 and looking back at the first nine chapters —

Each of the first nine chapters was unpacking one face of xuan de.

Chapter 2 discusses nurturing — the outward-toward-others direction of xuan de.
Chapter 3 discusses governance — xuan de applied to ruling the state.
Chapter 5 discusses unloading defaults — the semantic practice of xuan de.
Chapter 7 discusses not self-producing — xuan de's refusal to construct itself.
Chapter 8 discusses water — the physical analogy for xuan de.

All the practice instruction unpacked across nine chapters — in Chapter 10 it assembles into one complete quality of subject.

The next eight chapters of Paper 2 — Chapters 11 through 18 — all unfold outward from this quality.

Chapter 11 discusses hollow having. Chapter 12 discusses acting for the belly. Chapter 13 discusses valuing the self. Chapter 14 discusses grasping the dao. Chapter 15 gives the portrait of the subject who has walked through the six questions. Chapter 16 discusses two paths. Chapter 17 discusses the four grades of the nurturing subject. Chapter 18 discusses what happens when this subject collapses.

Chapter 10 is a vertebra in the spine of this book —

bringing all the practice together into a single concrete person.

Asking six times, "Can you...?"

This person, having walked through it, is called xuan de.
