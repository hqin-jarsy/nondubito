Chapter 28 of 81

君子不器

The Noble Person Is Not an Instrument

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

本章经文本章經文Chinese text for this chapter

帛书整理文本 · 本注采用帛書整理文本 · 本注採用Silk-manuscript text · the author’s edited Chinese reading

知其雄，守其雌，为天下溪；为天下溪，恒德不离；恒德不离，复归于婴儿。

知其荣，守其辱，为天下谷；为天下谷，恒德乃足；恒德乃足，复归于朴。

知其白守其黑，为天下式；为天下式，恒德不忒；恒德不忒，复归于无极。

朴散则为器，圣人用之则为官长，夫大制无割。

一、看你的 LinkedIn 头衔

看一看你的 LinkedIn 头衔。

你是不是一个 senior engineer，一个 PM，一个 designer，一个 founder，一个 manager，一个 director？

这些都是你的"器"——你的专业度、你的可用功能、你在某个具体位置上的"用"。

老子在《道德经》第二十八章问了一个完全不同的问题——

你是这个器，还是你不只是这个器？

你有这些能力，但你从来不只是这些能力。一个职业可以很重要，却不能把生活的其他方向全部说完。

这里借《论语·为政》的“君子不器”来与老子的“朴散则为器”对读。前一句是孔子的话，不是《道德经》第二十八章的原文。本注九卷以它为名，是为了突出这个对读的问题，而不是宣称它出自老子。

这一章给两类人各画一张画像：

一是给读者的——你怎么样不让自己变成单一的器。
二是给治理者的——你怎么样用人但不把人当作器。

两张画像合起来才是这一章的完整命题。

二、三组对偶

这一章开头有三组对偶——

"知其雄，守其雌，为天下溪。
知其荣，守其辱，为天下谷。
知其白，守其黑，为天下式。"

可以坦然接受一句表扬，也可以说“我是工程师”。问题不是承认成绩，而是让它取得最终解释权：“我已经很懂了，所以不必再听。”

举个具体的例子——

一个人在公司里某天被夸了，"你做得真好，你太厉害了"。

按"立在荣的位上"运作的人——立刻把"我是被夸的人"立成一个位，下次开会就想再被夸，行为开始围绕"维持被夸的姿态"转。

按"守在辱的位上"运作的人——听到夸不立"我已经厉害"的招牌，下次做事还是按事情本身的逻辑走，不为了再被夸做调整。

不是说"被夸的时候要假装自卑"——那也是立招牌，只是反向立。

第二十四章的自检在这里仍然适用：一个身份帮助我行动，还是替我关上门？守住另一面，不是要求自卑、忍受羞辱，而是即使得到肯定，仍给未曾尝试的方向留点地方。过去做得好，不是罪；不必为了继续成长先否认自己。

三、涵育位的三个方向

三组对偶之后，每一组都有一个"复归于 W"的尾句——

复归于婴儿。
复归于朴。
复归于无极。

本注把婴儿、朴、无极读作三种开放的方向：回到基本的感受与需要，不让分类穷尽整体，不把已有边界当成最后边界。这是阅读的地图，不是古文字义自动证明的体系。

“复归于婴儿”也不承诺身体真的恢复婴儿状态，或靠一种呼吸方法消除多年紧张。这里借它提醒成人：在身份之前，还有会疲倦、需要照顾的生命。

譬如你在一个忙乱的下午发现，自己已经几小时没喝水了。你一直是能解决问题的人，于是连疲惫都不愿承认。婴儿这个形象，不是要你放弃工作，而是让“需要”重新有资格被听见。一个称职的人也可以说：我先休息一下。

朴的提醒稍有不同。午饭时同事谈起音乐，你本来想说“这不是我的领域”，却发现自己其实想听。没有谁要求你立即转行。只是专业边界之外，仍有一些事可以进来。

无极则把目光放得更远：今天还不知道怎样命名的兴趣，不必因为没有现成的位置就被关掉。你不用承诺它会成为下一项成绩。先让它存在。三个方向合起来，不是要求一个人无限发展，而是不把今天的样子当成最后判决。

四、朴散则为器

第二十八章末段——

"朴散则为器，圣人用之则为官长，夫大制无割。"

这一段把整章收到一个非常具体的命题上——

朴一散就成了器。

朴 = 浑然未分的整体。
散 = 被凿、被非、被切开。
器 = 有具体用途的工具。

刀、碗、车、椅子——都是器。每一个都有具体功能，但每一个都不再是原本未分的整体。

器的比喻让用途清晰起来，但不能据此断言技能里绝无“德”。一个医生怎样治病、一个工程师怎样对待用户，本身就可能体现对人的尊重。专业工作并不天然低于“完整的人生”。

值得警惕的是，把一项工作变成人生唯一获准的方向。做了二十年后端开发的人，既可以继续热爱它，也可以开始学音乐、照顾家人或换一个领域。改变不必证明过去的二十年错了，不改变也不等于停止成为自己。

身份一旦被制度或习惯收得太紧，尝试别的方向会更难；但那个人从未因此真的只剩一个功能。他仍能拒绝、重新解释自己的经历、寻找支持。这些可能性不是由上司授予的。

五、圣人用之则为官长

接下来一句——

"圣人用之则为官长。"

本注把“用之”读到专业分工上，但这是一种当代展开，不是“之”字已经替所有管理方式盖章。合作需要人的能力，也需要知情、协商、合理边界和退出的空间。

例如经理请工程师做后端开发，同时认真讨论他转方向或休假的想法，不拿晋升作人身控制。项目未必能满足每一个愿望，但分歧应可以谈，离开不应被当作背叛。

老师可以支持学生的数学天分，而不替他宣布一生必须做数学家。父母可以提供机会，而不要求孩子替自己完成未竟的人生。

相反，若一切谈话最后都回到“你对我还有什么用”，对方的空间就被挤压了。被忽略的不是一件已经消失的整体性，而是始终存在、应当获得承认的人。

想象一次很普通的谈话。工程师说，自己想在下个季度试试另一个方向。经理的第一反应也许是为难：眼下这个项目谁来接？这个困难是真的，不必假装不存在。

但接下来的话可以很不一样。“你走了，我怎么办，所以你不能走”，与“我们一起看看交接需要什么，哪些日期可以调整”，谈的是两种关系。后者未必立刻有好方案，却承认对面坐着的不是一个永远可用的后端接口。

如果确实无法安排，经理也可以坦白说出限制，允许对方据此选择。尊重不等于所有愿望都获准；它要求限制能够被说明、被讨论，而不是把拒绝合作说成忘恩负义。

六、夫大制无割

“夫大制无割。”可以把它读成一种组织的尺度：有分工，而不把人割尽在分工里。不是保证这样的组织永远不倒，而是不给效率一张吞并生活的通行证。

把人困在岗位里，可能让尝试、信任和合作越来越难。尊重人的整体性，则为转向与修正留下条件，但并不保证人人都跨界成功。继续原来的工作也可以是自己的选择。

所以每一次招聘、评价、晋升和分派任务，都可以多问一句：我在谈这一次合作需要什么，还是在决定对方这辈子只能是什么？

七、整章给读者的

对自己，保留身份之外的余地。可以热爱一门手艺，承认自己的长处，也容许今天没有新的成绩。完整性不是一张只有持续进步才有效的执照。

对他人，谈能力与合作时别忘了，他可以有你看不见的打算。尊重不是奖给多才多艺者的奖章。即使一个人只会做一件事，也不能把他等同于那件事。

借“君子不器”对读这一章，我想留下的就是这点：我们能为一件事发挥作用，却不因此把整个人生交给那个用途。

I. Look at Your LinkedIn Title

Look at your LinkedIn title.

Are you a senior engineer, a PM, a designer, a founder, a manager, a director?

These are all your "instruments" — your professional identity, your deployable function, your "use" in a specific position.

In Chapter 28 of the Daodejing, Laozi asks a completely different question —

Are you this instrument, or are you more than this instrument?

You have these abilities, but you have never been only these abilities. A profession can matter deeply without exhausting the directions of a life.

Here I borrow “the junzi is not a vessel” from Analects 2.12 to read alongside Laozi’s “the uncarved becomes vessels.” The former is Confucius’s saying, not a line in chapter 28. It names this nine-part commentary because it captures a question I bring to the text, not because Laozi said it.

This chapter draws two portraits:

One for the reader — how not to let yourself become a single instrument. One for those who govern — how to use people without treating them as instruments.

Only together do these two portraits complete the chapter's full proposition.

II. Three Pairs of Opposites

This chapter opens with three pairs of opposites —

"Know the masculine, keep to the feminine, be the valley of the world.
Know the glorious, keep to the disgraced, be the valley of the world.
Know the white, keep to the black, be the pattern of the world."

You may accept praise and say “I am an engineer.” The problem is not acknowledging achievement but giving it final authority: “I already understand, so I need not listen.”

A concrete example —

Someone is praised at work: "You did great, you're amazing."

The person who operates from the position of glory — immediately takes "I am the praised one" as a fixed position; next meeting wants to be praised again; behavior begins revolving around maintaining the "praised" posture.

The person who keeps to the disgraced position — hearing the praise, does not plant a sign reading "I am already great"; next time, still acts according to the logic of the work itself, without adjusting to be praised again.

Chapter 24’s question still applies: does an identity help me act, or shut a door for me? Keeping the other side available does not require self-abasement or enduring humiliation. It leaves room for an untried direction even after praise. Past success is no fault; growth need not begin with denying yourself.

III. Three Directions of the Nurturing Position

After the three pairs, each has a closing phrase "return to W" —

Return to the infant.
Return to the unhewn.
Return to the inexhaustible.

I read infant, uncarved wood and the unbounded as three directions of openness: returning to basic needs and sensation, not exhausting a whole through classifications, and not treating an existing boundary as final. This is a reading map, not a system proved by word origins.

Returning to the infant promises neither bodily infancy nor the removal of years of tension through a breathing technique. It reminds the adult that before a job title comes a life that tires and needs care.

Imagine a hectic afternoon when you realize you have not had water for hours. Being the person who solves problems has made even tiredness hard to admit. The infant image need not tell you to quit working. It gives need a hearing again. Competence can say, “I need a break.”

The uncarved offers a slightly different reminder. At lunch, a colleague mentions music. You almost say, “That is not my field,” then discover you want to listen. Nobody requires an immediate career change. Something outside the professional boundary is simply allowed in.

The unbounded looks further: an interest you cannot yet name need not be shut out because it has no recognized place. You need not promise it will become an achievement. Let it exist first. Together, these directions do not demand unlimited development. They refuse to make today’s shape the final verdict.

IV. When the Unhewn Scatters, It Becomes Instruments

The closing passage of Chapter 28 —

"When the unhewn scatters, it becomes instruments. The sage uses them and becomes lord of the officials. Thus the great ordering does not cut."

This passage brings the whole chapter to a very concrete proposition —

Once the unhewn scatters, it becomes instruments.

The unhewn = the undivided whole. Scatters = is chiseled, negated, cut apart. Instruments = tools with specific functions.

Knives, bowls, carts, chairs — all are instruments. Each has a specific function, but none is the original undivided whole. Instruments have "use" but not "virtue" (de).

The tool image clarifies use, but does not prove that professional skill contains no de. How a doctor treats a patient or an engineer treats users can itself express respect. Professional work is not inherently inferior to a whole life.

The danger is making one job the only permitted direction. After twenty years in backend development, someone may still love it, or start learning music, caring for family or entering another field. Change need not condemn those twenty years; staying need not mean ceasing to become oneself.

Institutions and habits can make other directions harder to try. They never literally turn the person into nothing but a function. Refusal, reinterpretation and finding support remain human possibilities, not gifts bestowed by a manager.

V. The Sage Uses Them and Becomes Lord of Officials

The next line —

"The sage uses them and becomes lord of the officials."

I extend “using them” toward professional cooperation; the pronoun itself does not certify any management practice. Cooperation needs abilities, but also informed agreement, negotiation, reasonable limits and room to leave.

A manager can ask for backend work while taking seriously an engineer’s wish to change direction or take leave, rather than using promotion as personal control. Not every wish can be met, but disagreement must be discussable and departure need not be betrayal.

A teacher may support mathematical talent without announcing a lifelong destiny. Parents may offer opportunities without asking a child to complete their unfinished lives.

When every conversation ends at “What use are you to me?”, the other’s space narrows. What is ignored is not a wholeness that has vanished, but a person who has remained there all along.

Imagine an ordinary conversation. An engineer wants to try another direction next quarter. The manager’s first response may be concern: who will take over the current project? That difficulty is real; nobody needs to pretend otherwise.

But the next words can differ profoundly. “What would I do if you left? You cannot leave” is not “Let us see what a handover needs and which dates can move.” The second may not produce an immediate solution. It does recognize that the person opposite is not a backend interface available forever.

If the arrangement cannot be made, the manager can explain the limits and let the other person choose accordingly. Respect does not mean granting every wish. It means that limits can be explained and discussed, rather than calling the refusal of cooperation ingratitude.

VI. The Great Ordering Does Not Cut

“Great ordering does not cut.” Read this as a measure for organization: divide the work without exhausting the person in the division. It does not promise an immortal institution; it denies efficiency a license to swallow life.

Trapping people in roles can make trust and experimentation harder. Respecting their wholeness leaves conditions for revision, not a guarantee that everyone will succeed in a new field. Continuing the same work may also be their choice.

At each hiring, appraisal, promotion and assignment: am I discussing what this cooperation needs, or deciding what this person may be for the rest of their life?

VII. What This Chapter Gives the Reader

For yourself, keep room beyond a role. Love a craft, acknowledge strengths, and allow a day without new achievement. Wholeness is not a license valid only while you keep improving.

For others, remember that cooperation involves someone with plans you may not see. Respect is not a prize for versatility. Even a person skilled in just one thing cannot be equated with that thing.

This is what I take from reading the Analects saying beside this chapter: we may serve a purpose without handing our whole lives to that use.
