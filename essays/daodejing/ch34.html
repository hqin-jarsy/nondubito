第三十四章，共八十一章
Chapter 34 of 81

不立自己为大

Don't Declare Yourself Great

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

道泛呵，其可左右也，成功遂事而弗名有也。万物归焉而弗为主，则恒无欲也，可名于小，万物归焉而弗为主，可名于大。是以圣人之能成大也，以其不为大也，故能成大。

一、每件事都要经过他

有一种会议，很容易知道谁最重要。别人发言时，目光先转向同一个人；一项成果做出来，名字最后总落到他身上；连本来可以自己决定的小事，也要等他点头。

久而久之，大家学会的不是怎样判断，而是怎样猜他的意思。他未必觉得自己在控制。他可能觉得，自己只是特别负责，别人暂时还离不开他。

也有另一种负责人。该拍板的时候拍板，出了事不躲，但不把每个决定都收回自己手里。有人提出一个好办法，不必先把它包装成他的主意。团队有机会长出不依附于他个人的能力。

第二种做法不能保证团队永远成功。预算、能力、环境都还在。但它把一个问题摆清楚了：你是在帮助事情做成，还是在让每件做成的事证明你的重要？

第三十四章的“不为大”，可以从这里进入。

二、道可以在左，也可以在右

道泛呵，其可左右也。

这一句带着水的开阔。道并不被一处固定的位置收住，可以在左，也可以在右。先不急着把它翻成组织管理，停在那个不必占据唯一中心的画面里。

我们常把重要与显眼连起来。会场中间的位子，发言最多的声音，最终文件上的署名。确实，有些职责需要被看见；公开负责也比隐身操纵好。问题不在于站到台前，而在于是不是只能靠台前的自己来衡量一切。

一位好的编辑，可能是在作者卡住时问对一个问题，而不是把文章改成自己的声音。一个会带新人的同事，可能是陪他做完第一次，然后逐渐让开。工作的痕迹在事情里，不一定总在聚光灯下。

“其可左右”让我想到这种移动的余地。需要你时，能到场；不需要你作主时，也不把退开理解成自己不存在了。

三、做成，不等于占有

成功遂事而弗名有也。

事情完成了，却不把它据为己有。第二章的“为而弗志也，成功而弗居也”，第十章的“生而弗有，长而弗宰也”，都可以与此对读。重点是做与占有之间的距离。

这不是要求劳动者抹掉自己的贡献。该署名就署名，该付报酬就付报酬。一个领导说“大家不要计较功劳”，最后却让成果全部归自己，恰好背离了这句话。

区别在于：承认我做过什么，还是因此要求别人永远属于我。你帮助一个人入行，不等于拥有他之后的全部选择；你参与一个项目，不等于所有后续决定都必须由你批准。

把功劳说清楚，有时正能防止占有。真正参与的人不必消失，也没有一个人能吞掉其他人的劳动。做成了，认领自己的责任和贡献，然后让事情继续属于共同做事的人。

四、同一句话，两个方向

经文先说：

万物归焉而弗为主，则恒无欲也，可名于小。

随后又说：

万物归焉而弗为主，可名于大。

“万物归焉而弗为主”一共出现两次。同一个姿态，既可名小，也可名大。这里的重复值得慢读，而不必替老子编出为什么不选择另一种句式的心理活动。

从不居主、不把自己推到前面的方向看，它不显眼，可以叫小；从容纳万物的方向看，它又可以叫大。小与大不是一个人轮流贴上的谦虚和伟大标签，而是两种观看角度。

一个社区里，总有人维护着不显眼的联系：谁最近需要帮忙，哪场误会可以说开，哪件工具可以借用。他不必成为所有人的主人，关系仍在经过他的工作而延续。

当然，这不意味着维系关系的劳动就应该永远隐形。看见它、支持它，与不让劳动者成为主宰，是可以同时做到的。

五、不为大，为什么有助于成大

是以圣人之能成大也，以其不为大也，故能成大。

这一句的反转很动人：不急着证明自己伟大，反而让更大的事情有机会发生。把它带到生活里，可以看见具体的机制，而不必称为没有例外的成功定律。

负责人不抢走每个主意，其他人才愿意把尚未成熟的想法拿出来；决定不总在最后一刻被改回他的喜好，团队才有理由认真判断。空出来的不是权力真空，而是别人发挥能力的空间。

但负责人仍要说明方向、作出必要的决定、承担后果。“我不当主宰”不能成为回避困难的漂亮话。把所有事情交给团队，失败后再说“是你们决定的”，不是这里的退让。

它也不保证大家一定聚来。一个尊重人的组织仍会犯错，一个好产品仍可能无人发现。这里给的是减少占有、让协作更有可能的方向，不是不用经营就能收获成功的秘诀。

前几章对自夸、争夺和立强位的警惕，可以放在这里继续读。但承认成绩不会必然导致停滞，谦逊也不会自动得到奖赏。否则“不为大”又会变成一门更精巧的自我做大术。

六、不要只凭外观判断大小

第四十五章说“大成若缺”“大盈若冲”“大直如诎”“大巧若拙”。这些反差使惯常的眼光慢下来：完整未必处处封死，熟练未必总显得炫技。

这不是“真正优秀的人必定低调”的识人公式。有人善于公开表达，也确实做了好事；有人沉默寡言，也可能严厉控制身边的人。上不上封面、说话多不多，都不足以判定一个人究竟在怎样对待他人。

与其从外观猜本质，不如看具体关系：别人有没有发言的位置？贡献能否得到承认？可以提出反对吗？可以离开吗？关键时刻，谁承担责任？

一个人可以站在台前而不把台下的人都变成自己的背景；也可以坐在角落里，却要求所有人围绕自己的情绪转。大小的区别，不能只靠显眼与否来看。

七、把空间留给事情和别人

如果你带团队，可以试一次：下一次总结成果时，把每个人真正做过的事说清楚，也包括你自己。不要抢走别人的功劳，也不用假装自己毫无作用。

如果你做产品，介绍它解决了什么问题，比反复证明设计者有多天才更能帮助用户。介绍本身没有错，好东西也需要被发现；问题是宣传有没有取代对实际体验的关心。

如果你写作，可以认真谈自己的判断，接受反驳，而不要求读者先承认你是一个有洞见的人。文章的价值不需要读者把忠诚也一并交出来。

在亲密关系里，不拿付出来索取控制，也不等于永远不说自己的需要。你可以要求公平、表达委屈、划出边界。对方仍然是另一个人，不必用永远依附来偿还你的好。

回到那间会议室。负责人听到一个不是自己提出的办法，认真想了想，说：“这样更好，就按这个来。”

没有人因此少了一点分量。只是事情终于不必每次都绕远路，先经过他的伟大。

I. Everything Has to Pass Through Him

In some meetings it is easy to identify the important person. Before speaking, people look toward the same chair. A completed project eventually acquires the same name. Even small decisions wait for the same nod.

Over time, people learn less about making judgments than about guessing what he wants. He may not think of himself as controlling. He may simply believe that he is unusually responsible and that everyone still needs him.

Another leader makes necessary decisions and does not disappear when things go wrong, but does not gather every choice back into her own hands. A good idea need not first be repackaged as her idea. The team has a chance to develop capacities that do not depend on her personally.

This cannot guarantee permanent success. Budgets, competence, and circumstances still matter. But it clarifies a question: are you helping the work happen, or making every achievement prove your importance?

That is one way into Chapter 34's refusal to make oneself great.

II. The Dao Can Be on Either Side

The Dao spreads everywhere; it can be to the left or to the right.

There is something expansive and watery about the opening. The Dao is not confined to one fixed position. Before converting that into management advice, remain with the image of not having to occupy the sole center.

We often associate importance with visibility: the central chair, the most speaking time, the name on the final document. Some responsibilities genuinely need to be visible; public accountability is preferable to hidden manipulation. Being on stage is not the problem. Measuring everything by one's presence on stage is.

A good editor may ask the question that helps a writer move again, rather than make the work sound like the editor. A colleague training someone may stay for the first attempt, then gradually step aside. The work leaves traces in what becomes possible, not always in the spotlight.

“Either side” suggests that freedom to move. Be present when needed; when you need not decide, do not mistake stepping back for ceasing to exist.

III. Accomplishing Is Not Owning

It completes the work without claiming possession.

The adopted Chapter 2 speaks of acting without fixing the achievement to oneself and completing without occupying it; Chapter 10 speaks of nurturing without possessing or ruling. They can be read beside this line. The distance between doing and owning matters.

That is not an instruction to erase workers' contributions. Credit should be given and labor paid. A manager who says “Let's not worry about credit” while absorbing all of it violates the point.

The distinction is between acknowledging what I did and requiring other people to belong to me because I did it. Helping someone enter a profession does not purchase their future choices. Contributing to a project does not confer permanent approval rights over everything that follows.

Clear attribution can prevent appropriation. Contributors need not disappear; no individual gets to swallow everyone else's work. Own your responsibility and contribution, then let the work continue among the people making it together.

IV. One Sentence, Two Directions

The adopted text says that all things return to it while it does not become their master, and calls it small. It repeats the same phrase and calls it great.

“All things return to it, yet it does not become their master” occurs twice in all. The repetition deserves attention. We need not invent Laozi's private reasons for rejecting some alternative turn of phrase.

From the side of not occupying the commanding position, it is unobtrusive: small. From the side of accommodating all things, it is great. These are not labels of modesty and greatness a person alternately pins on themselves. They are two angles on the same posture.

Someone in a neighborhood maintains quiet connections: who needs help, which misunderstanding might be talked through, what tool can be borrowed. This person need not own the neighbors for those relationships to continue through their work.

Such labor also need not remain invisible forever. Recognizing and supporting it is compatible with refusing to make its practitioner everyone's master.

V. Why Not Making Yourself Great Can Help

The sage can accomplish something great because he does not set himself up as great.

The reversal is striking: less urgency to demonstrate one's greatness can leave room for something larger. In ordinary life we can identify mechanisms without declaring an exceptionless law of success.

When leaders do not seize every idea, people have more reason to share unfinished thoughts. When decisions are not constantly reversed to match one person's preferences, others have reason to exercise judgment. What opens up is not simply a vacuum. It is space for another person's ability.

The leader still owes direction, necessary decisions, and accountability. “I am not the master” must not become a beautiful excuse for evading difficulties. Delegating everything and then blaming the team is not restraint.

Nor will everyone necessarily gather around. A respectful organization can misjudge things. A good product can go unnoticed. This is a direction that reduces appropriation and makes collaboration more possible, not a shortcut that delivers success without work.

Earlier warnings about boasting and domination remain relevant. But acknowledging an achievement does not inevitably cause stagnation, and humility receives no automatic reward. Otherwise, refusing greatness becomes merely a subtler technique for inflating oneself.

VI. Do Not Judge Size Only by Appearance

Chapter 45 sets completion beside apparent incompleteness, fullness beside emptiness, straightness beside bending, and skill beside awkwardness. These contrasts slow our habitual judgments. Completeness need not seal every opening; expertise need not display a flourish at every turn.

This is not a formula saying that excellent people must keep a low profile. Some speak publicly and do valuable work. Others remain quiet while tightly controlling everyone around them. Magazine covers and speaking time do not tell us enough about how a person treats others.

Look instead at actual relationships. Can others speak? Are contributions acknowledged? Can someone disagree or leave? When things become difficult, who accepts responsibility?

A person can stand on stage without making the audience into a backdrop. Another can sit in a corner while requiring everyone to orbit their moods. Visibility alone does not settle the question.

VII. Leave Room for the Work and for Others

If you lead a team, try naming what each person actually did at the next review, including yourself. Do not take someone else's credit; do not pretend you contributed nothing.

If you make a product, explaining the problem it solves helps more than repeatedly proving the designer's genius. Promotion is not wrong: good work needs to be discovered. The question is whether promotion has displaced concern for the actual experience.

If you write, offer your judgment seriously and allow disagreement. A reader need not first certify your insight. An article's value does not require a pledge of loyalty from its audience.

In a close relationship, refusing to turn care into control does not require suppressing your needs. You may ask for fairness, express hurt, and set boundaries. The other person remains another person. Permanent dependence is not the price of your kindness.

Return to the meeting. The leader hears a proposal she did not originate, considers it, and says: “That's better. Let's do it that way.”

Nobody has become less substantial. The work simply no longer needs to take a detour through the leader's greatness every time.
