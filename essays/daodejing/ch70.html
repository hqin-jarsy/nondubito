为什么最简单的道理，最难做到

Between Saying It and Beginning to Do It

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

本章经文本章經文Chinese text for this chapter

吾言甚易知也，甚易行也，而人莫之能知也，而莫之能行也。言有君，事有宗，其唯无知也，是以不我知。知者希，则我贵矣，是以圣人被褐而怀玉。

一、收藏夹里，已经有很多答案

少说一点，多听一点。别把自己耗尽。不要总忙着证明自己。你可能读过这些话，也真心赞同，还把写得特别好的几篇收藏起来，想着以后再看。

可到了下一次争执，你仍然急着插话；到了需要休息的时候，又给自己加了一件事。不是完全忘了道理，有时你甚至一边记得，一边眼看着自己照旧做下去。

第七十章忽然像一个说了很多话的人停下来，轻轻叹气：“吾言甚易知也，甚易行也，而人莫之能知也，而莫之能行也。”话似乎很容易懂，也不是什么惊天动地的壮举，可真正理解、实行起来，却是另一回事。

我们不必把这句读成古人准确统计过世上没有一个人懂他。它把一种熟悉的落差说到极处：听见与活出来之间，还有一段不会自动消失的距离。

二、会复述，还不等于能在当时想起

“易知”“易行”可以说这些话并不依靠繁复玄奥的技巧，而“莫知”“莫行”指出人们实际仍受习惯、欲望和处境牵动。不能简单规定，前面的“知”字面上只是假装懂，后面的“知”才是另一种固定等级。

不过，借这组反差辨认自己的生活，确实很有用。你能解释“专注为什么重要”，与做事时不一次次伸手去摸手机，并不是同一件事。那只手常常比解释走得快：心里刚有一点空白，屏幕已经亮了。

一次照着做，也不等于从此稳稳拥有了它。今天能够认真听，明天遇到让自己不舒服的话，还是可能急着反驳。明白不是一张考过就永久有效的证书，更像一种需要在具体情境里继续练习的能力。

这样看，就不必在“我已经懂了”与“我毫无用处”之间跳来跳去。懂得一部分是真实的，还没做到也是真实的。它们指出接下来可以练什么，而不是要求你给整个人判一个总分。

老子这句叹息的价值，是把读者从继续搜集答案的兴奋里拉回一下：也许现在缺的，不是下一条更漂亮的话。

三、太平常的，最容易被越过去

简单的道理容易被略过，首先因为它不新鲜。“好好听人说话”，好像谁都知道，于是我们更愿意把时间用在更复杂、更显得有见识的东西上。可知道一句话常见，并不表示自己已经把它用好。

另一层难处，是生活并不总在奖励它。会上第一个发言的人容易被看见，慢一点想的人可能失去机会；团队口头上鼓励诚实，却总表扬那些从不报坏消息的人。人在这样的环境里，听懂了道理，也未必立刻有条件照做。

还有我们自己的习惯。想被认可，害怕丢脸，受到质疑时想赶快保护自己，这些都很常见，却不必被宣布为永远不变的人类本性。习惯可以调整，环境也可以改变，只是通常不是靠一次决心就全部完成。

所以“简单”与“容易”并不是同义词。一句话可以很少，进入生活却会碰到时间、压力、旧经验与别人的反应。听懂它像看见了对岸，真正走过去，还得找到可以落脚的地方。

这也说明实践不只是与自己较劲。若总在困倦时失去耐心，改善休息可能比再训自己一遍更有用；若工作要求你随时在线，谈清楚响应边界可能比责怪自己不够专注更实际。道理需要条件，并不因此变得不高尚。

四、别只攒招，看看它们从哪里来

言有君，事有宗。

本站采用的文字是这个次序，通行本常作“言有宗，事有君”。君与宗借统摄和根本的意象，说话与做事并非毫无所本的一堆零件。

回看这一组，少一点自我炫示，不急着争先，给别人留下判断的位置，对胜利仍有分寸——它们不是各不相干的技巧，都在试着让一个人的行动不以占满别人为代价。

若只攒招，学来的话可能彼此打架：这本书教你主动，那本教你退让；昨天说坚持，今天说放下。到现场，你只好猜哪句格言比较适用，越背越多，反而越难动。

抓住底下的理由，事情会清楚一点。你问的可以不是“这时该用哪一招”，而是“现在什么需要被保护，什么只是我放不下的姿态”。答案仍需判断，但至少不再只是在口号之间抽签。

一个熟练的人并非从此不用规则。他只是逐渐理解规则为什么在那里，知道哪些不能轻忽，哪些需要因情形调整。共同的根不是取消专业知识的万能钥匙，而是使知识不至于散成一堆不能相互照亮的碎片。

五、别人可以教你，却不能替你明白

其唯无知也，是以不我知。

这句话可以顺着前文，读为人们未能知道言与事所本，因而不理解说话者。它并不是字面直接宣布“理解不能从一人传到另一人”，也不必把不理解的人骂成愚蠢。

本系列愿意由此谈一个与学习有关的经验：别人可以告诉你，甚至讲得很清楚，但那份明白还需要你自己参与。传递信息很重要，示范、解释与训练也真的有用；它们不是无效，只是不能完全代替听者自己的理解。

你也许遇过，一句话从前听了许多次，后来在某个实际处境里突然变得具体。可能是走过弯路，也可能只是认真看了一个例子、与人谈了一会儿。真正明白不必靠受苦来换，更不需要老师故意让学生碰壁。

好的老师会帮你看见，解释为什么，给你机会把自己的疑问说出来。门需要你推，并不意味着他只能站在一旁，不准替你找钥匙、扶一下门、指出哪条路不安全。

读这套文章也是这样。它们可以提供一些入口，但不要求你把每一个解释照单全收。你可以带着自己的经验检验它，也可以不同意。若一篇谈不压人的文章，最后要求读者只准按作者的方式明白，未免自己先关上了门。

六、粗布里面的玉，不靠稀少证明珍贵

知者希，则我贵矣，是以圣人被褐而怀玉。

本站采用这一章句，通行本常见“知我者希，则我者贵”。其中有对理解者稀少、真正承接者可贵的感叹，也有不同断句与训释。本文不把它定成一句“因为没人懂我，所以我一定比别人高贵”，也不将它强说成只有一种语法的劝学口号。

不被理解，不能反过来证明自己正确。一个想法既可能因为深而难懂，也可能因为没有说清楚，甚至因为有错误。珍惜自己的判断，与愿意听取批评，不是两条相反的路。

真正动人的，还是后面的画面：穿着粗布衣服，怀里藏着玉。外面的普通，不足以说明里面没有分量。我们若只按包装辨人，就可能一再错过那些没有把自己做成亮眼形象的人。

但也别把这幅图倒过来，变成“越不起眼越有智慧，越善表达越空”。普通衣服不是道德证书，表达自己的能力也未必是虚荣。老子给我们的，更像是一次暂停外观判断的邀请，而不是换了一套外观判断。

对自己而言，怀玉可以意味着先认真养成那份实际的能力与品格，不必每时每刻检查别人有没有看见。与此同时，你也可以把工作讲清楚，争取合理的承认，向人求助。价值不全靠掌声，并不表示人应该从此不需要任何回应。

这与第六十七章不把自己锁死在形象里的读法相通。你可以有分量，而不必把全部生活花在证明有分量上；也可以还在学习，而不必假装怀里已经是一块毫无瑕疵的玉。

七、把一句话，放进一次真实的行动

不妨只选一句你早已赞同的话。不是最宏大的那句，而是今天可以碰见它的那句。比如“听完再回应”。下一次谈话，对方还在组织语言时，看看自己能否暂时不替他说完。

如果没做到，也先别急着再骂自己。究竟是怕被误会，还是时间太紧，或是那句话触到了旧伤？把困难说具体，才可能调整。笼统地宣布“我就是修养差”，反而什么也没说明。

也可以给行动留一点条件。想专注，把打扰关掉一会儿；想诚实，练习在较安全的关系里说出一个真实的不同意见；想不过度承诺，先把手头实际已有的工作数清楚。

这些都不像一次顿悟。它们甚至可能没有什么值得写进日记的光彩。但一句听过很久的话，正是在这样的小动作里，渐渐不再只是别人的句子。

能做到一次，是一次；发现还做不到，也可以成为更具体的认识。不要把修养也变成每天必须展示成功的表演。

八、走向下一句“我还不知道”

第七十章像是在一连串劝告以后停下来。前面谈了怎么用力、怎么带人、怎么面对竞争，这里问的是：这些话有没有真的进入生活，还是只是又一组我们愿意点头的话？

从第十章、第五十一章生而不有的玄德，到三宝与不争之德，最后落到一件粗布衣服和里面的一块玉。外面不必喧闹，里面也不能因此免于真实的功夫。

下一章会谈“知不知”：知道自己还有不知道的地方。它可以成为本章很自然的下一步。真正开始实践，常让人第一次发现，原来自己还没有以为的那么明白。

这不是读书失败了。也许恰好是那句话终于从收藏夹里走出来，遇见了真实的你。

I. The Familiar Sentence and the Waiting Phone

We know that listening should precede answering. We know attention needs rest, that not every opportunity deserves a yes, and that anger should not decide for us. We could explain each idea to a friend. Then, while the friend finishes speaking, we look at the phone.

It is an ordinary scene: recognizing a sentence does not mean having it available when the moment for action arrives.

In Chapter 70 of the Daodejing, the voice becomes almost intimate: “吾言甚易知也，甚易行也，而人莫之能知也，而莫之能行也.” My words are easy to know and practice, yet people do not come to know or practice them. We can hear a teacher's frustration without turning a literary tone into a biography the text does not provide.

After so many chapters about action, a question appears about reading itself. What do we do with something we can already repeat? Perhaps the next task is not finding a more original recommendation. Perhaps it is giving one familiar recommendation an actual place in the day.

II. Understanding the Sentence and Recognizing the Occasion

What seems a contradiction between “easy” and “nobody can” reflects a familiar experience. We can understand a sentence without yet recognizing all the situations it concerns. We can also apply advice once without establishing the conditions for remembering it when tired or under pressure.

That does not mean 易知 and 易行 literally designate only hearing and imitating, as opposed to a second, secret definition of understanding. It is an application of the passage's tension: something accessible in its formulation may remain demanding in life.

The phone makes this visible. “I want to concentrate” is clear. But the device stays within reach, a notification interrupts, and part of the job consists precisely in answering. The difficulty does not prove an inability to understand. It may show an isolated intention trying to work against a setting that rewards another behavior.

Understanding more includes recognizing that setting. Putting the phone away during a conversation, agreeing when messages need answering, or closing an application may help attention more than repeating its importance with greater conviction.

The distance between knowing and doing is not one moral defect. It may contain inadequate practice, fear, fatigue, adverse circumstances, or understanding still incomplete. Distinguishing them lets us work on something definite instead of merely reproaching ourselves for failing again.

III. The Simple Does Not Always Find Room

An ordinary recommendation can pass unnoticed because it seems to offer nothing new. We read “listen” and think we already know it. The sentence ceases to be an invitation and becomes a checked box: matter resolved, next idea.

Novelty promises a transformation that does not yet require changing a familiar habit. We can spend considerable time searching for a concentration method and very little preparing an hour in which concentration is possible. Searching preserves the feeling of progress without yet meeting the friction of change.

Incentives matter too. In a setting that rewards the first response, waiting to listen may cost visibility. In an organization celebrating permanent availability, rest may bring fear. We need not explain everything through an allegedly universal selfish nature always seeking victory.

We want recognition, belonging, and security in different ways, and learn habits for obtaining them. Some habits continue operating after they cease to help. A simple idea may therefore require practice, support, and changes in the environment, not heroic combat with an essentially defective self.

Advice becomes more useful as a situated question: what prevents me from listening here? Sometimes pride; sometimes fear that I will have no chance to speak afterward. The same visible behavior can need different responses. Recognizing the difference is already part of learning to act.

IV. Find the Reason Connecting the Practices

“言有君，事有宗.” Words have a governing principle; actions have a source. 君 and 宗 draw on images of authority and origin. Here I read them as an invitation to find a shared orientation beneath recommendations that otherwise look like a collection of rules.

Listening, not imposing our answer, caring for resources, declining to pursue every rival: a thread connects these practices. Each asks whether we are attending to the life and work before us or using the situation to sustain an image of ourselves.

If we only collect rules, we soon have too many. One asks us to speak clearly; another, to remain silent. One invites perseverance; another, stopping. Without understanding what they are trying to protect, mechanical application produces incompatible answers.

A shared principle does not magically solve every case. It helps formulate the relevant question: what needs protection, what information is missing, which capacity in another person am I obstructing? From there, speaking and remaining silent can both be acts of the same attention.

A good practitioner does not always recite an internal manual. They have learned to recognize differences previously invisible. Even that experience needs revision. Finding a root does not make us infallible or render study unnecessary. It offers a way of arranging what we have learned so that it can encounter new situations.

V. Teaching Can Be Shared Without Living for the Learner

“其唯无知也，是以不我知.” A common reading connects not knowing the underlying source with failing to understand the teaching or teacher. It would be inaccurate to translate this as a law that knowledge cannot be transmitted.

The application developed in this series preserves another observation: receiving an explanation and making it available within one's judgment are not quite identical. We can teach facts, techniques, and reasons. Learners participate by interpreting, questioning, and using them. Their participation does not make teaching useless. It is part of what teaching seeks.

A sentence heard many times can acquire another meaning after an experience. Sometimes that experience hurts. Sometimes observing, discussing, practicing with support, or understanding a good example is enough. Suffering is not the compulsory toll paid for authentic knowledge.

A teacher accompanying someone to a door does not merely point from a distance. They can explain how it opens, help someone having difficulty, and describe what is beyond it. What they cannot do is live on the learner's behalf. The image works if it joins assistance with participation, not if abandonment becomes a sign of pedagogical depth.

Chapter 65 approached this distinction through leading and teaching. Reading this book can also be shared work. Another person's words give us something real, and our experience asks questions that those words have not settled in advance.

VI. The Ordinary Cloth and the Jade

“知者希，则我贵矣.” The adopted wording connects the rarity of those who know with the value of the speaker or the way. The received version has “知我者希，则我者贵,” a different construction with different interpretations. We need not fix 则 exclusively as “follow” to hear a concern about uncommon understanding. Wang Bi connects rarity, depth, and value.

Being understood by few does not prove something true. Rejection does not certify genius. A teaching may be valuable and need a better explanation; it may contain errors criticism can reveal. Scarcity of recognition settles none of these questions by itself.

The chapter ends in an image: “圣人被褐而怀玉.” The sage wears coarse cloth and carries jade within. Outward simplicity, something precious held inside. Chapter 67 already asked us not to confuse a life with a fixed image of greatness.

The first application concerns other people. Not judging only by the wrapping means confusing neither titles with judgment nor modest clothing with wisdom. We need to see how someone works, listens, and answers for their actions. A brilliant appearance can accompany real value; a discreet one may hide no treasure.

The second concerns ourselves. Not everything valuable we do needs an immediate demonstration of its value. Yet asking for fair recognition, explaining work, or seeking help does not betray simplicity. The jade does not require silence while others appropriate what we have done. It separates a life's worth from continual display, not from every act of communication.

VII. Choose an Occasion, Not Another Collection of Maxims

Take one idea already familiar and identify a definite occasion. If it is listening, let someone finish before preparing the reply. If moderation, do not fill an opening for rest with another task. If keeping promises, check a condition before saying yes.

The action should be small and definite enough to observe. “Be better” tells us little about what to do. “During this afternoon's conversation, I will not look at the phone” allows preparation and a later account of what happened.

If we fail, the review can be curious rather than punitive. What distracted us? Which need appeared? Would another agreement or a change of setting have helped? The outcome need not become another identity of failure.

It also matters to recognize what we already do. Practice does not always begin at zero or require discounting what we have learned from others. A capacity may be developing even when it is not yet available on every occasion.

The purpose is to narrow the distance between an admired sentence and the life we can actually lead, not inaugurate another system of surveillance over ourselves. One real action may teach more than ten solemn commitments that never found a moment in which to begin.

VIII. Understanding Remains Open

After so much advice about strength and relationships, Chapter 70 returns us to how those words are received. We can hear the loneliness of a voice finding too little response, but also an invitation to listen differently: not merely to agree, but to make an honest trial in life.

The image of jade connects with the virtue that need not possess its results in Chapter 10, the treasures of 67, and the non-contention of 68. Depth need not become an identity proud of being misunderstood. It can continue learning how to speak and receive objections.

Chapter 71 will carry the question toward knowing that we do not know. It is an apt continuation. An actual practice usually reveals both what we understood and what remains incomplete.

Return to the phone. The next conversation does not require a new theory of attention. Perhaps it needs the device placed beyond the reach of a hand, and one sentence heard to its end. The simple will not have ceased to be simple. For once, it will also have found somewhere to happen.
