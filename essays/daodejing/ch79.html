Chapter 79 of 81

事情解决以后，心里的那一点怎么办

An Agreement Does Not Always Close the Wound

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

和大怨，必有余怨，焉可以为善？是以圣人右介而不以责于人。故有德司介，无德司彻。夫天道无亲，恒与善人。

一、散会以后，两个人还是不说话

争执终于处理完了。责任划清了，损失有人补，双方也接受了结果。主持调解的人松了一口气：总算解决了。

过了几天，却发现那两个人碰面仍然绕着走。该配合的工作可以做，多一句话却不愿意说。一个觉得自己虽然得到了补偿，却被当成了斤斤计较的人；另一个觉得自己已经退让，为什么对方还不肯翻篇。

《道德经》第七十九章开头说：“和大怨，必有余怨，焉可以为善？”它问的不是手续有没有办完，而是“和”过以后还剩下什么。一个正式结果能够终止争执，不一定能同时改变人在争执里受过的羞辱、恐惧和不信任。

这并不意味着调解没用，公正也没用。恰恰因为它们有用，我们才需要知道它们能做到哪里。不把结案当成圆满，才不会在处理过一次伤害以后，又用“你怎么还放不下”添上一层新的伤害。

二、为什么还有“余怨”

“必有余怨”四个字，说得很重。它提醒我们，重大冲突留下的东西，常常多于最初拿来解决冲突的那张清单。但也不必把它读成人永远不能和解的判决。有些关系会慢慢恢复，有些不会；有人后来能够原谅，也有人选择保持距离。

赔回一笔钱，可以补上经济损失，却未必补得上曾经被公开羞辱的那一刻。承认一句话说错了，可以停止争论，却不能要求听过那句话的人立刻忘记。事实、待遇、尊严、关系，并不总在同一个速度上修复。

还有一种余怨，来自解决方式本身。为了尽快结束，主持者让受损更多的一方“顾全大局”，让嗓门小的一方“再让一步”。表面上没有人继续闹，实际却把“不许再说”当成了“没有问题”。这种安静，很可能是在为下一次冲突积攒材料。

所以，老子的追问可以把我们的眼光从结尾往前挪：事情为什么会发展到非要大规模平息不可？哪些早期的不舒服曾经被当成小题大做？预防不是保证天下无争，而是别等所有人都伤透了，才承认他们原来真的有话要说。

三、先忙着裁判，还是先想办法不让事情坏到那一步

本系列采用的文字是“圣人右介而不以责于人”，通行本常作“圣人执左契，而不责于人”。这里存在“介”“契”及左右契券的解释问题，不能凭一个字就宣布传统的契约读法失效。

契约的读法本身很有力量：即便手里有向别人追索的凭据，也不必时时把关系逼到索取的尽头。底本论文另外提出一条重视调停的阅读路线，把“右介”读向崇尚居间、化解。这里保留这个思考方向，但把它作为哲学重读，不当成已经排除了其他解释的字义定论。

放到今天，两条路可以在一个地方相遇：有资格责问，不等于只剩责问这一种能力。系统出了一次故障，当然要查清谁做了什么；也要问，为什么一个人的失误就能变成无法挽回的事故？交接有没有留出时间，风险有没有渠道上报，发现问题的人会不会先被惩罚？

如果最后只得到“某个人不够认真”，更换这个人以后，同样的条件可能继续制造同样的错误。查条件不是替过错开脱。故意隐瞒、滥用权力、造成伤害，仍然需要相称的责任。只是责任不该成为停止思考的按钮。

日常里的调停还可以更早一点：分工刚开始含糊时问清楚，承诺做不到时及时重谈，发现有人一直没有机会说话时把时间让给他。前提是尊重当事人的意愿，而不是以“防止矛盾”为名窥探和控制所有关系。好的预防，应当让人更能表达，不是更不敢表达。

四、做得了调停的人，也不是没有极限的人

“有德司介，无德司彻。”底本论文把这句话读向不同实践的安排：能承担调停的人去调停，尚未具备这种能力的人，先在具体劳动中做事。这是一种可以展开的现代理解，却需要去掉几层容易误伤人的意思。

首先，“彻”涉及的解释并不单一。有与田亩、耕作相关的用法，也有征敛、税制的解释传统。拿《诗经》里一个耕田用例，不能就此排除它在别处的其他含义。“无德”读为尚在学习，同样是这里愿意采用的建设性理解，不是古文必然只表达“还没练好”。

其次，耕作并不比调停低一等，也不是不需要判断、不需要德性的工作。不能把这一章改造成按人格高低分配职业的表格。一个人此刻不擅长调解冲突，并不因此失去价值，更不该被指定去做某种低位劳动。

调停之难，倒值得认真说。你需要听见各方的诉求，又不被任何一方的叙述全部带走；承认伤害，又不把一个人永远钉死在他的错误上；知道什么时候还可以谈，什么时候先要停止危险、保护当事人。这不是“脾气好”三个字就能承担的任务。

也因此，调停需要能力、许可和边界。不能要求一个温和的人接管所有人的愤怒，不能强迫受害者见面和解，更不能把“不再追究”作为有修养的证明。有些事需要明确裁决，有些关系需要结束。能够承认调停的限度，也是承担的一部分。

五、“天道无亲”，不是一张好人必得奖的保单

“夫天道无亲，恒与善人。”这句话可以读成一种希望：世界不应当因为你是谁家的孩子、属于哪一边，就给你另一套标准；值得亲近的是善的实践，而不是亲疏。

但现实并没有向我们保证，诚恳做事的人总能得到好结果。有人认真修补关系，关系仍然结束；有人替别人挡住了伤害，却没有得到感谢。如果把“恒与善人”讲成可以检验的报偿规律，反而容易让受伤的人再背上一句“是不是你还不够善”。

我们可以保留它的伦理方向，而不冒充知道宇宙如何记账。像第二十七章“无弃人”所打开的思路，眼前这个人不该只由一次失误、一场争执或一种标签来定义。这是本系列把两章放在一起后的体会，不是要求读者相信善行一定能兑换好运。

同样，人的价值也不以此刻能提供多少贡献为条件。暂时无法调停，不能劳动，或者还在从伤害中恢复，都不意味着一个人被排除在值得善待的人之外。谈善，不能悄悄把人又改成一张效用表。

六、给解决之后，留一点时间

下一次处理冲突时，可以试着把两个问题分开问：哪些事情现在必须解决？哪些感受和关系需要更多时间？补偿、分工、停止伤害，不宜无限拖延；信任却不能靠截止日期催出来。

如果你是造成伤害的一方，道歉之后还可以做一件不抢眼的事：稳定地改变那个曾经造成伤害的行为，不要求对方不断表扬你的进步。如果你是受损的一方，也不必为了让大家舒服，立刻演出一个已经放下的自己。

如果你在中间，不妨留意自己是不是太想成为“成功调解的人”。当你急着给故事一个好结尾，就可能不自觉地催促当事人完成你的成就。允许他们继续有分歧，允许一段关系只恢复到基本合作，也许比握手合影更诚实。

当然，不是所有余怨都值得继续投入。有人借着旧账不断控制别人，有人把对方的边界也算作冒犯。这时可以承认：问题还在，但我不会无限度地留在这里。留有余地，不等于没有终点；不强求圆满，也包括不强求自己修好所有人。

七、不要把每一次见面都变成一次处理

第七十八章谈接住难处，这一章接着问：接住以后，能不能不急着宣告一切已经妥当？从第七十三章对后果的敬畏，到第七十七章不把所有成果据为己有，落到调停上，就是别把别人的关系也变成证明自己能力的作品。

最好的生活，未必是矛盾不断、每次又都被高明解决的生活。有时更值得努力的，是让承诺清楚一点，资源宽裕一点，退出不那么困难，让人不用每隔几天就为基本尊严打一场仗。

第八十章会把目光转向这样的日子：有饭可吃，有地方安住，邻居就在不远处，却不必时时卷进彼此的争夺。它并没有自动解答所有政治难题，但提出了一个不该被宏大目标淹没的问题——我们这么忙着治理、调解、争胜，究竟是为了让人过上什么样的生活？

理论底本：《大知道德经解·论文九》。系列论文：一、二、三、四、五、六、七、八、九。

I. Everything settled except the relationship

Two colleagues have argued for weeks. Eventually someone intervenes, hears both sides, and proposes a settlement. Each accepts part of the cost. The agreement is recorded, and the matter is closed.

The next day, they work together professionally. They no longer interrupt each other, but they no longer joke either. A question once asked across the desk now arrives by email, with someone else copied in. When one needs help, another colleague is approached first.

Nobody is breaking the agreement. Something nevertheless remains. The dispute ended on one level and continues on another: in the expectation of being heard, the memory of a sentence, the caution with which information is shared.

“和大怨，必有余怨”: when a great resentment is reconciled, resentment remains. Chapter 79 begins with this distance between settling a claim and restoring a relationship. It does not make justice useless. It warns that not everything damaged will fit inside the terms of a solution.

The scene also changes what we ask of the person mediating. Did they secure an agreement? That is important. We also need to know what the agreement protects, what remains unresolved, and what conditions will allow life afterward without repeating the harm.

II. Repair is not a return through time

The emphatic “必有余怨” stresses the limits of settlement. We need not turn it into a sentence condemning every conflict to an unhealable wound. Relationships can improve, people reconcile, and repairs sometimes make a new kind of trust possible.

What we cannot demand is an immediate return to the earlier moment. Compensation may address financial loss without removing humiliation. An apology can be sincere and still need time to become credible. Continuing to remember does not prove that someone is refusing to cooperate.

“焉可以为善？” How can this be called good? The question can invite us to look beyond the visible success of closing a case. Preventing avoidable harm, attending to tensions early, and establishing fair procedures may be better than displaying skill at extinguishing fires our arrangements keep starting.

Prevention, however, does not mean asking a vulnerable party to stay quiet for the sake of peace. Some disputes must become visible precisely because an unjust situation has looked undisturbed for too long. Conflict can begin protection rather than mark the collapse of genuine harmony.

We should ask what peace we are seeking: one in which complaints are no longer heard, or one in which there are fewer reasons to suffer. Those aims can produce very different settlements, even when both conclude with a quiet room.

III. Between a contract and a bridge

The adopted text reads “圣人右介而不以责于人.” The received version commonly describes holding the left portion of a contract, “执左契,” without exacting an obligation from others. Relationships between “介” and “契,” instruments of obligation, and readings of left and right have serious textual grounds.

Alongside that field of interpretation, this series proposes a philosophical reading of “右介” as favoring mediation or the work of a bridge. It is not a dictionary restoration proved beyond dispute. It lets us ask whether, when difficulty arises, we look primarily for a debtor from whom to extract payment, or for a relationship whose conditions need attention.

Imagine an incident caused by pressing the wrong button. It is easy to end the investigation at the person who pressed it. We should also ask why a single action could cause so much harm, which warnings existed, what training was provided, and whether the error could have been detected or reversed.

Reviewing the system does not erase individual responsibility. Serious negligence or deliberate harm requires proportionate accountability and protection. But humiliating one person does not repair a vulnerable design. It may also ensure that the next difficulty is concealed until it becomes larger.

Bridge-building sometimes begins before the incident: a question about unclear responsibilities, a complaint repeatedly made as a joke, a conversation that always ends with someone falling silent. Hearing those signals may reduce risk. It does not authorize monitoring every emotion or promise a process that can never fail. Useful prevention opens channels; it does not occupy people’s private lives.

IV. Mediation needs competence and limits

“有德司介，无德司彻”: the contrast permits several readings. The received tradition includes keeping the contract and exacting levies. “彻” also has associations with cultivation; the expression “彻田为粮” in the Book of Odes belongs to that field, but does not by itself exclude a fiscal meaning here.

The application followed in this essay imagines different capacities: someone able to sustain mediation undertakes it; someone not yet prepared need not pretend. Reading “无德” as a capacity still to be developed is a constructive proposal, not the obligatory literal meaning of lacking virtue. Nor is agricultural work morally inferior or free of ethical judgment.

The practical point is not to distribute occupations according to people’s supposed moral worth. It is to recognize that some responsibilities require training, consent, time, and support. Good intentions are not sufficient preparation for every conflict.

A mediator may need to hear incompatible accounts without immediately choosing the more persuasive one. They must also recognize when apparent symmetry conceals abuse. Not every conflict is suited to a joint meeting. Protecting someone may require distance, specialist intervention, or a formal decision.

Mediating does not mean swallowing all hostility from every side. People doing this work need boundaries, supervision, and the option to step back when they cannot act safely or competently. The task does not become nobler because support is absent.

Recognizing a limit allows learning or referral. That is more responsible than turning an identity as “the person who fixes everything” into an obligation whose cost is eventually paid by those seeking help.

V. A way without favorites

“夫天道无亲，恒与善人”: Heaven’s way has no favorites and accompanies those who act well. The line belongs to moral and cosmological reflection. It does not demonstrate that outcomes are distributed according to personal goodness.

Acting justly does not guarantee winning a case, receiving gratitude, or avoiding misfortune. Nor does an adverse result prove moral failure. Making the verse into a law of reward would be particularly cruel to people who have suffered injustice.

We can read it as an orientation: do not privilege our own merely because they are ours, and cultivate practices that do not require disposable people. Chapter 27 offers a connection with that refusal to abandon. We need not claim that nature literally protects everyone equally in order to take the commitment seriously.

Someone unable to undertake a particular responsibility still deserves respect and conditions in which to live. Their worth does not depend on becoming useful in the way we expect. Helping them learn may be a possibility; it is not the price of their dignity.

Heaven without favorites thus returns us to specific preferences. Do we hear a complaint differently when it concerns a friend? Do we seek the same evidence when an accusation threatens someone influential? Impartiality begins where it stops being convenient, not where it costs us nothing to praise it.

VI. Before and after the settlement

Before entering a dispute, clarify what is needed: protection, a decision, compensation, conversation, or several of these. Do not call every attempt to make two people stop troubling us with their disagreement mediation.

During the intervention, examine capacity and limits. Do we know enough? Can we listen without imposing reconciliation? Do we have a conflict of interest? Seeking help or referring a difficult situation may be an act of care rather than a personal failure.

After the agreement, leave room for what follows. Changes in work arrangements, review of commitments, or time without contact may be needed. Nobody should have to perform restored friendship to prove acceptance of a fair solution.

If we hold responsibility, review the design that made the harm possible. Not to replace every form of accountability with an impersonal explanation, but to ensure the next person does not reach the same place with the same dangerous button in front of them.

And when a small tension becomes audible, approach without invading: “This has come up several times. Is there something we should clarify?” The question offers a channel. The answer also belongs to the person giving it. Prevention does not entitle us to unlimited access to someone else’s emotional life.

VII. Not ordering the wound to disappear

Chapter 78 concerned receiving a burden rather than continually sending it downward. Chapter 79 adds that the burden may remain after the ceremony of resolution. Continuing to answer for it means accepting that a signature does not determine the pace of trust.

The net in chapter 73 recalled effects that do not always appear immediately. The distribution in chapter 77 brought attention to conditions that relieve or reproduce inequality. Here the insights converge: what remains within a relationship needs attention, and that attention must be able to alter real conditions.

Chapter 80 will imagine a simpler coexistence, in which daily life can be enjoyed without being continually dragged into ambition and war. We will not arrive there by requiring everyone to have no complaints. Space must be made for living, choosing, and asking for help.

Perhaps the colleagues will joke together again. Perhaps they will achieve only a safe and respectful working relationship. The ending is not ours to command. We can stop confusing a closed file with a repaired life, and do what is needed so that what follows does not wound in the same way again.
