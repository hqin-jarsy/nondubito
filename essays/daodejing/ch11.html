第十一章
Chapter 11 of 81

留位

Leaving Room

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

一、不是"无高于有"

第十一章历代读得最多的一种意思是——"无"比"有"更高。

辐条围着毂心那个空。茶杯有那个腔。房子有那个室。

这些"无"才是真正有用的。"有"只是辅助。

虚空为上，不作为最高。

这是被广为引用的"老子无为哲学"的一个来源。

按这个读法老子是一个消极主义者——追求虚空、轻视实有、把不作为抬高为最高智慧。

但读这一章是这样读吗？

老子在这一章里三个器物的例子，每一次都明明白白同时讲了"有"和"无"——

车要有辐条也要有毂心。
陶器要有陶壁也要有腔。
房子要有墙也要有室。

缺任何一面都不成器。

老子在立的不是"无高于有"。

他在立的是另一件事。

二、三个器物

我们一个一个看。

"卅辐同一毂，当其无，有车之用也。"

辐是车轮的辐条。毂是车轮中心的圆孔——车轴从里面穿过。三十根辐条围着一个毂，合成车轮。

车能走，关键在毂心那个孔——没有孔，轴没法穿过，轮子转不起来。

这里有什么是有，什么是无？

有：三十根辐条、毂的外壁、木头的边界。这些立起了车轮的形态。
无：毂心的孔。这是留出来的位——没被构占满。

两者合起来才是一个能用的车轮。

只有辐条没有毂孔，那就是一块实心圆木头——转是能转，但没法装到车上。
只有毂孔没有辐条，那就是一个孔悬在空中，不成轮子。

"埏埴而为器，当其无，有埴器之用也。"

揉陶土做器皿——碗、罐、杯。

器能盛东西，关键在器皿里面那个腔——没有腔，没地方放东西，器皿不成器皿。

有：陶壁、器形、做出来的边界。
无：器皿内部的腔。

陶壁立起形态，腔留出位让东西能放进去。两者都要。

"凿户牖，当其无，有室之用也。"

凿门窗造房子。

房能住人，关键在屋里那个空间——没有空间，人进不去。

有：墙、门、窗、屋顶。这些立起房子的边界。
无：墙内的空间。

墙立起形态，空间留出位让人能在里面活动。两者都要。

三个例子合起来，是同一件事在三个日常器物上的演示——

车、器、室都是人造物，都是人主动立的构（辐条要做、陶土要揉、墙要砌）加上人主动留的位（毂要挖、腔要掏、室要空）。

构和留位都是主动动作——都是积极的。

没有哪一个是"空虚"、"无所作为"的消极面。

三、留位是立构的一部分

把这三个例子的结构抽出来看。

无不是"没有"，是"没被构占满的位"。

毂心不是没有毂心——毂心那个位置本身在那里，是被留出来不填东西的。
器腔不是没有器腔——腔的位置有、范围有，只是不被填实。
室内不是没有室内——室有墙围合、有范围、有结构，只是中间不堆满。

"没被构占满"和"没有"是两回事。

没有是不存在。
没被构占满是存在但留着。

留给什么？

留给要在里面运作的东西——轴在毂里转，物在腔里盛，人在室里活动。

这就推到这一章的核心命题——

留位是立构的一部分，不是立构之外的另一件事。

车轮在做出来的时候就挖好毂心，陶器在揉出来的时候就留好腔，房子在砌墙的时候就留好屋内。

留位不是事后发现需要空间才去腾，是在立构的同一动作里就留出来。

这件事在 2026 年的工作场景里很容易看见——

一个好的 UI 界面，最难的不是堆功能，是知道什么时候不放。
一个好的团队日程，最好的安排不是把每一格都填满，是给突发情况和深度思考留空间。
一份好的产品 roadmap，最有用的不是列出 100 件要做的事，是知道哪 10 件做、哪 90 件不做。
一份好的代码架构，最重要的不是写死所有逻辑，是给未来的扩展留 hook。

这些都是同一件事——

留位不是事后发现需要空间才去腾。

是在做这件事的同一个动作里就留出来的。

设计这件 UI 的人，在画第一根线的时候就在留白。
排这份日程的人，在排第一个会的时候就在留空当。
写这份 roadmap 的人，在选第一件事的时候就在拒绝其他事。

辐条和毂心是同一个动作做出来的。
陶壁和腔是同一个动作做出来的。
墙和室是同一个动作做出来的。

这就是老子第十一章一上来就在拎的事。

四、利不是好处

"故：有之以为利，无之以为用。"

这两句是全章的收束。

但这一句里"利"字的现代读法跟老子原本的读法之间，有一个不小的距离。

现代汉语里"利"基本只剩结果性含义——"有好处"、"得益"、"便利"。

如果按现代"利"读这一句——

"有之以为利" = "有提供便利、好处"。

立刻把"有"读浅了。

按这个读法读下去，"无之以为用"看起来就比"有之以为利"更深——好处是浅的，用才是深的。

这是历代把老子读成"无高于有"的一个具体起点。

但古汉语的"利"字面意思不一样。

《说文》："利，銛也。"銛是锋利。

利的本义是——刀刃锋利，形态立到可以用的那个状态。

这是物性含义，不是结果含义。

按这个读法——

"有之以为利"——有立起来的形态，是可被利用的那个物性。辐条立了形态，所以可以受力；陶壁立了形态，所以可以盛物；墙立了形态，所以可以围合。

利是形态立到位的那个物性。

"无之以为用"——无（留位）让形态能实际运作。毂心让轴能转，腔让物能放进去，室让人能活动。

用是形态在运作的那个动态。

利和用合起来，才是一个完整的能用的器物——形态立到位（利）+ 形态实际在运作（用）。

有和无都是积极的——有立形态，无提供运作空间，缺一不可。

"利用"这个今天的常用词里，其实就保留着这一对。

利是形态立好了。
用是在形态里运作。

利之而用之。

今天"利用"被读成单一动作，已经看不出它本来是两个结构功能合起来的描述。

但词的字面记得。

五、有和无都积极

第十一章在做的事，是把第二章那句"有无相生"在物性尺度上的具体演示。

第二章只是把六对平铺出来——有无、难易、长短、高下、音声、前后。

第十章把其中一对（基础层/涌现层）收到个体人身上演示（营/魄）。

第十一章把其中另一对（有/无）在物性上演示（构/留位）。

两章结构同构——

第十章：人身尺度。基础层和涌现层都要在，不能只修一层。
第十一章：物性尺度。有和无都要在，不能只立构不留位。

老子一对一对拎出来，每一对都走到读者眼前。

收回到这一章的核心——

无不是消极的空。
无是给运作预留的位。

立构的同一动作里就包含了留位。

有和无都是积极的。两者合起来才有一个能运作的系统。

只有构没有留位——系统凝固，里面什么都动不了。
只有留位没有构——没有边界，运作无形可依，整个系统不成形。

两者必须同时在。

读完第十一章再回头看任何一件事——

一辆车、一个杯子、一间房子、一个团队、一份 roadmap、一段代码、一个公司、一段关系——

都在演示同一件事。

形态和留位是同一个动作做出来的。

一、Not "Non-Being Above Being"

The most common reading of Chapter 11 across the centuries goes like this — "non-being" is higher than "being."

Spokes gather around the empty hub. A clay vessel has that hollow. A room has that interior space.

It is the "not-having" that is truly useful. "Having" is merely auxiliary. Emptiness is supreme; non-action is the highest wisdom.

This reading is one of the main sources for what is widely cited as "Laozi's philosophy of wu wei" — a passivity that prizes the void, disdains the actual, and elevates non-action as the highest wisdom.

But is that what this chapter teaches?

In every one of Laozi's three examples, he explicitly and equally names both the "having" and the "not-having" —

A wheel requires spokes and the hub.
A vessel requires the clay wall and the hollow.
A room requires walls and the interior space.

Either face missing and the thing does not function.

Laozi is not establishing "non-being above being."

He is establishing something else entirely.

二、Three Vessels

Let us go through the examples one by one.

"卅辐同一毂，当其无，有车之用也。"

A wheel's spokes converge on the hub — the cylindrical hole at the center through which the axle passes. Thirty spokes gather around one hub, forming a wheel.

The wheel rolls because of the hub's hole — without the hole, the axle cannot pass through and the wheel cannot turn.

What is having and what is not-having here?

Having: the thirty spokes, the hub's outer wall, the wooden boundary. These establish the wheel's form.
Not-having: the hole at the hub's center. This is the room left — the position not filled.

Together they make a usable wheel.

Only spokes, no hub hole — you have a solid disc of wood. It can spin, but cannot be mounted on an axle.
Only a hub hole, no spokes — a hole suspended in air, no wheel formed.

"埏埴而为器，当其无，有埴器之用也。"

Knead clay into a vessel — bowl, jar, cup.

A vessel holds things because of the inner hollow — without the hollow, nothing goes in, no vessel is a vessel.

Having: the clay wall, the vessel's form, the boundary it establishes.
Not-having: the hollow inside.

The clay wall establishes form; the hollow leaves room so things can be placed within. Both are needed.

"凿户牖，当其无，有室之用也。"

Cut doors and windows to build a room.

A room houses people because of the interior space — without it, no one can enter.

Having: walls, doors, windows, roof. These establish the room's boundary.
Not-having: the space within.

The walls establish form; the space leaves room so people can move inside. Both are needed.

Three examples, one demonstration — vehicle, vessel, room are all human constructs: an actively constructed form (spokes shaped, clay kneaded, walls built) plus an actively left position (hub carved out, hollow scooped, interior left open).

Form and leaving-room are both active moves — both affirmative.

Neither is the passive "emptiness" side.

三、Leaving Room Is Part of Construction

Let us pull the structure out of these three examples.

Not-having is not "absence." It is "a position not filled by any construct."

The hub is not absent — the position of the hub is there, left unfilled.
The hollow is not absent — its position exists and has extent, simply not filled solid.
The interior is not absent — walls enclose it, it has scope and structure, simply not piled full.

"Not filled by any construct" and "absent" are two different things.

Absent means not existing.
Not filled by any construct means existing but held open.

Held open for what?

For what will operate inside — the axle turning in the hub, objects placed in the hollow, people moving in the room.

This leads to the core proposition of the chapter —

Leaving room is part of construction, not something separate from it.

The wheel is carved with its hub hole already in place. The vessel is kneaded with its hollow already held open. The room is built with its interior already left clear.

Leaving room is not making room after the fact when you discover you need space. It is left open in the same act as the construction itself.

This is easily visible in the work of 2026 —

A good UI does not pile on features — the hardest part is knowing what not to place.
A good team schedule does not fill every slot — the best arrangement leaves gaps for the unexpected and for deep thinking.
A good product roadmap does not list 100 things to do — it knows which 10 to do and which 90 to refuse.
Good code architecture does not lock every path — it leaves hooks for future extension.

All the same thing —

Leaving room is not something you do after discovering you need space.

It is done in the same act as building.

The person designing the UI leaves the whitespace on the very first line they draw.
The person scheduling the calendar leaves the gap when placing the very first meeting.
The person writing the roadmap refuses other work when selecting the very first item.

Spokes and hub are made in the same act.
Clay wall and hollow are made in the same act.
Walls and interior are made in the same act.

This is what Laozi lifts up at the start of Chapter 11.

四、Li Does Not Mean "Benefit"

"故：有之以为利，无之以为用。"

These two lines close the chapter.

But the modern reading of the character 利 (lì) has drifted considerably from what Laozi intended, and that drift changes everything.

In modern Chinese, 利 has narrowed to a results-sense — "benefit," "advantage," "profit."

Read the line with that sense — "Having provides benefit" — and "having" is immediately made shallow. Following through, "not-having provides use" looks deeper than "having provides benefit."

This is one concrete starting point for the centuries-long reading of Laozi as holding "non-being above being."

But the classical meaning of 利 is different.

The Shuowen dictionary: "利，銛也." Xian means sharp-edged.

The primary meaning of 利 is — a blade sharpened to the state where it can be used: the property of a form fully established.

This is a material-property meaning, not a results meaning.

Read the line with this sense —

"有之以为利" — Having establishes the form; this is the material property of being usable. The spokes establish form and can therefore bear load; the clay wall establishes form and can therefore hold things; the walls establish form and can therefore enclose.

利 is the material property of a form fully established.

"无之以为用" — Not-having (leaving room) lets the form actually operate. The hub lets the axle turn; the hollow lets things be placed within; the interior lets people move.

用 is the dynamic of the form in actual operation.

利 and 用 together — form fully established (利) plus form in actual operation (用) — make one complete functioning thing. Having establishes form; not-having provides the operating space; neither dispensable.

Both are affirmative.

The compound 利用 (lì yòng), still common in Chinese today, preserves this pair.

利: the form is ready.
用: operating within the form.

First establish it, then operate within it.

The compound is now read as a single action ("to utilize"), and the original structural distinction is invisible. But the characters remember.

五、Both Having and Not-Having Are Active

Chapter 11 demonstrates on the material scale what Chapter 2's line "being and non-being produce each other" (有无相生) stated abstractly.

Chapter 2 simply laid out six pairs — being and non-being, difficulty and ease, long and short, high and low, sound and voice, before and after.

Chapter 10 demonstrated one of those pairs — foundation layer / emergent layer — within the individual person (ying / po).

Chapter 11 demonstrates another pair — having / not-having — at the material scale (construct / leaving-room).

The two chapters share the same structure —

Chapter 10: at the scale of a person. Foundation layer and emergent layer must both be present; you cannot cultivate only one.
Chapter 11: at the scale of material things. Having and not-having must both be present; you cannot construct without leaving room.

Laozi lifts each pair in turn, walking each one in front of the reader's eyes.

Back to this chapter's core —

Not-having is not passive emptiness.
Not-having is room reserved for operation.

Leaving room is built into the same act as construction.

Having and not-having are both active. Together they make a system that can operate.

Only form, no room left — the system freezes; nothing can move inside.
Only room, no form — no boundary, no shape to operate within; the whole system has no form.

Both must be present at once.

Read Chapter 11 and look at anything you encounter —

A car, a cup, a room, a team, a roadmap, a codebase, a company, a relationship —

All demonstrating the same thing.

Form and leaving-room are made in the same act.
