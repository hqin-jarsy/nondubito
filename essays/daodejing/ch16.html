第十六章
Chapter 16 of 81

两条路

Two Roads

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

一、看见还是没看见

两个人在街上看见同一片叶子落下。

第一个人看见——

一片叶子掉在地上。

第二个人看见——

这片叶子归根了。所有叶子最后都归根。所有生命都有它的根，最后都回到它的根。这是一件每一刻都在发生的事。

两个人接下来怎么走，完全不一样。

第一个人继续走他的路。他看到的只是"一片叶子"。

第二个人——他刚才那一瞬间看见了一件结构上的事。看完之后，他做什么、说什么、想什么，都被这件事影响了一下。

老子第十六章在说的，就是这两种人的区别——

看得见这件结构性事情的人 和 看不见的人。

两条 path 从这里分开。

而且老子直接告诉你——

一条走下去凶。
一条走下去沕身不殆。

这一章是这本书里非常少有的——老子明白说出判断的章。

但要读懂这两条 path，得先从工夫起点读起。

二、守情，不是守静

第十六章第一句——

"至虚极也，守情表也。"

这一句是整章的工夫起点。也是这一章最大的字差所在。

帛书：守情表也。
通行本：守静笃。

守情 vs 守静——差一个字，工夫方向完全不同。

按通行本读"守静"——主体把自己收到一个安静的状态里。工夫指向自己。

按帛书读"守情"——

但是"情"这个字今天读起来很别扭。

今天人读"守情"会立刻想到"守住感情"——这就完全读不通了。老子怎么会让人守感情？

要读通这一句，得先把"情"字的真意立起来。

三、情，不是感情

古"情"字不表感情。

这件事先秦典籍里有非常清晰的证据。

《尚书》《左传》中没有一个"情"字表感情。

《论语》用"情"两例，《孟子》用四例——全部是"本性、实情、真实"，无一例感情义。

《墨子》用"情"二十五次，分别表实情、情理、禀赋、真实——无感情义。

《易传》用"情"十四处，全部是"真实"那一支——其中有一句"圣人立象以尽意，设卦以尽情伪"——

情伪对举。情和伪是反义。

伪 = 假。情 = 真。

"情"作"感情"义是郭店楚简（战国中后期）才开始的。老子早于郭店楚简——他用"情"只能是先秦早期用法。

所以——

情 = 本性、实情、真实。

老子说"守情"——是守住事物的本然真实样貌，不加伪、不扭曲、不殖民。

是一种看见真实并守住真实的工夫。

工夫的方向——指向事物，不是指向自己。

再加"表"字。

表 = 外显处、显明位、可见的外在。

守情表 = 守住事物的真实在外显处让你看见的那一面。

合起来——

致虚极——主体把自己虚到极处，不带预设。
守情表——守住真实在外显处让你看见的那一面。

两者是一对——主体虚到极处，才能看见事物的本然样貌。

这是外向观照的工夫。

按通行本读，这一章是内在修养章——主体追求自己的静态，再向外看一眼万物。

按帛书读，这一章是外向观照章——主体的虚是为了让事物能在外显处被看见。

这又是 Paper 2 反复出现的方向——通行本字差合起来把老子的工夫从外向滑向内向，从指向世界滑向指向自己。

帛书让老子盯着世界看。
通行本让老子修自己。

按帛书读，老子是一个永远在看世界的人。

四、观万物各归其根

主体虚到极、守住真实——能看到什么？

下一句——

"万物旁作，吾以观其复也。"

万物在你周围各自运作，你观察它们的复。

复是什么？回归。

什么回归？

"天物云云，各复归于其根。"

天下万物纷纭万千，每一个都回到自己的根。

这是这一章的核心观察。

每一棵树从种子长出，经过枝叶繁茂，最后落叶归根。
每一个人从婴儿长到成人，一生各种际遇，最后也归一个位。
每一段关系从开始到深入到淡去，最后也归一个状态。
每一个产品从设计到发布到迭代到淘汰，最后也有归处。
每一家公司从创立到扩张到衰落，最后也归于土。

万物各复归其根——这不是悲观，这是观察到的结构。

每一个具体的事物，都在按这个路径运作——长出，经过，归根。

这件事每一刻都在发生。

只是大多数人忙着抓住正在长出的那部分，看不见整个回归路径。

老子告诉你——

致虚极、守情表——主体把自己虚到极处不预设，守住真实让事物在外显处被看见——

你就能看见这件事。

看见每一个具体的事物在它的运作链里归根。

看见每一个构最后都回到它来的位。

五、常不是常识

老子接下来用古文的递进结构——每一步独立拎出来——

归根曰静——回到自己的根这件事，叫"静"。

注意这个"静"不是"安静不发声"——是回到本位的那个状态。一个东西回到它的根，它就回到了本来的位，不再被外力推来推去。

静——拎出来独立一字。

是谓复命——这个静，就是复命。

复命 = 恢复本然的运作方向。一个东西回到它的根，它就回到了自己本来的方向。

复命——拎出来独立。

常也——这个复命，就是常。

这个"常"字今天读起来很容易被误解。

读者一看到"常"就想到——常识、平常、经常、常理。

但老子的"常"不是这些。

老子的常——是恒常运作的结构本身。

万物各复归其根、归根则静、静即复命——这件事本身是恒常的。不是今天这样明天那样，是每一刻都这样。每一个东西都在按这个结构运作。

这个结构就是常。

跟第一章那个"恒道"的"恒"是同一件事——

恒不是"永恒不变"（某个静态物一直不变）。

恒是永不灭的每一刻新涌出（这个运作结构每一刻都在发生）。

常 = 这个动态结构的恒常运作本身。

"知常，明也。"

能看见这个恒常运作的结构，就是明。

明在老子那里不是"聪明"——是看见。

知常 = 看见常 = 看见万物各复归其根这件事本身。

这里要拎清一个读法——

知常不是"知道一条叫做常的规律然后背熟它"。

知常是——在此刻看见这个运作结构的具体显现。

此刻看见一棵树的叶子落下——看见这一片叶子归根，也看见所有叶子都会归根，这就是知常的一个具体瞬间。

此刻看见一个朋友的关系慢慢淡掉——看见这一段关系归根，也看见所有关系都会归一个位，这又是知常的一个具体瞬间。

此刻看见自己的一个项目走完了它的 lifecycle——看见这一个项目归根，也看见所有项目最后都归根，又是知常的一个具体瞬间。

知常不是对抽象规律的知识掌握，是对此刻发生之事的看见。

每一刻每一个事件都可以是知常的时机——只要你看见这件事和万物回归其根的结构相连就够了。

六、两条 path

铺到这里，老子把整章最关键的两条 path 摆出来——

不知常的 path——

"不知常，妄，妄作，凶。"

四步。

不知常——看不见恒常运作的结构。
妄——一旦开始行动，行动就成了妄。妄 = 不依据实情、不按结构的乱来。
妄作——妄落到具体动作上就是妄作。乱行动、乱作为、乱殖民。
凶——结果凶。

老子用四个字画出这条 path——从一个看不见的起点，四步直接到凶。

注意：凶不是道德惩罚，是结构性后果。

不按结构运作的行动迟早会跟结构冲突，冲突到一定程度就是凶。

知常的 path——

"知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，沕身不殆。"

七步。

知常容——知常的人自然能容。因为他看见每一个东西都有自己的根、都有自己的运作方向，他不把自己的构强加到别人身上。

容乃公——容的人自然公。他对万物一视同仁，不偏袒任何一方。

公乃王——公的人自然能王。王在这里不是统治者——是主导位、枢纽位。一个真正公的人自然走到枢纽位上，因为所有人都信任他。

王乃天——王的人自然合天。天 = 天地自然运作的方式。真正的王位运作方式和天地一致。

天乃道——天的运作方式自然合道。

道乃久——合道的运作自然久。

沕身不殆——最终身进入深藏的位，不危殆。

七步合起来不是道德规范的累加——是同一个觉察在不同位上的自然展开。

两条 path 对比——

不知常 → 妄 → 妄作 → 凶（四步）
知常 → 容 → 公 → 王 → 天 → 道 → 久 → 沕身不殆（七步）

老子把两条 path 都摆出来，摆得对称、清楚、直接——

- 两条 path 都从知不知常开始
- 走歪的 path 短，直接到凶
- 走好的 path 长，层层展开到不殆
- 终点对比鲜明：凶 vs 沕身不殆

放到 2026 年读——

这两条 path 我们每天都在两条之间切换。

某一个会议，你听了别人的观点没听进去，直接按自己的预设回应——
那一刻你在妄的位上（看不见对方运作的结构，凭自己的构乱说）。

某一个决定，你慢下来看见了"这件事最后会归到什么样的根"——
那一刻你在知常的位上。

某一段关系，你抓着想让它一直停在最好的状态——
那一刻你不知常（不知道关系也会归根）。

某一段关系，你看见它已经走到了它的根——
那一刻你知常，你可以容它的自然变化。

这一章给你的工具非常具体——

你此刻在哪条 path 上？

不是问你"你是不是一个好人"——这又把它读成道德判断了。

是问你此刻这件事上，你能不能看见这件事跟万物回归其根的结构相连？

看不见——你接下来的动作就是妄作的概率很大。
看见了——你接下来的动作自然容、公，往不殆的方向走。

每一件事都可以问一次。

每一次问，都是一次自检。

七、说明白，不强迫

老子在这一章里明白地说出了他的判断——

一条 path 凶。
一条 path 不殆。

他没有装作中立——没有摆出"两条路都可以哈，你随便选"的姿态。

但他也没有强迫——

没有说"你必须走好的 path"。
没有说"走歪了就完了你这辈子救不回来"。
没有说"你必须按我说的来"。

他只是把两条 path 摆出来——清清楚楚、直直白白。

懂的读者会认出自己此刻在哪条 path 上，然后自己决定下一步怎么走。

不懂的读者看到只会觉得是普通的善恶劝诫。

老子不承担让所有读者都懂的义务——他说清楚了，剩下的是读者自己的事。

这跟前面几章一脉相承——

第十章六问"能……乎"——对有志者的询问式教育。
第十二章"是以圣人之治也，为腹而不为目"——对治理者的直接建议。
第十三章"吾所以有大患者，为吾有身"——老子用自己做示范。
第十六章——把看清楚的两条 path 直接摆给读者看。

老子从来不是温柔地"让你自己选哈都可以"——他每一章都明白说出他看见的。

但老子也从来不强迫——他说完了，读者自己决定怎么办。

这就是涵育式教师始终如一的姿态——

说真话，不操控。

让读者自己是主体。

读完这一章，回到开篇那两个看叶子的人——

老子在指着第二个人说："看，这个人看见的不一样。"

但老子没逼第一个人变成第二个人。

他只是说：你看，这两个人接下来的路径不一样。

剩下的是读者自己的事。

一、Seeing or Not Seeing

Two people on the street watch the same leaf fall.

The first person sees —

A leaf hitting the ground.

The second person sees —

This leaf has returned to its root. Every leaf returns to its root in the end. Every living thing has its root; every thing returns to its root in the end. This is something happening at every moment.

What these two people do next is completely different.

The first person continues on their way. They saw only "a leaf."

The second person — in that instant saw a structural fact. After seeing it, whatever they do, say, or think is touched by it.

What Laozi discusses in Chapter 16 is the difference between these two kinds of people —

Those who can see this structural fact and those who cannot.

Two paths diverge here.

And Laozi tells you directly —

One path ends in disaster (凶).
One path ends in entering deep without danger (沕身不殆).

This chapter is one of the very rare ones where Laozi explicitly states a judgment.

But to understand these two paths, you need to start with the practice entry point.

二、Guard the Real — Not Guard the Still

Chapter 16's first line —

"至虚极也，守情表也。"

"Reach the utmost emptiness; guard the reality-display."

This line is the chapter's practice entry point. It is also where the chapter's biggest textual variant falls.

Silk manuscript: guard 情 (qíng) — the surface of reality.
Standard text: guard 静 (jìng) — stillness, quietude.

Guard the real vs. guard the still — one character different, the direction of practice completely changes.

Standard-text reading "guard stillness" — the subject collects themselves into a still and quiet state. Practice directed at oneself.

Silk-manuscript reading "guard 情" —

But 情 is a stumbling block for today's readers.

Modern Chinese reads 情 as almost exclusively meaning "feelings" or "emotion." If you read "guard 情" as "guard feelings" — it makes no sense at all. Why would Laozi have people guard their feelings?

To read this line correctly, the true meaning of 情 in classical Chinese must first be established.

三、Qing Is Not Feeling

The ancient character 情 does not mean feelings.

The textual evidence in pre-Qin classics is extremely clear.

In Shangshu and Zuozhuan — no single instance of 情 meaning feelings.

In Analects, 情 appears twice; in Mencius, four times — all instances mean "natural condition, factual reality, genuine nature," none meaning feelings.

In Mozi, 情 appears twenty-five times, variously meaning actual situation, reason, capacity, genuine — no feelings meaning.

In Yizhuan (Commentary on the Book of Changes), 情 appears fourteen times, all meaning "genuine reality." One line reads: "圣人立象以尽意，设卦以尽情伪" —

情 and 伪 (false/artificial) are set as antonyms. 伪 = false. 情 = genuine.

情 meaning "feelings/emotions" only begins in the Guodian bamboo texts (mid-to-late Warring States period). Laozi predates Guodian — his use of 情 can only be the early pre-Qin usage.

So —

情 = natural condition, actual reality, genuine nature.

Laozi saying "guard 情" — is guarding the genuine reality-as-it-actually-is of things; not adding false constructs; not distorting; not colonizing.

This is a practice of seeing the real and holding to the real.

The direction of practice: toward things, not toward oneself.

Add the character 表 (biǎo).

表 = the outward display, the visible exterior, the face of the real that appears before you.

守情表 = guard the genuine reality as it manifests in the outward display.

Together —

致虚极 — the subject empties to the utmost, setting aside all preconceptions.
守情表 — guard the genuine reality of things as it manifests in visible display.

These two are a pair — the subject emptied to the utmost, then able to see the genuine reality of things in their outward display.

This is outwardly directed observation practice.

Standard-text reading turns this chapter into an inner-cultivation chapter — the subject seeks their own still state, then takes one look at the ten thousand things from outside.

Silk-manuscript reading turns this chapter into an outwardly directed observation chapter — the subject's emptiness is for things to be able to display their genuine reality to be seen.

This again follows Paper 2's recurring direction — standard text variants together shift Laozi's practice from outward-facing to inward-facing, from pointing at the world to pointing at the self.

The silk manuscript's Laozi is always staring at the world.
The standard text's Laozi is cultivating himself.

Reading the silk manuscript, Laozi is a person who never stops looking at the world.

四、Observing All Things Return to Their Roots

The subject emptied to the utmost, guarding the genuine reality-display — what can be seen?

Next line —

"万物旁作，吾以观其复也。"

"The ten thousand things rise all around; I observe their returning."

The ten thousand things operate all around you; you observe their return (复).

Return to what?

"天物云云，各复归于其根。"

"The things of heaven, in their manifold abundance, each returns to its root."

Every tree grows from a seed, through leafy flourishing, and in the end returns to root.
Every person grows from infancy to adulthood, through all manner of circumstance, and in the end returns to a position.
Every relationship begins, deepens, fades — in the end returns to a state.
Every product is designed, launched, iterated, discontinued — in the end has its return.
Every company is founded, expands, declines — in the end returns to earth.

All things each return to their root — this is not pessimism; this is observed structure.

Every concrete thing operates along this path — emergence, passage, return to root.

This is happening at every moment.

Only most people are busy grasping the part that is still emerging, and cannot see the whole return path.

Laozi tells you —

Reach the utmost emptiness, guard the genuine reality-display — the subject emptied to the utmost with no preconceptions, guarding what is genuinely displaying —

You can see this.

See every concrete thing returning to its root in its chain of operation.

See every construct in the end returning to the position it came from.

五、Chang Is Not Commonsense

Laozi follows with the classical Chinese incremental structure — each step stated independently —

归根曰静 — Returning to one's root is called "stillness."

Note: this "stillness" is not "quiet, making no sound" — it is the state of returning to one's original position. When something returns to its root, it returns to its original position, no longer being pushed around by outside forces.

Stillness — one independent character.

是谓复命 — This stillness is called "returning to the mandate."

Returning to the mandate = returning to one's original direction of operation. When something returns to its root, it returns to its original direction.

Returning to the mandate — one independent phrase.

常也 — This returning to the mandate is called chang (常).

The character 常 (cháng) is easily misread today.

Readers seeing 常 immediately think — common knowledge, ordinary, routine, often-occurring, conventional wisdom.

But Laozi's 常 is none of these.

Laozi's 常 — is the constantly operating structure itself.

All things each return to their root, returning to root is stillness, stillness is returning to the mandate — this event itself is enduring (常). Not that today it's like this and tomorrow it's different — every moment it is like this. Every thing operates by this structure.

This structure is chang.

The same thing as Chapter 1's "enduring dao" (恒道) — 恒 is not "eternally static" (some static thing never changing); 恒 is the new yielding at every moment that never extinguishes (this operating structure is happening fresh at every moment).

Chang = the enduring operation of this dynamic structure itself.

"知常，明也。"

"Knowing chang is called brightness (明)."

Being able to see this enduringly operating structure is brightness.

明 in Laozi is not "cleverness" — it is seeing.

Knowing chang = seeing chang = seeing the event of all things each returning to their root.

The reading to clarify —

Knowing chang is not "knowing a rule called 'chang' and memorizing it."

Knowing chang is — seeing this operating structure in its concrete manifestation at this moment.

This moment seeing a leaf fall — seeing this one leaf returning to its root, also seeing all leaves return to their root, this is one concrete instant of knowing chang.

This moment seeing a friendship gradually fade — seeing this relationship return to its root, also seeing all relationships return to a position, another concrete instant of knowing chang.

This moment seeing a project complete its lifecycle — seeing this one project return to its root, also seeing all projects return in the end, another concrete instant of knowing chang.

Knowing chang is not possession of knowledge of an abstract rule — it is seeing the present event connect to the structure of all things returning to their root.

Every moment every event can be an occasion for knowing chang — as long as you see this event connect to the structure.

六、Two Paths

With this foundation, Laozi places the chapter's most critical two paths before us —

The path of not knowing chang —

"不知常，妄，妄作，凶。"

Four steps.

Not knowing chang — cannot see the enduringly operating structure.
Mang (妄) — the moment action begins, action becomes mang. Mang = operating not in accordance with the actual situation, not following the structure — going off on one's own.
Mang-act — mang landing in concrete actions is mang-acting. Acting wildly, acting arbitrarily, colonizing arbitrarily.
Disaster (凶) — the result is disaster.

Laozi draws this path in four characters — from a blind starting point, four steps straight to disaster.

Note: disaster is not moral punishment — it is structural consequence.

Action that does not follow the structure will sooner or later collide with the structure; collide hard enough — that is disaster.

The path of knowing chang —

"知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，沕身不殆。"

Seven steps.

Knowing chang, capacity (容) — the person who knows chang naturally has capacity. Because they see every thing has its own root and its own direction of operation, they do not impose their constructs on others.

Capacity leads to impartiality (公) — the person with capacity is naturally impartial. Treating all things equally, not favoring any one party.

Impartiality leads to kingship (王) — the impartial person naturally reaches the pivotal position. 王 here is not ruler — it is the leading position, the pivot position. A truly impartial person naturally arrives at the pivot because everyone trusts them.

Kingship leads to heaven (天) — the pivotal one naturally aligns with heaven. Heaven = the mode of operation of nature and the cosmos. True pivotal-position operation aligns with heaven and earth.

Heaven leads to the dao — heaven's mode of operation naturally aligns with the dao.

The dao leads to endurance (久) — operation in alignment with the dao naturally endures.

Entering deep without danger (沕身不殆) — in the end the self enters deep concealment, without danger.

Seven steps together are not the accumulation of moral norms — they are the natural unfolding of one observation across different positions.

Two paths set side by side —

Not knowing chang → mang → mang-act → disaster (four steps)
Knowing chang → capacity → impartiality → kingship → heaven → dao → endurance → entering deep without danger (seven steps)

Laozi places both paths side by side — symmetrically, clearly, directly —

- Both paths start from whether or not one knows chang
- The wrong path is short, going straight to disaster
- The right path is long, unfolding layer by layer to no-danger
- End points sharply contrasted: disaster vs. entering deep without danger

In 2026 —

We are switching between these two paths every day.

One meeting, you did not absorb what someone said and responded straight from your preconceptions —
In that moment you were at the position of mang (cannot see the other's operating structure; rambling from your own construct).

One decision, you slowed down and saw "where this thing will end up" —
In that moment you were at the position of knowing chang.

One relationship, you tried to hold it fixed in its best state —
In that moment you did not know chang (did not know relationships also return to their root).

One relationship, you saw it had already walked to its root —
In that moment you knew chang; you could have capacity for its natural change.

The tool this chapter gives you is very concrete —

Which path are you on at this moment?

Not asking "are you a good person" — that would again be reading it as moral judgment.

Asking: on this particular thing at this moment, can you see this thing connect to the structure of all things returning to their roots?

Cannot see — what you do next has a high probability of being mang-action.
Saw it — what you do next naturally has capacity, impartiality, moves in the direction of no-danger.

You can ask once for every thing.

Every asking is one self-check.

七、Speaking Clearly, Not Compelling

In this chapter, Laozi clearly states his judgment —

One path ends in disaster.
One path ends in no-danger.

He did not play at neutrality — did not posture "both roads are fine, pick whichever you like."

But he also did not compel —

Did not say "you must walk the right path."
Did not say "go wrong and you're finished, no way back."
Did not say "you must follow my guidance."

He simply placed both paths clearly, directly.

The reader who understands will recognize which path they are on at this moment, then decide for themselves what to do next.

The reader who does not understand will read it as ordinary moral exhortation.

Laozi does not carry the obligation to make every reader understand — he said his piece; the rest is the reader's own matter.

This runs consistently through the preceding chapters —

Chapter 10's six questions "can you...?" — inquiry-mode teaching for the aspiring.
Chapter 12's "in the sage's governance, act for the belly and not for the eye" — direct advice to the ruler.
Chapter 13's "the reason I have great trouble is that I have shen" — Laozi using himself as demonstration.
Chapter 16 — places the clearly seen two paths directly before the reader.

Laozi never gently "lets you choose, either way is fine" — every chapter clearly speaks what he sees.

But Laozi also never compels — he says it, readers decide for themselves.

This is the nurturing teacher's unchanging posture —

Speak the truth; do not manipulate.

Let the reader be the subject.

Reading the chapter and returning to its opening — the two people watching the leaf fall —

Laozi is pointing at the second person and saying: "Look — this person sees differently."

But Laozi does not force the first person to become the second.

He simply says: look, these two people's paths ahead are different.

The rest is the reader's own matter.
