Dazhi Daodejing Commentary
第三十五章，共八十一章
Chapter 35 of 81

乐与饵，留不住天下

Music and Bait Cannot Hold the World

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

一、补贴拉来的用户

每个做过增长的人都知道这件事——

用补贴拉来的用户，补贴一停就走。用优惠拉来的用户，优惠一停就走。用噱头拉来的用户，噱头一过就走。

这些用户来了，但留不住。因为他们是被具体的好处吸引来的——一旦好处没了，他们就继续往前走。

老子在《道德经》第三十五章里，两千四百年前就把这件事说清楚了——

乐与饵，过客止。音乐和食物可以让路过的人停下来。但只是停一下。音乐停了，食物吃完了，过客就继续走了。

老子给执政者一个简单的对照——你想留住过客（短暂停一下），还是想让天下持续归来？这一章字面非常直白。就是一个对照，一个选择。

二、执大象，天下往

第三十五章开头：

"执大象，天下往，往而不害，安平泰。"

持守大象，天下都来归向，归来之后没有伤害，能安宁、平和、泰然。

"大象"不是大象动物——是大的样子、大的形象。但接 ch34"可名于大"立的字脉——真正的大不立自己为大——所以"大象"是没有具体形态的大（接 ch41"大象无形"）。"执大象"——持守这个无形的大。整句——治者持守这个无形的大象，天下都来归向治者。

这接 ch32"侯王若能守之，万物将自宾"——ch32：侯王守朴 → 万物自宾。ch35：执大象 → 天下往。两个字脉同义——治者守住道，天下自然归来。

"往而不害，安平泰"——归来之后没有伤害，能安宁、平和、泰然在场。天下归来到治者这里，没有伤害（不会受伤、不会被殖民），能安宁、平和、泰然在场。接 ch28 末段"夫大制无割"——大的运作不会断，天下归来后不被切碎、不被殖民。

三、乐与饵，只能留住过客

接下来是这一章的核心对照——

"乐与饵，过客止。"

音乐和食物可以让过客停下来。老子用最朴素的场景——一个路过的人，闻到食物香、听到音乐响，就停下来。这是常人被吸引的方式。

给执政者的字面拎——你以为给百姓乐和饵就能留住他们？不能。乐与饵只能让过客止（短暂停下），不能让天下往（持续归来）。给具体好处的治者只能留住"过客"，留不住"天下"。

为什么？因为乐与饵是有具体形态的吸引——有声、有味、有形。一旦音乐停了、食物吃完了，过客就会继续往前走。靠具体好处吸引的，留不住人。

放到 2026 年看——补贴、优惠、噱头、短期激励——都是"乐与饵"。有具体形态（看得见的好处），但会用完（补贴会停、优惠会过期）。靠这些吸引来的用户、员工、关注者——都是过客。好处一停，就走。

一家公司靠高薪挖来的人，更高的薪水一出现就走。一个 app 靠补贴拉来的用户，补贴一停就卸载。一个平台靠噱头吸引的关注，噱头一过就取关。一个团队靠 perks（免费午餐、按摩、团建）留住的员工，perks 一砍就离职。这些都是乐与饵。留得住过客，留不住天下。

四、道，看不见摸不着，但用不完

接下来老子讲道是怎么吸引人的——

"故道之出言也，曰：啖呵其无味也，视之不足见也，听之不足闻也，用之不可既也。"

道表现出来时是这样的——品尝它没味道，看它看不见，听它听不到，用它绝不会用尽。

前三句接 ch14——ch14："视之而弗见，听之而弗闻，捪之而弗得。"道在视、听、触三种感官层都不可被把握。ch35 这里说道在味、视、听三种感官层都不可被把握。道在感官层没有具体形态可见。

"啖呵其无味也"这一句有一处关键字差。帛书写"啖呵其无味也"——啖是品尝（《说文》"啖，噍啖也"——张口品尝），是个动作。通行本写"淡乎其无味"——淡是淡而无味，是个状态。

一字之差——啖（帛书）= 你去品尝道的时候，发现没有味道（保留治者主动接触道的动作）。淡（通行本）= 道是淡的（只剩道的客体属性，治者的主动动作消失了）。本注按帛书"啖"读——保留治者主动去品尝、去接触道时发现"无味"的字面。

整段对照——

乐与饵——过客可以闻到（有味）、看到（有形）、听到（有声）——所以过客止。

道——治者去品尝（啖）发现无味、去看不可见、去听不可闻——所以表面上没有什么显眼的"好处"可以拿出来给百姓。

这是给执政者最关键的对照——道没有显眼的"好处"可见，但治者要持守的恰恰是这个无显眼好处的道。

五、用之不可既

整章的最终落点，在最后四个字——

"用之不可既也。"

用它绝不会用尽。"既"是吃完（《说文》"既，小食也"——本义是吃完），引申为完结、用尽。用道，绝不会用尽。

把整章的对照立死——

乐与饵——有具体形态可见，但会用完（音乐会停、食物会吃光）——所以只能让过客止（短暂停下）。

道——在感官层无形可见，但用之不可既（用之无穷无尽）——所以能让天下往（持续归来）。

这就是给执政者的最终承诺——你持守道，看上去没有显眼的好处可以拿出来——但用之无穷。乐与饵会用完，道用不完。这就是为什么乐与饵留得住过客但留不住天下，道留得住天下。

这接 ch4"道冲，而用之或不盈"和 ch6"绵绵呵其若存，用之不勤"——道的用是不可穷尽的。这是道在世间的真正吸引力——不是在感官层的具体好处，是在用的层面的不可穷尽。

通行本这里写"用之不足既"——不足是"不够"。帛书写"用之不可既"——不可是断然的"绝不能"。本注按帛书"不可"读——用之绝不会用尽——保留断然的语气。

六、给读者的

这一章给读者的，是一个非常具体的辨别——

你靠什么吸引人？

如果你靠"乐与饵"——补贴、优惠、噱头、高薪、perks、短期刺激——这些有具体形态（看得见的好处），但会用完。靠这些吸引来的，都是过客。好处一停，就走。

如果你靠"道"——真正持久的价值、真正的意义、真正让人成长的东西——这些看不见、摸不着、说不清（无味、不可见、不可闻），表面上没有显眼的好处。但用之不可既——用不完。靠这个吸引来的，是天下。持续归来，不会因为某个好处停了就走。

放到你的具体位置上——

做产品——靠补贴拉用户（乐与饵），补贴停了用户走。靠真正解决问题的价值留用户（道），用户长久留下。

带团队——靠 perks 和高薪留人（乐与饵），更好的 offer 一来人就走。靠真正的成长、真正的意义、真正被尊重留人（道），人真心留下。

做内容——靠标题党、噱头、蹭热点吸引关注（乐与饵），热点过了关注走。靠真正有价值的内容（道），读者持续追随。

任何关系——靠取悦、讨好、给好处维系（乐与饵），好处停了关系淡。靠真正的理解、真正的在场维系（道），关系长久。

每一个都是同一个辨别——乐与饵看得见，但会用完，留得住过客。道看不见，但用不完，留得住天下。

不是说不能给好处（乐与饵本身没错，过客来了也是来了）。是要看清——靠好处吸引来的，会因为好处停止而离开。真正能让人持续归来的，是那个看不见、说不清、但用不完的东西。

老子两千四百年前给执政者的这个选择，今天每个做事的人都还在面对——你想留住过客，还是想让天下持续归来？

如果是后者——持守那个无味、不可见、不可闻，但用之不可既的东西。天下自然归来。

I. Users Who Come for the Subsidy

Everyone who has done growth work knows this —

Users who come for the subsidy leave when the subsidy stops. Users who come for the discount leave when the discount ends. Users who come for the gimmick leave when the gimmick fades.

They came, but they can't be kept. Because they were attracted by concrete benefits — once the benefits are gone, they move on.

Laozi said this plainly in Chapter 35 of the Daodejing, 2,400 years ago —

Music and food make the passing traveler stop. But only for a moment. When the music stops and the food is gone, the traveler moves on.

Laozi gives those who govern a simple contrast — do you want to hold the passing traveler (a brief stop), or do you want the world to keep flowing back? This chapter is direct. One contrast. One choice.

II. Hold to the Great Image, and the World Will Come

Chapter 35 opens:

"Hold to the great image, and the world will come. Coming, it will not be harmed; it will be at peace, at ease, at rest."

"The great image" (大象) is not an elephant — it is the great form, the great appearance. But connecting to ch. 34's word-thread "can be called great" — true greatness does not declare itself great — so "the great image" is greatness without concrete form (connecting to ch. 41 "the great image has no form"). "Hold to the great image" — hold to this formless greatness. Whole line: the ruler who holds to this formless great image — the world flows toward the ruler.

This connects to ch. 32 "if lords and kings can keep to it, the ten thousand things will of themselves come to attend" — ch. 32: ruler keeps to the unhewn → ten thousand things attend of themselves. Ch. 35: hold to the great image → world flows back. The two word-threads are synonymous — the ruler holds to the Dao, and the world naturally flows back.

"Coming, it will not be harmed; it will be at peace, at ease, at rest" — flowing back to the ruler, it is not harmed (not injured, not colonized), able to be at peace, at ease, at rest in its presence. Connecting to ch. 28's closing "the great ordering does not cut" — the great operation does not break down; the world that flows back is not cut apart, not colonized.

III. Music and Food Can Only Hold the Passing Traveler

The chapter's core contrast —

"Music and food — the passing traveler stops."

Laozi uses the plainest scene — a person passing by smells food, hears music, and stops. This is how ordinary people are attracted.

The literal point for those who govern — do you think giving people music and food will hold them? It won't. Music and food can make the passing traveler stop (stop briefly), but cannot make the world flow back (flow back continuously). A ruler who gives concrete benefits can hold passing travelers but not the world.

Why? Because music and food are attractions that have concrete form — there is sound, taste, shape. Once the music stops and the food is gone, the traveler moves on. What is attracted by concrete benefits cannot be kept.

In 2026 — subsidies, discounts, gimmicks, short-term incentives — all are "music and food." They have concrete form (visible benefits) but will be used up (subsidies stop, discounts expire). Users, employees, and followers attracted by these things — are all passing travelers. Benefits stop; they go.

A company that poaches someone with high pay — a better offer comes and they go. An app that attracted users with subsidies — subsidies stop, they uninstall. A platform that attracted attention with gimmicks — gimmick fades, they unfollow. A team retained with perks (free lunches, massages, outings) — perks cut, they quit. All of these are music and food. Holds the passing traveler; cannot hold the world.

IV. The Dao — Invisible, Intangible, Inexhaustible

Laozi then describes how the Dao attracts —

"Therefore when the Dao speaks, it says: when you taste it, it has no flavor; when you look for it, it is not enough to see; when you listen for it, it is not enough to hear; yet in use it cannot be exhausted."

The first three lines connect to ch. 14 — ch. 14: "look for it and you cannot see it; listen for it and you cannot hear it; reach for it and you cannot get it." The Dao cannot be grasped through the three senses of sight, hearing, and touch. Ch. 35 says the Dao cannot be grasped through taste, sight, and hearing. The Dao has no concrete form to be perceived at the sensory level.

"When you taste it, it has no flavor" (啖呵其无味也) contains a critical textual difference. The silk manuscript writes "dan (啖) — when you taste it" — dan means tasting (Shuowen: "dan: the action of opening the mouth to taste") — an action. The received text writes "dan (淡) — bland, flavorless" — a state.

One character's difference — silk manuscript's dan: when you go to taste the Dao, you find it has no flavor (preserves the ruler's active action of approaching the Dao). Received text's dan: the Dao is bland (only the object attribute of the Dao remains; the ruler's active action disappears). This commentary follows the silk manuscript — preserving the face-value moment of the ruler actively going to taste and approach the Dao and finding "no flavor."

The whole passage's contrast —

Music and food — the traveler can smell it (has flavor), see it (has form), hear it (has sound) — so the traveler stops.

The Dao — the ruler goes to taste (dan) and finds no flavor; goes to look and cannot see; goes to listen and cannot hear — so on the surface there are no conspicuous "benefits" to offer people.

This is the most critical contrast for those who govern — the Dao has no conspicuous "benefits" visible, but what the ruler must hold is precisely this Dao with no conspicuous benefits.

V. In Use It Cannot Be Exhausted

The chapter's final landing point, in its last four characters —

"In use it cannot be exhausted."

"Ji" (既) means finished, eaten up (Shuowen: "ji: a small meal finished" — the original meaning is finishing eating), extended to mean completed, exhausted. In using the Dao, you absolutely cannot exhaust it.

The chapter's contrast is nailed shut —

Music and food — have concrete form visible, but will be used up (music stops, food runs out) — so they can only make the passing traveler stop (briefly).

The Dao — no concrete form perceptible at the sensory level, but in use cannot be exhausted (inexhaustible in use) — so it can make the world flow back (continuously).

The final promise to those who govern — hold to the Dao, and there appear to be no conspicuous benefits to offer — but use it and it is inexhaustible. Music and food run out; the Dao does not run out. This is why music and food hold the passing traveler but not the world; the Dao holds the world.

This connects to ch. 4 "the Dao is empty, yet in use it may not be filled to the brim" and ch. 6 "continuous, as if it exists; in use it is not exhausted" — the Dao's use is inexhaustible. This is the Dao's true attractiveness in the world — not concrete benefits at the sensory level, but inexhaustibility at the level of use.

The received text writes "in use it is not sufficient to be exhausted" — "not sufficient" (不足). The silk manuscript writes "in use it cannot be exhausted" — "cannot" (不可), an absolute statement. This commentary follows the silk manuscript — the use is absolutely inexhaustible — preserving the absolute tone.

VI. What This Chapter Gives the Reader

What this chapter gives readers is a very concrete discernment —

What do you use to attract people?

If you use "music and food" — subsidies, discounts, gimmicks, high pay, perks, short-term stimulation — these have concrete form (visible benefits) but will run out. Those attracted by these are all passing travelers. Benefits stop; they go.

If you use "the Dao" — genuinely lasting value, genuine meaning, things that genuinely make people grow — these are invisible, intangible, indefinable (no flavor, not visible, not audible); on the surface there are no conspicuous benefits. But in use they cannot be exhausted. Those attracted by this are the world. They keep coming back; they don't leave because some benefit stopped.

Applied to your specific position —

Making products — use subsidies to attract users (music and food); subsidies stop, users leave. Use genuinely problem-solving value to keep users (the Dao); users stay for the long term.

Leading a team — use perks and high pay to keep people (music and food); a better offer comes and people go. Use genuine growth, genuine meaning, genuine respect to keep people (the Dao); people genuinely stay.

Creating content — use clickbait, gimmicks, trend-riding to attract attention (music and food); trends fade, attention goes. Use genuinely valuable content (the Dao); readers follow continuously.

Any relationship — maintain it through pleasing, flattering, giving benefits (music and food); benefits stop, the relationship cools. Maintain it through genuine understanding, genuine presence (the Dao); the relationship lasts.

Each is the same discernment — music and food are visible but run out; they hold passing travelers. The Dao is invisible but inexhaustible; it holds the world.

Not that you can't give benefits (music and food are not wrong in themselves; passing travelers coming is still coming). The point is to see clearly — those attracted by benefits leave when the benefits stop. What can make people genuinely keep coming back is that invisible, indefinable, but inexhaustible thing.

The choice Laozi gave those who govern 2,400 years ago is a choice everyone doing anything still faces today — do you want to hold passing travelers, or do you want the world to keep flowing back?

If it is the latter — hold to that thing with no flavor, invisible, inaudible, but inexhaustible in use. The world will naturally flow back.
