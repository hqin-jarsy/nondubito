第十七章
Chapter 17 of 81

四等

Four Grades of Leadership

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

一、一张分等表

老子第十七章给出了一张治理者的分等表。

四等。从最高到最低。

最高的——百姓知道他在，但感觉不到被管。
第二等——百姓爱戴他、称颂他。
第三等——百姓害怕他。
最低的——百姓反抗他、嘲笑他、不再服从。

这张表两千四百年前写下。今天读还是一份当下可用的诊断工具——

你的老板在哪一等？
你用的那个平台在哪一等？
你的政府在哪一等？

以及——

你自己呢？

你作为团队 lead、作为创始人、作为父母、作为内容创作者、作为任何处在"影响别人位置"上的人——

你自己在哪一等？

这一章是给所有处在"影响别人位置"上的人的镜子。

每一刻照一下。

二、太上：在位但不殖民

第一等。老子写——

"太上，下知有之。"

最高等的治理者，下面的百姓知道有他在。

这里有一处关键的字差。

帛书：下知有之。
通行本：不知有之。

一字之差。

按通行本读——百姓根本不知道治理者存在。治理者完全隐身。

按帛书读——百姓知道治理者在，但日常感受不到他对自己的殖民。

两个读法之间的差距很大。

通行本读法把"最高的治理者"读成一个隐士——从结构里消失了。

帛书读法把"最高的治理者"读成一个在位的涵育者——在那里托底（基础层），但不在百姓的涌现层立构（不殖民百姓的心、志、欲）。

百姓感觉不到被管——但不等于不知道他存在。

这是涵育和隐身的区别。

涵育者在。他在做托底的工作。让百姓有饭吃，有稳定的生活，有运行良好的环境。但他不立显眼的构去抓百姓的注意——他不每周开 all-hands 做激励演讲，不上播客做 thought leadership，不每件事都发表观点。

百姓知道有这个人在——背景式的知道。像知道家里有水有电有暖气在那里运行。

但百姓的注意力不围绕他组织——百姓在过自己的日子，做自己的事，活自己的命。

这是当代非常少见的领导风格。

最常见的"最好的 CEO"画像是反过来的——有 charisma，有 vision，上 Forbes 封面，每个员工每天都想到他。

老子说这其实是第二等。

老子的最高一等是——有一个人在那里把基础设施搭好、把系统维护好、把环境托住，员工每天专注做自己的事，知道有这个人在，但很少想到他。

什么样的领导属于这一等？

那种把团队建好之后让每个人自己运作，自己退到背景里的 founder。
那种孩子知道父母在那里托底，但孩子的生活注意力在自己身上的父母。
那种平台运作很顺，用户每天使用得很自然，但很少想到背后那个公司的产品负责人。

这一等的标志是——

被治理的人感受到的是自己在活，不是被别人安排在活。

三、亲誉之：被爱戴的代价

第二等。老子写——

"其次，亲誉之。"

百姓亲爱他、称颂他。

读到这一等，很多读者的第一反应是——

这不就是最高的领导吗？被员工爱戴、被百姓称颂——这不就是好领导吗？

老子说这已经是降级。

为什么？

因为要让百姓亲爱他、称颂他，治理者必须立起一些可被爱、可被称颂的构——做了值得称颂的事、立了值得爱戴的形象、积累了可被认可的政绩。

百姓的亲爱称颂是对治理者立的构的反应。

治理者有可被看见的构——百姓才能爱他。

而太上的治理者没有可被看见的构——他在基础层涵育，不立显眼的构让自己成为被爱被称颂的对象。

亲誉之这一等的关键区别——百姓的注意力到治理者身上。

注意力一到治理者身上，就从涵育滑到了有立构的治理关系。

这不是坏——比畏之好、比侮之好很多。

历史上和当代有大量优秀的"亲誉之"治理者——他们立了好的构（好的产品、好的政策、好的形象），百姓真的爱他们。

但这已经不是太上。

最关键的区别——

亲誉之的领导带走百姓的注意力。
太上的领导让百姓的注意力留在百姓自己身上。

当一个员工每天醒来想着"我的 CEO 真厉害"——他在亲誉之的位置上。
当一个员工每天醒来想着"我今天要做这件事"——他在太上的位置上（不管他的 CEO 自己怎么想）。

这种降级的诱惑很大——

因为亲誉之这一等的治理者感觉很好。被爱戴、被称颂、被引用、被尊重——立构者会从这种反应里得到大量满足感。

太上的治理者反而感觉不到这种满足——因为百姓的注意力不在他身上。他在背景里运作，几乎没人感谢他、爱他、称颂他。

走太上这一等的治理者必须对"被爱被称颂"完全不挂——这要求他自己不在身份位上守。

这就是为什么太上这一等历来很少。

不是没人有能力做。是大多数人没法忍受"我做了这么多但没人感谢我"那种结构性的孤独。

四、畏之和侮之

第三等。老子写——

"其次，畏之。"

百姓害怕他。

畏之的治理者用威来治。立刑法、立规矩、立权威——百姓怕他，所以守规矩。

这是用立构的方式强迫百姓配合。

畏之的治理者已经在殖民百姓的行动——百姓的行动不是从自己的涌现里出来的，是被恐惧推动的。

第四等。老子写——

"其下，侮之。"

百姓侮辱他。

治理者立的构被百姓识破、反抗、嘲笑、不服从。

这是治理崩坏的状态。

治理者还在立构、还在试图殖民，但百姓已经不买账。

侮之不是百姓的问题。是治理者立的构已经和百姓的生活脱节太远，百姓再也不能被骗、不能被压、不能被召唤。

帛书把第四等单独用"其下"标出——太上、其次、其次、其下——四等的高下清楚。

通行本三个"其次"连用——四等的高下感被磨平了。

这又是一个 patch 方向——

帛书的具体高下结构被磨成通行本的平铺四等。读者读通行本看不到清晰的下降梯度，只看到"四种治理者并列"。

按帛书读，四等是有方向的——从高到低，一步一步降。

五、立构越多越低

把四等放在一起看——

太上：在基础层托底，在涌现层不立构。百姓注意力在自己身上。
亲誉之：立可被爱的构。百姓注意力到治理者身上。
畏之：立威势的构。百姓行动被恐惧推动。
侮之：立的构脱离现实。治理崩坏。

四等之间的差别非常具体——立构的多少和方向。

太上几乎不立构——只在基础层托底。
亲誉之立了"可被爱的构"。
畏之立了"威势的构"。
侮之立的构已经脱离现实。

立构越多，等级越低。

这是这一章的脊梁。

读到这里你可能会注意到一件事——

这条结构原则其实在前面的章节里反复出现过。

第二章讲不立"美"的构（立美则甩丑）。
第三章讲不上贤（不立贤的构）。
第五章讲天地不仁（不启用仁的尺子）。
第十二章讲不在涌现层立构。

整本《道德经》在反复说同一件事——

立构是有代价的。

第十七章把这件事在治理关系上展开——治理者立构越多，和被治理者之间的关系越糟。

这不是道德判断。是结构事实。

六、贵言和谓我自然

太上的治理者具体怎么做？

老子接下来两句——

"猷呵，其贵言也。"

"猷呵"——深思熟虑啊。"贵言"——很珍贵地用言，少说话。

太上的治理者很少说话。

为什么？

因为一开口就是立构。

说话是把自己的想法立成一个可被别人接收的形式——这个形式一旦进入百姓的位，就是在百姓的涌现层上立了一个外来的构。

太上的治理者不殖民百姓的涌现层，所以他贵言——少立构。

这不是沉默。这是不让语言去做涵育该自己完成的工作。

接下来一句是太上治理者的最高表现——

"成功遂事，而百姓谓我自然。"

事情办成了。百姓说：我自己这样的。

注意这里——

"我自然"的"我"是百姓的我。

百姓说："我自己自然就是这样啊。"

百姓没有想"啊这是 CEO 安排的"、"啊这是政府帮的"、"啊这是我老板做的"。

他们想的是——"我自己在这样活着，本来就这样。"

治理者彻底让位让功——

不让功劳归于自己，让功劳归于百姓。

百姓感受到的是自己在活，自己在做事，自己在长，自己有这一切的功劳。

治理者在背景里运作，让百姓认为这一切都是自己的。

这是涵育的最高表现——

一个真正的涵育者，最后会让被涵育者忘了他存在——不是物理上忘了，是结构上不挂在他身上。

被涵育者会说："我自己长成这样的。"

涵育者听到这句话——这就是他工作做完的标志。

不是被感谢。
不是被纪念。
不是被引用。

是被涵育者认为是自己长出来的。

七、你在哪一等

回到开篇那张表。

四等不是道德评判。

不是说"太上是好人，侮之是坏人"。

四等是结构位置——治理者每一刻的立构动作决定他此刻在哪一等。

一个治理者可以在不同场景里处于不同的等级——对某件事他在太上位，对另一件事他可能滑到亲誉之位，对第三件事甚至滑到畏之位。

每一刻的动作都在重新定位。

所以读完这一章，回头看一遍——

你被治理的方向：

你的老板，对你的工作——在哪一等？
你的公司文化，对每个员工——在哪一等？
你用的那个平台，对用户——在哪一等？
你看的那个 newsletter，对读者——在哪一等？
你成长过程中你的父母，对你——在哪一等？

你治理的方向：

你做团队 lead 时，对你的下属——你在哪一等？
你做内容创作者时，对你的读者——你在哪一等？
你做父母时，对你的孩子——你在哪一等？
你做产品负责人时，对你的用户——你在哪一等？
你做朋友时——这件事其实也适用，对你身边的人——你在哪一等？

不需要每一个都达到太上才行——绝大部分人在绝大部分场景里都在亲誉之或畏之的位置上。这是事实。

但这张表的价值不是用来标签自己。

是用来校准方向——

下一个动作，你可以让自己往哪一等的方向上挪一格？

少说一句话——往太上方向挪一格。
不去争一次被认可的瞬间——往太上方向挪一格。
让对方自己得出结论而不是直接告诉他——往太上方向挪一格。
看见自己想立一个"被爱的构"，先停一下——往太上方向挪一格。

每一次挪动，都是一次涵育动作的具体落实。

不是一辈子达到太上才算数。

是每一次动作都可以朝那个方向走一步。

读老子最大的收获不是知识。

是当下手里多了一张校准方向的表。

四等是一张表。
两条 path 是一张表。
七个画像也是一张表。

读完每一章——

你带走一张可以在生活里用的表。

这是这本书今天还活着的原因。

一、A Grading Table

Chapter 17 of the Laozi gives a grading table for those who govern.

Four grades. From highest to lowest.

The highest — the people know they exist, but do not feel governed.
Second grade — the people love and praise them.
Third grade — the people fear them.
Lowest — the people defy, mock, and no longer obey them.

This table was written two thousand four hundred years ago. Reading it today it is still an immediately usable diagnostic tool —

What grade is your manager?
What grade is the platform you use?
What grade is your government?

And —

What grade are you?

You as team lead, as founder, as parent, as content creator, as anyone in a "position that influences others" —

What grade are you?

This chapter is a mirror for everyone in a position that influences others.

Hold it up at every moment.

二、The Highest: Present but Not Colonizing

First grade. Laozi writes —

"太上，下知有之。"

"The very highest: those below know they exist."

Here is a critical textual variant.

Silk manuscript: 下 (xià) know they exist — "those below know they exist."
Standard text: 不 (bù) know they exist — "the people do not know they exist."

One character different. Enormous difference.

Standard-text reading — the people don't even know the ruler exists. The ruler is completely invisible.

Silk-manuscript reading — the people know the ruler is there, but in daily experience do not feel themselves being governed.

The difference is large.

Standard-text reading turns the "highest ruler" into a hermit — structurally disappeared.

Silk-manuscript reading turns the "highest ruler" into a present-but-not-colonizing nurturer — there, doing foundational work (foundation layer), but not establishing constructs in the people's emergent layer (not colonizing the people's hearts, minds, desires).

The people know, in a background way, there is this person there. Like knowing there is water, electricity, heat at home, all running.

But the people's attention does not organize around this person — the people are living their own lives, doing their own things, operating their own destinies.

This leadership style is extremely rare today.

The most common image of the "best CEO" is actually the reverse — charismatic, visionary, on Forbes covers, every employee thinking about them every day.

Laozi says this is actually second grade.

Laozi's first grade — there is a person there who sets up the infrastructure, maintains the system, holds the environment stable; the staff focuses on doing their own work; they know this person exists, but rarely think about them.

What leader fits first grade?

The founder who builds a great team and then lets everyone operate themselves, withdrawing to the background.
The parent whose child knows they are there as a foundation, but whose life's attention is on the child's own things.
The product manager whose platform runs so smoothly that users use it naturally every day, but rarely think about the company behind it.

First grade's marker —

Those governed feel they are living their own lives, not being arranged by someone.

三、Loved and Praised: The Cost of Being Adored

Second grade. Laozi writes —

"其次，亲誉之。"

"Next: the people are close to them and praise them."

Reading second grade, many readers' first reaction is —

Isn't this the highest level of leadership? Loved and praised by the people — isn't this a great leader?

Laozi says this is already a step down.

Why?

Because to be loved and praised, the ruler must establish constructs that can be loved and praised — doing things worthy of praise, establishing an image worthy of love, accumulating achievements that can be recognized.

The people's love and praise is a response to constructs the ruler established.

The ruler has constructs that can be seen — the people can love them.

But the first-grade ruler has no constructs that can be seen — they do foundational nurturing, establishing no conspicuous constructs to make themselves an object to be loved and praised.

Second grade's critical distinction — the people's attention has moved to the ruler.

Once attention is on the ruler, the relationship has slipped from nurturing into a governed relationship with established constructs.

This is not bad — far better than fear, far better than contempt. History and the present are full of excellent "loved-and-praised" rulers — they established good constructs (good products, good policies, good images) and the people genuinely love them.

But this is already not first grade.

The most critical distinction —

The loved-and-praised leader takes the people's attention.
The first-grade leader leaves the people's attention with the people themselves.

When an employee wakes each morning thinking "my CEO is brilliant" — they are at the loved-and-praised position.
When an employee wakes each morning thinking "I need to do this thing today" — they are at the first-grade position (regardless of how their CEO thinks of themselves).

The temptation toward this downgrade is enormous —

Because the second-grade ruler feels good. Being loved, praised, cited, respected — the construct-establisher receives enormous satisfaction from this response.

First-grade rulers conversely cannot feel this satisfaction — because the people's attention is not on them. They operate in the background, almost no one thanking them, loving them, praising them.

Taking the first-grade path requires being completely unattached to "being loved and praised" — which requires the ruler themselves to not be guarding the identity position.

This is why first grade has been rare across history.

Not that no one has the ability. It is that most people cannot endure "I've done so much but no one thanks me" — that structurally imposed solitude.

四、Feared, and Scorned

Third grade. Laozi writes —

"其次，畏之。"

"Next: the people fear them."

The feared ruler governs by authority. Establishing punishments, rules, power — the people fear them and so follow the rules.

This is using the construction-of-constructs to force the people to cooperate.

The feared ruler has already colonized the people's actions — the people's actions are not emerging from their own emergence layer; they are driven by fear.

Fourth grade. Laozi writes —

"其下，侮之。"

"At the bottom: the people scorn them."

The constructs the ruler established have been seen through, resisted, mocked, no longer obeyed by the people.

This is governance in collapse.

The ruler is still establishing constructs, still trying to colonize — but the people no longer accept. The ruler's constructs are already so far from the people's lives that the people can no longer be deceived, can no longer be suppressed, can no longer be summoned.

Scorn is not the people's problem. It is that the constructs the ruler established have divorced from the people's actual life so completely that governance fails.

The silk manuscript marks the fourth grade with a distinct "其下" — the very highest, next, next, at the bottom — the four grades' hierarchy is clear.

The standard text uses three consecutive "其次" — the hierarchy's directional feel is flattened. Readers of the standard text see "four grades of ruler listed in parallel" rather than a clear downward gradient.

This is another patch direction — the silk manuscript's concrete hierarchy structure is flattened into the standard text's parallel listing.

Reading the silk manuscript, the four grades have direction — from high to low, step by step descending.

五、The More Constructs, the Lower the Grade

Placing all four grades side by side —

First grade: doing foundational-layer holding, establishing no constructs in the emergent layer. People's attention on themselves.
Second grade: establishes constructs that can be loved. People's attention moves to the ruler.
Third grade: establishes constructs of authority. People's actions driven by fear.
Lowest: constructs established are divorced from reality. Governance fails.

The difference between grades is very concrete — the quantity and direction of constructs established.

First grade almost establishes no constructs — only foundational-layer holding.
Second grade establishes "loveable constructs."
Third grade establishes "authoritative constructs."
Fourth grade has constructs already divorced from reality.

The more constructs established, the lower the grade.

This is this chapter's spine.

Reading to this point you may notice —

This structural principle has in fact appeared repeatedly in the preceding chapters.

Chapter 2 discusses not establishing the construct "beauty" (establish beauty and ugliness appears).
Chapter 3 discusses not elevating the worthy (not establishing the construct of the worthy).
Chapter 5 discusses heaven and earth being "not ren" (not activating the yardstick of ren).
Chapter 12 discusses not establishing constructs in the emergent layer.

The whole Daodejing is saying the same thing repeatedly —

Establishing constructs has a cost.

Chapter 17 unfolds this in the governance relationship — the more constructs the ruler establishes, the worse the relationship with those governed.

Not moral judgment. Structural fact.

六、Precious Words, and "We Did This Ourselves"

What does the first-grade ruler specifically do?

Laozi's next two lines —

"猷呵，其贵言也。"

"Considered, ah — how precious their words are."

The first-grade ruler speaks rarely.

Why?

Because opening one's mouth is establishing a construct.

Speaking is putting one's own thoughts into a form receivable by others — that form, once entering the people's positions, is establishing an external construct in the people's emergent layer.

The first-grade ruler does not colonize the people's emergent layer, so they value their words — speaking rarely.

This is not silence. It is not using language to do the work that nurturing should do by itself.

The next line is the first-grade ruler's highest expression —

"成功遂事，而百姓谓我自然。"

"Achievements accomplished, affairs completed — and the people say: we ourselves are naturally thus."

Notice —

The "我" (I/we) in "我自然" (we are naturally thus) is the people's "I."

The people say: "We ourselves are naturally like this."

The people are not thinking "ah, the CEO arranged this," "ah, the government helped," "ah, my manager did this."

They are thinking — "I am living like this myself, naturally this way."

The ruler completely yields position and credit —

Not letting the achievement belong to themselves; letting it belong to the people.

The people feel they are living their own lives, doing their own things, growing themselves, having all of this as their own achievement.

The ruler operates in the background, letting the people believe all of this is their own.

This is the highest expression of nurturing —

A genuine nurturer, in the end, makes those nurtured forget they exist — not physically forget, but structurally not-anchored-to-them.

The nurtured one says: "I grew into this myself."

The nurturer hearing this — this is the sign their work is done.

Not being thanked.
Not being commemorated.
Not being cited.

Being believed by the nurtured to have grown on their own.

七、Which Grade Are You

Return to the chapter's opening table.

Four grades are not moral verdicts.

Not "first grade is a good person, lowest grade is a bad person."

Four grades are structural positions — the ruler's construct-establishing actions at every moment determine which grade they are in at that moment.

A ruler can be in different grades in different scenarios — in one thing at first grade, in another slipped to second grade, in a third even slipped to third grade.

Every moment's action re-positions.

So reading through this chapter, look back once —

The direction you are governed:

Your manager, regarding your work — what grade?
Your company culture, regarding each employee — what grade?
The platform you use, regarding users — what grade?
The newsletter you read, regarding readers — what grade?
Your parents growing up, regarding you — what grade?

The direction you govern:

You as team lead, regarding your reports — what grade are you?
You as content creator, regarding your readers — what grade?
You as parent, regarding your child — what grade?
You as product manager, regarding your users — what grade?
You as friend — this applies too; regarding those around you — what grade?

No need for every instance to reach first grade — the vast majority of people in the vast majority of scenarios are at second or third grade. That is the reality.

But this table's value is not for labeling yourself.

It is for calibrating direction —

For the next action, which grade can you move yourself one notch toward?

Say one fewer sentence — one notch toward first grade.
Not contest one moment of being recognized — one notch toward first grade.
Let the other person reach their own conclusion rather than telling them directly — one notch toward first grade.
See yourself about to establish a "loveable construct," pause first — one notch toward first grade.

Each move is one concrete instance of a nurturing action.

Not having a lifetime of first grade that counts.

Each action can take one step in that direction.

Reading the Laozi, the greatest gain is not knowledge.

It is carrying in hand right now a table for calibrating direction.

The four grades are one table.
The two paths are one table.
The seven photographs are also one table.

Reading each chapter —

You take away one table you can use in life.

This is why this book is still alive today.
