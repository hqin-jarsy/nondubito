越管，越乱

When More Control Creates More Trouble

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

以正治邦，以奇用兵，以无事取天下。吾何以知其然哉？夫天下多忌讳而民弥贫，人多利器而国家滋昏，民多伎能而奇物滋起，法物滋彰而盗贼多有。是以圣人之言曰：我无为也而民自化，我好静而民自正，我无事而民自富，我欲无欲而民自朴。

一、那张越填越长的表

一个团队出过一次错，于是多了一张审批表。后来又出一次错，表上便多了两个签字。谁也说不清从哪一天开始，大家最熟悉的已经不是怎样把事情做好，而是怎样让每一格都填得挑不出毛病。

事情却没有因此变顺。等待多了，解释多了，真正碰到麻烦时，大家先问的也是“这算谁的责任”。本来为了减少错误而设的流程，慢慢长成了另一项需要全员照料的工作。

《道德经》第五十七章让管理者回头看一眼自己的手：你想解决的问题，有没有一部分正是这只手制造的？这不是说有规则就一定更糟，而是提醒我们，干预也有代价，不能因为它叫“管理”，就不再检查它造成了什么。

这一章最有力量的地方，是把视线从治理者的忙碌，移到普通人能不能好好生活、好好做事。你增加了多少要求，不是成绩本身；人在这些要求之下，究竟更有能力，还是更难动弹，才是该问的事。

二、秩序、奇招之外，还有一种本领

以正治邦，以奇用兵，以无事取天下。

治理邦国需要常规秩序，用兵讲究出其不意，而要取得天下，老子却提出“无事”。三句话放在一起，有一种突然放开手的感觉：最大的事情，未必靠不断增加动作来完成。

“取天下”首先处在古代政治的语境里。把它读成今天的赢得人心、维持长久合作，是我们由此展开的应用，不是说这三个字原本就只指办好一家公司。正、奇、无事之间也不必排成一张适用于所有处境的等级表。

秩序有它的价值。责任说清楚，程序可预期，合作才不必每天从猜心开始。奇招也可能解决一时的难题。问题出在，一个带头人若只剩这两种本领，便容易把每一次不顺都看成“还要加规定”，或“还要想办法摆布别人”。

“无事”提醒的是第三种本领：知道哪些事不必由自己发动，哪些安排应该停下来。不是把问题搁着不理，而是不为了显示自己在场，就让所有人跟着自己的焦虑转。这与第二十九章、第四十八章对强取和多事的警惕相通。

带一个团队，有时可以靠一次巧妙出击赢下项目；要让一群人愿意长期投入，却还需要稳定、公平、可信的环境。人不能始终活在你的下一次奇招里。真正可靠的领导，不只是会出手，也让别人知道，自己做事时不必时时防着那只手忽然伸进来。

三、规则为什么会反过来增加问题

老子问“吾何以知其然哉”，随后列出四组对照：忌讳越多，百姓越贫；利器越多，国家越昏乱；伎能越多，奇物越兴起；法物越彰显，盗贼越多。

“天下多忌讳而民弥贫”，很容易在生活里找到对应。若做一件正常的事也要穿过层层许可，人的时间、精力和收入就可能消耗在门槛上。限制本来是为了防错，却也可能堵住了谋生和合作的路。

“人多利器而国家滋昏”，则把问题指向越来越精巧的争夺手段。“利器”的具体所指有不同解释，王弼将它说成用来利己的器具。放到今天，可以想到一种处境：所有人都在升级赢过别人的工具，却没有人再问，大家共同要做的事情还在不在。

“民多伎能而奇物滋起”，不必读成对一切技术与手艺的否定。这里值得我们警惕的，是聪明被引向了什么。人若把最好的本领都用来制造噱头、讨好指标、绕开问责，花样越来越多，真正有用的东西却可能越来越少。

最后，“法物滋彰而盗贼多有”，把治理的反作用推到眼前。我们不能由此推出，写明禁止偷窃就会制造小偷，或越少法律社会就越好。更贴近生活的机制是：规则若繁密到难以遵守、奖惩又只看表面，人便可能把心思转向如何规避；掌握规则的人还可能把它当成寻租和刁难的工具。

公司里的员工学会把数字做漂亮，实际问题往后挪；平台把申诉的门关得过紧，用户只好反复换路；家里每一种兴趣都先遇到一句“不许”，孩子渐渐只会隐瞒。这些现象并不是所有禁令必然造成的，但足以提醒我们：不能只看规则的愿望，还要看规则把人推向了哪里。

保护人免受暴力、欺诈和剥削的规则，恰恰可能为人打开空间。老子这段话今天最值得带走的，不是一句“禁止必然制造被禁止之物”，而是一个需要反复检验的问题：你正在减少伤害，还是只让控制显得更周密？

四、把“自”还给别人

我无为也而民自化，我好静而民自正，我无事而民自富，我欲无欲而民自朴。

四句话里，最容易被忽略的不是“我”，而是那个反复出现的“自”。不是我替所有人长好，不是我替所有人端正，也不是我把一种理想人格灌进他们身体里。人有自己的生活，有在做事中学习、在相处中调整的能力。

“我无为而民自化”，可以从父母教孩子做一件小事开始理解。示范、保护、提供材料都重要，但若每一步都抢着代做，孩子始终只是站在旁边的人。退开一点，是让成长真正发生在他那里，而不是表演自己教得多好。

“我好静而民自正”，可以从团队里的安静理解。方向不天天变，犯错不立刻受羞辱，意见能够说出来，人就更有机会发现和改正问题。这样的安静并非不问结果，而是不靠不断惊动大家来维持秩序。

“我无事而民自富”，关心的是人的生活别被无谓的折腾耗空。平台把基础服务做好，让使用者不必把大量精力花在迎合它；组织少一点突然发动的形式主义，让真正的工作能够持续。这能改善谋生的条件，却不是说管理者一退场，贫困便会自动消失。

“我欲无欲而民自朴”，又把镜头转回带头的人。“欲无欲”可以读作不再带头煽动无穷的追逐：你自己天天用地位和排场区分高低，却要求下面的人不争不抢，很难让人信服。不让自己的贪求成为所有人的任务，是一种实在的节制，不是消灭一切愿望。

这四种退让都需要一个托底的环境。最好的管理者定清方向、给足条件，也愿意在有人受欺负时介入；父母给探索空间，也不撤走孩子需要的照料；平台开放生长，也要处理真正的伤害。第五十一章所说的生养而不占有，在这里成了治理的尺度：支持确实给到，人生却不由你接管。

五、不靠忙碌证明自己的“我”

前面自问时是“吾何以知其然哉”，后面圣人自述却连用四个“我”。这种叙述视角的转移，很适合让读者听见一个带头者怎样交代自己的位置。不过，不能只凭两个代词，就断定古文已经划分了两种不同的心理结构。

真正值得体会的，是这些“我”后面跟着什么。不是“我使他们服从”，不是“我让他们变成我想要的样子”，而是我少做什么，我约束自己什么，好让他们有机会做什么。

有些领导很难忍受别人不需要自己。一场会开得顺利，也要临时换个问题；一个方案已经成熟，也要留下几处自己的改动。不一定是恶意，有时只是怕：如果我没有动作，我的价值在哪里？

一个更稳的“我”，能够把自己的价值放在别处。看见团队成员已经可以独立作判断，不是失落，而是知道前面的支持没有白费。自己仍然负责，仍然有工作，但不必把所有人的依赖当成存在的证明。

这并不是把带头者变成没有需要的人。合理的报酬、认可和休息都可以要。只是这些需要不该通过让别人永远离不开自己来满足。能退开一步，往往不是因为无所谓，而是因为终于不必拿别人的无能来证明自己的重要。

六、下一次加规定之前

如果你是管理者、父母，或任何需要与人共同做事的人，这一章可以化成一次很具体的检查。

先看那些不断增加的要求。它防住了什么问题？让谁承担了成本？事情有没有真的改善，还是只是多了证明“我们管过”的材料？该保留的保留，该简化的简化，已经没有用途的允许它结束。减少多余的控制，也是一项需要负责地完成的工作。

再看你所谓的“放手”。你是否说明了目标，提供了资源，让人知道遇到困难可以找谁？若这些都没有，退后可能只是把风险转嫁给别人。真正的信任不是“反正你自己看着办”，而是给出支撑之后，不再替人把每一步走完。

最后看看自己在带什么头。你希望大家踏实，却是否只奖励最会抢镜的人？你希望大家诚实，却是否一听坏消息就发怒？你自己的反应，常常比墙上写的原则更能决定别人接下来怎么做。

回到那张越填越长的表。也许团队需要的不是再加一格，而是有人愿意问：我们为什么已经不敢直接把事情做好？把这个问题问清楚，可能比发出下一道指令更费心，也更有用。

“我无为而民自化”，不是管理者从此消失，而是他的在场不再挤满整个房间。他把必要的事做好，也把本来属于别人的空间还回去。于是，人终于不只是服从安排，而能够在自己的生活和工作里，逐渐长出本领。

I. One More Box to Tick

A team makes a mistake, so a new approval form is introduced. Another mistake adds two more signatures. Nobody quite remembers when the real expertise in the organization shifted from getting the work done to making sure every box was filled in beyond reproach.

The work has not become easier. There is more waiting and more explaining. When something goes wrong, the first question is whose responsibility it is. A procedure meant to prevent errors has gradually become another job that everyone must help maintain.

Chapter 57 of the Daodejing asks the person in charge to look back at their own hand. How much of the trouble they are trying to fix has that hand helped create? The point is not that rules necessarily make things worse. It is that intervention has costs, and calling it management does not exempt it from examination.

The chapter shifts attention from how busy the ruler appears to how people are able to live. More instructions are not an achievement in themselves. Have those instructions made people more capable, or merely made ordinary action harder? It is a question that can unsettle even a well-intentioned manager.

II. Beyond Order and Clever Moves

Govern a state through the regular; wage war through the unexpected; obtain the world through freedom from needless affairs.

The opening brings three approaches together. Government calls for order, warfare for surprise, but the largest undertaking receives the most unexpected recommendation: wushi, not generating affairs. Instead of adding another instrument of control, Laozi asks whether constant intervention is itself part of the difficulty.

“Obtaining the world” belongs first to an ancient political setting. Applying it to earning trust or sustaining a business is a modern extension, not the original phrase's exclusive meaning. Nor do the three approaches give us a universal ladder on which ordinary rules always rank below clever tactics and every form of restraint always wins.

Order matters. Clear responsibilities and predictable procedures spare people the work of guessing what others want. An ingenious move may also solve a particular problem. But a leader who has only those two habits can begin to treat every difficulty as a request for another regulation or another way of maneuvering people into position.

The third capacity is knowing what need not be initiated by you. It includes ending arrangements that no longer help and allowing others to work without continually being redirected. This connects with the warnings about grasping and multiplying intervention in Chapter 29 and Chapter 48. Not every pause is neglect. Sometimes it is the refusal to organize everyone else's day around your anxiety.

You may win a project with a clever maneuver. Sustaining a group of people who want to do good work together also requires reliability, fairness, and trust. They cannot live indefinitely inside your next brilliant move. A dependable leader lets them know not only when help will arrive, but when the hand above them will remain still.

III. What the Rules Teach People to Do

Laozi asks how he knows this and offers four contrasts: proliferating prohibitions accompany deeper poverty; sharper instruments accompany a more confused state; multiplying clever skills produce unusual contrivances; increasingly conspicuous regulations accompany more thieves.

The first contrast directs attention to the cost of restrictions. If an ordinary task requires a maze of permissions, time and income may be consumed at the gates. The restriction that was meant to prevent wrongdoing can also obstruct legitimate work and cooperation. We need to ask not only what a rule forbids but what it makes unnecessarily difficult.

The second turns toward the instruments of advantage. The exact scope of “sharp instruments” has received different interpretations; Wang Bi explains them as things used to advance one's own interests. In a contemporary setting, imagine everyone acquiring more elaborate ways to beat everyone else, while the shared purpose of the organization fades from view. The tools improve and the work deteriorates.

The third need not condemn craftsmanship, technology, or intelligence. It asks where ingenuity is being directed. People can devote considerable talent to making appearances impressive, satisfying metrics, or escaping accountability. An organization may become extremely inventive about everything except the problem it was supposed to solve.

The final contrast makes the unintended effects of governing hard to ignore. It does not establish that writing down a prohibition on theft creates thieves, or that fewer laws invariably produce a better society. A more useful mechanism to examine is what happens when rules become hard to follow while rewards depend on visible compliance. Evasion becomes attractive; people who control the rules may gain opportunities to obstruct others or extract favors.

An employee learns to improve the number and postpone the underlying problem. A platform makes an appeal so difficult that users keep searching for ways around it. In a family where every new interest first meets a prohibition, a child may learn concealment rather than judgment. These are possibilities to investigate, not outcomes of every restriction. They show why the intention behind a rule is not the same as what it teaches people to do.

Rules against violence, fraud, or exploitation can create space rather than close it. The chapter's challenge is therefore more demanding than a slogan against regulation. Are you reducing actual harm, or making control more elaborate? If the answer is uncomfortable, adding another rule will not necessarily make it better.

IV. Returning the Word “Themselves”

I practice non-coercive action, and the people transform themselves. I favor stillness, and the people set themselves right. I do not multiply affairs, and the people prosper through their own activity. I desire freedom from grasping desire, and the people return to simplicity.

The easily missed word in these four declarations is “themselves.” The ruler does not claim to manufacture everyone's development, install their character, or live their lives for them. People learn through doing, respond to one another, and acquire capacities that cannot simply be delivered from above.

A parent teaching a child an ordinary task can make the first declaration tangible. Demonstration, materials, and protection matter. But if the adult takes over each step, the child remains a spectator. Moving back allows the learning to happen in the child rather than turning the occasion into a demonstration of the adult's competence.

The second declaration becomes visible in a team with a steadier atmosphere. Its direction does not change every morning. A mistake does not immediately bring humiliation. Problems can be spoken about before they become disasters. This stillness is not indifference to results; it is the absence of constant disturbance used to enforce attention.

The third concerns lives that are not exhausted by unnecessary demands. A platform provides sound basic services instead of requiring its users to keep adapting to its whims. An organization reduces ceremonial tasks so that useful work can continue. Those changes may improve the conditions under which people make a living. Withdrawal alone does not guarantee prosperity, and poverty does not disappear because a manager has decided to intervene less.

The fourth brings the leader's desires back into view. “Desiring not to desire” need not mean extinguishing every wish. It can mean refusing to turn one's appetite for status, display, or endless expansion into everyone else's assignment. If you continually rank people by prestige and possessions, an instruction to stop competing will not carry much conviction.

These forms of restraint require support beneath them. A manager provides resources and intervenes when someone is being mistreated. A parent makes room for exploration without withdrawing necessary care. A platform permits growth while addressing genuine harm. The nonproprietary nurturing of Chapter 51 becomes a practical standard: the support is real, and the supported life still belongs to someone else.

V. An “I” That Does Not Need to Fill the Room

There is a shift in voice from Laozi's question to the sage's repeated declarations. The Chinese also moves between the pronouns wu and wo. That is a useful place to notice a change in perspective, but the pronouns alone do not prove that the text is distinguishing two psychological kinds of self.

What matters for this essay is what follows the repeated “I.” Not “I made them obey” or “I turned them into what I wanted,” but what I refrain from imposing so that they have room to act. Responsibility is still present. Its measure is no longer how thoroughly everyone depends on the person responsible.

Some leaders find it difficult to be unnecessary for a moment. A meeting is going well, so they introduce an urgent new question. A proposal is ready, so they make changes that will bear their signature. This need not come from malice. It may come from a fear: if I have not intervened, where is the evidence that I matter?

A steadier leader can locate value elsewhere. When a colleague develops independent judgment, the achievement is not a loss of authority but evidence that earlier support has done something worthwhile. There remains plenty to do without ensuring that every piece of work requires another appearance by the person at the top.

That person may still want fair pay, acknowledgment, and rest. The point is not to erase their needs. It is to stop meeting those needs by keeping other people permanently dependent. Being able to step back is sometimes the moment when another person's growing competence stops feeling like a threat.

VI. Before Adding the Next Requirement

For anyone responsible for working with others, the chapter suggests a concrete review. Begin with the requirements that keep accumulating. What problem does each one prevent? Who pays its cost? Has the situation improved, or do you merely have more material showing that management took action? Keep what protects, simplify what can be simplified, and allow obsolete demands to end.

Then examine what you call trust. Have you explained the purpose, provided the resources, and made it clear where help can be found? Without those things, stepping back may simply transfer risk to someone with less power. “You work it out” is not always an act of confidence. Support should arrive before freedom is announced as a substitute for it.

Finally, look at the example you set. You want people to work steadily, but do you reward whoever attracts the most attention? You want honesty, but do you become furious at bad news? Your reactions often tell people more about what is safe than the principles displayed on the wall.

Return to the expanding approval form. Perhaps the team does not need another box. Perhaps someone needs to ask why it has become so difficult to do the work directly and responsibly. Answering that may take more care than issuing a new instruction. It may also help more.

The sage's non-action does not mean that the responsible person disappears. It means their presence no longer occupies all the available space. They do what needs to be done and return what belongs to others. People can then become more than competent followers of an arrangement: they can gradually become more capable within lives and work of their own.
