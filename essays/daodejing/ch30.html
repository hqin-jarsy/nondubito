Chapter 30 of 81

树结果，不是抢果

Trees Bear Fruit; They Don't Seize It

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

以道佐人主，不以兵强于天下，其事好还。师之所居，楚棘生之，善者果而已矣，毋以取强焉。果而勿骄，果而勿矜，果而勿伐，果而不得已居，是谓果而不强。物壮则老，谓之不道，不道早已。

一、crush the competition

我在硅谷工作了快 20 年，常听到一套关于"强"的语言：

Crush the Competition
Dominate the Market
We're Going to Win
Move Fast and Break Things

这套语言把"做事"等同于"显得强"、"压倒对手"、"占据 dominance"。

但其实硅谷还有另外一套语言，是关于做成什么：

Making Great Products
Making a Dent in the Universe
Simplicity
Anticipating Demands

我更想问的是：事情做成之后，我们用它做什么？解决了一个问题，还是从此需要它不断证明“我很强”？

第三十章的“果”给了我一个好用的形象。果实成熟，事情完成。字义与果实的联系可以帮助体会这个“完成”，却不意味着战争像果树结果一样自然、必然或正当。

果子熟了，摘下来。不是在树旁竖起一块牌子，从此要求所有人承认这棵树属于你的功劳。把这个比喻带回工作，它问的不是能不能庆祝，而是完成之后，你还肯不肯停手。

二、不以兵强于天下

第三十章开头——

"以道佐人主，不以兵强于天下，其事好还。"

“不以兵强于天下”先是一条对武力的限制。不能只把它读成“不要炫耀武力”：不炫耀的征服仍可能是征服，安静的压迫也仍是压迫。

“其事好还”提醒人注意暴力的回返：报复、军备竞逐、受创的社会和下一代要承担的代价。但这不是宇宙替人执行的一条公正报应律。伤害未必回到最初下令者身上，常常先落到无辜者身上。

正因为代价会落到别人身上，掌权的人更不能拿“我承担后果”一句话替所有人签字。

三、军队走过，荆棘长出

接下来一句非常具体——

"师之所居，楚棘生之。"

军队驻扎之处，荆棘生长。这个画面让胜利的叙事停在土地上：战争扰乱耕作，人离开田地，荒草灌木接管曾经有人照料的地方。

不必再替这句话编出“土壤被踩实，所以几年只能长荆棘”的植物学定律。它真正让人看见的是那些没有写进捷报的事：谁没有回来种地，谁的家已经空了，战争结束后谁还在付账。

放到今天看——一场惨烈的商战之后，留下的是一地"荆棘"——烧光的现金，透支的团队，破坏的行业生态，被消耗的信任。

"crush the competition"听起来很爽，但 crush 完之后，师之所居，楚棘生之——你站的地方，长满了荆棘。胜利的代价是一片荒地。

四、结了果就停

接下来是这一章的核心——

"善者果而已矣，毋以取强焉。"

“果而已”可以读得很朴素：完成必要的事，不借着完成再扩大强制。它并不禁止解释、复盘或追责；尤其涉及武力，越不能用“事情结束了”来阻止别人追问。

工作也是如此。上线之后要查问题、听投诉、修漏洞。停下来的，是把成果不断变成自我扩张的理由，不是把善后工作也丢下。

后面五个“果而”是一连串告诫。不必把毋、勿、不、弗排成一架到处通用的强度阶梯，才能听见这些句子的分量。

五、五个"果而"

接下来五句是这一章的核心展开——

"果而勿骄，果而勿矜，果而勿伐，果而不得已居，是谓果而不强。"

把这五句放到完成之后的那个瞬间，读起来就很具体。

“勿骄”：别因为赢了，就觉得自己可以不再听人说话。“勿矜”：不必把成果抱在怀里，变成永远正确的凭证。“勿伐”：不必让每次谈话都回到你的功劳上。这是对行为的体会，不靠拼接几个字源来证明一套心理学。

“不得已”尤其需要小心。每一个动用强制的人，都可能说自己别无选择。但他有没有认真考虑别的路？代价是否相称？谁有权质疑，什么时候停止？不得已是一项需要说明的判断，不是免于说明的通行证。

最后，“不强”把问题拉回行动本身：即使已经取得结果，也不能再以结果为理由继续压人。不是只把姿态放低，而是真正给强制设下边界。

六、物壮则老

整章的收束——

"物壮则老，谓之不道，不道早已。"

“物壮则老”把强盛放回时间里。今天有力，不等于可以无限加码；一项已经完成的事，也不必靠继续扩张来证明自己。

但不能倒过来把衰老看成“不道”的罪证。身体会老，生命会受伤，这不是人的道德失败。企业衰落、制度失灵也各有具体原因，不能拿一句话包办所有解释。

本章的警告更适合留给仍有选择的人：已经到了该收手的地方，还要不要用强？它与第九章“不若其已”相照，提醒我们承认完成，而不是责备脆弱。

七、做强，不立强位

这一章给读者的工具非常具体——

做事像树结果。把事情做成（果），但不立"我强"的招牌（不强）。

放到 2026 年的竞争语境——

做产品做到极致（果），但不需要 crush 对手（不立强位）。一家公司可以把产品做到行业最好，但不需要把"我们要 dominate"立成招牌。做强（产品实际好）和立强位（显示自己 dominate）是两件事。

做事做到位（果），但不需要在每个场合显示自己赢了（不立强位）。一个人可以在工作上做到很高水平，但不需要在每次会议、每次互动里显示"我比你强"。

带团队带出成果（果），但不需要把成果当作自己的强位招牌（不立强位）。一个 leader 可以带出很强的团队，但不需要把团队的成果当作"看我多能带"的招牌。

每一个都是同一件事——做强 ≠ 立强位。做强是实际把事情做成（果）。立强位是显示自己强、压别人（不道）。

做成了，可以高兴，可以让做事的人得到应有的报酬和承认。非要把所有功劳抹掉，也可能是在抹掉别人的劳动。

区别在下一步：这份成果是让问题告一段落，还是让你从此有权要求更多服从？一个好产品不需要替创始人的全部人生作证，一次成功也不需要把失败过的人变成反面教材。

果而已。把事情做好，把后果照看好。至于那块证明自己很强的牌子，未必还要竖起来。

I. Crush the Competition

I've worked in Silicon Valley for nearly 20 years. I hear one language about "strength" constantly:

Crush the Competition.
Dominate the Market.
We're Going to Win.
Move Fast and Break Things.

This language equates "doing things" with "appearing strong," "overwhelming rivals," "occupying dominance."

But Silicon Valley also has another language — about making things:

Making Great Products.
Making a Dent in the Universe.
Simplicity.
Anticipating Demands.

My question is what we do with a completed task. Have we solved a problem, or acquired something that must keep proving how strong we are?

The chapter’s 果 offers a useful image: fruit ripens, a task reaches completion. The association helps us imagine completion; it does not make warfare as natural, inevitable or legitimate as a tree bearing fruit.

Pick the ripe fruit. Do not erect a sign demanding that everyone acknowledge your glory forever. Brought back to work, the metaphor does not forbid celebration. It asks whether you can stop once the task is done.

II. Do Not Impose Strength Through Arms

Chapter 30 opens —

"One who assists a ruler with the Dao does not impose strength upon the world through arms. Such affairs tend to rebound."

“Do not use arms to dominate the world” first limits actual force. It cannot mean only “do not show it off”: quiet conquest remains conquest, and discreet oppression remains oppression.

“Such affairs tend to return” warns of retaliation, arms races, damaged societies and costs passed to the next generation. It is not a cosmic law distributing just deserts. Harm may never reach the person who gave the order; it often reaches innocent people first.

Precisely because others bear the cost, a ruler cannot sign for everyone by saying, “I accept the consequences.”

III. Where Armies Pass, Thorns Grow

The next line is very concrete —

"Where armies have camped, brambles and thorns spring up."

Thorns grow where armies camp. The image brings the story of victory down to the land: warfare interrupts cultivation, people leave their fields, scrub takes over places once tended.

It needs no invented botanical rule that compacted soil can grow only thorns for years. Look instead at what the victory bulletin leaves out: who never returned to sow, whose home is empty, who is still paying after the war has ended.

Looking today — after a brutal commercial battle, what remains is a field of "brambles": burned cash, exhausted teams, destroyed industry ecosystems, consumed trust.

"Crush the competition" sounds satisfying. But after the crushing, where armies have camped, brambles spring up — the ground where you stand is thick with thorns. Victory's price is a wasteland.

IV. Bear Fruit and Stop

Now comes the chapter's core —

"The skilled person bears fruit and that is all. Does not use it to seize the position of strength."

“Achieve the result and stop” can be read plainly: finish what is necessary without using completion to extend coercion. It forbids neither explanation nor review nor accountability. Especially after force has been used, “it is over” cannot silence questions.

At work too, release is followed by investigation, complaints and repairs. What stops is the conversion of results into reasons for self-expansion, not the responsibility to put things right.

The five phrases beginning 果而 that follow are admonitions. They need no universal strength ranking of Chinese negatives to carry weight.

V. Five "Bears Fruit and"

The next five lines are the chapter's central unfolding —

"Bears fruit and is not arrogant. Bears fruit and does not boast inwardly. Bears fruit and does not display outwardly. Bears fruit and finds himself in this position only because he had no other choice. This is called bearing fruit without imposing strength."

Put the five lines at the moment after completion and they become practical.

Do not grow arrogant: winning does not entitle you to stop listening. Do not cling to merit: an achievement is no permanent certificate of being right. Do not boast: every conversation need not return to your contribution. These are readings of conduct, not a psychology proved by stitching together word origins.

“Having no alternative” needs particular care. Anyone using force can claim necessity. Were other routes seriously considered? Is the cost proportionate? Who may challenge the decision, and when does it stop? Necessity is a judgment requiring explanation, not an exemption from explaining.

The final warning against force returns to conduct itself. A result does not license further domination. The point is not merely a modest pose, but an actual limit on coercion.

VI. Things in Their Prime Age

The chapter's closure —

"Things in their prime begin to age. This is called not following the Dao. What does not follow the Dao ends early."

“Things grow strong and then old” places strength back within time. Present power does not license endless escalation; a finished task need not keep expanding to prove itself.

But aging is not evidence of moral failure. Bodies age and lives are wounded. Nor can one sentence explain every business decline or institutional breakdown; each has particular causes.

The warning is better addressed to those who still have a choice: having reached a stopping point, will you press on by force? It echoes chapter 9’s “better to stop,” asking us to acknowledge completion, not blame vulnerability.

VII. Getting Strong, Without Staking a Position of Strength

What this chapter gives the reader is very concrete —

Do things like a tree bears fruit. Make things happen (fruit), but don't plant the sign "I am strong" (not strength).

In the competitive context of 2026 —

Make the best product (fruit) — without needing to crush rivals (not staking strength). A company can make the best product in the industry without making "we dominate" into a sign. Getting strong (product is actually good) and staking a position of strength (appearing dominant) are two things.

Do the work fully (fruit) — without needing to display victory in every context (not staking strength). A person can reach a very high level of work without needing to signal "I am stronger than you" in every meeting, every interaction.

Lead a team to results (fruit) — without making the results into a sign of personal strength (not staking strength). A leader can build a strong team without making the team's results into "look how well I lead."

Each is the same thing — getting strong ≠ staking a position of strength. Getting strong is actually making things happen (fruit). Staking a position of strength is displaying strength, overwhelming others (not following the Dao).

You may be glad when something works. Those who did the work deserve pay and recognition; erasing all credit can erase other people’s labor too.

The distinction comes next: does the result bring a problem to a close, or entitle you to demand more obedience? A good product need not testify for its founder’s entire life. A success need not turn everyone who failed into a cautionary tale.

Achieve the result and stop. Do the work well; care for its consequences. The sign declaring how powerful you are may not need to go up at all.
