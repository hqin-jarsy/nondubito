第十四章
Chapter 14 of 81

执今之道

Grasp the Dao of Now

秦汉（字大知）· Han Qin

一、执今还是执古

打开任何一本《道德经》注本，翻到第十四章最后那一段，你会看到——

"执古之道，以御今之有。"

握住古代的道，来运作今天的事。

读法是：道在古代，藏在经书里，今天的任务是回望去找。

这是两千年来这一章按通行本的读法。

但帛书写的不是"执古"。

帛书写的是"执今"——

"执今之道，以御今之有。"

握住今天的道，来运作今天的事。

一字之差，方向反了。

不是回望，是当下。
不是还原一成不变的古道，是握住此刻正在运作的道。

这是反传统的——

道不在古代。
道在今天。
每一刻都在运作。
每一刻都是新的。

但要读懂这个命题，得先从这一章前面三段铺垫读起。

二、三个感官都抓不到

第十四章一上来三句——

"视之而弗见，名之曰微。
听之而弗闻，名之曰希。
搏之而弗得，名之曰夷。"

老子说：你用眼睛看它，看不到。你用耳朵听它，听不到。你用手摸它，摸不到。

三个感官，三个否定。

为什么是这三个感官？

因为这是人抓取具体物的三个最基本方向。你认识一个东西，总是通过它在某个感官上留下的响应——它有颜色（视觉），它有声音（听觉），它有形状（触觉）。这三者是把世界切成"具体物"的三套刀。

老子说，这三套刀在道身上都切不下来——视觉切不到色，听觉切不到音，触觉切不到形。

道不在任何一个感官切片里。

这件事在 2026 年有一个非常贴的当代场景——

机器学习工程师每天都在面对一件事：

你训练一个模型，它能捕获很多 feature。模型在 training data 上学会了识别 X、识别 Y、识别 Z。

但总有一些信号是模型抓不到的——它们没在训练数据里露过面，或者它们在 feature space 之外的维度。

这些信号还在 data 里。data 比 model 大。永远有"模型抓不到但确实存在"的部分。

模型抓不到，不是说那些信号不存在。是模型的工具碰不到它们。

老子说的"三感官抓不到道"是同一个结构——

感官是抓取具体物的工具。但世界比工具大。永远有一部分"工具抓不到但确实在"的部分。

这部分就是道。

三、抓不到不等于没有

老子接下来一句——

"三者不可致诘，故混而为一。"

致诘 = 追问到底。

不能分别问"为什么视之不见、为什么听之不闻、为什么搏之不得"——因为这三个否定指向的不是三件事。

它们合起来指向同一个位。

这个位是什么？

这正是道的位置。

每个感官方向上——感官对道的响应趋近于零。视觉响应→0，听觉响应→0，触觉响应→0。

但三个"趋近于零"合起来不是等于零——

反而从这三个方向都抓不到这件事里，浮出了一个"在"的位。

这里要把一个误读挡住——

抓不到不等于没有。

这是这一章最要拎清的一件事。

感官上没有响应（没有可抓的具体物）不等于不存在。

道在那里——只是不以"可感官抓取的具体物"的形式在。

回到机器学习的类比——模型 underfit 的时候，loss function 是非零的。这个非零的部分代表"模型没捕获的信号"。

模型抓不到，不是说那些信号不存在。是它们以"模型可以表征"的形式不存在。但它们确实在 data 里。

老子在第十四章这里立的是同一件事——道不是没有，道是以"感官可抓取的具体物"的形式不存在。

不存在的是"以可感官抓取的形式存在"这件事，不是存在本身。

四、无状之状

老子接下来命名这个浮出来的位——

"一者，其上不谬，其下不惚，寻寻呵，不可名也，复归于无物。"

其上不谬，其下不惚——上面不亮，下面不暗。

既不是光明的神圣物，也不是黑暗的虚无。两种极端的描述都不适用。

寻寻呵——绵绵不断。

读到这里要补一笔。"呵"这个字——

清代音韵学家孔广森提出过一个推测：《诗经》里的"兮"字本来应该读"呵"。1973 年马王堆帛书出土后证实了这个推测——通行本里所有的"X 兮"，在帛书里都原本写作"X 呵"。

呵是先秦汉语里这个语气词的本字。读"啊"，不读"希"。

这件事很重要——它让老子的口吻回到口语感。

老子不是在写一本文绉绉的经书。他是在指着一件事向你感叹——

"寻寻啊，不可名也。"

——绵绵不断啊，没法给它取名字。

这是老人对着一个东西指着说话的口吻，不是经文宣读。两千年来通行本把"呵"字改成"兮"再读成"希"，老子的口吻被文绉化了一层。

继续看命名——

"是谓无状之状，无物之象。"

这两句是这一章最精妙的两个命名。

无状之状——没有状态的状态。

注意这不是"无状"（没有状态），是"无状之状"——

"没有任何具体状态"本身是一种状态。

无物之象——没有物的形象。

同理不是"无象"，是"没有具体物的那种形象"——

"没有任何具体物"本身是一种形象。

这两个命名在做一件精确的事——

把"彻底否定之后还剩下的那个位"命名出来。

道在感官测度上趋近于零——视觉响应→0，听觉响应→0，触觉响应→0，具体物形式→0，具体状态→0。

一切具体化的测度都趋零。

但是——

所有测度趋零的那个极限本身，还是一个位。

这个位不是零——它是"所有具体测度都趋零但整体并不为零"的那个极限状态。

"是谓沕望。"

沕望——恍惚，仿佛有又仿佛无。

注意：沕望不是模糊。

模糊是抓不清。
沕望是精确描述"抓不住但在"这种结构状态。

模糊是工具的局限。
沕望是被抓取对象本身的位置。

道不模糊。道很清楚——它就在"测度趋零但位存在"的那个精确位置上。

五、随迎首尾

接下来一句——

"随而不见其后，迎而不见其首。"

你跟着它走——看不到它的尾。你迎着它来——看不到它的头。

这两句是对道的时间运作的描述——

道不只是"当下静态"无状无物。它在时间上也无首无尾。

你从后面追它，追不到它的终点。你从前面迎它，找不到它的起点。

道一直在动，但没有一刻可以说"这是开头"，也没有一刻可以说"这是结束"。

这是因为——道不是一件被生产出来的东西。

它不是从某个时刻开始的，也不会在某个时刻结束。

它是凿构循环每一刻都涌出来的余项。没有"第一次涌出"，也没有"最后一次涌出"——只要凿构循环还在跑，余就还在涌。

六、道在今天

铺到这里，可以读最后一段——

"执今之道，以御今之有，以知古始，是谓道纪。"

握住今天的道，来运作今天的具体事物，由此可以知道古代的起始。这就是道的节律。

"今"字极其关键。

道不是古代定下来藏在经书里等我们去抓的古物——

道是今天在运作的。

每一刻都在运作，每一刻都是新的。

这跟前面三段是一脉相承的——

第二、三段建立：道在感官测度的极限之外，但位存在。
第四段命名：那个位是"无状之状、无物之象"。
第五段描述时间运作：随迎无首尾，道一直在动但无起止。

第六段收束：既然道无起止、一直在动——那它就不是某个古代时刻一次性放下的古物，是每一刻都在涌出的运作。

握的不是那个被放下的古物。
握的是此刻正在涌出的运作。

执今之道。

但还有一个问题——

老子说"执今之道，以知古始"。

握的是今天的道，怎么能反过来知道古代的起始？

这看起来矛盾。

但其实不矛盾——

今天的道里不包含古代的具体内容，但包含"道永不灭、每一刻新涌出"这件事本身。

今天的余是今天的，古代的余是古代的——具体内容不一样。

但"余永不灭"这件事在古今是一样的。

今天能看清道是怎么运作的人，也就能明白古代的起始——因为古代的起始和今天一样，都是这个"余永不灭、每一刻新涌出"的结构。

不需要回到古代去找古道。

今天的道里就包含着古代道的运作方式——因为它们结构一样。

"是谓道纪。"

这就是道的节律。

道一直在运作（纪），但没有首没有尾（每一刻都是新的涌出）。

整章的推理链到这里收住：

从感官否定 → 浮出位 → 命名位 → 描述位的时间运作 → 推出执今 → 反推古始 → 道纪。

每一句都服务于最后一句。

七、为什么是今不是古

最后回到开篇那个字差——执今 vs 执古。

帛书"执今"和通行本"执古"，字面差一字。

但按帛书读，整章是一条完整的推理链——从三感官否定一路推到道纪。每一句都有它的位置，整章成章。

按通行本读"执古之道"，前面那一整套结构追问失去了服务对象——前面做了一整章的复杂推导（三感官否定、无状之状、随迎首尾），最后只是"抓住古道就好"。

前面推导的目的是什么？

按通行本读，前面和后面接不上——老子先做一大堆复杂的结构追问，然后突然跳到"抓住古道"。章不成章。

按帛书读——章成章。前面所有结构追问都在为最后的"执今能知古始"服务。

帛书让这一章成为一件完整的结构工作。

通行本让这一章变成一段神秘主义的颂歌加一句保守主义的结论。

这是 Paper 2 总观察的又一次演示——通行本的字差不是恶意，每一处都不是。但每一处的方向同向，加起来让老子的精确结构追问，被磨成了对古代的回望式赞颂。

按帛书读，老子要说的事很清楚——

道不在古代经典里等你去找。

道在今天每一刻的运作里。

握住此刻正在涌出的余。
用它来运作此刻的事。
由此就能明白古代的起始——

因为这件事古今一样。

每一刻都是新的。

一、Grasp Now or Grasp the Past

Open any commentary on the Daodejing and flip to Chapter 14's final passage. You will read —

"执古之道，以御今之有。"

"Grasp the ancient dao, to govern the things of today."

The reading: the dao resides in antiquity, hidden in the classics, and today's task is to look back and find it.

This has been the standard reading for two thousand years.

But the silk manuscript does not say "grasp the ancient" (执古).

The silk manuscript says "grasp today's" (执今) —

"执今之道，以御今之有。"

"Grasp the dao of today, to govern the things of today."

One character different, direction reversed.

Not looking back — looking at the present.
Not restoring an unchanging ancient dao — grasping the dao that operates at this very moment.

This is counter-traditional —

The dao is not in antiquity.
The dao is in today.
Operating at every moment.
Every moment new.

But to understand this claim, you need to read the chapter's earlier passages first.

二、All Three Senses Come Up Empty

Chapter 14 opens with three lines —

"视之而弗见，名之曰微。
听之而弗闻，名之曰希。
搏之而弗得，名之曰夷。"

"Look at it and you do not see it — named: subtle. Listen to it and you do not hear it — named: rare. Reach for it and you do not grasp it — named: level."

Laozi says: look at it with your eyes — cannot see. Listen with your ears — cannot hear. Touch with your hands — cannot grasp.

Three senses, three negations.

Why these three senses?

Because these are the three most basic directions in which people grasp concrete things. You know something through the responses it leaves on your senses — it has a color (sight), a sound (hearing), a shape (touch). These three are the three knives that cut the world into "concrete things."

Laozi says: these three knives find no purchase on the dao — sight finds no color, hearing finds no sound, touch finds no form.

The dao is not in any sensory slice.

In 2026 there is a very close contemporary analogue —

Machine learning engineers face this every day:

You train a model and it captures many features. The model learned to recognize X, Y, Z in the training data.

But there are always signals the model cannot capture — they never appeared in the training data, or they exist in dimensions outside the feature space.

These signals are still in the data. Data is larger than model. There is always a portion "the model cannot grasp but genuinely exists."

The model cannot grasp it — this does not mean those signals do not exist. It means the model's tools cannot reach them.

Laozi's "three senses cannot grasp the dao" has the same structure —

The senses are tools for grasping concrete things. But the world is larger than the tools. There is always a portion "tools cannot reach but genuinely present."

This portion is the dao.

三、Ungrasped Does Not Mean Absent

Laozi's next line —

"三者不可致诘，故混而为一。"

"The three cannot be interrogated to exhaustion; therefore they are blended as one."

Cannot separately ask "why does sight not see, why does hearing not hear, why does touch not grasp" — because these three negations do not point to three things.

Together they point to the same position.

What position?

This is precisely the dao's position.

In each sensory direction — the sense's response to the dao tends toward zero. Visual response → 0, auditory response → 0, tactile response → 0.

But three "tending toward zero" together do not equal zero —

From these three directions all failing to grasp the thing, a position of "being" floats up.

The crucial misreading to block here —

Ungrasped does not mean absent.

No sensory response (no graspable concrete thing) does not mean non-existence.

The dao is there — just not present in the form of "a graspable concrete thing."

Back to the machine learning analogy — when the model underfits, the loss function is non-zero. That non-zero portion represents "signals the model didn't capture."

The model cannot grasp them does not mean those signals do not exist. They exist in the data but not in a form the model can represent.

What Laozi establishes here is the same thing — the dao is not absent; it simply does not exist in the form of "a sensorially graspable concrete thing."

What does not exist is "the form of existing-as-sensorially-graspable." Existence itself does not not-exist.

四、The Formless Form

Laozi then names this emergent position —

"一者，其上不谬，其下不惚，寻寻呵，不可名也，复归于无物。"

"This one — its top is not bright, its bottom is not dark; threading on and on, ah, it cannot be named; it returns to the realm of no-thing."

Not bright at the top, not dark below — neither the extreme of luminous divinity nor the extreme of dark nothingness. Neither extremity of description applies.

Threading on and on (寻寻呵) — continuous, unbroken.

A note here. The character 呵 —

The Qing dynasty phonologist Kong Guangsen proposed that the character 兮 in the Book of Songs was originally read as 呵. The 1973 excavation of the Mawangdui silk manuscripts confirmed this: everywhere the standard text has "X 兮," the silk manuscript originally writes "X 呵."

呵 is the original character for this modal particle in pre-Qin Chinese. It reads like "ah," not "xi."

This matters — it restores Laozi's register to colloquial. He is not reciting a scripture. He is pointing at something and exclaiming to you —

"Threading on and on, ah — it cannot be named."

The tone of an elder pointing at something and marveling, not of a canon being intoned. For two thousand years the standard text's "兮" read as "xi" has formalized Laozi's register by one layer.

Continuing the naming —

"是谓无状之状，无物之象。"

"This is called the form of no-form, the image of no-thing."

These two lines are the most precise naming in the chapter.

The form of no-form — not "formless" (no form), but "the form of no-form" —

"Having no particular form" is itself a form.

The image of no-thing — similarly, not "imageless," but "the image of no-thing" —

"Having no particular thing" is itself an image.

What these two namings do precisely —

They name the position that remains after total negation.

The dao's sensory measurements all tend toward zero — visual response → 0, auditory response → 0, tactile response → 0, particular-form → 0, particular-state → 0.

All particular measurements tend toward zero.

Yet —

The limit itself to which all measurements tend zero is still a position.

This position is not zero — it is the limit state in which "all particular measurements tend toward zero yet the whole is not zero."

"是谓沕望。"

"This is called wu-mang."

Wu-mang — elusive, as-if-present-yet-absent, indistinct.

Note: wu-mang is not vagueness.

Vague means unclear in grasp. Wu-mang precisely describes the structural state of "ungrasped but present."

Vague is a limitation of the tool. Wu-mang is the position of the object being grasped.

The dao is not vague. The dao is very clear — it is at precisely the position of "measurements tend toward zero but the position exists."

五、Following and Meeting, Head and Tail

Next line —

"随而不见其后，迎而不见其首。"

"Follow it and you cannot see its rear; face it and you cannot see its head."

These two lines describe the dao's temporal operation —

The dao is not only "statically formless and thing-less" in the present moment. In time it also has no head and no tail.

Follow it from behind — cannot reach its end. Face it in front — cannot find its beginning.

The dao is always moving, but there is no moment you can call "this is the beginning" and no moment you can call "this is the end."

This is because — the dao is not something produced.

It did not begin at some moment, and it will not end at some moment.

It is the remainder that the chisel-construct cycle yields at every moment. There is no "first yielding" and no "last yielding" — as long as the chisel-construct cycle runs, the remainder keeps yielding.

六、The Dao Is in Today

With this foundation, read the final passage —

"执今之道，以御今之有，以知古始，是谓道纪。"

"Grasp the dao of today, to govern the concrete things of today, and thereby know the beginning of antiquity. This is called the thread of the dao."

The character 今 — today, now — is crucial.

The dao is not an ancient thing set down in the past, waiting in the classics for us to retrieve it —

The dao operates today.

Operating at every moment. Every moment new.

This follows continuously from the earlier passages —

Passages two and three establish: the dao is at the limit beyond sensory measurement, but the position exists.
Passage four names: that position is "the form of no-form, the image of no-thing."
Passage five describes the temporal operation: no head, no tail; the dao is always moving but has no beginning or end.

Passage six concludes: since the dao has no beginning or end, is always moving — it is not an ancient thing set down once and for all; it is the operation yielding anew at every moment.

What you grasp is not that set-down ancient thing.
What you grasp is the operation yielding at this moment.

Grasp the dao of today.

But one question remains —

Laozi says "grasp the dao of today, and thereby know the beginning of antiquity."

You are grasping the dao of today — how does that let you know antiquity's beginning?

This looks contradictory.

But it is not —

The dao of today does not contain the specific content of antiquity, but it contains "the dao yields without ceasing, new at every moment" as such.

Today's remainder is today's; antiquity's remainder was antiquity's — the specific content differs.

But "the remainder yields without ceasing" is the same in antiquity and today.

The person who can see clearly today how the dao operates can also understand antiquity's beginning — because antiquity's beginning and today are the same: the structure of "the remainder yields without ceasing, new at every moment."

No need to return to antiquity to find the ancient dao.

The dao of today contains the operating mode of the ancient dao — because they share the same structure.

"是谓道纪。"

"This is called the thread of the dao."

The dao operates always (纪 — thread, discipline), but has no head, no tail — every moment is a new yielding.

The full chain of reasoning closes here:

Sensory negation → position floats up → name the position → describe the temporal operation → conclude: grasp today → know antiquity → the thread of the dao.

Every line serves the final line.

七、Why Now and Not the Past

Return to the opening textual variant — grasp today (执今) vs. grasp the past (执古).

Reading the silk manuscript's "grasp today," the whole chapter is a complete chain of reasoning — from the three sensory negations all the way to the thread of the dao. Every sentence has its place, the chapter holds together.

Reading the standard text's "grasp the past," the elaborate structure of the earlier passages loses its addressee — Laozi does a full chapter of complex structural inquiry (three sensory negations, formless form, no head no tail), and then simply arrives at "just hold onto the ancient dao."

What was all the earlier reasoning for?

Reading the standard text, the earlier and later passages do not connect — Laozi makes a long complex structural inquiry, then suddenly jumps to "hold onto the ancient dao." The chapter does not cohere.

Reading the silk manuscript — the chapter coheres. All the structural inquiry serves the final "grasping today can know the beginning of antiquity."

The silk manuscript lets this chapter be a complete piece of structural work.

The standard text turns it into a passage of mystical verse plus a conservative conclusion.

This is another demonstration of Paper 2's general observation — the standard text's textual variants are not individually malicious, but each pushes in the same direction, turning Laozi's precise structural inquiry into a backward-looking hymn to antiquity.

Reading the silk manuscript, what Laozi wants to say is clear —

The dao is not in ancient classics waiting to be retrieved.

The dao is in the operation of every moment today.

Grasp the remainder yielding at this moment.
Use it to govern the concrete things of this moment.
And you will understand antiquity's beginning —

Because this is the same thing across antiquity and today.

Every moment is new.
