第六篇：从范布伦到菲尔莫尔——程序回避的终结

Essay 6: Van Buren to Fillmore — The End of Procedural Avoidance

Han Qin (秦汉)

一、程序回避的终结

上一篇结尾说过，那道在密苏里第一次划下的地理裂缝，会沿着兼并和扩张被一寸一寸刻得更深。从1837年范布伦就职，到1853年菲尔莫尔离任，这段十六年的历史，正是这道裂缝从一条还能用程序手段绕开的缝，变成一道几乎贯穿一切的断层的过程。

要抓住这段时间的结构意义，最准的说法是这样。在此之前，奴隶制这个最深的余项，常常还能被程序性的回避暂时容纳，用一条纬线，用一次妥协，用含糊的措辞，把它圈在某个地理范围里，让全国政治的其余部分照常运转。而在这十六年里，几乎每一个重大的全国性问题，银行，政党的组合，领土的扩张，对外的战争，新州的加入，联邦法的执行，甚至总统的继任，都开始反馈回同一个问题：奴隶制能不能扩张，能扩张到哪里。这条链条不是后人硬安在一串互不相干的事件上的，它就是当时的人一环扣一环亲历的顺序。

这里要先立一个判断，避免一种很容易掉进去的写法。这段历史不该被讲成一串软弱的总统中间，偶尔插进一个精力旺盛的扩张者。他们面对的约束都不只是个人的，而是结构性的：分裂的政党，以参议院为中心的讨价还价，悬而未决的领土前途，以及一场再也不肯安分地待在旧地理线之内的奴隶制争端。换句话说，这套构容纳那个最深余项的老办法，正在一件一件地失灵。

这套构在此前容纳奴隶制，靠的主要是一种空间上的隔离——把奴隶制圈在南方，用一条地理线把蓄奴和自由分开。这个办法有一个致命的前提：它要求疆域不再变化。而这套构有一股不断向外扩张的引力，它根本停不下来。每一片新得来的土地，都会逼问一句，这一块算南边还是北边？于是那条线就必须被重新去划。这十六年，正是这个内在矛盾彻底爆发的时候。

二、恐慌与一个被摧毁的总统

范布伦上任时，恰好赶上美国陷入1837年恐慌。这场衰退源于国内和国际相互叠加的压力：杰克逊摧毁了第二银行，宠物银行的使用，土地投机的膨胀，要求以硬币缴纳土地款的铸币流通令，英国的金融紧缩，以及美国银行催收贷款。

范布伦在制度上的核心回应是独立国库。1840年，独立国库法最终通过，把联邦资金交由财政部自己管理，而不是交给商业银行。这部法在1841年被辉格党废除，又在1846年波尔克任内以更持久的形式重新建立。独立国库是杰克逊式硬币宪政最持久的制度遗产之一，但它同时也是一种拒绝重建金融中枢的选择，金融的结构仍然悬而未决。

但在政治上，范布伦被这场萧条彻底摧毁了。到1840年，银行倒闭，储户赔钱，农民破产，危机击碎了民主党在国会里的力量。1840年的大选因此变得著名——辉格党推出哈里森和泰勒，打出小木屋和苹果酒的旗号，把哈里森塑造成平民英雄，又把范布伦描绘成贵族派头的纨绔。这里有一个关键的现代修正：这个人设把现实完全颠倒了过来，哈里森出身富裕，而范布伦反倒出身于较为寒微的酒馆人家。

三、泰勒先例——一个没有政党的总统

1841年4月哈里森的突然死亡，制造了第一次真正考验总统继任的宪法难题。宪法第二条没有清楚地说明，总统死亡时，那个"同样的东西"指的是总统的职位本身，还是仅仅是它的权力和职责。泰勒拒绝了那个较小的解读，坚持要被称为总统，拒绝"代理总统"这个称呼。国会随后也以总统相称来强化他的主张。

所谓"泰勒先例"，并不是一种自动生效的宪法清晰性，而是一次由主张、由国会的默认、以及由此后的反复重复共同产生的制度结算。第二十五修正案最终把这种理解写进了宪法的文本。继任的规则，和此前总统连任的限制一样，都是先有先例，后有条文。

但泰勒在宪法上的成功，并没有转化成可治理的政党局面。他本是民主党人，因为地域和派系的平衡被选进辉格党的竞选搭档，上任后很快以违宪和州权为由否决了辉格党的银行立法。辉格党正式把他开除出党。泰勒的处境有一种双重性：他无法制定一套连贯的执政议程，却证明了一件事——一个缺乏政党支持的总统，仍然能够用否决权去阻挡国会的多数。杰克逊大幅扩张的那个否决权，已经成了总统手里一件即便没有政党也照样管用的武器。

四、显然天命与扩张的引力

德克萨斯的兼并，在波尔克还没上任之前，就已经把领土与奴隶制的问题重新打开了。泰勒在1844年重启兼并谈判，却没能凑齐参议院三分之二的票数，于是在离任前不久转而采用联合决议的方式，只需要两院各自的简单多数。在当选总统波尔克的支持下，德克萨斯在1845年12月作为一个蓄奴州加入了联邦。

"显然天命"这个说法，正是在这个时刻进入流通的。编辑约翰·奥沙利文在1845年鼓吹兼并德克萨斯时写道，美国有显然的天命去铺满整个大陆。这套修辞把天意的语言、人口增长的自信、对欧洲的对抗意识，以及一种靠迁移和定居获得政治权利的理论，熔在了一起。它也明目张胆地无视墨西哥的主权和原住民的政治体。

较新的研究和公共史更强调征服、种族等级、对原住民的剥夺，以及把墨西哥人和原住民族当作政治行动者来抹去这几件事。边疆史研究还指出，那种只在华盛顿和墨西哥城之间展开的国与国叙事，给科曼奇、阿帕奇、纳瓦霍等控制着墨西哥北方大片土地的原住民强权，留下的概念空间太小了。

波尔克愿意对英国虚张声势，却把更强硬的胁迫留给了墨西哥。1846年的俄勒冈条约最终以北纬四十九度线划分，而不是口号里的五十四度四十分。扩张的引力又一次显形：自由疆域的扩展与对墨西哥人和原住民的征服和剥夺，是同一个扩张动作的两面。而每一次向外的扩张，都会把奴隶制这个余项重新激活一次。

五、墨西哥战争与奴隶制回到中心

兼并之后，波尔克把军队派进了努埃西斯河和格兰德河之间那片有争议的地带，又派人去谈判购买加州和新墨西哥，在使命失败和流血冲突之后，他用流血的消息去争取国会对战争的支持。战斗发生在有争议的领土上，而不是发生在归属毫无争议的美国土地上，这一点必须写清楚。

亚伯拉罕·林肯的"斑点决议"，以一种不寻常的精确陈述了辉格党在宪法上的论点：他要求波尔克指出美国公民的血究竟洒在了哪一个确切的地点上，并回答那个地点究竟是不是西班牙或墨西哥的领土。林肯是在挑战波尔克那个政治上最有效的公式——"美国的血洒在美国的土地上"。行政部门不能仅仅靠把军队开进一片有争议的边疆，再援引随之而来的冲突，就把这片边疆变成毫无争议的国家领土。

从华盛顿靠先例，到杰斐逊靠既成事实的引力，到波尔克用一场边境冲突去启动战争，总统在对外和战争事务上的主动权，一直在被一点点推大。波尔克这一次走得尤其远，几乎是用军队的部署亲手制造了开战的条件。林肯的斑点决议之所以重要，正在于它点破了这套手法。这个关于行政战争权的余项，当时没有被解决，往后也不会被解决，它会在更晚的战争里一再重现。

战争的结果是1848年的瓜达卢佩伊达尔戈条约：墨西哥割让了约五十二万五千平方英里的土地，约相当于它战前领土的百分之五十五，这片割地包括了今天的加州、内华达、犹他、新墨西哥、亚利桑那和科罗拉多的大部分。而这片领土，立刻就把奴隶制的问题推回了全国政治的正中心。

六、威尔莫特但书——旧政党体系装不下了

把战争政治转向地域政治的决定性枢轴，是威尔莫特但书。1846年8月，戴维·威尔莫特提议在一项战争拨款法案上，附加一条简单的限制：禁止奴隶制存在于任何从墨西哥获得的领土里。这条但书从未成为法律，但它的重要性恰恰在于它的失败。

它一旦被提出来，就让领土的获取再也无法与奴隶制扩张的问题分开。它也开始把全国性的政党沿着地域的界线撕裂——北方的民主党人和北方的辉格党人可以联合起来支持它，去对抗南方的抵抗。它是一个机制，揭示出旧政党体系那些跨地域的联盟，已经不再可靠地能够把奴隶制这个议题吸收进去了。

这件事在框架里的分量极重。这套构能够容纳的，是横切的党争——那种把人按观点切成不同群体、而这些切割彼此交叉、不与某一片土地重合的纷争。而它无法容纳的，是沿着地理线把国家劈成两半的纷争。威尔莫特但书所揭示的，正是这套构原本用来容纳纷争的那台机器——跨地域的政党——开始出问题了。当奴隶制扩张这个问题开始让党内的北方人和南方人分道扬镳，让政党本身沿着那条地理线裂开，容纳纷争的渠道就开始沿着地理对齐了。这正是上一篇所担心的那种最危险的局面，第一次在全国政党的层面成为现实。

七、1850年妥协——用含糊来拖延

加州随即把领土问题逼成了眼前的危机。1848年1月加州发现黄金，移民的涌入迅速把它推向建州。加州人寻求以一部自由州的宪法加入联邦，这威胁到了自密苏里以来一直维持着的自由州与蓄奴州在参议院里的平衡。

1850年妥协并不是一部法律，而是五部法令。加州作为自由州加入，犹他和新墨西哥获得了领地政府，德克萨斯的边界被划定，华盛顿特区废除了奴隶贸易，国会还通过了一部更严厉的逃奴法。克莱发起了结算的努力，但在参议院否决了一揽子打包方案之后，斯蒂芬·道格拉斯把它拆开，分别推动各项法案通过。泰勒生前反对这个方案，而菲尔莫尔上任后转为支持，并签署了这些法令。

这里有一个值得明说的现代修正。新墨西哥和犹他那部分结算的文本，是刻意含糊的——它们在组建这些领地时，并没有写进任何明确的联邦层面对奴隶制的限制，而是把问题往后推，让奴隶制的未来去取决于以后的领土政治和州政治。这场妥协的典型方法是靠含糊来拖延，而不是靠原则来结算。

1850年妥协是对那个不可吸收的余项一次最清楚的失败闭合。密苏里用的是一条明确的地理线，1850年用的则是刻意的含糊。这两种方法看起来相反，一个求明确，一个求含糊，但它们的命运是一样的：无论是用一条清晰的线，还是用一团刻意的含糊，去按住一个既不能肯定也不能否定的余项，结果都是按不住。

八、逃奴法——把奴隶制的强制机器全国化

这场妥协里最具爆炸性的部分，是1850年的逃奴法。它要求执法人员仅凭索取者一方宣誓的证词就去逮捕被指认的逃亡者，它付给专员的报酬是裁定支持索取者给十美元、认定证据不足只给五美元，它还对任何阻挠抓捕或窝藏逃亡者的人处以最高一千美元的罚款和最长六个月的监禁。这部法律还剥夺了被指认者获得陪审团审判的权利，并禁止他们为自己作证。

正是这部法律，构成了这一篇真正的结构转折点。它把奴隶制的强制机器全国化了。在此之前，许多北方人可以把奴隶制想象成一件发生在南方的、与自己有距离的事，而这部法律把奴隶制变成了在他们自己社区里由联邦力量强制执行的事。这套构容纳奴隶制这个余项的老办法，核心就是把它地理化，而逃奴法做的，恰恰是摧毁了这个空间隔离的办法。那个最深的余项，第一次溢出了它被圈定的地理范围，开始在全国被强制执行。

逃奴法还逼着每一个北方的普通人对那个余项做出个人的选择。在空间隔离的旧办法下，一个北方人可以什么都不做，保持道德上的距离。而逃奴法取消了这种距离——你要么配合，要么旁观，要么反抗，无论哪一种，你都已经被卷了进去。那个最深的余项，一旦溢出了它被圈定的地理范围，就不再是一个可以远远旁观的抽象问题，而变成了一件逼到每个人面前、要求每个人表态的具体的事。

哈丽叶特·比彻·斯托的小说，1851到1852年先以连载的形式出现，1852年出版成书，在第一年就卖出了约三十万册。没有争议的是它的规模和反应——这部小说成了一桩大众层面的反奴隶制轰动，加剧了公众的讨论，也加深了地域的两极分化。而它的写作，正与逃奴法直接相关，它把那部法律所制造的道德冲击，变成了千百万人共同的阅读体验。

九、结语

把从范布伦到菲尔莫尔这十六年放回这个系列，最值得记住的，是那个贯穿全篇的结构转折：奴隶制这个最深的余项，逃出了程序回避的容器。

扩张的引力一次次把它激活，每一片新得来的领土，从德克萨斯到墨西哥割地，都立刻把它重新顶到正中心。容纳纷争的政党机器，开始沿着地理线裂开，威尔莫特但书揭示出旧政党那些跨地域的联盟，再也装不下它了。而1850年的逃奴法，则摧毁了把它隔离在南方的那个空间办法，把它的强制机器伸进了北方的每一个社区。

至于这中间几次妥协，最准的理解是把它们看成对一个不可吸收的余项一次次失败的闭合。密苏里用一条纬线，1850年用一套含糊的法令，方法都一样：用空间上的、程序上的安排，把一个两面同体的东西暂时按住。它们都在短期内降了温，但也都在中期把道德和宪法的赌注抬得更高，因为那个余项既不能被肯定也不能被否定，任何一次按压，都只是把它往后推，同时让它顶起来的力道变得更大。

接下来登场的，是这套构容纳能力彻底失灵的年代。一部试图用居民自决去解决领土问题的新法，会把堪萨斯变成流血的战场，而最高法院一次试图一劳永逸地解决奴隶制问题的判决，则会把这套构推到断裂的边缘。那是皮尔斯和布坎南的故事，也是这套构在内战之前最后一次徒劳地试图闭合的故事。

I. The Exhaustion of Procedural Avoidance

The sixteen years from Van Buren's inauguration in 1837 to Fillmore's departure in 1853 mark a decisive structural transformation: the crack first drawn at Missouri deepened, with each new territorial acquisition, into a fault line that now ran through everything. The old methods of procedural avoidance — a latitude line here, a compromise there, a carefully ambiguous clause somewhere else — could no longer contain what they had merely postponed.

The core structural fact of these years is this: nearly every major national question came to feed back into a single question about slavery's expansion. The Panic of 1837, the question of banking, territorial wars, the admission of new states, the enforcement of federal law — each event pulled the unresolved residue of slavery back to center stage. This chain was not imposed by later historians; it was the lived sequence that contemporaries experienced link by link.

The inherited strategy for managing slavery had relied on spatial segregation: confine it to the South, draw a geographic line, and let the rest of national politics proceed as if the question were settled. But this strategy rested on a fatal premise — that the territory would remain fixed. The constitutional order had, from its founding, an expansionist gravity that could not be stopped. Every new acquisition reopened the question of which side of the line it fell on. These sixteen years are the moment when that internal contradiction finally exploded.

II. Panic and a Destroyed Presidency

Van Buren took office just as the country fell into the Panic of 1837. The crisis had multiple causes — Jackson's destruction of the Second Bank, the explosion of land speculation, the Specie Circular demanding hard currency for federal lands, and tightening credit from British banks. Van Buren's institutional response was the Independent Treasury: separating federal funds from the commercial banking system entirely. The bill passed in 1840, was repealed by the Whigs in 1841, and was reinstated under Polk in 1846 in a more durable form.

Politically, Van Buren was destroyed by the depression. By 1840, the Whigs had constructed a new kind of campaign around Harrison and Tyler — the Log Cabin and Hard Cider campaign that presented Harrison as a man of the people and Van Buren as an aristocratic dandy. The reversal was largely fabricated: Harrison came from a wealthy Virginia family, while Van Buren had grown up above a tavern. But the new style of mass-mobilization politics, organized around popular imagery rather than policy debate, had arrived.

III. The Tyler Precedent

Harrison's death in April 1841, just a month into his term, produced the first genuine constitutional test of presidential succession. The Constitution's language was ambiguous: when the president died, did the vice president become president, or merely act as president? Tyler resolved the question by taking the presidential oath and refusing any title but President. Congress eventually accepted this position, and every subsequent vice president who succeeded to the office followed Tyler's example. The Twenty-Fifth Amendment finally codified this understanding in 1967.

The Tyler precedent illustrates a recurring pattern in this constitutional order: its most important rules are not written in advance but are established by someone acting under pressure, then repeated until they become settled. The rule of succession, like the two-term convention, was precedent before it was text.

Tyler's constitutional success did not translate into governing capacity. He had been placed on the Whig ticket for regional balance but was a Democrat at heart, and he vetoed the Whig bank bills on states' rights grounds. The Whigs expelled him from the party. What this episode demonstrated was that the veto power Jackson had expanded now functioned as an independent executive weapon — a president without a party could still block a congressional majority. Stripped of domestic leverage, Tyler turned to the annexation of Texas to establish political relevance.

IV. Manifest Destiny and the Logic of Expansion

Texas annexation had been blocked by Van Buren out of fear of both Mexican retaliation and renewed slavery controversy. Tyler reopened negotiations in 1844, failed to win two-thirds of the Senate, and resorted to a joint resolution requiring only simple majorities from both chambers. Texas entered the Union as a slave state in December 1845. Mexico broke off diplomatic relations and the boundary remained disputed.

It was in this moment that "Manifest Destiny" entered circulation. Editor John O'Sullivan wrote in 1845 of America's "manifest destiny to overspread the continent." The phrase fused providential language, demographic confidence, anti-European assertion, and a theory of political entitlement rooted in settlement and migration. It also casually erased Mexican sovereignty and Indigenous political communities. Newer borderlands history has particularly emphasized what the nation-to-nation narrative between Washington and Mexico City obscures: the Comanche, Apache, and Navaho empires that controlled vast stretches of northern Mexico and shaped the conditions of conquest.

Polk won the 1844 election partly on the promise of both Texas and Oregon. He ultimately accepted the 49th parallel for Oregon while deploying much harder pressure against Mexico. Expansion's gravity was again visible: the same movement that extended the domain of free settlement also required the conquest and dispossession of those already living on the land. And every expansion reactivated the slavery question.

V. The Mexican War and Slavery's Return to Center

After annexation, Polk sent troops into the disputed strip between the Nueces and the Rio Grande, then used the resulting skirmish to request a declaration of war. The critical fact — that the fighting occurred in contested territory, not unambiguously American soil — was the premise of Lincoln's Spot Resolutions of December 1847. Lincoln demanded that Polk identify exactly where American blood had been shed and whether that spot was in fact Spanish or Mexican territory. He was challenging the administration's claim that the war was defensive — arguing that a president cannot simply deploy troops into disputed borderland and then use the resulting clash to transform that land into uncontested national territory.

This episode fits a pattern running through the series: the executive's unilateral power over war and foreign affairs had been expanding since Washington issued the Neutrality Proclamation without congressional authorization. Polk took this the furthest yet, essentially engineering the conditions for war. Lincoln's resolutions named the technique. The question they raised — how far can executive action go in creating irreversible facts before Congress can weigh in — was not resolved then and would recur in every subsequent war.

The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo in February 1848 transferred roughly 525,000 square miles — more than half of Mexico's prewar territory — to the United States. The new lands immediately forced the slavery question back to the national center.

VI. The Wilmot Proviso: The Party System Breaks

The pivot from war politics to sectional politics was the Wilmot Proviso. In August 1846, David Wilmot proposed attaching a simple restriction to a war appropriations bill: slavery would be prohibited in any territory acquired from Mexico. The proviso never became law. Its importance lay precisely in its failure.

The moment it was introduced, territorial acquisition could no longer be separated from the question of slavery's expansion. More consequentially, it began to shear the national parties along geographic lines: northern Democrats and northern Whigs could unite in support while southern members of both parties resisted. The old party coalitions — which had organized support across sectional lines — could no longer reliably absorb the slavery issue.

This is the structural crisis the Proviso revealed. The constitutional order had managed conflict partly by routing it through cross-sectional parties: because a party contained both northerners and southerners, partisan identity could cut across sectional identity. That cross-cutting was the crucial mechanism preventing the country from dividing purely along geographic lines. When the slavery expansion question began forcing intra-party splits along those same geographic lines, the cross-cutting weakened and the country inched toward pure North versus South. The most important containment device was failing from within.

VII. The Compromise of 1850: Deliberate Ambiguity

California's gold rush brought the territorial crisis to an immediate head. By 1849 California was moving toward statehood, and its prospective free-state constitution threatened the Senate balance that had held since Missouri. Taylor's approach — push California and New Mexico directly toward statehood, bypassing the territorial stage where Congress would have to rule on slavery — was derailed by his death in July 1850. Fillmore's succession changed the political arithmetic, and Clay's omnibus package, broken into separate bills by Stephen Douglas, passed in a series of votes.

The five measures admitted California as a free state, organized Utah and New Mexico as territories, settled the Texas boundary, abolished the slave trade in Washington, D.C., and enacted a new Fugitive Slave Law. The territorial legislation for Utah and New Mexico is the key to understanding the Compromise's method: it said nothing explicit about slavery. The question was deliberately left to future local politics. This was not resolution by principle but deferral by ambiguity — the same basic technique as the Missouri Compromise, now in a different form.

Missouri had tried an explicit geographic line; 1850 tried deliberate vagueness. Both methods failed for the same underlying reason: neither addressed the core two-sidedness of the residue. A constitutional order cannot permanently manage something it can neither endorse nor repudiate. Every such arrangement merely postponed the reckoning while raising the stakes.

VIII. The Fugitive Slave Law: Nationalizing the Enforcement Machine

The most explosive element of the Compromise was the new Fugitive Slave Law. It required federal commissioners to issue arrest warrants on the sworn testimony of the claimant alone, paid those commissioners ten dollars for rulings in favor of claimants and five dollars for rulings against — effectively incentivizing capture — fined anyone who harbored or impeded an escaped person up to a thousand dollars, and denied the accused any right to testify in their own defense or receive a jury trial.

This law marks the true structural turning point of this essay. It nationalized the enforcement apparatus of slavery. Before 1850, many northerners could maintain a kind of moral distance from slavery as something that happened elsewhere, in the South, in a different legal world. The Fugitive Slave Law demolished that distance. Federal power now reached into northern communities to compel participation: you could be required to assist in a capture, fined for refusing, prosecuted for harboring. The deepest residue had escaped its geographic container and was being enforced everywhere.

There was a further dimension. The law eliminated the possibility of non-involvement. Under the old spatial segregation, a northerner could simply do nothing and maintain a kind of neutral distance from the issue. The Fugitive Slave Law made doing nothing a choice — compliance, passive witnessing, or resistance were all positions that now had consequences. The residue, once it overflowed its geographic bounds, was no longer an abstract problem that could be observed from a distance. It demanded a response from everyone it touched.

Harriet Beecher Stowe's novel, published serially in 1851–52 and in book form in 1852, sold roughly 300,000 copies in its first year in the United States alone. It converted the moral shock produced by the Fugitive Slave Law into a mass reading experience, deepening polarization while dramatizing what the law had made visible: that the enforcement of slavery was no longer a distant southern institution but a northern social fact.

IX. Conclusion

The sixteen years from Van Buren to Fillmore are best understood as the period in which slavery escaped every procedural container the constitutional order had devised to hold it. Territorial expansion — each acquisition from Texas to the Mexican Cession — reactivated the question each time. The party system began to fracture along sectional lines, revealing that the cross-cutting coalitions which had been the primary containment device were no longer reliable. And the Fugitive Slave Law destroyed the spatial strategy entirely, extending slavery's enforcement apparatus into northern communities and eliminating the moral distance that strategy had depended on.

The compromises of this period — Missouri's line and the 1850 package — are best understood as failed closures of an irresolvable residue. Both temporarily lowered the temperature, buying time at the cost of raising the eventual stakes. Neither could address the underlying two-sidedness: a residue that can be neither fully affirmed nor fully denied cannot be managed permanently by any procedural arrangement, however clever.

What came next was the complete collapse of containment capacity. A new law attempting to settle the territorial question through popular sovereignty would turn Kansas into a battlefield. A Supreme Court decision attempting to resolve slavery's constitutional status once and for all would push the entire system to the edge of fracture. That is the story of Pierce and Buchanan — the order's last futile attempt at closure before the war.
