那条律，不是别人给的

The Law Was Not Given to You

你脑子里那个说"你应该"的声音，是谁的？

That voice in your head saying "you should" — whose voice is that?

Han Qin (秦汉) · 自我即目的理论系列 — 伦理哲学 Self-as-an-End Theory Series — Moral Philosophy · March 2026

从小到大，有人在你脑子里装了很多条"应该"。你应该体谅父母。你应该顾全大局。你应该在乎别人的感受。后来你长大了，又遇到了更多来源：老板、朋友、互联网、某本书、某个你佩服的人。每一个都带着一条"应该"。

问题不是那些"应该"对不对。问题是：哪些是你的，哪些不是你的？

大多数人从来没问过这个问题。他们把所有"应该"混在一起，总体上感觉压抑，总体上感觉焦虑，却说不清楚不同要求究竟从哪里来。本文的分析从拆开三个经常重叠的词开始。

先说明术语：下文对“法律、道德、伦理”的三分，是 Self-as-an-End 框架为了分析而作的专门定义，不是汉语、英语或哲学史上唯一的标准用法。现实语言里，morality 与 ethics 经常互换，伦理学也包含许多并不符合本文批评对象的传统。

三个词，三件不同的事

法律在本文中指可由公共权威执行的外部规则：违反之后有制度性后果。许多现代实定法承认自己由人制定、可以修改或废除；但自然法、神法以及各种宗教法传统确实声称法律根源超出人类约定。因此，这里区分的不是法律在历史上如何为自己辩护，而是外部可执行性与主体内部立法这两种结构。

道德在本文中指主体能够认作自己的内在律：它不是因为惩罚而被执行，而是主体愿意为它负责。主体和自己的律对齐，就是道德；偏离，就是不道德。所谓“自然生长”不是说道德与教育、关系和历史无关，而是说外部影响最终必须经过主体的审视与自我立法。它更像水在给定压力下到达沸点后的相变；沸点并不永远固定在一百度，条件也属于结构的一部分。

伦理在本文的专门用法中，指伪装成主体内在道德的外部要求。它把一个人或共同体的律外推成人人都必须承认的结论：“你应该这样”，“好人都这样做”，“这才是正确的”。这不是对伦理学全部传统的词典定义，而是本文要批评的一种殖民性操作。

汉语常把“伦理”与“道德”连用；本文把这种重叠当作分析入口，而不是声称它是一场持续两千年的有意偷换。“伦”带有关系与次序的意味，容易把注意力引向人际规范；本文则把“道德”专门定义为主体与其能够认作自身之律的关系。

每个哲学家都以为自己站在所有层级之外

两千年来，伦理学中的许多体系都尝试把某个位置上看到的道德风景，论证为适用于所有人、所有时代的普遍真理。也有怀疑普遍主义、强调情境、关系、德性或差异的传统；本文的批评针对的是把自身位置隐藏成无位置的那一种伦理论证。

这不必被归结为某个哲学家的傲慢。本文所批评的普遍化伦理隐含假定：存在一个层级无关的位置，可以从那里对所有道德现象做统一描述。本文拒绝这个假定。每一个人说话时，都从自己的位置说话；你能看到已经经过的风景，却无法直接看见尚未到达的位置。

从本文框架看，康德的义务论可以被理解为一种特定高度上的真实体验——到了那里，“不得不”就是这样的感觉。问题出现在第一人称的“不得不”被翻译成对所有理性存在者的普遍要求时。本文不需要宣布康德全部伦理学“虚假”；它质疑的是这一步普遍化是否隐藏了自己的发展位置。

从本文框架看，功利主义关于最大多数人之最大幸福的主张，可以被理解为从群体性视野出发的真实道德直觉；问题在于把计算结果视为层级无关的唯一标准。

从本文框架看，德性伦理学关于“什么样的人是好人”的追问，描绘了某种人格图景；本文质疑的是把其中任何单一图景未经进一步论证地当成所有人的人性标准。

本文愿意保留三种传统在各自位置上的洞见，同时拒绝把任何一种洞见未经论证地提升为层级无关的唯一标准。

尼采看到了一半

尼采是最尖锐地揭示道德谱系与权力关系的思想家之一，但并不是历史上第一个怀疑普遍道德的人。他看到：把自己的道德外推成对别人的要求，可能同时是一种权力行为。他对怨恨与价值制造的诊断打开了要害，也因“强者／弱者”的框架而留下自己的盲点。

从本文框架看，尼采把批判的方向推进得过远：他把问题放在道德本身，并以“超越道德”、权力意志与超人的语言回应。

本文提出另一种切分：问题不在主体能够认作自身之律的“道德”，而在把某种道德外推为对他人的强制要求。按这一专门定义，尼采的批判把两者一并处理了，因而错过了本文想保留的差异。

他看到了殖民的本质，却没有看到道德和伦理不是同一件事。

真正的道德是到达，不是安装

拆掉了伦理学，不是进入虚无。恰恰相反——拆掉的是别人给你立的法，露出来的是你自己存在的律。

道德不是从外面加进来后原封不动执行的模块。它是外部经验、关系与教育经过主体审视后，成为主体愿意承担的律。仍借相变作比喻：水在给定压力下到达沸点会改变状态，压力变化时沸点也变化；同样，主体的“到达”从不脱离条件，却也不能被条件替主体完成。

这就是为什么你能感受到某些道德标准是真实的，而另一些不是。真实的那些，你是到达的；不真实的那些，是被装进来的。装进来的那些，你在执行它们时，会有一种隐隐的异物感——你在执行一条律，但你不是那条律。

真正的道德不是执行。是成为。

你不是在服从那条律。你是那条律。

善恶是方向，不是身份

善恶不是两种人。是同一个人和自己所在阶段的律之间的关系。对齐是道德的，偏离是不道德的。

同一个行为，从不同的发展高度看，道德判断会翻转。这不是道德相对主义——不是说"反正都是相对的，谁也没有资格说谁"。恶是真实的，痛苦是真实的，被压制的人不会因为施压者有局限性就减轻痛苦。

区别在于：没到，不是恶。一个人还没发展到能看到某件事的伤害性，不是恶，是还没到。但往回压——一个本来在展开的人，被人为地强行压回去——那个施力的方向就是恶。

"没到"是位置。"往回压"是方向。恶是方向，不是位置。

道德不传递，只共振

那些被你装进去的"应该"里，有一部分来自你真正尊敬的人。你看到他们，你感动了。这种感动是真实的。但它的结构和伦理学的结构不同。

伦理学说：我到了这里，所以你也应该到这里。

感动说的是：你的律和我的律，碰巧振动在接近的频率上，能量放大了。

这是共振，不是传递。两个独立的主体，各自持有各自的律，各自在展开，谁也没有要求谁。但当两边的展开频率接近，就会产生共鸣——你会感受到那种内心被震动的感觉。这不是评判，不是学习，不是服从。这是两个自由振动的主体偶然进入了同一个频率。

共振不可强求，不可设计。你能做的只有一件事：把自己这边振好。

反过来，不共振也不构成绑架的理由。你和他没有共振，不等于他不道德——那是在拿你的律去量他，又变成了伦理。你可以不喜欢，可以保持距离，可以反对。但"你不这样做就是不道德"——那句话，就是你刚刚反对的那件事。

最低要求只有一条

这篇文章说的全部事情，可以压缩成一句：你给自己立法，只给自己立法。

但还有一个实践层面的最低要求，比"不要越界"更基础、更可操作：不撒谎。你做了什么，说做了什么。为了谁，说为了谁。

殖民不是最大的恶。以爱的名义殖民才是。赤裸的"我要你这样"，至少还保留了对方识别压制的机会——你知道自己被要求了，你可以拒绝。但包装过的版本——"我这样说是为你好"，"你这样做才是真的对自己负责"——连这个机会都剥夺了。你被要求了，但你以为那是关怀；你的空间被压缩了，但你以为那是爱。

这个要求听起来低到多数人都能做到，实践中却经常被利益、恐惧与自我美化破坏。

知道自己在做什么，然后诚实地说出来——这本身就是最基本的道德状态。

Han Qin · 学术论文全文（SAE Theory） · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19037566

From the time you were small, people installed a lot of "shoulds" in your head. You should appreciate your parents. You should think of the bigger picture. You should care about how others feel. Then you grew up and encountered more sources: bosses, friends, the internet, books, people you admired. Each one brought its own "should."

The question isn't whether those shoulds are right or wrong. The question is: which ones are yours, and which ones aren't?

Most people never ask this question. They mix all the shoulds together, feel generally oppressed or anxious, but cannot say where the different demands come from. This analysis begins by separating three terms that often overlap.

A terminological note: the three-way distinction below is a stipulation within the Self-as-an-End framework, not the only standard usage in English or in the history of philosophy. In ordinary and academic language, morality and ethics often overlap, and ethical traditions include much more than the target criticized here.

Three Words, Three Different Things

Law, in this essay, means an external rule enforceable by public authority: violation carries institutional consequences. Many modern systems of positive law recognize that their rules are human-made and revisable. Natural-law, divine-law, and religious legal traditions, however, have explicitly claimed an origin beyond human convention. The structural distinction here is therefore not how every law justifies itself, but external enforceability versus a subject's internal legislation.

Morality, in this essay, means an internal law a subject can recognize as their own: it is not followed merely because punishment threatens, but owned as something for which the subject is answerable. "Grows" does not mean untouched by education, relationships, or history. It means that outside influence must pass through scrutiny and self-legislation. The process is more like water reaching a phase transition under specified pressure; the boiling point is not always 100°C, because conditions belong to the structure too.

Ethics, in this essay's technical usage, means an external demand disguised as the subject's own morality. It extrapolates one person's or community's law into a conclusion everyone must already own: "You should be like this," "Good people do this," "This is the right way." This is not a dictionary definition of the whole discipline of ethics; it names the colonizing operation criticized here.

Chinese often compounds ethics and morality as “伦理道德.” This essay treats that overlap as an analytic entry point, not as evidence of a deliberate two-thousand-year sleight of hand. The character “伦” carries senses of relationship and order, which can direct attention toward interpersonal norms; here, “morality” is stipulated more narrowly as the relation between a subject and a law they can recognize as their own.

Every Philosopher Thought They Stood Outside All Levels

For two thousand years, many ethical systems have tried to argue that a moral landscape visible from one position is universally binding across people and times. Other traditions have doubted universalism or emphasized situation, relationship, virtue, and difference. The target here is the argument that hides its own position by presenting itself as a view from nowhere.

This need not be reduced to the arrogance of individual philosophers. The universalizing ethics criticized here implicitly assumes a level-independent position from which all moral phenomena can be described. This essay rejects that assumption. Everyone speaks from a position: you can see landscapes you have traversed, but you cannot directly see positions you have not reached.

Within this framework, Kant's deontology can be read as a genuine experience from a particular developmental height — "cannot not" is what obligation can feel like there. The question begins when this first-person necessity becomes a universal demand on every rational being. The essay need not declare all Kantian ethics false; it asks whether that universalizing step conceals its own position.

Within this framework, utilitarianism's appeal to the greatest happiness of the greatest number can be read as a genuine moral intuition from a group-oriented vantage point. The problem begins when its calculation is presented as the single level-independent standard.

Within this framework, virtue ethics asks what kind of person is good and develops a picture of character. This essay questions the promotion of any one such picture, without further argument, into the human standard for all people and times.

This essay preserves insights from all three traditions at their respective positions while refusing to promote any one of them, without further argument, into the only level-independent standard.

Nietzsche Saw Half of It

Nietzsche was among the sharpest thinkers to expose the relation between moral genealogy and power, though hardly the first person in history to doubt universal morality. He saw that extrapolating one's morality into demands on others can also be an act of power. His diagnosis of ressentiment and value-creation opens the target while retaining blind spots of its own in the opposition between strong and weak.

Within this framework, Nietzsche pushed the direction of critique too far: he located the problem in morality itself and responded through the language of overcoming morality, will to power, and the Übermensch.

This essay proposes a different cut. The problem is not the “morality” a subject can recognize as their own law, but the extrapolation of one morality into compulsory demands on others. Under that technical distinction, Nietzsche treated both together and therefore missed the difference this essay wants to preserve.

He saw the colonizing structure of ethics. He just didn't see that morality and ethics are not the same thing.

Real Morality Is Arrival, Not Installation

Dismantling ethics is not entering nihilism. Quite the opposite — what is dismantled is the law others made for you; what is revealed is the law of your own existence.

Morality is not a module installed from outside and then executed unchanged. It is what outside experience, relationship, and education become after a subject scrutinizes them and accepts answerability for a law. Keep the phase-transition metaphor, but keep its conditions too: water changes state at a boiling point set partly by pressure. Likewise, a subject never "arrives" outside conditions, yet conditions cannot perform self-legislation on the subject's behalf.

This is why you can feel that some moral standards are real while others are not. The real ones: you arrived there. The unreal ones: they were installed. The installed ones carry a faint sense of foreignness when you execute them — you are carrying out a law, but you are not that law.

Real morality is not execution. It is becoming.

You don't obey the law. You are the law.

Good and Evil Are Directions, Not Identities

Good and evil are not two kinds of people. They are the relationship between one person and the law at their own stage. Alignment is moral; deviation is immoral.

The same action, viewed from different levels of development, flips in moral judgment. This is not moral relativism — not "everything is relative so no one can judge anyone." Evil is real. Suffering is real. A person being suppressed is not less harmed because the one doing the suppressing has limited vision.

The distinction is this: not having arrived is not evil. Someone who hasn't yet developed to the point where they can see the harm in something is not evil — they just haven't arrived yet. But actively pushing back — taking something that was unfolding and forcing it back — that applied force is evil.

"Not yet arrived" is a position. "Pushing back" is a direction. Evil is a direction, not a position.

Morality Is Not Transmitted. It Resonates.

Among the "shoulds" that were installed in you, some came from people you genuinely admired. You saw them, and you were moved. That feeling was real. But its structure is different from ethics.

Ethics says: I arrived here, so you should arrive here too.

Being moved says: your law and mine happen to be vibrating at similar frequencies, and the energy amplified.

This is resonance, not transmission. Two independent subjects, each holding their own law, each unfolding, neither demanding anything of the other. But when the two frequencies come close, they amplify — you feel that interior vibration. Not judgment. Not learning. Not submission. Two freely vibrating subjects that happened to enter the same frequency.

Resonance cannot be forced or designed. The only thing you can do is get your own vibration right.

Conversely, absence of resonance is not grounds for moral blackmail. You and someone else not resonating does not mean they are immoral — that would be measuring them by your law, which is ethics again. You can dislike it, keep your distance, oppose it. But "if you don't act this way you are immoral" — that sentence is the thing you just argued against.

The Only Minimum Requirement

Everything this essay says can be compressed into one sentence: legislate for yourself, and only for yourself.

But there is one practical minimum requirement, more basic and more operational than "don't overstep": don't lie. Say what you did. Say for whom you did it.

Colonization is not the greatest evil. Colonization in the name of love is. Naked "I want you to be this way" at least preserves the other person's chance to recognize the pressure — you know you're being required, you can refuse. But the packaged version — "I say this for your own good," "doing it this way is really taking responsibility for yourself" — takes even that away. You were required, but you thought it was care; your space was compressed, but you thought it was love.

This minimum sounds low enough that most people could meet it. In practice, interest, fear, and self-flattery violate it often.

Knowing what you're doing, and then saying so honestly — that is the most basic moral condition there is.
