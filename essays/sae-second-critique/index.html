SAE第二批判：十二讲

The SAE Second Critique: Twelve Readings

如果这是你第一次接触 SAE，建议先读《SAE基础：人如何成为目的》；这一部接在《SAE第一批判：十二讲》之后，最好先读过那里。

《第一批判》审认识一侧，《第二批判》审实践一侧：一个人给自己立的法，没有外部担保者，不向别人发令，立法者自己随时可以改——它的“不得不”凭什么约束立它的人，约束到哪里，与别人的不得不怎样相处。审的是 SAE 自己的话，最重的几刀落在最早、说得最用力的那几句上。它放进去的是三样东西：一条公设，一个域内零点，一次选择。认不可免，承可拒；凭的是站；诸法之间，交叠而不统一。

这十二篇按论证单位重写，不机械摘要原书的六章。原书仍是完整论证与权威底本：阅读全文 ↗ · DOI ↗

第 01 篇
一条法凭什么约束立它的人

一个人给自己立的法，没有外部担保者，不向别人发令，立法者随时可以改它。它的不得不凭什么约束立它的人，约束到哪里，与别人的不得不怎样相处——这部批判先把刀转向自己最早、说得最用力的那几句。

第 02 篇
拆掉三件“必然”

“这不是选择”“退出即降格”“一切从唯一的公理推出”——实践一侧那组处处必然的说法，被拿到刀口下。不得不降为挂在位置上、可以逐件离开；法不是非的定理；九种“不得不”各自放回原处。

第 03 篇
分野不在“内部”：与康德

说康德的法是普遍的、SAE 的法是内部的，不准：康德自己就认为伦理的立法不能来自外部。真正的分野在普遍法那一步与法权的义务；对他人的德性义务，两边一半相接。康德立一个理性的事实，这部书不立；他立悬设，这部书也不立。

第 04 篇
他能说不：实践一侧的零点

凡是冲着他人意志的动作——支配，胁迫，欺骗，说服——都在动作里认了他能说不；在这类动作里，这一认不能被施行一致地否认。奴隶主要防奴隶逃跑，却不必防牲口起义。认不可免，承可拒。

第 05 篇
代价就是余项

照自己的法做一件事，在自己账上留下的是代价，而代价就是余项——这一刀没有收纳进去的那一部分。一次失约，朋友的失望是后果；他自己账上记的，是“答应了的事要做到”这一次没有收进去。代价跟着法走，不跨人比较，也不是价格。

第 06 篇
说代价，不是强制

接受一个权力，行使一个权力，追求与放弃一项价值，停与不停，各有代价，也各有它常被忽视的理由。别人的代价，我能读，能依自己的法而行，能不追加后果；不能读他的构成，不能读他的层级，不能把我的读数变成他的代价。

第 07 篇
出示多于持有：自家的案卷

一条法或一个不得不，出示了它不曾挣得的效力凭证，就是冒领。病例只从本框架自己已发表的文字里取：冒领无条件，冒领演绎，冒领横向，以层级数人，终审与今站，驱动，标准，免费，未记来源的效力，趋同。判的是冒领，不定罪于人。

第 08 篇
防范之梯

一个否认别人能说不的人，他自己的动作替他作证：关栏，禁识字，逃奴法与路条，防起义，骗入死地。梯子的每一级读出他的动作取用了对方哪一样回应；他嘴上否认的，正是动作一直在对付的。施行不一致之戳，打在立场上，不打在人身上。

第 09 篇
自律、执迷与无常

幻相为什么自然：前件写不出来，沉淀越深越像本来如此，行得通就以为对谁都行得通，代价总在后来。两道实践背反：取消不了与可以离开；不辍是自律，还是执迷。自律三件：能停而不辍，被追问时通道开着，改法认账。

第 10 篇
一本登记簿

诊断要有一本账可对：本框架说出的每一条不得不，站在什么上面。每一条写内容、地位、担保来源、反对者与分支，另加依赖与立处；定义也写替代与代价，并注明不作证据。七条公设，几种构形，有人住的分支，两条本分支之法。登记不证明任何一条，只让每一条站在它挣得的位置上。

第 11 篇
凭的是站

一条自己立的法凭什么约束立它的人？不凭一个事实，不凭外加的力，不凭理性的事实；凭的是站。承了，就站在了一个位置上：站在那里，有些做法取消不了——不是做不出来，是做了，站在这里与取消它这两句话放不到一个人身上。约束到他站的地方为止，没有例外。

第 12 篇
交叠，而不统一

撤去普遍法的排他效力以后，诸法之间剩下的是交叠：定义所保，只在形式，间歇，不能预设、只能认出，不作推论的前提。这不是各行其是：在谁错上让一步，在什么可以免于检验上一步不让。交叠之外的伤害，由外部的构承担。判否，不判是；说代价，不命令。

Foundational SAE Text · The Critique on the Practical Side

The SAE Second Critique: Twelve Readings

《SAE第二批判：十二讲》

New to SAE? Begin with SAE Foundations: How a Person Becomes an End. This critique follows The SAE First Critique: Twelve Readings, which is best read first.

The First Critique audited the side of cognition; the Second audits the side of practice. A law a person gives himself has no outside guarantor, commands no one else, and can be revised by its maker at any time — by what does its cannot-not bind him, how far, and how does it live alongside the cannot-nots of others? The words examined are SAE’s own, and the heaviest cuts fall on its earliest, most forceful sentences. The answer puts in three things: a postulate, an in-domain zero point, a choice. Concession is unavoidable; adoption can be refused; it rests on where one stands; between laws, overlap without unification.

These twelve essays are independent reader-facing rewrites organized around arguments, not summaries of the book’s six chapters. The book remains the complete and authoritative source: read the book ↗ · DOI ↗

Essay 01
By What Does a Law Bind Its Maker?

A law a person gives himself has no outside guarantor, commands no one else, and can be revised by its maker at any time. By what does its cannot-not bind him, how far, and how does it live alongside the cannot-nots of others? The critique turns first on SAE's own earliest, most forceful sentences.

Essay 02
Dismantling Three “Necessities”

“This is not a choice,” “to exit is to be degraded,” “everything follows from the sole axiom” — the practical side’s claims of necessity everywhere go under the knife. The cannot-not is lowered to one that hangs on a position and can be left matter by matter; the law is no theorem of Negativa; nine kinds of cannot-not are each returned to their place.

Essay 03
The Divide Is Not “Inner”: With Kant

To say Kant’s law is universal and SAE’s is inner is inexact: Kant himself held that ethical legislation cannot come from outside. The real divide lies at the step to universal law and at duties of right; on duties of virtue toward others the two half meet. Kant posits a fact of reason; this book posits none, and sets up no postulates of practical reason either.

Essay 04
He Can Say No: The Zero Point on the Practical Side

Every act aimed at another’s will — domination, coercion, deception, persuasion — concedes in the act that he can say no, and within such acts the concession cannot be denied with performative consistency. The slaveholder must guard against escape; no one guards against livestock rising up. Concession is unavoidable; adoption can be refused.

Essay 05
Cost Is Remainder

Doing something by one’s own law leaves cost on one’s own books, and cost is remainder — the part this cut did not take in. When a man breaks an appointment, the friend’s disappointment is a consequence; what his own books record is that the rule “a promise made is a promise kept” was not taken in. Cost follows the law, is never compared across persons, and is not a price.

Essay 06
Stating the Cost Is Not Coercion

Accepting a power, exercising one, pursuing or giving up a value, stopping or not stopping: each has its cost, and each a reason it is overlooked. With another’s cost I can read, act by my own law, and decline to add consequences; I cannot read his constitution, cannot read his level, and cannot turn my reading into his cost.

Essay 07
Displaying More Than It Holds: The Case File at Home

A law or a cannot-not that displays a credential of validity it never earned is a false claim. The cases come only from SAE’s own published texts: the unconditional, deduction, the lateral, counting levels by heads, the final verdict and the present station, the drive, the standard, costlessness, unsourced validity, convergence. What is judged is the false claim; no person is convicted.

Essay 08
The Ladder of Precautions

Someone who denies that others can say no is testified against by his own acts: fencing in, banning literacy, fugitive-slave laws and passes, guarding against uprising, luring to death. Each rung shows which response of the other his acts draw on; what he denies in words is what his acts deal with all along. The stamp of performative inconsistency falls on a position, never on a person.

Essay 09
Autonomy, Obsession, and Fickleness

Why illusion comes naturally: the antecedent goes unwritten, the deeply settled sounds like how things are, what works for me seems to work for all, and cost always comes later. Two practical antinomies: cannot be cancelled versus can be left; unceasing as autonomy, or as obsession. Autonomy’s three marks: able to stop yet unceasing; the channel open under interrogation; revising the law with the books kept.

Essay 10
A Register

Diagnosis needs a ledger to check against: what each cannot-not the framework has stated actually stands on. Each entry gives content, status, source of warrant, opponents and branches, plus dependencies and domicile; even definitions list alternatives and costs, and are marked as no evidence. Seven postulates, a handful of configurations, the inhabited branches, two laws of this branch. Registration proves nothing; it makes each claim stand where it has earned.

Essay 11
It Rests on Where One Stands

By what does a law a person gives himself bind him? Not by a fact, not by a force from outside, not by a fact of reason: it rests on where one stands. To adopt is to take up a position, and for one standing there certain things cannot be cancelled — not because they cannot be done, but because “I stand here” and “I cancel this” cannot sit together in one person. The binding runs as far as where he stands, without exception.

Essay 12
Overlap, Not Unity

With the exclusive validity of universal law withdrawn, what remains between laws is overlap: secured by definition, formal only, intermittent, made out rather than presupposed, never a premise for giving orders. This is not each to his own: one step is yielded on who is wrong, none on what may escape examination. Harm beyond the overlap falls to external constructs. Rule out, never rule in; state the cost, give no command.
