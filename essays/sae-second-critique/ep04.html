他能说不：实践一侧的零点

He Can Say No: The Zero Point on the Practical Side

Han Qin (秦汉)

只剩一条的那把尺

《第一批判》用一把尺子量过所有候选的地基：能不能形成一个可以理解、又在施行上一致的反对立场？量下来，只剩一条：“没有施行一致的反对立场，非因此是公理——迄今唯一的一条。”任何否认“有非”的人，在否认的那一刻已经划了一次界，已经在用非。

方法论0 第二版把它定为全部系列的唯一公理，并写明“名单是开放的：新的候选到来，过同一道检验”。这条公理约束的是否认的动作，不是行动。它不告诉任何人该做什么。

实践一侧，第二批判还要用到一样东西。它过的是同一道检验——但只在一类动作里过得了。这部书给它起的名字两半都说准了：零点，是说找不到一个可以理解、又施行一致的反对立场；域内，是说只在这一类动作里如此。

域内零点

这一条是这样说的：凡是以他人的意志为对象的动作——支配，胁迫，欺骗，说服——都在动作之中认了他能说不；在这类动作里，这一认不能被施行一致地否认。

这里说的认，不是赞成他，也不是给他什么地位；是说那个动作已经把他能说不算了进去——一个说得出口、别人听得懂、也接得过去的不，不只是挣扎。认的是这份能力；他是不是目的，在这部书里另有来处。

威胁之所以成其为威胁、欺骗之所以成其为欺骗，是因为它们冲着一个能理解、能选择的人。没有人去威胁一块石头，搬开就是。这个预设是动作自己带着的，不论得手与否，不是谁承担进来的。对方实际能不能拒绝，动作得手没有，另据事实判断。

两个候选，与一个奴隶主

实践一侧的候选有两个，要分开看。第一个：否认自己有任何自立之法。这个否认在施行上一致吗？一致。一个照别人的规矩过、或者走一步看一步的人，说“我没有自己的法”，说这句话并没有替他立下一条法——除非他把这句话当成自己守的规矩。所以“人人都有一条自立之法”当不了零点，否认它的人站在另一条分支上。

第二个：否认他人能说不，把他当作物。这里情形不同。奴隶主不以奴隶为目的，却要防他逃跑。防牲畜，栏再高、锁再牢，对付的都只是那头牲畜；防奴隶，要逃奴法，要路条，要其他奴隶主一起不以这个奴隶为目的。这些不是更高的栏，是冲着别人去的：逃奴法管的是收留他的人，路条是给路上遇见他的人看的——制度禁止的，正是第三个人的回应。因为奴隶的“不”能向第三个人喊出来，第三个人听得懂这是一个不，也接得过去。

他防的每一样，都对着那份能力的一面：能想，能说，能找人，能与别人合力。他的账因此有两本：一本记物，一本记须防之人。第二本合不上，是他自己的动作在作证。他嘴上否认的那份能力，他的动作一直在认。（这道防范的阶梯，第八篇一级一级走完。）

不是第二条公理

它与那条唯一的公理不同，不同在哪里，要说准。它不分立场：不论你站在哪一条分支上、持哪一组设定，它都成立。但它只管冲着他人意志的动作。它当然可以写成“若你做这一类动作，则……”；可这个“若”说的是你在做什么，不是你接受了什么，按动作做的是什么来认，不按做的人怎么称呼它。那条公理没有之外，任何否认都在它里面；不做这类动作的人在域内零点之外，却不是它的反对者。（一个孤身的人为将来的人写一本书，意在说服，仍在它的范围里。）

这部书让它过了《第一批判》的正门。施行测试，过了：胁迫一个人，同时说他没有说不的能力——胁迫冲着的正是那份能力。归约测试，原有的四种结果都对不上。它不是那条公理换了件衣裳——这里的矛盾说的是对方的“不”，不是否认者自己那一刀。它不挂在一条可以换掉的公设上——公设可以换，动作只能做或不做。它也不是新的公理——它的担保不跨一切场域。于是这部书添了一档：承重点落在一类动作上。它入册的地位是域内零点，不入公理区，也不入公设区。

它的用处是诊断，不替任何具体的判断作保。要说一个人冒领了什么，仍得去读他实际做了什么、防了什么，读得对不对，可以复核；一个动作算不算冲着别人的意志，也可以争。若拿零点去替一个具体的判断作保，那本身就是一种冒领。它也不改变《第一批判》的清点：“有一样东西越过全部四个维度——在迄今受审的候选中，只有这一条。”

认不可免，承可拒

由此，第一案的判词先有了核心的一半：认不可免，承可拒。认，由动作交出，不能施行一致地否认；可它认的是一份能力，不是地位。论文4 划下的限相同：指出一个人正在作判，“不使他的主张发生施行矛盾”——施行一致与否，管不到地位。

承，是把“别人也是目的”立为自己法的前提，这一步仍是选择，可以拒绝。《自己的法》早有一句近于此：“不是你创造了这条法，是你到达了它，然后由你自己承担它。”于是有三种账。嘴上否认别人能说不、动作上却一直对付着那份能力的人，记着两本；认而不承的人，账是一本——那是另一条分支；承了的人，他的法里从此有一条不得不。

从认到承之间的那一步，没有零点可以替人走。读过价值论的人要留意一个字：价值论说“认这个事实”，认的是他者是目的；在这部书的分法里，那落在承一边。域内零点保住的东西更窄，也更硬：在每一个冲着你的动作里，我都已经把你的“不”算了进去。

The Ruler That Left Only One

The First Critique measured every candidate foundation with a single ruler: can an intelligible and performatively consistent opposing position be formed? Measured so, one remained: “There is no performatively consistent opposing position; Negativa is therefore an axiom — so far, the only one.” Whoever denies that there is Negativa has, in the act of denying, already drawn a line, and is already using it.

The second edition of Methodology 0 made it the sole axiom of all the series and wrote: “The list is open: when a new candidate arrives, it passes the same test.” What this axiom binds is the act of denial, not action. It tells no one what to do.

On the practical side the second critique needs one more thing. It passes the same test — but only within one class of acts. The book’s name for it is exact on both counts: a zero point, because no intelligible and consistent opposing position can be found; in-domain, because this holds only inside that one class.

The In-Domain Zero Point

Here is the statement: every act that takes another’s will as its object — domination, coercion, deception, persuasion — concedes, in the act, that he can say no; and within acts of this class, the concession cannot be denied with performative consistency.

The concession meant here is not agreeing with him, and not granting him any standing. It is that the act has already counted in his capacity to say no — a no that can be spoken, that others understand, and that they can take up, not mere struggling. What is conceded is that capacity. Whether he is an end has, in this book, a different source.

A threat is a threat, a lie a lie, only because it is aimed at someone who can understand and choose. Nobody threatens a stone; you move it. The presupposition is carried by the act itself, whether or not it succeeds; no one had to undertake it and bring it in. Whether the other can in fact refuse, and whether the act worked, are judged separately, on the facts.

Two Candidates, and a Slaveholder

The practical side offers two candidates, and they come apart. The first: denying that one has any self-given law at all. Is that consistent? It is. A person who lives by others’ rules, or takes things a step at a time, and says “I have no law of my own,” has not laid down a law in saying so — unless he makes the sentence itself a rule he keeps. So “everyone has a self-given law” cannot be a zero point; whoever denies it stands on another branch.

The second: denying that another can say no, and treating him as a thing. Here it is different. The slaveholder does not take the slave as an end, yet he has to guard against his running away. Against livestock, however high the fence, what is guarded against is only that animal. Against a slave he needs fugitive-slave laws, travel passes, and other slaveholders who likewise refuse to take this slave as an end. These are not higher fences; they are aimed at other people. The fugitive-slave law governs whoever might take him in; the pass is for whoever meets him on the road. What the institution forbids is precisely the response of a third person — because the slave’s no can be called out to a third person, understood as a no, and taken up.

Everything he guards against faces one side of that capacity: being able to think, to speak, to seek others out, to join forces. So he keeps two sets of books: one lists a thing, the other a person to be guarded against. The second set never squares with the first, and it is his own acts that testify. The capacity his words deny, his acts concede all along. (Essay 08 walks this ladder of precautions rung by rung.)

Not a Second Axiom

Where it differs from the sole axiom must be said exactly. It does not depend on standpoint: whatever branch you stand on, whatever assumptions you hold, it holds. But it governs only acts aimed at another’s will. It can be written “if you do an act of this class, then …” — but the “if” is what you are doing, not what you have accepted, and the act is classed by what it does, not by what the doer calls it. The axiom has no outside; every denial falls within it. Whoever does no act of this class stands outside the in-domain zero point — without being its opponent. (A solitary person writing a book to persuade readers yet unborn is inside it.)

The book passes it through the First Critique’s front door. The performative test it passes: coerce a man while saying he has no capacity to say no, and the coercion is aimed at exactly that capacity. The reduction test finds none of the four outcomes on the list. It is not the axiom in another garment — its contradiction concerns the other’s no, not the denier’s own line. It is not hung on a replaceable postulate — a postulate can be swapped out, an act can only be done or not done. It is not a new axiom — it does not cross every field. So the book adds a disposition: the load falls on a class of acts. It is registered as an in-domain zero point, in neither the axiom region nor the postulate region.

Its use is diagnostic, and it vouches for no particular verdict. To say someone falsely claimed something, one must still read what he actually did and guarded against, and that reading can be checked; whether a given act was aimed at another’s will can be disputed. To lend the zero point’s warrant to a particular judgment would itself be a false claim. And it changes nothing in the First Critique’s count: “One thing crosses all four dimensions — among candidates examined so far, exactly one.”

Concession Is Unavoidable; Adoption Can Be Refused

This gives the core of the book’s first verdict: concession is unavoidable; adoption can be refused. Concession is handed over by the act and cannot be consistently denied — but what it concedes is a capacity, not a standing. Paper 4 draws the same limit: pointing out that a person is already judging “does not render his claim performatively self-defeating”; performative consistency does not reach as far as standing.

Adoption is setting “others are ends too” as a premise of one’s own law. It is still a choice, and it can be refused. One’s Own Law had a sentence close to this: “you did not create this law; you arrived at it, and you bear it yourself.” Three kinds of books follow. Whoever denies in words that others can say no, while his acts keep dealing with that capacity, keeps two sets. Whoever concedes without adopting keeps one — that is another branch. Whoever has adopted has, from then on, a cannot-not in his law.

The step from concession to adoption is one no zero point can take for anyone. A reader of Value Theory should mind one word: where Value Theory speaks of conceding “this fact” — that the other is an end — the act it describes falls, in this book’s division, on the side of adoption. What the in-domain zero point secures is narrower and harder: in every act aimed at you, I have already counted on your no.
