自律、执迷与无常

Autonomy, Obsession, and Fickleness

Han Qin (秦汉)

幻相为什么自然

《第一批判》说幻相“不是认识的事故——有构处，即有它的条件”。实践一侧，这部书找到四个条件。其一，前件写不出来。一个不得不总挂在一个位置上，可“取消不了”这一句自己不带“站在这里”。这一层从位置条件的不得不直接推得出。一个人可能强烈地感到不得不，却没有同时注意它的位置条件——这一层只记作假说：前者说的是句子，后者说的是感受。

其二，一条假说：“沉淀越深的构，越容易以‘本来如此’的口吻出场”。法也一样，道德律写四条“是Self在15DD运作时自然如此的结构”，就是这种口吻。其三：一条法在自己身上行得通，就容易被说成对谁都行得通。《第一批判》说过同形的话：“抱负不是病，免检的抱负才是。”这里照此：普遍化的冲动不是病，免检的普遍化才是。其四：代价延迟；行动的当下，它以不花什么的样子出场。

四条地位不同，方向相同。《第一批判》的风险律在这里照原样立，前提与限定一个字不省：在位置条件不随不得不自动保全、新的援引未同步复核的条件下，一个不得不被援引得越广，越出位置条件的点就越多——幻相的风险随之升高；它“不担保实际的幻相必然增多”。

取消不了，与可以离开

第一道背反。正题：站在一个位置上，有些做法取消不了。反题：那个位置可以离开。两句都是行动论自己的正式主张，化解也在它自己那里：“它说的是两个陈述不相容，不是一种心理上的做不出。”

第二批判只补上幻相论的读法：背反出于位置索引的脱落。取消不了，是在位置之内，两个陈述不能同时成立；可以离开，是对那个位置，一件事一件事地说。两句各在各的地址上为真；《第一批判》那句“能停而不辍，才是自律”，是这道背反现成的接缝。

还有一处细账。退出的代价，本框架有四种说法：《理性不得不》说降格；《第一批判》与行动论都说“仅此而已”；道德律说退出 15DD 运作之后，Self“仍然有立法能力”。四种说法不能都对。这部书照后三者说这一笔代价，并写明这一说法站在论文4 的公设上。

不辍是自律吗

第二道背反。正题：不辍是自律的标志——这不是稻草人，《理性不得不》写过：“真正的自律是持续的立法运动：主体每时每刻都在给自己立法，立法停止的那一刻，自律就不再运作。”反题：《第一批判》写过：“能停而不辍的，也可能是执迷；把自律与执迷分开，是实践批判的工作。”

原句后一半说的是必要——“立法停止的那一刻，自律就不再运作”；前一半用“是”连起来，读得出充分：只要不辍，就算自律。不辍是必要的，不够。判据的候选是：被追问的时候，修改与复核的通道开不开。执迷，是不辍而通道自闭。另一端是无常：遇到方便就停，改了法不认账。

于是这部书给自律三件：能停而不辍；被追问时通道开着；改法认账。第一篇许下要说清的三者——自律、执迷与无常——到这里分开了，也与那里先分的一条相接：修改不是撂下。

至死不改，不是执迷

这条判据要和本框架已有的一条对齐。学习系列给“不得不”下过一条试金石，“外部否定不能将其熄灭”；从外面看，这与执迷很难分。可它同时写了一道限：试金石所指的外部否定“是社会性阻断……不是事实性反证”；“如果事实性反证出现而你仍然不调整方向，那不是14DD在场，那是13DD失灵”。社会的阻断熄不灭它，事实的反证它却要接住——通道开着。

价值论还有一句更要紧的：“一个人可以认定某事为其一生所守，至死不改，这与可修订并不冲突：可修订说的是那个位置开着，不是说它必须被使用。”所以至死不改不是执迷；位置关上了才是。

这三件，是这部书这一支给“自律”的定义，有反对者：以法之不可修为忠诚的一支。《自己的法》里有最近的一句：“你不需要停下来问‘这个方向还对不对’，因为方向没变。”这部书不把自己的定义冒领成自律的普遍定义。康德的德福背反，这里不设：本框架的料里，逼不出它。

诊断的纪律

诊断别人，也是一种主张；所以这部书给自己的诊断立五条纪律。点名栏位，不点名人：一纸合格的诊断写的是何种凭证被出示、账上何栏无此条目、出处何在，“不写的是——出示者是何居心、该当何罪”。读结构，不读内心：只读动作、账目、通道与防范的样式，不读动机。第一人称的认领归本人：“第一人称认可不能由他者授予，也不能由他者撤销。”

层级只记运作，按时刻与域：道德律自己说过，研究单位“是事件不是人——15DD是行为层级而非身份标签”；这部书不立“没有 15DD 的人”这样一个实质的主张，只立这一条纪律。判否与方向报告分记，都可撤，没有吸收态。冲突论：“线索不是判准，痕迹也不是判决。”

诊断也是主张。误诊也有类型，恰是冒领的镜像：未经复核的指控以定案自居，一宗个案之诊被拓展成对整个框架的判决，对旧版的诊断不经重审而加于新版。价值论给过一个最清楚的形状：一个宣称看得出你真正认可什么的审计，“他若接受，结论成立；他若否认，否认被读作结论的又一份证据”。这样的诊断，自己就是一宗冒领。这一章收在自己身上：它立起的每一条诊断，“也立在被追问之下”。

Why Illusion Comes Naturally

The First Critique said illusion is “no accident of cognition — where there are constructs, there are its conditions.” On the practical side the book finds four. First, the antecedent goes unwritten. A cannot-not always hangs on a position, yet the sentence “this cannot be cancelled” does not on its own carry “as one stands here.” That much follows from the shape of the thing. That a person may feel a cannot-not strongly without noticing its condition is recorded only as a hypothesis: the first speaks of sentences, the second of feelings.

Second, recorded as a hypothesis: “the deeper a construct’s sedimentation, the more readily it enters in the voice of ‘it was always so.’” Laws too — Moral Law calls its four “the structure that the Self naturally is.” Third: a law that works for oneself is easily said to work for anyone. The First Critique had a line of the same form: “ambition is no disease; exempted ambition is.” So here: the urge to universalize is no disease; exempted universalizing is. Fourth: cost is deferred; at the moment of action it appears as if it cost nothing.

The four differ in status and point one way. The First Critique’s law of risk is set up here word for word, premises and limit included: where the position-condition is not automatically preserved with the cannot-not and new invocations are not reviewed in step, the more widely a cannot-not is invoked, the more points at which it oversteps its condition — and the risk of illusion rises; it “does not guarantee that actual illusions multiply.”

Cannot Be Cancelled; Can Be Left

The first antinomy. Thesis: while one stands in a position, some things cannot be cancelled. Antithesis: the position can be left. Both are Action Theory’s own formal claims, and so is the resolution: “What it states is that two statements are incompatible, not a psychological inability to bring oneself to act.”

The second critique adds only the reading from its doctrine of illusion: the antinomy arises when the index of position drops out. “Cannot be cancelled” says that, within the position, two statements cannot both hold; “can be left” is said of the position, one matter at a time. Each sentence is true at its own address, and the First Critique’s “able to stop, and yet unceasing — that is autonomy” is the ready-made seam between them.

One small account remains. The cost of exit is stated four ways in the framework: Reason Cannot Not says degradation; the First Critique and Action Theory say “that is all”; Moral Law says that after leaving 15DD the Self “still has the capacity to legislate.” The four cannot all be right. The book states the cost as the last three do, and marks that this statement stands on Paper 4’s postulate.

Is Unceasing Autonomy?

The second antinomy. Thesis: not ceasing is the mark of autonomy — no straw man, since Reason Cannot Not wrote: “True autonomy is the persistent legislative motion: the subject gives itself law moment by moment; the moment legislation stops, autonomy ceases to operate.” Antithesis: the First Critique: “what is able to stop yet unceasing may also be obsession, and parting autonomy from obsession is the practical critique’s work.”

The sentence’s second half states a necessary condition; its first half joins the terms with “is,” and so can be read as sufficient: as long as one does not cease, it counts as autonomy. Not ceasing is necessary, and not enough. The candidate criterion is whether, under interrogation, the channel of revision and review is open. Obsession is not ceasing while the channel has closed itself. At the other end lies fickleness: stopping whenever it suits, changing the law without keeping the books.

So the book gives autonomy three marks: able to stop, yet unceasing; the channel open under interrogation; revising the law and keeping the books on it. These are the three distinctions Essay 01 promised to make clear — autonomy, obsession and fickleness — and they meet the first one drawn there: revising is not dropping.

Unaltered to the End Is Not Obsession

The criterion must agree with one the framework already had. The learning series gave the cannot-not a touchstone — “External opposition cannot extinguish it” — which from outside is hard to tell from obsession. But it drew a limit: the opposition meant “is social blockage … not factual refutation,” and “if factual refutation appears and you still refuse to adjust, that is not 14DD presence but 13DD failure.” Social blockage cannot put it out; factual refutation it must take up — the channel is open.

Value Theory says something that matters even more: “A person may hold something as what he will keep to the end, unaltered, and this does not conflict with revisability: revisability says the position is open, not that it must be used.” So keeping something unaltered to the end is not obsession. Only a closed position is.

The three marks are this branch’s definition of autonomy, and they have an opponent: the branch that counts a law’s unrevisability as loyalty. One’s Own Law holds the nearest sentence: “You do not need to stop and ask ‘is this direction still correct?’ The direction has not changed.” The book does not falsely claim its definition as the universal one. And Kant’s antinomy of virtue and happiness it does not set up at all; nothing in the framework’s material forces it.

The Discipline of Diagnosis

To diagnose others is also to make a claim, so the book binds its own diagnoses with five rules. Name columns, not persons: a competent diagnosis writes which credential was shown, which column lacks the entry, where the sources are — and not “what the shower intended, and what he deserves.” Read structure, not hearts: acts, accounts, channels and patterns of precaution, never motive. First-person claiming belongs to the person: “First-person avowal can be neither conferred by another nor revoked by another.”

Levels record operations only, by moment and domain: Moral Law itself says “the unit of study is the event, not the person; 15DD names a behavioral layer, not a personal identity”; and the book makes no substantive claim such as “there are no 15DD people” — only this discipline. Rulings-out and direction reports are kept apart; both can be withdrawn; there is no absorbing state. The Conflict Treatise: “cues are not criteria, and traces are not verdicts.”

A diagnosis is also a claim. Misdiagnosis has its own types, the mirror of false claiming: an unreviewed accusation carrying itself as a settled case; one case stretched into a verdict on a whole framework; an old version’s diagnosis laid on the new. Value Theory gives the clearest shape, an audit that claims to see what a person really avows: “if he accepts it, the conclusion holds; if he denies it, the denial is read as further evidence for it.” Such a diagnosis is itself a false claim. The chapter closes on itself: every diagnosis it set up “also stands under interrogation.”
