交叠，而不统一

Overlap, Not Unity

Han Qin (秦汉)

剩下的是交叠

行动论交来的那一问，还有最后一截：它“与普遍性的关系”。康德把普遍立法当作自律的形式，把目的王国当作诸理性存在者在共同法则下的联合。《第一批判》撤去了那道检验的排他效力，留下了形式：“它的普遍性只在形式上——每个主体都不得不遵守自己立的法（这是自律的形式）；它的内容却因主体而异。”普遍法的排他效力撤去以后，诸法之间剩下的，是交叠。

交叠有一个准确的结构。定义所保：两个人都在 15DD 运作的时候，都不疑他者为目的；这份重合由定义给出，分析为真，不带新信息。只在形式：“SAE 共识是姿态汇聚·非命题汇聚。”间歇：交叠只在两个人同处 15DD 运作的时刻与域上——“同一个人在不同时刻可以在 15DD·14DD·12DD 不同层级运作”。

不能预设，只能认出——在相遇里，由各人从第一人称认出来。《自己的法》：两个 15DD 的主体，各自推出了同样的律，“但这不是共识，不是共享，是各自到达。你不能拿你的到达去确认他的到达”。也不作推论的前提：“你我都把人当目的，所以你也该如何如何”，就是冒领横向。

这不是各行其是

这听起来很像相对主义，这部书把反驳说到最强再答：你们不准任何一条法约束拒绝它的人；你们说代价不能跨人比；你们从外面连一个人在 15DD 运作都不能判。那么，一个以支配别人为法的人，与一个不疑别人是目的的人，在你们这里不过是两条分支，登记的资格一样平等。回答分四层。

其一，交叠不是碰巧。在那里，没有人要求别人先证明自己是目的——这一样不空：内容不收敛，形式上共有的那一样，恰恰是人与人相处时用得着的那一样。其二，复数不是同等：“登记的资格平等，效力各按自己的账结算，跨支的效力仍须挣得。”以支配为法的人，照样为他的每一刀付账。其三，从外面有读数：别人看不见一个人的法，看得见他锁住了谁的路——封住别人拒绝、修订、退出的通道，被追问之后关上自己复核的门。怎样都行的地方，没有这样的读数。

其四，否认能说不的支配住不稳。它一刻不停地防着它嘴上否认的那份能力，账是两本；这一戳跨得过分支，因为它凭的不是这部书的法，是支配者自己的动作。坦然的支配——认你能说不，只是不以你为目的——这一戳打不到它。对它，这部书说得出它站在哪里，它锁人时付的反缚，它在外面留下的痕迹；这部书以我们之名站在它的对面，所据是承了的不疑——那是我们自己的承担，不冒充对它的反证。

让一步，一步不让

四层合起来，与相对主义的分界就在这里：这部书拒绝的不是存在不同的法，而是任何主张因此免于事实、推理和自身前提的检验。它不对拒绝者说应当，却也不说怎样都行。它说得出每一条法站在哪里，付什么，在外面留下什么痕迹。

“在谁错上，这部书对他让了一步；在什么可以免于检验上，一步不让。”它说不出坦然的支配者错：跨分支的优劣判据，这部书没有，我们站在对面，也补不出这一判据；这些说得出的，更不取得替他立法的资格。

说不出他错，也不替他立法，是这部书付的代价，不藏着：拒绝者不受这部书的法约束，他对别人的伤害，交叠之内拦不住。康德付的是另一笔：一条约束得了一切理性存在者的法，和一个不求演绎、自行确立的起点。“两笔代价，各在各的账上。”

交叠之外

于是有一条边界：各人自己的法管各人自己，交叠内外都管；交叠够不着、拒绝者的法又拦不住的那一部分——他对别人的伤害——由外部的构承担。这句话语料里没有原话，这部书把四处拼起来立，并写明根据：《自己的法》——“法律是外部构……它不声称自己是道德，只说这是规则，违反了有后果”；法学——“法对15DD是地板不是天花板”，守法“是因为对方需要法”；冲突论——法“是我们不在场时的替代与脚手架，不是我们本身”；权利论的两种处理：核验与法庭。

这条主张带着它的反对者——康德：一条普遍法则同时约束接受它的人与拒绝它的人——也带着它的代价。交叠之外的约束，要由制度另立；交给构，不等于已经证明了构的正当与有效，更不保证它不会反过来支配人；没有交叠，也不等于每件事都要制度化：拒绝、不合作、离开，许多事到此为止。制度的理论另有去处：制度论与政府论。

两个人之间的程序是有限的。“共识不自动生成第三个第一人称的立法者”；两个人谈成的，“是共同做事，不是共同的法”，“制度可以让人互相找到，不能替双方产生「我们」”。跨支的效力可以主张，但要挣得：打在否认能说不的支配上的那一戳，凭支配者自己的动作，随域内零点跨过分支——跨过去的是诊断，不是法；过界之后，它不带任何处置。

判否，不判是

从外面，可以判一个人此刻不在 15DD 运作，不能判他在。判否要有一样实在的东西：这一次运作的某个必要条件，连同它没有满足，都已经独立核实——比如他封了对方说不、改正、退出的通道。只是看到一般的风险痕迹，那是方向报告。冲突论给了一个最清楚的痕迹：宣布止点而同时关闭一切复核通道，“是这个止点可能只是拖着的一项强风险信号”，足以使它的公共可信度显著下降，“不足以替主体完成内部的分类”。两样都可撤，没有吸收态：“线索不是判准，痕迹也不是判决。”

判否之后，一个人能做的，是他自己的法容许的：不合作，退出，反对——“退出=纯粹的物理通信链路静音（物理切断）——非本体判决”。站在这里的人自己取消不了一道边：不封对方说不的通道，也不给对方的拒绝追加后果。退出是自己不再出力；追加后果，是替他的不定罚。

这部书收在一句话上：诸法之间，交叠而不统一；判否，不判是；说代价，不命令。它的法对读者，不因读而生效：读者按自己的法读它；相合的条款，他自己承担了，才在他的法里起效；读不到的地方，他照自己的法另走，这部书登记那一支，连同它的条件与代价。道德律早有一句近于此：“读者作为独立立法主体阐述自己的版本·欢迎替代。”

What Remains Is Overlap

Action Theory’s question had one last part: the cannot-not’s “relation to universality.” Kant made universal legislation the form of autonomy, and the kingdom of ends a union under common laws. The First Critique withdrew the exclusive force of that test and kept a form: “Its universality is formal only: every subject cannot but keep the law it has itself laid down (that is the form of autonomy); the content of the law differs from subject to subject.” With universal law’s exclusive force withdrawn, what remains between laws is overlap.

Overlap has a precise structure. It is secured by definition: when two people both operate at 15DD, neither doubts the other is an end; the coincidence is analytic and carries no new information. It is formal only: “SAE consensus is posture convergence, not proposition convergence.” It is intermittent: it holds only at the moments and in the domains where both operate so — “the same person, at different moments, can operate at 15DD, 14DD, or 12DD.”

It cannot be presupposed, only made out — in an encounter, by each person, from the first person. One’s Own Law: two 15DD subjects may each arrive at the same law, “but this is not consensus, not sharing — it is each arriving independently. You cannot use your arrival to confirm another’s.” And it is never a premise of inference: “you and I both treat people as ends, so you too ought to …” is the false claim of the lateral.

This Is Not Each to His Own

It sounds very like relativism, and the book meets the objection at its strongest: you let no law bind anyone who refuses it; cost cannot be compared across persons; you cannot even rule that someone is at 15DD. Then a man whose law is to dominate others and a man who doubts no one’s being an end are, for you, two branches with the same entitlement to be registered. The answer comes in four layers.

First, overlap is not luck. In it, no one requires another to prove first that he is an end — and that one thing is not empty: contents do not converge, and the one thing held in common in form is precisely the one people need in dealing with each other. Second, plural is not equal: “the standing to be registered is equal; validity is settled on each road’s own account; cross-branch validity must still be earned.” A man whose law is domination still pays, on his own books, for every cut. Third, there are readings from outside: no one sees another’s law, but anyone can see whose road he has locked — another’s ways to refuse, revise or leave sealed; his own door to review shut once questioned. Where anything goes, there are no such readings.

Fourth, domination that denies the other’s no does not live stably. It guards ceaselessly against the very capacity it denies, and keeps two sets of books; that stamp crosses branches, because it rests on the dominator’s own acts, not on this book’s law. Candid domination — conceding the other can say no and simply not treating him as an end — the stamp does not reach. Of it the book can say where it stands, the back-binding it pays when it locks someone in, the traces it leaves; and “this book stands opposite the branch in our name,” on the ground of its own adopted non dubito — which is our undertaking, not a refutation of him.

One Step Given, Not One More

Here the book marks where it parts from relativism: what it refuses is not the existence of different laws, but any claim’s exemption, on that account, from examination by fact, by reasoning, and by its own premises. It says no ought to the refuser; nor does it say anything goes. It can say where each law stands, what it pays, and what traces it leaves outside.

“On who is wrong, this book gives way one step to him; on what may be exempt from examination, not one step.” It cannot say the candid dominator is wrong: it has no criterion of better and worse across branches, and standing opposite him does not supply one. Still less does anything it can say give it the right to legislate for him.

That is the cost the book pays, and it is not hidden: the refuser is not bound by its law, and the harm he does to others cannot be stopped within the overlap. Kant pays a different cost — a law that binds every rational being, and a starting point that seeks no deduction. “Two costs, each on its own books.”

Beyond the Overlap

So one boundary: each person’s law governs him inside the overlap and outside it alike; what the overlap cannot reach and the refuser’s law will not stop — the harm he does to others — is borne by external constructs. The book found no sentence in the corpus saying this, so it assembles the claim from four places and states them: One’s Own Law — law “is external construct … it simply says: these are the rules, and violating them has consequences”; the Law series — “For a 15DD, law is a floor, not a ceiling,” kept “because the other needs law”; the Conflict Treatise — law is “the substitute and the scaffolding for when the ‘we’ is not present; it is not the ‘we’ itself”; Rights Theory’s two modes, verification and the court.

The claim carries its opponent — Kant, whose universal law binds accepter and refuser alike — and its cost. Constraint beyond the overlap must be set up by institutions; handing something to a construct does not prove the construct legitimate or effective, still less guarantee it will not turn round and dominate people; and not everything needs institutions — refusal, non-cooperation, leaving: many matters end there. The theory of institutions goes elsewhere, to SAE’s theories of institutions and of government.

Between two people, the procedure is limited. “Consensus does not automatically generate a third first-person legislator”; what two people work out is “doing something together, not a shared law,” and “institutions can let people find each other, but cannot produce a ‘we’ on behalf of both.” Cross-branch validity can be claimed, but must be earned: the stamp on denial-of-no domination crosses branches through the zero point, by the dominator’s own acts — and what crosses is a diagnosis, not a law; it brings no measures with it.

Rule Out, Never Rule In

From outside, one can rule that a person is not operating at 15DD at this moment; one cannot rule that he is. A ruling-out needs something solid: some necessary condition of this operation, together with its failure, independently verified — that he has sealed off the other’s ways to say no, to correct, to leave. General traces of risk yield only a direction report. The Conflict Treatise gives the clearest trace: declaring a stopping point while closing every channel of review “is a strong risk signal,” enough to lower the declaration’s credibility, “not enough to complete the internal classification for the subject.” Both kinds can be withdrawn; neither is an absorbing state: “cues are not criteria, and traces are not verdicts.”

After ruling someone out, a person can do what his own law allows: not cooperate, leave, oppose — “Exit = pure physical communication-link silencing … not ontological judgment.” One edge he cannot cancel while he stands where he stands: he does not seal the other’s way to say no, and he does not add consequences to the other’s refusal. To exit is to stop lending one’s strength; to add consequences is to set a penalty on his no.

The book ends in one line: between laws, overlap without unification; rule out, never rule in; state the cost, give no command. Its own laws do not take effect on the reader by being read. The reader reads by his own law; a clause that fits takes effect in his law only once he himself undertakes it; where it does not reach him, he goes another way, and the book registers that branch, with its conditions and its cost. Moral Law had said something close: “the reader as independent legislative subject articulates their own version; substitution is welcomed.”
