拆掉三件“必然”

Dismantling Three “Necessities”

Han Qin (秦汉)

我们说过的那些“必然”

围绕实践的不得不，本框架说过一整组必然性的话。《自己的法》说，主体到了 15DD 就“不得不反对任何主体的余项被压制。这不是选择，不是高尚，是存在方式”；“你没有‘不反对’的选项”；这条律是“无条件的（没有任何前提可以让它失效）”。《理性不得不》说得更重：停下来的主体会“被14DD的手段结构吞噬，沦为工具”；“退出即降格”。

15DD，是本框架层级序列里的一层：在那里，一个人不只把自己当目的，也把别人当目的；余项，是一次划界没能穷尽的那一部分。第二批判把这组“必然”逐一拿到刀口下，看哪些真是必然。被拆的，都是自己的话。

头一句话经不起一个简单的追问：那个“没有‘不反对’的选项”，是证出来的，还是断言的？《自己的法》自己也写着：“对齐是道德的，偏离是不道德的。”在它自己的书里，偏离是可能的。

其一：它挂在位置上

设想一个人，站在把自己和别人都当目的的位置上，然后在一件事上离开它：为了自己这一年的评价，把那个团队看成瞒过去也无所谓的人，改了数字，什么也不说。这个设想里有矛盾吗？没有。行动论把这一幕写得很准：单看他改没改数字，“还定不了”，他也可能是怕，是被压着；他若真这样离开了，“在这一件事上，这一条不得不对他不在了，仅此而已……离开是按件的”。做不成的只有一件：一边说我是把人当目的的人，一边说那个团队瞒过去也无所谓——“不是因为有谁不准，是因为那两句话在同一个人身上放不到一起。”

所以这里要降一格。拆掉的是没有条件、没有选项这一说；留下的，是一种挂在位置上的不得不，分两层：共同的一层多半是怎样看待人，站在那个位置上就取消不了；个案的一层是这一次做什么，是本人的判断，可以被问，可以修订。那个位置，一件事一件事都可以离开；离开的价，记在离开的人自己的账上。这笔账说的是他那条法在他身上的效力；他对那个团队做下的事，不由这笔账结清。

那个价要说准。“沦为工具”“退出即降格”，把价说成了身份的丧失，说重了。《第一批判》早已说过这个价是多少：停止这场运作的主体，“其以自身为目的，不再经由这场运作得到实现——仅此而已；这不取消他规范上的目的地位，更不授权任何人把他仅仅当作手段”。论文4 从一条公开承担的公设——人是目的本身——说得更直接：“能力受损、被迫改变或未获他人承认，不构成撤销目的地位的根据。”站在这条公设上看，离开的人失去的，是那条法在这一件事上对他的实现，不是他是谁。不接受这条公设的人仍可以说“降格”，那是另一条分支，带着它自己的条件与代价。

撤去马达

同一处还要拆掉一股力。本框架写过：余项“不得不有外部出口（避免内部崩溃），唯一合法出口是另一个15DD主体”；追问是“16DD的engine”；两个人各自的目的相遇，共同去做的那件事，“是两个各自的目的遇到一起以后逼出来的做法”。这些句子里都藏着一台马达：余项推着人走，出口逼着人转向别人，相遇逼出共同的做法。

《第一批判》在认识一侧早已拆过这台马达：“余项只是一个缺口……它自己不推动任何东西。”余项指出亏在哪里，向哪里去的出处也就在那里；走不走，先走哪一处，在走的人。两个人共同去做的那件事，照行动论自己的分法，是“这一次做什么”，是两个人的判断——“理由不会自己变成行动”；说它是“逼出来的”，就把判断说成了一股力。

《第一批判》自己也留下了一处：它把实践的不得不称作“SAE 的内在驱动力”。第二批判不改它一字，只说清它该怎么读：“驱动力”在这里读作条件——给人继续走下去的条件，不推着人走。《第一批判》自己有一句撑着这个读法：“能停而不辍，才是自律；不能停而不辍，只是机器在转。”撤去马达，不是撤去走路的人：一个人照自己的法走下去，那股劲是他的。

其二：不是从唯一的公理推出来的

拆掉没有条件之后，还剩一个更庄严的说法：实践的法，是从唯一的公理推出来的。道德律第一篇写：“SAE的公理只有一个——非。本系列里所有的内容都从这一个公理在多Self情境下的具体展开演绎而来。”权利论也说，自律与道德律诸定理，“皆由它推出”。非，在本框架里指划界这个动作：把“这”和“不是这”分开。

说一切都从它推出，这一说，《第一批判》已经撤过。设想一个只有否定的最小系统：它把任一命题翻成反面，再翻回来；否定在其中存在，不可否认，也能反复作用，却生不出余项，生不出反身，生不出一条向前生长的序列——“非推不出万物”。方法论0 第二版把这一撤回写成了明文：“SAE 唯一公理：非。其余皆各系列内之公设与定理。”

那么道德律的四条是什么？四条并不一样。第一条，待具体他者为目的，与 15DD 的定义说的是同一件事；它对 15DD 分析地为真，重说定义不能拿来当证据。其余三条比那个定义多出了东西：读作定义，就是扩充了 15DD，要说明这份扩充从哪里来；读作实质的主张，就要各自的担保。哪一种读法，都通向同一个处置：它们不是从非推出来的。“定理”这个词，一个词装了两样东西；这部书引这四条时，逐条说清它们各是什么。

其三：九种“不得不”

最后拆开的，是一类一直混用的说法。由内而外的这类说法，在本框架已发表的文字里，至少有九种：没有条件、没有选项（《自己的法》）；本体式，明确拒绝假言（《理性不得不》）；构成条件式——“Self一旦在15DD运作，就不得不如此；不如此就不在15DD运作”（道德律）；挂在位置上，一件事一件事可以离开（行动论，形状借自冲突论，后者说“我可以停止这一操作”）；朝内的义务（权利论、意义论）；结构上的无法不，自然呈现的方向（方法论00、内观系列）；经得起审视、外部否定熄不灭（学习系列）；他者的目的进入我的约束（句式论）；双重否定式（总论）。

九种之间，差的不是措辞。有的不挂任何条件，有的挂在定义上，有的挂在位置上；有的不许退出，有的可以离开，有的可以停；有的明说是义务，有的明说不是命令。一类说法同时载着九样东西，力就可能从一样借到另一样：把某个位置之内取消不了的东西，说成任何人都离不开的必然；或者拿对一个位置分析地成立的东西，去充当对任何人都有分量的约束。拆法与《第一批判》拆“守恒”一样：逐一分开，各自放回原处，站得住的都留下。

三件东西拆下来，实践一侧“处处必然”的那组说法塌去大半，这部批判真正的问题露了出来：一条挂在位置上、可以离开、没有外部担保的不得不，凭什么约束站在那个位置上的人？答案的形状可以先说：资格来自承——把“别人也是目的”接过来，立为自己法的前提，不去检验对方配不配；这一步可以拒绝。边界由代价标出。普遍性来自法与法之间的交叠，以及从外面读得出的痕迹。全书的规范语法，一句话就够：不强制，只说代价。这句话在《第一批判》已有原型：“不是被强制执行，是违之有价。”

The Necessities We Once Claimed

Around the practical cannot-not, the framework once made a whole set of necessity-claims. One’s Own Law: at 15DD a subject “cannot not oppose the suppression of any subject’s remainder. This is not a choice, not nobility — it is a mode of being”; “you have no option of ‘not opposing’”; the law is “unconditional (no precondition can cause it to lapse).” Reason Cannot Not went further: a subject who stops is “swallowed by the 14DD instrumental structure and reduced to a tool”; “To exit is to be degraded.”

15DD, in the framework’s vocabulary, is the level at which a person takes not only himself but others as ends; the remainder is the part a single demarcation fails to exhaust. The second critique now takes these “necessities” one by one to the edge of the knife. Everything taken apart is the framework’s own.

The first sentence does not survive one simple question: that “no option of not opposing” — was it proved, or asserted? One’s Own Law itself also writes: “Alignment is moral; deviation is immoral.” In its own pages, deviation is possible.

First: It Hangs on a Position

Imagine a person who stands where he takes both himself and others as ends, and then leaves that position in one matter: for the sake of his own review this year, he regards the other team as people it would be fine to keep in the dark, changes the number, and says nothing. Is there a contradiction in the picture? None. Action Theory writes this scene with care: whether he changed the number “does not settle it on its own” — he might be afraid, or held down; and if he really does leave, “in this one matter, this cannot-not is no longer there for him, and that is all … Leaving goes matter by matter.” Only one thing cannot be done: saying he treats people as ends while saying the team may be kept in the dark — “not because anyone forbids it, but because those two statements cannot sit together in one person.”

So the claim comes down a grade. What falls is the claim of no condition and no option. What remains is a cannot-not that hangs on a position, in two layers: a shared layer, mostly about how people are regarded, that cannot be cancelled while one stands in the position; and a case layer, about what to do this time, that is the person’s own judgment and can be questioned and revised. The position can be left, matter by matter; the cost of leaving goes on the leaver’s own books. That account concerns his law’s hold on him; what he did to the other team is not settled by it.

The cost must be stated exactly. “Reduced to a tool” and “to exit is to be degraded” state it as a loss of status — and overstate it. The First Critique had already priced it: a subject who halts this operation “no longer realizes its being-an-end through this operation — that is all; this cancels neither its normative standing as an end, nor licenses anyone to treat it merely as a means.” Paper 4 says it more directly, from a postulate it openly undertakes — that a human being is an end in itself: “impaired capacity, coerced change, or the absence of others’ recognition does not constitute a ground for revoking standing as an end.” Seen from this postulate, the one who leaves loses the realization of his law in one matter, not who he is. Whoever refuses the postulate may still say “degraded”; that is another branch, with its own conditions and cost.

Removing the Motor

In the same place a force has to come down. The framework wrote that the remainder “cannot not have an external outlet (avoiding internal collapse); the only legitimate outlet is another 15DD subject”; that interrogation is “the engine of 16DD”; that what two people do together is “forced into being once two separate purposes meet.” Each sentence hides a motor: the remainder pushes, the outlet forces, the meeting compels.

On the cognitive side the First Critique had already taken this motor apart: “The remainder is only a gap … by itself it pushes nothing.” A remainder shows where the debt lies, and so where a direction issues from; whether to walk, and which way first, rests with the one who walks. What two people do together is, by Action Theory’s own division, “what to do this time” — their judgment; “reasons do not turn into action by themselves.” To call it “forced into being” turns a judgment into a force.

The First Critique itself left one such word standing: it called the practical cannot-not “the inner drive of SAE.” The second critique changes not a word of it, and only says how to read it: “drive” here reads as a condition — the condition for going on, not a push from behind. The First Critique supplies the reading itself: “able to stop, and yet unceasing — that is autonomy; unable to stop, and unceasing — that is only a machine turning.” Removing the motor does not remove the walker; when a person walks on by his own law, the strength is his.

Second: Not Deduced from the One Axiom

With the unconditional taken down, a more solemn claim remains: that the practical law is derived from the sole axiom. Moral Law wrote: “SAE has only one axiom—negativa. All content in this series is deduced from this single axiom’s specific unfolding in multi-Self situations.” Rights Theory spoke of autonomy and the moral-law theorems as following from it. Negativa, in this framework, names the act of demarcation — parting “this” from “not-this.”

The First Critique had already withdrawn “everything follows from it.” Picture a minimal system with nothing but negation: it flips any proposition into its opposite and back. Negation exists there, cannot be denied, can be applied again and again — and generates no remainder, no reflexivity, no forward-growing sequence: Negativa does not entail all things. The second edition of Methodology 0 put it in writing: “The sole axiom of SAE, across all its series: Negativa. Everything else is a postulate or theorem within its series.”

Then what are Moral Law’s four “theorems”? They are not alike. The first — to treat the concrete other as an end — says what the definition of 15DD already says; it is analytically true of 15DD, and restating a definition is no evidence. The other three carry more: read as definition, they enlarge 15DD and owe an account of the enlargement; read as substantive claims, each needs its own warrant. Either way, they are not theorems of Negativa. One word, “theorem,” was carrying two things; the book now says of each of the four what it is.

Third: Nine Kinds of Cannot-Not

The last thing taken apart is a family of sayings long run together. In the framework’s published texts the inside-out kind comes in at least nine forms: no condition and no option (One’s Own Law); ontological, refusing the hypothetical (Reason Cannot Not); a constitutive condition — “once a Self operates at 15DD, it cannot-not be so; not being so is not operating at 15DD” (Moral Law); hung on a position, leavable matter by matter (Action Theory, after the Conflict Treatise, which says “I may stop this operation”); an inward-facing duty (Rights Theory, Meaning Theory); a structural cannot, a direction that emerges of itself (Methodology 00, Introspection III); what survives scrutiny (the learning series); another’s purpose entering my constraint (the sentence-form paper); a double negation (the Methodology Overview).

The nine differ in more than wording. Some hang on no condition, some on a definition, some on a position; some allow no exit, some can be left, some can be stopped; some call themselves duties, some say outright they are not commands. When one word carries nine things, force can be borrowed from one to another: what cannot be cancelled within a position is passed off as a necessity no one escapes, or what holds analytically of a position is made to weigh on anyone at all. The method is the First Critique’s with “conservation”: part them, return each to its place, keep whatever holds.

With three things taken down, the story of necessity everywhere has largely collapsed, and the critique’s real question stands bare: a cannot-not that hangs on a position, can be left, and has no outside warrant — by what does it bind the one who stands there? The shape of the answer can be given now. Entitlement comes from adoption — taking up “others are ends too” as a premise of one’s own law, without testing anyone’s worth; this step can be refused. The boundary is marked by cost. Universality comes from overlap between laws, and from traces readable from outside. The book’s whole normative grammar fits in one line: no coercion — only the cost, stated. Its prototype is in the First Critique: “not enforced, but priced — violation has its cost.”
