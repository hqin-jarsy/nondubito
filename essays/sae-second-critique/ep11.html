Essay 11 of 12

凭的是站

It Rests on Where One Stands

Han Qin (秦汉)

三样东西，一个判词

康德在这里立了一个理性的事实，不求演绎。第二批判在这里不立事实。它放进去的是三样东西，种类各不相同：一条公开承担的公设——人是目的本身，管地位；一个域内零点——以他人意志为对象的动作，认了他能说不，管认；一次选择——把不疑立为自己法的前提，管承。

《第一批判》交来过一案：“即使结构判断成立，从‘不能收纳’到‘当承认’的效力，仍须由实践的批判另行取得”。第二批判把它改成了第三篇说过的两问。判词分四拍。地位：一条公设，不由任何能力推出。认：域内零点；认的是能力，不是地位。诊断：否认别人能说不、却冲着别人的意志行事的人，账上是两本——这是冒领，作证的是他自己的防范。承与法：一次选择，可以拒绝；承了以后，他的法里才有一条由内而外的不得不。

合起来一句：认不可免，承可拒。认的一半，在它的域里是施行上的必然；承的一半，是选择。判词里没有一处由“他者是目的”推出“当承”。

改形后的两问

他者是目的，若是一个事实，它能不能成为一个人进入 15DD 的理由？能作他自己判断里的一项理由，不能替他承。由一个事实直接推出“当承”，是把一个场域的判决带到另一个场域照常发判——《第一批判》幻相论里的冒领跨场域效力；而且第一人称不可代写：事实再真，也写不成他的法。一时的 15DD 运作可以不经承出现——内观系列说那个方向“自然呈现”；让它成为他法里的一条，靠的是承。

若是一条公设，谁承担？地位一行，论文4 在它的对象域里公开承担；姿态一行，论文6 承担。落到一个人身上，承担的是承了的人，以“我们”之名。《第一批判》说过：“承担一门既有的哲学，它便因承担而成为自己的”。批判自己不承担它：批判只审，只记。

还有原来那一案。方法论00 论证：B 收纳不了 A，所以“必须”以 A 为目的本身——结构意义上的“无法不”。这部书收它的前一半，照录为结构判断：两个位置不相代；后一半读作承。两头都没有一道推理，可以把效力从“不能收纳”运到“当承认”。《第一批判》说的“另行取得”，在这里有了答案：由承了的人，在他自己的法里取得；批判不能替任何人取得。方法论00 那条路的断处不在起点，在从不可吞噬到目的本身那一步。

凭的是站

现在可以回答开头那一问了：一条一个人自己立的法，凭什么约束他？先说它不凭什么。不凭一个事实：从“他者是目的”推不出他当承。不凭一股外加的力：没有谁站在他的法外面替它执行，这部书也不是。不凭理性的事实：康德在这里立了一个自行确立的起点，这部书不立。

它凭的是站。承了，就是站在了一个位置上：不疑别人是目的。站在那里，有些做法就取消不了——不是做不出来，是做了，站在这里与取消它这两句话，就放不到一个人身上。“它说的是两个陈述不相容，不是一种心理上的做不出。”所以这条法的约束，不在一个人外面推他，在他自己的两句话之间。

只要他还站在这里——实际仍持这个前提，而不只是嘴上说——那几件事他就取消不了；一次受阻或没做到，不独自证明他已经撤了这个前提。约束到哪里为止，也就清楚了：到他站的地方为止。一件事上离开了，那件事上这一条对他就不在了；还站着的地方，没有例外。

会不会太轻

这样说，会不会太轻——一个人想不站就不站，法岂不是随他的便？承可拒，这一点不打折扣。可承不是从空地上起步的。在承之前，认已经在了：他冲着别人的意志做事的每一次，动作都已经把对方当作一个能说不的人来对付。认给承的，只是一个起点，不是理由。不承，是一条有人住的分支：一个人可以认对方能说不，却不把他当目的——他的账是一本，站在哪里、付什么，记在他自己的账上。

所以轻不轻，要分开答。对站着的人，约束一点不轻：到他站的地方为止，没有例外；他可以在一件事上离开，可离开要入账，不能当作没站过。对没有站的人，这部书本来就没有一句应当。“法不逼人承；它让人看清，承与不承，各在自己的账上落下什么。”

时间上也是这样。法约束今天站在这里的人，不替明天的那个人签字：明天的他可以改。但改与撂下不同。在这部书这一支里，一条法对立它的人的约束，延续到他认了账地改它为止——认账，是自己说得清，不是向谁报备。三样要分开记：在一件事上离开；改整条法；仍持这条法而这一次没做到。对别人已经作出的承诺，不借一次离开消账。

射程，与论证不到的那一半

首案的射程分三行，三者不是同一个集合。地位一行，按已经承担的对象域——完备主体，准主体（婴儿是最常见的例子），行使能力降低的人：“生理或认知损伤可以降低行使带宽，不降低份位厚度。”承一行，按有相应承担运作的人；诊断一行，按动作。对还说不出不的婴儿，否认者的防范落在第三个人身上——瞒着谁，把他与谁隔开；婴儿的地位用不着替他找能力的证，由公设保着。论文4 判经典硬件上的 AI 不是主体，照录；动物，两处的读数不同，照录，不裁。

退场的边界，这部书不判，只立三条禁线：不以能力的消失定退场；不以他人不再认可定退场；不以宣告定退场——无论宣告出自旁人还是本人。三条说的是不凭这几样撤地位、判退场，不代判一个人事实上是否已经退出。

《第一批判》寄来的那一句，在这里能论证到哪里，就说到哪里：停止这场运作的主体，“其以自身为目的，不再经由这场运作得到实现——仅此而已；这不取消他规范上的目的地位，更不授权任何人把他仅仅当作手段”。前一半由公设推出。“不授权任何人”，对承了的人，是他自己法里的不得不。对没有承、又否认那人能说不的人，能诊断的是他的冒领；对不否认、只是不以那人为目的的人，说得出他站在哪里——说不出他错。“严格只能严格到公设与零点担保的地方，不再多。”连涵育的积极那一半——第三方殖民你时我不沉默——这部书也记作尚待证成，不由薄前提白取。

Three Things, and a Verdict

At this point Kant set a fact of reason, which seeks no deduction. The second critique sets no fact. It puts in three things of different kinds: a postulate openly undertaken — a human being is an end in itself — which governs standing; an in-domain zero point — acts aimed at another’s will concede that he can say no — which governs concession; and a choice — setting non dubito as a premise of one’s own law — which governs adoption.

The First Critique had handed over a case: “even if the structural judgment stands, the validity from ‘cannot be taken in’ to ‘ought to acknowledge’ must still be obtained separately by the practical critique.” The second critique reshaped it into the two questions met in Essay 03. Its verdict comes in four beats. Standing: a postulate, derived from no capacity. Concession: the in-domain zero point; a capacity, not a standing. Diagnosis: whoever denies that others can say no while acting on their will keeps two sets of books — a false claim to which his own precautions testify. Adoption and law: a choice, refusable; only after it is there an inside-out cannot-not in his law.

In one line: concession is unavoidable; adoption can be refused. The concession half is, within its domain, a performative necessity; the adoption half is a choice. Nowhere in the verdict does “the other is an end” yield “one ought to adopt.”

The Two Questions

If the other’s being an end is a fact, can it be a reason for a person to enter 15DD? It can be one reason within his own judgment; it cannot adopt for him. To infer “ought to adopt” straight from a fact carries one field’s verdict into another and issues it there as usual — the First Critique’s false claim of cross-field validity. And the first person cannot be written by another: however true the fact, it cannot write his law. A passing 15DD operation can occur without adoption — Introspection III says the direction “naturally emerges”; what makes it a line in his law is adoption.

If it is a postulate, who undertakes it? The standing row is undertaken openly by Paper 4 in its domain; the posture row by Paper 6. Brought down to a single person, the one who undertakes it is whoever has adopted it, in the name of we. The First Critique: “undertake an existing philosophy, and by the undertaking it becomes one’s own.” The critique itself undertakes nothing; it only examines and records.

And the original case. Methodology 00 argued that B cannot take A in, and therefore “must” treat A as an end in itself — a structural “cannot not.” The book keeps the first half as a structural judgment: the two positions do not substitute for each other. The second half it reads as adoption. At neither end is there an inference that carries validity from “cannot be taken in” to “ought to acknowledge.” The First Critique’s “obtained separately” has its answer: validity is obtained by whoever adopts, within his own law; a critique cannot obtain it for anyone. Methodology 00’s road does not break at its starting point; it breaks at the step from “un-enclosable” to “end in itself.”

It Rests on Where One Stands

Now the question the book opened with can be answered: by what does a law a person has given himself bind him? First, what it does not rest on. Not on a fact: from “the other is an end” it does not follow that he ought to adopt. Not on a force from outside: no one stands outside his law to enforce it, and this book does not either. Not on a fact of reason: Kant set up a starting point that establishes itself; this book sets up none.

It rests on where one stands. To adopt is to have taken up a position: not doubting that others are ends. For one who stands there, certain things cannot be cancelled — not because they cannot be done, but because, once done, the two statements, that he stands here and that he cancels this, cannot sit together in one person. “What it states is that two statements are incompatible, not a psychological inability to bring oneself to act.” So the binding does not push at him from outside; it lies between two statements of his own.

As long as he still stands here — still actually holds the premise, not merely says so — those things he cannot cancel; a single obstruction, or a single failure, does not by itself show he has withdrawn it. Where the binding stops is then plain: where he stands. Where he has left in a given matter, in that matter the line is no longer there for him; where he still stands, there is no exception.

Too Light?

Is this too light — if a person can cease to stand whenever he likes, is the law not at his pleasure? That adoption can be refused is not discounted at all. But adoption does not start from empty ground. Before it, concession is already there: every time he acts on another’s will, the act has already dealt with the other as someone who can say no. Concession gives adoption a starting point, not a reason. Not adopting is an inhabited branch: one can concede that the other can say no and still not treat him as an end — one set of books, its cost on its own account.

So the answer comes in two parts. For one who stands, the binding is not light at all: it runs as far as where he stands, without exception; he may leave in a given matter, but the leaving goes on the books and cannot be treated as though he never stood. For one who does not stand, the book never had a single ought. “The law does not force anyone to adopt; it lets him see what adopting and not adopting each leave in his own books.”

Time works the same way. The law binds the person who stands here today; it does not sign for tomorrow’s person, who may revise. But revising is not dropping. In this branch, a law binds the one who laid it down until he revises it with the books kept — keeping the books meaning he can say it plainly himself, not reporting to anyone. Three things are entered apart: leaving in one matter; revising the whole law; holding the law and not managing it this time. And a commitment already made to others is not written off by one act of leaving.

Reach, and the Half It Cannot Argue

The verdict’s reach runs in three rows that are not one set. Standing goes by the domain already undertaken — complete subjects, subjects-to-be such as infants, people whose capacities are reduced: “Physiological or cognitive damage can lower exercise-bandwidth without lowering station-thickness.” Adoption goes by those who perform it; diagnosis, by acts. For an infant who cannot yet say no, the denier’s precautions fall on third persons — whom to keep in the dark, whom to keep away — and his standing needs no proof of capacity; the postulate keeps it. AI on classical hardware Paper 4 judges no subject, which is recorded; on animals two readings stand side by side, undecided.

On leaving the world, the book draws three lines and no conclusion: exit is not to be judged by loss of capacity, nor by others’ ceasing to acknowledge, nor by declaration — whether the declaration comes from others or from the person himself. These say what does not suffice to revoke standing or to rule an exit; they do not settle whether anyone has in fact exited.

And the First Critique’s sentence is settled as far as it can be argued: a subject who halts “no longer realizes its being-an-end through this operation — that is all; this cancels neither its normative standing as an end, nor licenses anyone to treat it merely as a means.” The first half follows from the postulate. “Nor licenses anyone” is, for whoever has adopted, a cannot-not in his own law. Of one who has not adopted and denies the other can say no, the book can diagnose the false claim; of one who does not deny it and simply does not treat the other as an end, it can say where he stands — and it cannot say he is wrong. “Strictness reaches only as far as the postulate and the zero point warrant, and no further.” Even cultivation’s positive half — not keeping silent when a third party colonizes you — the book records as not yet justified, rather than take it for free from a thin premise.
