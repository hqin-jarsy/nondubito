Essay 06 of 12

说代价，不是强制

Stating the Cost Is Not Coercion

Han Qin (秦汉)

接受一个权力，行使一个权力

接受一个权力。权力论写过拒绝那一边的价：“选择拒绝的代价是退出该叙事承诺的尊严位置”——那一份是叙事收回去的，属于后果。接受那一边的代价，这部书没有查到一句现成的话，于是自己写：一个人让一个权力在自己的行动次序里占了一个位置，那个位置上有些事，此后由这个权力来排。他的法里若有“这几件我自己排”，这一条这一次没有收进去。它常被忽视，因为它常和一份好处一起到来，看上去像买卖。可论职责拒绝了这种读法：“承认性权利不由承认权力换来”。两本账不能对冲。

行使一个权力。权力论把要锁住对方的那一种权力叫凿削。一个凿削的施权者压不到底，于是“被迫永久供力镇压这股余热。这是凿削结构的力学代价，不是 A 的算计”。这份代价有两层：一层是施权者自己的余项被绑进维持的动作，压不到零；一层是可调的自由度被一寸一寸占用，可以耗尽。它常被忽视，因为它慢，而且不一定落在顶端：“一个凿削结构的顶端可以善终而结构整体的账从未清零”。

不是所有行使权力的动作都有这份代价：权力论写明，不执行锁定的权力施为不产生反缚成本；又补了一句，涵育也付成本，“低成本不是零成本”。这里还要钉一根桩：代价的高低，与冒领成不成立无关。权力论自己说过，触发反缚的判别在维持的对象，不在维持成本的高低。便宜的压迫照样是压迫。

追求，放弃，停与不停

追求一项价值，是一次落刀：这一段时间、这一份力气给了这一行，没给别的。价值论写明，接续不是义务——一个承担者面前有一座合法的桥，他可以不去接续，“不因此在价值地位上有任何亏损”。所以追求的代价不落在地位上，落在别的行此刻没有被走的那一段上。它常被忽视，因为追求看上去只有所得。

放弃一项价值：从高处的源回走，“没有价值上的亏损”；让掉一项，不改变被让项的价值地位；只有适用条件已经出现、却主动作废一个源，才“不属于中性的回走”，而它改变的也只是“那一行的可达，不是那一行的在场”。放弃的代价落在可达、窗口与行动一侧，不落在地位上。它常被两头说错：或者说放弃毫无代价，或者说放弃就是丢了自己。

停，和不停。《第一批判》在这里交来两笔。停者之价：停下的人，“其以自身为目的，不再经由这场运作得到实现——仅此而已”。“推进当不当行”：“可推进是能力，不是义务”。第二批判的回答是：推进与不推进，两边都有代价，都说出来；当不当行，在各人自己的法里。不推进的人没有走的那一段记在他自己的账上，正如推进的人没有走的那些路记在推进者的账上。停的代价常被说重，不停的代价常被说没。

防范之梯上的价

一个要把别人当作物来管的人，得常设一套维持：栏要修，路条要查，逃奴法要执行，旁人要一起不收留。控制永远做不完。

这套维持替他分担了什么吗？权力论写过：“反缚不挪不摊”；“装置化分工不能稀释 T6，只能复制 T6”——T6 就是它的反缚定理。照这两句读，每一个亲手查路条、亲手拒收的人，做的是自己的一次锁定，记在自己的账上；奴隶主那一份，一分不少。只收到要求的旁人，不替他登记一份完整的反缚；哪些节点承担，逐项辨认。

维持之劳会耗尽，那是权力论的构层；落在本章账上的，是他压不到零的那一份余项。这里只读价，证词在下一章（第七、八篇）。

别人的代价，我能做什么

这部书不讨论横向的应当。那么，别人的代价，我能拿它做什么？能做的有三样。第一，读：外部读得到的是动作与通道——他关没关自己的复核，别人的改正与退出之路通不通；读的是这些痕迹变化的方向，不是一次判决，也不是他的层级。第二，依自己的法而行：不合作，退出，反对。行动论写明，“退出是不再联络，不是判他不配”；《自己的法》说：“反对是你自己的余项在展开，是你的‘不得不’，不是对他的立法。”

第三，他对我说了不，我不因此给他追加后果。行动论写过一个握着评价权的人：“新人说不，就真接受，事后不因此少给什么。”这里守的是不把对方的拒绝另造为报复的理由；退出、停止额外合作，不在此禁。

不能做的也有三样。不能读构成：外部看不到一个人的法在他里面怎么做成。不能读层级：这部书只按时刻与域记录运作，不以 15DD 给人定类。不能把我的读数变成他的代价：价值论写明，社群“可以给出承认，也可以收回承认”，撤掉的是“社会位置与支持，不是这个承担者自己的价值内容”。收回的，是后果一栏的东西；他那一笔代价，仍在他自己的账上，由他自己说。

四栏地址，五条约束

由此，一个行动有了它的地址，四栏：谁的法；哪个位置；哪件事，哪一刻；哪一条分支。地址写清了，越界也就有了实践一侧的定义。《第一批判》说，越界是“把判据带出它的场域、仍照常发判”；实践一侧照此：把一条法带出它的地址，仍照常要求。

五条约束，全书照守。代价不是惩罚——反缚“是力学成本，不是报应”，道德律把“为了‘让坏人付出代价’的行为”排除在 15DD 之外。代价不产生资格——付出再多，也不使一项内容因此成为你的。代价不是后果。代价不写成勋章，也不写成降格。代价不跨人比较——没有一把同时站在两套法之外的尺。

最后一条有它的分寸。在一个人自己的一次判断里，比较风险、时机与做不做得到，并不受此限——行动论写明，划线“不以一把跨人、跨来源的共同总尺为前提；这不排除在一件事里比较风险、时机与做不做得到”。禁的是把两个人的代价放上同一杆秤，不是禁一个人把自己的那一笔想清楚。

Accepting and Exercising a Power

Accepting a power. Power Theory had written down the cost of refusing: refusal “costs you the position of dignity promised by the narrative” — something the narrative takes back, which is consequence. For the cost of accepting, the book found no ready sentence, and writes one. A person lets a power take a place in the ordering of his own actions, and from then on some things in that place are arranged by it. If his law contains “these few things I arrange myself,” that line was not taken in. It is overlooked because it so often arrives with a benefit; it looks like a purchase. But On Accountability refuses the trade: “A recognition-based right is not got in exchange for recognizing a power.” The two accounts cannot be set off against each other.

Exercising a power. Power Theory calls the power that sets out to lock the other in chiseling power. A chiseling power-holder cannot press the other’s remainder to zero, and so “is compelled to supply force permanently to suppress it. This is the mechanical cost of the severing structure, not A’s calculation.” The cost has two layers: his own remainder bound into the work of maintenance, which cannot be pressed to zero; and his adjustable freedom taken up inch by inch, which can be used up. It is overlooked because it is slow, and because it does not necessarily land on the top: “the apex of a severing structure can end well while the structure’s account is never cleared.”

Not every exercise of power carries this cost: power that performs no locking, Power Theory says, generates no back-binding — and it adds that cultivation, too, has a cost, “low cost is not zero cost.” And one stake must be driven in: how high or low a cost runs has nothing to do with whether a false claim holds. Power Theory itself says the trigger lies in the object of maintenance, not in the size of its cost. Cheap oppression is still oppression.

Pursuing, Giving Up, Stopping

Pursuing a value is a cut: this stretch of time and strength goes to this row and not the others. Value Theory says taking up a value is no obligation — a bearer may decline a legitimate bridge “and stands no worse in value for that.” So the cost of pursuit falls not on standing but on the other rows not walked right now. It is overlooked because pursuit seems to bring only gains.

Giving up a value: walking back from a higher source is “without loss in value”; yielding an item does not change its standing. Only actively voiding a source when its conditions have arrived is “not a neutral walking-back,” and even that alters “the accessibility of that row, not the presence of that row.” The cost of giving up falls on reach, windows and action, not on standing. It is misstated from both ends: either it costs nothing, or it is losing oneself.

Stopping, and not stopping. The First Critique sent two items here. The price of the one who stops: he “no longer realizes its being-an-end through this operation — that is all.” And “whether one ought to advance”: “advanceability is a capacity, not a duty.” The second critique answers that advancing and not advancing both have costs, both are stated, and whether one ought to is settled in each person’s own law. The stretch the non-advancer does not walk goes on his books, as the roads the advancer did not take go on his. The cost of stopping is often overstated; the cost of not stopping is often talked away.

The Ladder, Read for Its Cost

Someone who means to manage others as things must keep a whole apparatus running: fences mended, passes checked, fugitive-slave laws enforced, neighbors who must all join in refusing shelter. The controlling is never done.

Does the apparatus share his burden? Power Theory says back-binding “does not move and does not spread thin,” and that “apparatus-mediated division of labor cannot dilute T6; it can only replicate T6” — T6 being its theorem of back-binding. So everyone who checks a pass with his own hands, or turns the man away with his own hands, performs a lock of his own, and it goes on his own books; the slaveholder’s share is not a fraction smaller. A bystander who merely received a demand is not booked for a full share; which nodes bear it is identified item by item.

The labor of maintenance can be exhausted — that is the layer Power Theory calls the construct. What lands on this chapter’s books is the share of his remainder that cannot be pressed to zero. This chapter only reads the price; the testimony is taken in the next (Essays 07–08).

What I Can Do with Another’s Cost

The book discusses no lateral ought. What, then, can I do with someone else’s cost? Three things. First, read: what can be read from outside is acts and channels — whether he has shut his own review, whether others’ paths to correct and to leave are open; what is read is the direction of these traces, not a verdict and not his level. Second, act by my own law: not cooperate, exit, oppose. Action Theory: “exit is no longer keeping in touch, not a judgment that the other is unworthy.” One’s Own Law: “Opposition is your own remainder unfolding, your own ‘cannot not,’ not legislation upon them.”

Third, when he says no to me, I add no consequence for it. Action Theory writes of a man who holds the power of evaluation: “if the new hire says no, he truly accepts the no, and does not give him any less afterward because of it.” What is kept is a refusal to turn the other’s no into a pretext for retaliation; exit, and stopping extra cooperation, are not forbidden by it.

And three things I cannot do. I cannot read constitution: no one sees from outside how a person’s law is made in him. I cannot read level: the book records operations by moment and domain, and sorts no one by 15DD. I cannot turn my reading into his cost: a community “may confer recognition and may withdraw it,” Value Theory says, but what it removes “is social position and support, not this bearer’s own value content.” What is withdrawn belongs in the consequence column; his cost stays on his own books, his to state.

An Address in Four Columns, and Five Constraints

From this, an act has its address, in four columns: whose law; which position; which matter, which moment; which branch. With the address written out, overstepping gets a practical definition too. The First Critique called overstepping “carrying a criterion out of its field and issuing verdicts as usual”; on the practical side, it is carrying a law out of its address and making demands as usual.

Five constraints, kept through the whole book. Cost is not punishment — back-binding “is a mechanical cost, not retribution,” and Moral Law places outside 15DD the act of “making the bad person pay.” Cost confers no entitlement — however much you pay, that does not make a content yours. Cost is not consequence. Cost is written up neither as a medal nor as degradation. Cost is not compared across persons — no ruler stands outside both laws at once.

The last constraint has its proper limit. Within one person’s own judgment, comparing risk, timing and feasibility is untouched by it: drawing a line, Action Theory says, “does not presuppose a common master scale across persons and across sources; this does not rule out comparing risk, timing and feasibility within one matter.” What is ruled out is weighing two people’s costs on one scale, not thinking carefully about one’s own.
