明末清初——占领一座都城之后

From Ming to Qing — What Taking a Capital Does Not Give You

Han Qin (秦汉)

1645年，刘明偀向新朝提出一份恢复漕运的建议。要办的事并不玄妙：粮额怎么定，运粮的人怎么安排，船从哪里来，河道怎么通。朝廷把建议交给有关部门处理。1

北京已经换了主人，粮食却不会因此自己走到北京。

这件小事很适合拿来读明清交替。我们容易被军队入城、皇帝登基吸引，以为最重要的变化已经发生。可是坐进衙门的人，很快就要面对那些没有随着城门打开而解决的工作。

一份提案得到批转，也还不是船已装粮、河已疏通。新政权要接手的不只是旧名称，而是名称底下许许多多仍在等着落实的关系。

一、李自成不是没有试着治理

李自成在北京实行追赃助饷，拷掠旧官与富户，这是不能淡化的暴力。但若再往下写成“大顺没有任官，没有行政制度，只会打仗”，事实就被一个太整齐的判断挤掉了。

《明史》记载了大顺在西安设官、在北京调整官署、区别授用旧明官员，以及向地方派官。它并不是到了北京，才第一次想到自己需要政府。2

这些安排并不替拷掠开脱。它们反而让问题更具体：已经有官名、任命与命令，为什么军队供给仍会这样侵入人的家产和身体？设置征收者，与形成可持续、可约束的征收办法，相隔多远？

“掠夺不是财政”能表达重要批评，却不能代替对各种收入与供给方式的查问。把所有来源都抹掉，只剩暴力，反而看不清暴力怎样与新设的行政组织结合。

常用来作对照的萧何，也不只是收下秦朝图籍，汉朝便有了治理能力。他还要组织留守、补充兵员、提供粮饷。档案把情况记录下来，人还得把记录接成行动。3

大顺在北京停留四十二天，说明这段占领很短，不说明它所有的政治活动只存在四十二天。撤离北京之后，相关军队与组织还在继续。李自成死亡的具体说法也有歧异，不必替一个简洁结论强行选定一种。

我们可以承认大顺做过建设，也可以批评这些建设如何失败、如何伤人。承认前者，并不减轻后者。

二、南明不是只剩一套空架子

南京原有的官署和人员，给1644年建立的新朝廷提供了基础。继承、监国、登基、分配驻军，都不是在一张白纸上进行。史可法与各镇的往来，也是在处理真实的军队，而不是替没有兵的朝廷写几封空信。4

难处在于，名义上的统属未必能带来协调。军队有自己的驻地、供给和指挥关系；几个将领得到朝廷的职位，不等于他们会在同一个时刻向同一个方向用力。

南明的抵抗还不断改变组合。何腾蛟、堵胤锡与原大顺部众发生合作，原大西将领孙可望、李定国进入永历政权的关系之中。旧日敌我没有永远固定在起点上。4

海上的张名振、张煌言与郑氏力量，又让抵抗不能只按一张陆地版图来理解。船、人、港口和补给连接起来，形成新的活动空间，也面对既有居民和资源，不是退到一片无人之地就可以从头造国。

1659年郑成功军队夺取瓜洲、镇江，进围南京，但没有攻下南京。1661年进攻台湾，1662年郑成功去世，1683年郑氏后继政权投降，分别属于不同阶段。5

把这些过程只称为“苟延残喘”，会让后来二十多年的组织、选择与牺牲从叙述中消失。它们没有取得最后胜利，不等于当时什么都没有做。

三、清朝继承旧制度，也改变旧制度里的人

清朝在1644年前已在建设自己的文官机构。1628年的文馆、1631年的六部、1636年的内三院，是不同阶段；蒙古八旗、汉军八旗也分别形成。八旗不是一个可以直接等同于单一族称的标签。6

入关后，留用部分明朝官员、任命新人、接管图籍与财政记录，都有具体选择。不是所有旧官原样留任，也不是每个地方职位都实行同样的满汉对设。

旧表册能够告诉新朝从哪里征粮，却不保证表上的人仍在那里，船仍能用，河仍能走。刘明偀的漕运建议之所以重要，就在于把“继承明制”拆回几项必须重做的事情。

奏章怎么送、谁经手，也会调整。保留官署名称，不表示它与皇帝、宫廷和其他官署的关系完全没变。历代帝王祭祀的名录同样会被重新安排：声称延续历史的人，也在决定怎样讲述这段历史。16

理藩院的沿革显示，清朝对不同区域与群体使用不同治理安排，但不能把后来扩展的全部职掌和疆域，提前塞到1644年。承认差异的行政技术既有前史，也有变化。

“嫁接”可以是一个比喻，却不能让人以为两套完整制度一接上，就会自然长成一棵树。每次留用、改任、征收与分类，都可能改变具体人的处境。

四、命令落到身体上

剃发尤其不能只作为“文化融合的成本”带过去。

1644年的相关命令曾被撤回，1645年又重新推行。后一命令按到达之日起限定时日，使服从变成身体上必须显示的标记。7

对于发布者，这可能是迅速辨认归顺的办法；对于被要求改变身体的人，却涉及他们如何理解祖先、礼俗与自身。知道一项命令的统治用途，不等于已经承认它有权这样要求。

江阴、嘉定的经过，也不只是几个惨烈的数字。交接、请命、动员、守城、攻城与屠杀，一步步改变了人还能做什么。地方记述中既有亲见，也有传闻和后来的解释；伤亡总数需要另外辨析，不应为了让读者震动而选用未经核实的最大数字。8

但谨慎使用数字，不是把暴力写轻。嘉定记述还涉及反清力量杀害已经剃发的居民，这提醒我们，个人可能同时被不同阵营要求表明忠诚。指出这层强迫，也不抵销清军屠杀的责任。

我们必须分开四件事：某个政策在争夺中起了什么作用，它产生了什么后果，它是否别无选择，以及它是否正当。前两项的说明不能自动完成后两项的论证。

后来统治稳定，并不会把当年的遭遇变成必付而合理的代价。服从也可能来自没有别的活路，不宜一概当作内心同意。

五、不能只按胜负给三方打分

李自成、南明与清朝，很容易被排成三个教学模型：一个只会破坏，一个无力建设，一个成功兼备。经过这些具体处境，再回看这个模型，就会发现它省掉了太多。

失败者做过建设，胜利者也依靠暴力。继承的一方会修改，反对旧秩序的一方也会借用旧职官。最重要的界线，并不总是穿过三个阵营，而常常穿过同一次决定。

第一个问题是，打断原来的统治以后，怎样把供给、沟通和裁断接起来？任命负责的人只是开始。

第二个问题是，保留下来的究竟是什么？纸上的税额、征收的权利与履行的能力，未必能一起继承。

第三个问题是，谁来决定一个人属于哪里？出身、旗籍、官职和对王朝的忠诚不是同一回事。人也可能拒绝别人替自己作出的归类。

第四个问题是，长久存在究竟证明了什么？它能说明一些维持统治的工作做成了，不能单独证明每个承受统治的人都得到了应有的对待。

如果把人当目的，评价就不能只在最后赢家那里结账。一个失去家人的居民，没有义务用未来王朝的寿命来解释自己的损失。

六、让粮食到达，也让意见回来

回到1645年的漕运。确定粮额、安排运户、修船、疏河，一项也不会因为天下换了姓便自动完成。新朝在这里面对的，正是所有宏大胜利背后平凡而不可省略的工作。

但把粮运到京城，还不是全部。若原来的配额已经不合适，承担运输的人能不能说明？船工、粮户与沿途居民付出的代价，能不能回到决定配额的人面前？

一个组织可以把命令顺利传下去，却让经验很难传回来。它也可以在暂时混乱中找到某种有效合作，后来又把合作压成不能更改的服从。这些可能性值得逐件看，不必先用胜利替它们排序。

下一篇将读康雍乾时期的调整。新的奏折通道、军机机构和财政办法确实改变了办事方式，但并不把其他官署一笔抹去，也不让人的处境从此无须再问。

占领一座都城，改变了谁能下命令。能不能接住人的生活，还要从命令发出以后继续看。

史源与说明

命令、制度记载和地方叙事承担不同的说明责任。以下不以获准提案代证执行，也不把后出史书视为完整现场记录。

2. 《明史》卷309，大顺设官授职、追赃与战事；与清实录卷五有关北京交接的记载对读。清方编纂立场须保留距离。

3. 《史记》卷53，萧何的图籍、留守与供给工作，不以单一动作解释汉朝建立。

4. 《明史》卷120、卷274、卷280及卷309，南明建朝、四镇及原顺、西军的合作关系。

5. 《清史稿》卷224，海上行动、1659年南京战役、台湾及1683年结局；不将围城写成占城，不将不同领导者合为一人。

6. 《清史稿》卷114、卷115、卷130，机构与旗制沿革；各阶段职掌不同。

7. 《清实录》卷五、卷十七，1644年撤回与1645年重新推行剃发。命令的时限按接到命令起算，不等于全国同日执行。

8. 《明史》卷277、《江阴城守纪》、《嘉定屠城纪略》。区别亲见、传闻、修辞和后出解释；未据此计算可靠的总伤亡。

In 1645, Liu Mingying submitted a proposal for restoring the grain-transport system. It concerned four practical matters: quotas, the households responsible for carriage, boats and waterways. The new court referred it to the relevant department.1

Beijing had changed hands. Grain did not travel there on the strength of that fact.

The proposal is a useful entrance into the Ming–Qing transition. An army enters a capital and a ruler takes the throne; from a distance, the decisive work appears finished. Up close, officials must discover which records still correspond to people, which demands can be met and which vessels can actually move.

Referral for consideration is not a completed voyage. What the new regime inherited was not only a collection of offices but unfinished relationships between instructions and lives.

I. Li Zicheng Did Attempt to Govern

Li Zicheng's forces subjected former officials and wealthy residents of Beijing to extortion and torture in the pursuit of military funds. That violence should not be softened. It should also not be used to support the inaccurate claim that his Shun regime appointed no officials or created no administration.

The Ming history records institutions established at Xi'an, changes to offices in Beijing, differentiated appointments of former Ming personnel and commissions for provincial posts. Shun did not first discover the need for government after entering the capital.2

These facts make the criticism more exact. Offices and appointments existed; why did supplying the army nevertheless become an assault on bodies and property? How far was the appointment of revenue collectors from a sustainable arrangement that could also constrain their demands?

Saying that plunder is not public finance makes a moral distinction. It does not reconstruct every source of supplies. Violence can work through an administrative organization, not merely where organization is absent. Recognizing that possibility is essential to understanding the failure.

The familiar comparison with Xiao He, who preserved Qin records for the emerging Han, needs similar care. His work also included organizing the rear, replenishing troops and supplying the army. Records were resources for action, not a government that unfolded automatically when the files were opened.3

The forty-two days in Beijing describe one brief occupation, not the entire institutional history of Shun. Forces and relationships continued after the retreat. Accounts of Li Zicheng's death also differ; one should not settle the dispute merely to give the story a clean ending.

Acknowledging construction is not absolution. It lets us ask what that construction accomplished, what it failed to connect and how it harmed those expected to sustain it.

II. The Southern Ming Had More Than an Empty Framework

Nanjing already possessed offices and personnel. The court established there in 1644 could work with existing resources while contesting succession, authority and military deployment. Shi Kefa's dealings with commanders concerned actual forces, not an entirely disembodied claim to legitimacy.4

The problem was coordination. Armies had particular bases, supply arrangements and chains of command. Several generals could hold appointments from the same court without becoming capable of concerted action. A title could name a relationship that still had to be made effective.

Resistance also changed its composition. He Tengjiao and Du Yinxi worked with former Shun forces. Sun Kewang and Li Dingguo, formerly associated with the Great Western regime, entered relationships with the Yongli court. Yesterday's enemies did not remain immovably assigned to their original columns.

Maritime activity further complicates a land-based map. Zhang Mingzhen, Zhang Huangyan and the Zheng forces connected ships, ports, personnel and provisions. The sea was not an empty refuge, and Taiwan was not vacant ground on which a government could be invented without encountering existing lives.

In 1659, Zheng Chenggong's forces took Guazhou and Zhenjiang and besieged Nanjing. They did not capture it. The Taiwan campaign began in 1661; Zheng died in 1662; the surrender of his successors belongs to 1683. Those are distinct stages involving different actors.5

Describing all this as merely lingering before an inevitable end erases organization and choice because they did not produce the final victory. Failure is an outcome to explain, not permission to declare the preceding work nonexistent.

III. Inheriting Institutions Also Changes Them

The Qing entered China proper with institutions already under development. The literary office of 1628, six ministries of 1631 and Three Inner Academies of 1636 belong to successive stages. The Mongol and Han-martial Banners developed at different moments too. Banner membership cannot simply be substituted for a single ethnic designation.6

After 1644, retaining former Ming officials was a selective process, accompanied by new appointments. Nor did the paired Manchu and Han staffing found in central institutions apply uniformly to every office in every locality.

Documents mattered. A register could identify a grain obligation, but it could not guarantee that the registered household remained, that a boat was usable or that the canal was open. Liu Mingying's proposal makes the phrase “inheriting the Ming system” answerable to several tasks rather than one grand act.

The movement of memorials also changed. Preserving an office's name did not preserve every relationship it had with other offices or the palace. Even the list of rulers honored in state ritual could be revised: the inheritors of history were helping decide which history the state would recognize.16

The development of the Lifan Yuan, responsible for particular relations beyond the ordinary provincial administration, illustrates differentiated government. Its later responsibilities and territorial reach should not all be projected backward onto 1644. Accommodation had precedents and acquired new forms; it was not invented once and for all at the conquest.

Grafting is an evocative metaphor, but institutions are not complete branches that grow together naturally once joined. Every retained appointment, changed quota or new classification could alter someone's position. Inheritance itself was an intervention.

IV. When Obedience Has to Appear on the Body

The queue order cannot be passed over as an unfortunate price of cultural integration.

An order concerning hair was withdrawn in 1644. Enforcement was renewed in 1645, with a time limit counted from receipt. Political submission was to become visibly legible on the body.7

For those issuing the instruction, this could serve as a quick test of compliance. For those receiving it, hair could concern ancestry, custom and self-understanding. Explaining a policy's utility to its enforcers does not establish their right to impose it.

The histories of Jiangyin and Jiading include handovers, petitions, mobilization, defense, assault and massacre. These stages matter: they changed what people could still do. Local accounts combine witnessed events, reports from others and later interpretation. Their casualty figures require separate scrutiny rather than selection for maximum emotional force.8

Care with numbers is not a reduction of the violence. The Jiading narrative also records anti-Qing forces killing residents who had already shaved their heads. Individuals could be forced to demonstrate loyalty by more than one side. Naming this additional coercion does not cancel responsibility for Qing massacres.

Four questions must remain separate: what role a policy played, what consequences it produced, whether it was unavoidable and whether it was justified. Answers to the first two do not automatically answer the others.

Later political stability cannot convert an earlier atrocity into a necessary and legitimate investment. Compliance may show that someone lacked a survivable alternative. It does not always reveal assent.

V. Beyond a Scorecard of Winners and Losers

It is tempting to arrange the three contenders into a lesson: Shun could destroy but not build; the Southern Ming could preserve forms but not defend them; the Qing combined the necessary abilities. The concrete histories make that diagram harder to maintain.

Losing regimes undertook construction. Winning ones used violence. Those preserving an inheritance changed it; those rejecting an old order borrowed its offices. Some of the important distinctions run through a single decision rather than between the three camps.

First, how does life continue after authority is interrupted? Feeding, communicating and adjudicating remain work to be done. Assigning responsibility is only the beginning.

Second, what travels with an inherited system? A written quota, the right to demand it and the capacity to meet it may become separated. The grain-transport problem shows how much work lies inside the apparently simple word “continuity.”

Third, who defines belonging? Origin, registration in a Banner, official appointment and dynastic loyalty are different relationships. People may also understand their own lives differently from the categories imposed on them.

Finally, what does durability prove? A long-lived government has managed to sustain certain arrangements. Longevity does not by itself establish that every person under its power could correct a mistaken demand or obtain protection from abuse.

If people are ends, their losses cannot be entered only in the eventual winner's accounts. A survivor has no obligation to interpret a destroyed household through the lifespan of the dynasty that followed.

VI. Letting Grain Arrive—and Experience Return

Return to the canal in 1645. Quotas, carriers, boats and water require attention after the dramatic political moment has passed. No change in the ruling house completes that work by itself.

But getting grain to Beijing is not the whole task. If a quota no longer fits the circumstances, can those responsible for meeting it explain why? Can the experience of boat crews, grain-producing households and communities along the route reach the people who decide what is due?

An organization can transmit orders efficiently while making experience difficult to return. An improvised cooperation can solve a real problem and later harden into an obligation no participant may question. Neither possibility is illuminated by ranking everything according to the final military result.

The next essay follows reforms under Kangxi, Yongzheng and Qianlong. New reporting channels, a military council and altered fiscal practices changed how work was done. They did not abolish every other office, and they did not remove the need to hear from the people whose lives those offices arranged.

Taking a capital changes who can issue commands. Whether a government can meet people's needs has to be investigated after the commands leave the palace.
