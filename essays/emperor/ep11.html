东汉——命令走到村庄，还要经过谁

Eastern Han — Who Carries a Command to the Village?

Han Qin (秦汉)

皇帝在诏书上写下一个决定，事情还没有办成。有人要把它送出去，有人要读懂它，有人知道今年哪几户逃了荒、哪座桥不能过。到了村庄，纸上的一道命令才开始碰见它要改变的生活。

东汉近两百年的历史，可以从这些人之间的联系读起。刘秀恢复了汉的名号，却不能把王莽以前的社会原样搬回来。他需要地方上的帮助，也要重新安排军队、土地和官职。后来使朝廷头疼的那些联系，有些恰恰是它建立时就离不开的条件。

这不是一台机器开机以后慢慢老死的故事。它更像一张不断补缀的网：有的地方断了，有人接上；有的地方越补越紧，紧到消息和人都过不去。

一、交还兵符，不等于从此没有话说

刘秀怎样对待一起打天下的人，常被浓缩为“给钱，不给权”。比刘邦温和，这个印象有根据；但把所有功臣都放进一间富贵而无权的养老院，就会错过真正的安排。

贾复、邓禹交出军事职务后，仍参与重要议论。朱祐在39年交还大将军印，也没有停止建言。军队由谁指挥、封邑的收益归谁、朝廷遇到问题听谁的意见，是几件可以分别处理的事。1

这种分别很重要。如果我们只把人分成“掌权”与“退休”两类，就看不见知识和信任怎样继续被使用，也看不见那些没有兵权的人还能从哪里影响决定。军人的经验并不会因为战争结束就失效，文官也不会因为拿到了印绶便自动懂得一切。

尚书的地位上升，使大量文书更直接地走向皇帝。仲长统后来批评三公权轻，可以帮助我们理解这种偏移，却不能据此说三公、太傅从此只剩好听的头衔。制度仍给他们规定职责；名义职责能有多大效力，还要看具体局面。2

把信息收得更近，可能让一个决定快些作出，也可能让更多事情挤在同一个门口。问题不只是门后坐着的人够不够聪明，还包括谁能进门、谁有机会解释，以及没有得到答复的人还能去哪里。

二、帝国不是先没有中间人，后来才长出豪族

樊宏的父亲樊重经营田地、养殖和器用，家族后来修筑营壁、收容依附的人。这样的地方组织，在刘秀称帝以前便已存在。它提供庇护，也形成依赖；那些来投的人获得安全，并不意味着从此与庇护者平等。3

因此，豪族不能只写成国家运行到后期才冒出来的故障。建国、供给、地方秩序，本来就用到了这些关系。一个地方有事，朝廷需要有人知道去哪里找粮、找工匠、找愿意协商的人。取消中间人的称号，并不会取消这些工作。

袁氏、杨氏跨代出任高官，确实显示了家世和人脉的力量。但家族传下来的也包括经学、学习条件和办事经验。“有优势”不等于“每个人都没有能力”，正如“有人有能力”也不能证明入口没有被垄断。4

察举尤其把这个问题摆在眼前。地方推荐某个人，说他值得任用，朝廷凭什么相信？132年左雄提出年龄与经术、文书能力等要求；133年的试验又牵出不当推荐，对有关官员追责。后来对考核方式仍有争论。5

这不是人人平等的公开考试。没有钱求学、没有机会被看见的人，仍可能站在门外。但它也不是一条从此完全堵死的管道。推荐、复核、追责都有人尝试过，尝试能改变多少，才是值得继续问的问题。

地方网络可以传递知识，也可以挡住新来的知识。通常不是两群截然不同的人在做这两件事，而是同一批联系同时具有两种作用。

三、谁有资格说“这是皇帝的命令”

幼主即位，会使太后、外戚、重臣和内廷人员的位置变得敏感。但“没有成年皇帝”不等于“没有制度”。太后有宫属、有传达命令的官职，也有处理文书的程序。真正困难的，是这些授权彼此冲突时，谁能作出各方接受的判断。6

邓绥摄政期间有限制开支、审查案件、组织校勘典籍等活动。我们可以追问效果和限度，却不能把这一段统治写成两个男人争权之间的空白。她也是在办事的人。

外戚与宦官也不是一架每次都整齐起落的跷跷板。郑众帮助和帝除掉窦宪，后来在邓绥摄政时仍获增封。125年的宫廷冲突中，宦官之间并不同心。135年允许宦官养子袭爵的规定，更提醒我们：他们也有家族、后继者和宫外关系，不是只有皇帝这一根绳可抓。7

168年，窦武、陈蕃谋划清除有权势的宦官。太后并未赞成不加区分地诛除整群人。计划泄露后，局面迅速转成对宫门、书吏、符玺和军队的争夺。谁控制文书和认证，谁就能让自己的行动以“奉诏”的面目出现。8

这比“皇帝太小，所以坏人掌权”更令人不安。一个盖着印的命令，可能确实出自有权的人，也可能出自被逼迫的书吏。前来执行的士兵需要判断，却未必有核实的通道。争夺的不仅是权力，还有让别人相信权力正在合法行使的条件。

四、有人仍能纠错，不等于纠错没有代价

党锢之祸中的“党”，不是今天有党纲和党员证的政党。士人、官员、学生之间有声誉和交往的网络，也有人把这种联系转成指控。评价谁值得任用，本身就触及权力如何分配。

166年发生逮捕，167年有人获释却被禁仕，169年再起迫害，176年连坐范围继续扩大。这些动作伤害的不只是被抓走的人。旁观者会学习：什么话不要说，谁的信不要回，跟谁来往可能毁掉全家。9

但故事不能停在“从此无人再批评”。179年和海上言，连坐范围得到收缩。184年黄巾起事前后，吕强等人主张解除党禁，朝廷有所行动；吕强本人随后又遭迫害。9

没有纠错、纠错很危险、纠错来得太迟，是三件不同的事。说清后两件，不是替迫害减轻责任。恰恰相反，它让我们看见：本来还有可以走的路，却有人把路变窄，甚至让指出路的人付出性命。

一个朝廷可以要求读书人解释秩序的道理，却不能保证他们只在方便的时候运用这些道理。认真相信一种标准的人，会发现执政者也可能不符合标准。把这种发现当成罪，并不能让问题消失，只会改变问题以后被说出来的方式。

五、饥饿不会自己组织成一支军队

张角的太平道不仅有一句口号。访问、弟子、消息传递、治疗仪式和“三十六方”的组织，使分散的人能够相认，并知道下一步做什么。记载中的治病主张可以说明组织怎样吸引人，不是疗效已经获得验证。10

生病、缺粮、无处申诉的人，需要的不只是一种解释，还可能需要有人上门、有人照看、有人愿意听。新的联系从这些需要中生长，却不会自动变成同一种行动。起事需要安排；计划被告发后，行动又被迫提前。不能把184年写成各地在同一时刻整齐按下开关。

这也不是中国历史上此前从未出现过宗教性动员。陈胜起事的征兆、赤眉的巫祝材料，都不容我们把早先的反抗一概叫作世俗行动。儒家经典里也并非只有无条件服从，《孟子》对失去为君资格者的批评就是另一种语言。它不等于人人实际享有反抗权，却足以提醒我们：当时可用的说法不只一条。11

汉廷在184年仍能组织中央兵力、征募和地方协同，击败黄巾的主要力量。之后的战事却没有因此全部结束。更重要的是，谁来征人、运粮、守住一片地方，逐渐改变了掌握资源的人与中央之间的关系。

朝廷不是把“权力”这个抽象东西交出去以后突然发现收不回来。它授权了一些具体工作。做这些工作的人有了部队、供给和相互依赖；要把它们交给另一个人，需要解决的事情已经比收回一纸任命多得多。

六、184、188、189、196、220，不是同一天

188年，刘焉提出强化州牧地位，试图处理地方监察无力和动乱。任命可以增强治理，也改变了地方力量的组合。但刘焉仍须与地方人士合作，这些人后来也可能与他冲突。不是所有“大族”都站在同一边，更不是一道命令就使每一州同时独立。12

董卓有更早的边地军旅经历。189年朝廷要求他改任或交兵时，他用军队的依赖关系为拒绝辩解。那是一个参与者的说辞，却也暴露出问题：纸上可以换统帅，士兵的信任、军需和生路却不能随印绶自动转移。

何进、袁绍把外军引入宫廷斗争，何进被杀，随后发生对宦官的大规模杀戮，董卓进入并控制局面。这是一串有选择、有意外、有暴力责任的过程，不是“中央已死”以后一个空位自然被填上。12

甚至到194年，史书仍记下献帝检查赈济粥食的一件小事：已有粮食拨出，人却仍在死亡，他要求核查实际煮出的数量。这不能证明救济普遍成功，却说明最后几十年并非完全没有行政和追问。13

196年，朝廷迁到许，在曹操保护和控制下继续使用汉的官职、名号与授权，同时重新组织供给。220年才是正式的汉魏禅代。皇帝的行动受到限制，不等于“皇帝”这个位置已不再影响别人怎样行事。

七、网断在哪里，就要具体看哪里

东汉留给我们的，首先是授权的后续问题。帮忙的人承担了长期任务，也积累了资源；怎样监督他、替换他，同时不让那件事无人继续做？

其次是反馈的代价。一个坏消息今天没有传进来，可能不是下面太平，而是传消息的人已经知道不能传。晚来的纠正值得认真记录，也不能替早先失去的人补回生命。

第三是没有被照顾的需要。它们不会因为不在公文里就消失，却也不会自行决定要走向宗教、军队还是新的地方合作。有人必须把它们接起来，给出说法，组织行动。余项不是会自行行军的物质。

第四是应急之后的工作。为一次危机授予的资源，危机过去并不自动归零。谁继续供应，谁承担旧承诺，谁有权结束授权，都需要新的安排。

最后，是不要让已知的结局吞掉过程。知道东汉终于灭亡，不意味着左雄的试验、和海的上言、某次救济检查从一开始就毫无意义。它们让我们看见，历史中的人确实曾经动手修过，而不是两百年都在等待预先写好的终点。

下一篇的曹操、刘备、孙氏与司马氏，继承的也不只是一个空壳。他们面对的是仍在工作的人、关系和要求。重建秩序，不会是选好一套模型再往里放人的过程。

命令走到村庄，需要中间有人。一个秩序能走多远，也取决于这些人是否只能替上面传话，还是能把下面的话带回来。

An emperor issues an order. Someone still has to take it down the road. Someone must explain it, find the households named in it, and discover that the bridge is gone or the grain it demands has already been eaten.

Government happens along that journey. The Eastern Han, conventionally dated 25–220 CE, depended on people who connected its court to the lives it governed. Some of the relationships later blamed for weakening the dynasty had helped make its restoration possible.

That is a more useful starting point than an old machine slowly wearing out. A machine metaphor makes it easy to overlook people who repair a connection, close one to outsiders, or discover that a message can no longer travel back.

I. Returning a command did not mean having nothing to say

Liu Xiu, the restored dynasty's founding emperor, is often credited with retiring his military companions in comfort instead of killing them. There is an important contrast with the early Western Han here. But comfort, command, and participation in government were not a single package that could only be retained or surrendered whole.

Jia Fu and Deng Yu continued to participate in important discussions after relinquishing military positions. Zhu You returned his general's seal in 39 and subsequently offered proposals. Income from an estate, command of troops, and access to deliberation could be arranged separately.1

That distinction changes the story. Military experience did not become useless when fighting ended. Nor did transferring an official seal transfer every relationship and every piece of practical knowledge associated with its former holder. Removing someone's command could reduce one danger while preserving a contribution the court still needed.

The growing importance of the imperial secretariat brought documents and decisions closer to the emperor. Zhongchang Tong later criticized the diminished power of the highest ministers. His criticism matters, but it does not erase the responsibilities assigned to those offices in administrative texts. A stated responsibility and the ability to exercise it are different kinds of evidence.2

Direct access could speed a decision. It could also leave many decisions waiting at the same door. The question was not simply whether an intelligent adult sat behind it. Who could enter? Who could explain a difficulty? Where could an unanswered report go next?

II. The intermediaries were there from the beginning

Fan Hong's family offers a glimpse of local organization before the restoration. His father Fan Zhong managed agriculture and other resources; the family's fortified settlement sheltered dependent people during disorder. Such protection could preserve lives while deepening dependence. Receiving shelter did not make a household its protector's equal.3

Powerful local families were therefore not merely an unwanted growth that appeared after an originally direct relationship between emperor and household had decayed. The new dynasty already needed local knowledge, supplies, cooperation, and defense. Abolishing an intermediary's title would not abolish the tasks being performed.

Families such as the Yuan and Yang maintained connections to high office across generations. Their advantages included access to learning as well as access to patrons. Neither fact cancels the other. A capable official might benefit from an unfair entrance; inherited advantage did not prove that every beneficiary lacked ability.4

Recommendation exposed the difficulty. A local authority presented someone as worthy of office. How could the court know? In 132, Zuo Xiong proposed requirements concerning age and competence in classical learning or administrative writing. A disputed candidate in the following year's examinations led to scrutiny of recommendations and penalties for some of those responsible. Subsequent officials continued arguing about the tests.5

This was not an open competition with equal opportunities. Education, travel, and being noticed still required resources. Yet it was also not a completely sealed channel. People tried to connect reputation with an assessment that another office could check.

A family network could carry knowledge and keep other knowledge out. Often the same relationships did both. What mattered was whether an outsider's experience, or an inconvenient result, had a route into the decision.

III. Who could make an order count as the emperor's?

Several Eastern Han rulers came to the throne as children. Their accession sharpened questions about who could act for them. It did not create a space without institutions. Empress dowagers had titles, court personnel, and procedures for issuing and transmitting commands. The harder problem was resolving conflicts between these powers.6

Deng Sui's regency included expenditure restrictions, judicial review, and work on transmitted texts. The accounts do not certify the universal success of her policies. They do show that a regency could be a period of government, rather than an empty interval between men's struggles for control.

The empresses' relatives and the eunuchs of the inner court were not two permanently unified teams taking turns on a seesaw. Zheng Zhong helped Emperor He remove Dou Xian in 92, yet received an enlarged fief during Deng Sui's regency in 107. Eunuchs took different sides in the succession conflict of 125. A rule allowing adopted sons to inherit their titles in 135 also makes nonsense of the claim that they had no family interests or relationships outside the palace.7

In 168, Dou Wu and Chen Fan prepared action against powerful eunuchs. The dowager did not agree to an indiscriminate removal of the whole group. Once the plot was exposed, control of gates, scribes, documents, seals, and troops became decisive. Opposing parties claimed to be acting for the ruler.8

This was more than a contest for influence over a young person. It was a contest over what could be recognized as an authorized command. A soldier facing a sealed order might have little means of discovering who had compelled the scribe to write it. The mechanisms for authenticating authority had become part of the struggle for authority itself.

IV. A correction can be possible and still cost a life

The people persecuted under the “partisan prohibitions” did not belong to a political party in the modern sense. Officials, students, scholars, and supporters had networks of association and reputation. Those connections could sustain criticism or become the basis of an accusation.

Arrests in 166 were followed by release with exclusion from office in 167, renewed persecution in 169, and a further widening of liability in 176. The damage extended beyond those arrested. Others learned which letters not to answer and which friendships might place their families at risk.9

But “criticism was silenced forever” is not an accurate ending. He Hai's intervention in 179 helped narrow collective liability. In 184, amid the Yellow Turban crisis, Lü Qiang and others advocated lifting the prohibitions. Changes were made; Lü Qiang himself was subsequently persecuted.9

No correction, a dangerous correction, and a correction that comes too late are different conditions. Recording the latter two does not soften the violence. It shows that possibilities existed and that people made them harder to use. A late reversal cannot restore the lives already lost.

The court required educated people to explain the standards of good government. It could not ensure that they would apply those standards only when convenient. People who take a principle seriously may find that a ruler violates it. Punishing that discovery does not remove the discrepancy. It teaches the next observer what reporting it may cost.

V. Hunger does not organize itself into an army

Zhang Jue's movement offered more than a slogan. Visits, disciples, messages, healing rituals, and the organization of thirty-six divisions helped dispersed people recognize one another and coordinate action. Reports of healing describe the movement's practices and appeal; they are not clinical evidence of effectiveness.10

Someone who was ill or hungry might need care, a listener, and a person willing to come to the door. Those needs could sustain new relationships, but they did not dictate a single political outcome. Organizers supplied explanations and arrangements. When the planned rising was betrayed, action came early. The events of 184 were not a synchronized switch being thrown everywhere at once.

Nor was this the first revolt to involve religious practices. The accounts of Chen Sheng's omens and the Red Eyebrows' spirit mediums prevent a simple contrast with supposedly secular predecessors. Confucian texts also contained arguments against rulers who had forfeited their claims. The relevant passage in the Mengzi did not confer an enforceable right of resistance on everyone, but obedience was not the only language available.11

The Han court still assembled central troops, recruits, and regional support to defeat major Yellow Turban forces. That achievement did not end every subsequent conflict. Meanwhile, the work of recruiting, feeding, and keeping troops together altered the relationships through which force could be exercised.

The court did not hand over an abstract quantity called power and then suddenly discover that it was missing. It authorized tasks. People acquired supplies, followers, obligations, and expectations through performing them. Transferring the task again required more than recalling an appointment letter.

VI. Five dates, not one collapse

In 188, Liu Yan advocated strengthening provincial governors in response to ineffective supervision and regional violence. The resulting appointments changed the possibilities for combining local administration with armed force. They did not instantly turn every province into an independent state. Liu Yan still needed local cooperation, and some early supporters later became opponents.12

Dong Zhuo's military relationships also had a history before the Yellow Turbans. In 189 he resisted orders concerning his position and troops, invoking the soldiers' dependence on him. That was an interested participant's justification, not a survey of every soldier's wishes. Nevertheless, it pointed to a real difficulty: a new commander's seal did not automatically bring new arrangements for supplies and trust.

He Jin and Yuan Shao brought outside forces into a court struggle. He Jin was killed, extensive killings of eunuchs followed, and Dong Zhuo entered the crisis and asserted control. These were decisions and violent actions, not the inevitable filling of an organizational vacuum.12

Even afterward, not every administrative activity ceased. The annals describe Emperor Xian checking famine relief in 194: grain had supposedly been allocated, yet people continued dying, so the preparation and issue of gruel were investigated. One episode cannot establish that relief worked throughout the country. It does prevent us from treating the dynasty's final decades as entirely devoid of inquiry and government.13

In 196 the court moved to Xu under Cao Cao's protection and control. Han offices and authorizations remained useful while supplies were reorganized. The formal abdication came in 220. The emperor's restricted freedom and the political importance of the imperial office were not mutually exclusive.

VII. Look at where the connection breaks

Five questions remain. First, what happens after assistance becomes a durable responsibility? A useful intermediary may acquire the means to pursue interests of his own. How can his mandate be checked, transferred, or ended without leaving the work undone?

Second, what does reporting a problem cost? The absence of unwelcome news may indicate that messengers have learned to keep quiet, not that conditions have improved. A correction matters even when it comes too late to repair everything.

Third, who takes up needs the accepted procedures leave unanswered? An illness or unresolved grievance does not disappear because a permitted report has no place for it. Nor does it march into rebellion by itself. People must interpret, connect, and organize. The remainder in this account is not a substance acting without human choices.

Fourth, what follows an emergency? Resources granted for a campaign do not automatically vanish when the original threat recedes. Old promises, supplies, and dependencies require further decisions. Calling a measure temporary does not settle those decisions in advance.

Finally, what do we lose by reading backward from the dynasty's end? Zuo Xiong's tests, He Hai's petition, and an inquiry into relief were not meaningless simply because Han eventually fell. They show people trying to alter the conditions in which they lived. Their efforts and the destruction of their opportunities both belong in the story.

Cao Cao, Liu Bei, the Sun family, and the Sima would inherit more than a broken shell. Offices, knowledge, troops, and claims remained. Rebuilding would not mean choosing a model and then filling it with interchangeable people.

An order needs intermediaries to reach a village. A government also needs them to bring back what the order failed to see.
