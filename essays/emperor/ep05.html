春秋——天子还在，谁来作决定

Spring and Autumn — The King Remains, but Who Decides?

Han Qin (秦汉)

天子还在，有些事情却已经要去找别人。

这比天子突然消失更难理解。称号、礼仪、朝见的语言还在使用，军队、粮食和决定权却不再总是随它们一起到来。一个人名义上处于最高位置，不等于每一件需要最高决定的事都能由他办成。

春秋的故事就从这道缝隙里展开。不是旧秩序一夜化为乌有，而是同一套关系中的不同部分，逐渐被不同的手握住了。

一、迁走的是朝廷，不是整片土地

通常以公元前770年平王东迁作为春秋时代的开端。洛邑并不是到这时才为安置一个失势君主而建的象征城市，《召诰》已经讲述周人在洛水一带的早期营建。但重新以那里为中心，改变了朝廷取得资源和支持的条件。1

一个朝廷可以搬运器物，田地、道路、要塞以及在当地劳动和作战的人，却不能这样装车带走。谁能护送，谁能供应，谁能使一条路保持畅通，就会在新的处境里获得分量。

这种依赖并不意味着王室马上没有自己的军队。前707年的繻葛之战，《左传》仍写到王的军队和联合出战的诸侯军。问题在于，拥有军队与能够独立处理所有重大冲突，相距很远。2

齐、晋、楚等力量也不是在东迁那一年同时长成后来地图上的样子。各自的地域、邻居、资源与同王室的关系不断变化。名位、可用资源、动员能力、别人接受决定的意愿，开始越来越难集中在一个位置上。

所以，“空”只能是追问的起点：究竟哪一部分空了？若一切都毫无意义，为什么强者还要借用王的承认？

二、替天子做事的人

地方受到威胁，需要援助；彼此发生争执，需要有人协调；几支军队一起行动，需要商量谁出什么、谁先谁后。王不能独自办成的事情，并不会因此不再需要办理。

齐桓公与管仲所关联的霸政，就在这样的关系中取得位置。会盟、救援、军事行动、仲裁与负担，让“尊王”不只是一句口号，也让霸者不能完全只为自己行事。

前651年葵丘之会，王使送来祭肉，齐桓公坚持按礼拜受。《左传》把这个动作留了下来。力量已经在别处聚集，承认却仍需要通过旧的位置表达。3

晋文公时，践土的王命与河阳召王，又把这种张力推得更明显：诸侯可以实际推动王的行动，同时还要为这次行动寻找能够被书写、被接受的名义。

这里不能把所有人都写成早已看穿、只是在演戏。不同人可能真诚重视共同形式，也可能利用它，更多时候，两者混在一起。共享语言的价值，就在它并不完全属于其中任何一个使用者。

霸政也不是毫无组织的个人魅力。齐桓公死后的争位暴露了交接困难，不等于所有行政关系同时消失。晋文公的三军与后来逐步形成的卿族格局，也不能压成他一次设计好的“六卿制度”。4

前546年的弭兵交涉有往来、例外、先后与戒备，恰好让我们看见共同安排怎样在不信任中被做出来。它没有带来永久和平，却不能因此被当成从未有过作用。4

三、为什么还要僭礼

季氏用了八佾。孔子愤怒，不只是因为舞者多了几排，而是一个较低位置的人在使用更高位置的形式。

这件事同时说明两点：规范受到挑战；挑战者仍愿意占有规范所赋予的可见地位。如果礼只剩彻底无意义的空壳，何必一定要用这一种舞？5

朝见也是如此。关系可以被拒绝、重组、借用，却不能一概说整个春秋再无人朝王。葵丘、践土、河阳各有不同安排，正表明名义与行动之间还有需要处理的联系。

《论语》谈礼乐征伐从天子、诸侯、大夫乃至陪臣发出，并用十世、五世、三世作警告。这里讲的是相应权力主体难以长久不失，不是三次权力转移各自花了多少代人的计时表。5

其中仍有值得抓住的洞见：受托办理事务的人，会积累使事务运转的资源；这些资源又可能使他摆脱原先的委托者。名位能规定谁应当决定，却未必能使实际决定一直留在那个人手里。

四、郑庄公的箭，和当晚的慰问

郑庄公的故事，先从家里开始。弟弟段与他同母，母亲武姜偏爱弟弟。京地、不断扩大的控制范围、支持者与准备行动，使这场冲突不只是抽象的长幼顺序之争。6

郑与周王室之间也不只有“臣服”两个字。王室职务、人质交换、割取作物，都是彼此依赖又互不信任的具体形式。人质尤其不是一种没有生命的信用工具，而是被放进关系风险之中的人。

前707年的叙述次序是：王夺去郑伯相关政职，郑伯不朝，王率诸侯伐郑。战斗中，王的肩膀被射中，却仍能维持军阵。郑庄公没有让部下继续追击，当晚还派祭足去慰劳王。2

这一箭动摇了不可轻易挑战的最高位置，慰问却保留了某种关系。庄公拒绝的要求，与他仍愿意承认的名位，并不完全相同。

如果只写“这一箭射死了天子的威信”，后半夜的行动就变得多余。恰恰是这段没有省掉的过程，让我们看见春秋政治：你可以在一件事上战胜对方，却仍需要同对方处在一个可以继续往来的世界里。

五、楚不只在里面，也不只在外面

楚的王号挑战了周的等级语言。但它的形成不是熊通突然想到一个新称呼那么简单。《史记》也记熊渠先给诸子王号、后来又撤回。地位与力量的关系，是反复被尝试、衡量和争夺的。7

熊通请求提升名位而未获满足，后来自己称王。读到这里，很容易替周王室开出一个反事实处方：早给他升格不就好了？可是，一次拒绝不能证明另一种选择必然化解后来的全部冲突。

更重要的是，楚并未从此完全退出共同语言。召陵的贡茅之问、楚庄王问鼎，都在争论与周有关的责任、位置和象征。一个人可以挑战他在关系中的位置，而不抛弃这段关系的所有内容。8

楚对土地的组织也有新尝试。陈被纳入县的安排，随后因申叔时的异议又恢复其后，显示改变不是一个不可撤销的开关。军事胜利之后怎样治理，仍要经过理由、反对和决定。7

这使“构外挑战者”更适合作为一种局部位置，而不是楚的永恒本性。它在不同事情上使用、拒绝或改造共同形式。体系不应把对某项要求的拒绝，自动解释为对一切合作的拒绝。

六、管仲：把事情办起来以后

《国语·齐语》把管仲相关的治理安排写得十分具体：人在哪里住，平日怎样协作，到了军事行动时怎样相互配合。职业、邻里、婚丧与互助进入同一幅组织图景。9

四民分业因而不只是一张控制名单。熟悉的人一起工作，可以传递技艺、减少临时组织的困难，也可能把人的未来锁进已经分好的位置。《齐语》还保留农民中适任者可以被举用的说法，不能把这套安排直接概括成所有流动都已禁止。

国与鄙有不同任务和负担，但差别需要具体说，不能一步改写成一边拥有全部权利、另一边没有任何权利。今天熟悉的身份表，未必就是古人关系的准确说明。

《管子·海王》关于盐铁的讨论，则让财政换了一种进入日常的方式。直接要求每个人交一笔钱容易招致反对，借必需品及其价格取得收入，看起来可以不把征取摆在眼前。10

不过，账面上变得不显眼的要求，仍由购买和使用的人承担。对机构来说更容易取得收入，与对居民来说负担更轻，不能直接画等号。

这里也不能把《管子》各篇都当作管仲任内的政策档案，进而断言盐铁生产销售已经由他一人全面垄断。这些不同层次的材料，给我们的是可讨论的治理思路，不是一张现成的施政总表。

管仲的工作并未用利益把关系全部替掉。它恰恰借助邻里、信任和共同生活来组织事情。《史记》还说他死后齐国继续遵行其政。11 有些安排能够离开创设者继续，有些交接却仍然困难。把两者分开，才能看见改进究竟发生在哪里、又停在哪里。

七、冰和水之间的人

我仍愿意用“相变”来形容春秋：旧形式与新的力量分布长时间并存，变化不同步，不同位置上的人仿佛生活在不同温度里。

但比喻不能替人作决定。盟约会被遵守或撕毁，旧礼会被修正或强行维护；同一个困难，也可能引出合作、迁移、战争或者等待。历史不是冰一到某个温度就只能融成同一种水。

孔子尤其不适合只被放在“明知旧制度已死仍要复原”的位置上。“知其不可而为之”在《论语》中是石门守门人的评价，不是孔子的一份失败自白。而冠与拜礼的不同取舍，又让我们看见他如何在具体事情上判断哪些可以变、哪些不该轻易放弃。12

保存与改变的难处在于，你不能只凭旧或新给一个做法打分。你得说清它在维护什么，要求谁付出什么，还能不能听见被它影响的人。

八、留给战国的，不是一张空白纸

子产铸刑书以后，叔向来信反对，子产也作了回应。规则公开怎样改变争议，人们是否会只据文字相争，都进入了讨论。子产又不肯毁掉议论执政的乡校，把批评看作自己可以听取的东西。13

这些不是现代法治与民主已经全部到位，却是具体的人在试着改变决定与受影响者之间的关系。制度创新不必等到一个时代正式结束，才被允许开始。

“正名”也不只是把称号摆整齐。《论语》将名与言、事务、礼乐、刑罚以及人们如何行动连在一起：人需要知道自己面对的要求是什么意思，而不能只在权力已经作决定之后猜测。14

后来所谓三家分晋，也有智氏败亡、前403年周王承认韩赵魏及晋最终消失等不同节点。旧关系被打破、实际地位形成、名义得到承认，不总发生在同一天。15

因此，春秋留给战国的是已有的经验、办法和未解的要求，不是旧制度清空以后等待新思想填入的真空。战争使一些改变更迫切，也使许多生活遭到破坏；它不会自动保证留下来的方案最好。

天子还在，决定权却移动了。下一篇，我们将看到竞争怎样越来越直接地进入制度设计，又怎样让“能够赢”与“值得让人生活其中”之间的距离变得尖锐。

The king is still there. For some matters, however, people have to go elsewhere.

This is harder to understand than a king simply disappearing. Titles, rites, and the language of royal audiences remain in use, while troops, grain, and decisions no longer reliably arrive with them. Occupying the highest position is not the same as being able to settle every question requiring the highest decision.

The Spring and Autumn story opens in that gap. An old order does not vanish overnight. Different parts of its relationships increasingly come to be held by different hands.

I. A court can move; its whole landscape cannot

King Ping's eastward move in 770 BCE conventionally marks the beginning of the period. Luoyi was not a symbolic city invented to accommodate a powerless ruler. The “Announcement of Shao” already describes earlier Zhou work in the Luo River region. Making it the renewed center nevertheless altered the court's access to resources and support.1

A court can pack its objects. Fields, roads, strongpoints, and the people who work or fight there cannot be loaded in the same fashion. Those able to escort, supply, and secure routes acquire weight in the new situation.

Dependence does not mean the royal house immediately loses its own troops. The Zuozhuan account of the battle of Xuge in 707 BCE still describes royal forces and allied contingents. Possessing an army and independently resolving every major conflict are different capacities.2

Qi, Jin, Chu, and other powers also do not appear in their later configurations at the instant of the move. Territory, neighbors, resources, and royal connections change over time. Recognized rank, available means, mobilizing capacity, and willingness to accept decisions become harder to gather in one position.

“Hollow” is therefore a starting question rather than a complete answer. What has become hollow? If everything associated with the king has ceased to matter, why do powerful people still seek his recognition?

II. Doing work the king cannot finish alone

A threatened community needs help. A dispute calls for coordination. Several armies must agree on contributions and precedence. Work does not cease to be necessary because the king cannot undertake it alone.

The leadership associated with Duke Huan of Qi and Guan Zhong grows within these relationships. Meetings, assistance, campaigns, arbitration, and burdens make “honoring the king” more than a slogan. They also prevent a leading lord from acting entirely for himself.

At Kuiqiu in 651 BCE, a royal envoy brings sacrificial meat, and Huan insists on receiving it with the appropriate bow. The Zuozhuan preserves the act. Power has gathered elsewhere, but recognition still passes through an older position.3

Under Duke Wen of Jin, royal authorization at Jiantu and the summoning of the king to Heyang intensify the tension. Lords can practically arrange royal movement while still needing an acceptable account of what that movement means.

Calling all of this theater assumes too much about the participants. Some may value the shared forms, some exploit them, and many do both. A common language matters partly because it never belongs completely to one of its users.

Hegemonic leadership is not merely personality without organization. The succession struggle after Duke Huan's death exposes a serious weakness without showing that all administration disappears. Duke Wen's three armies likewise should not be confused with the later configuration of Jin's powerful ministerial houses.4

The negotiations to reduce warfare in 546 BCE involve exchanges, exceptions, precedence, and suspicion. They show a common arrangement being made amid distrust. Its failure to produce permanent peace does not make its limited effects imaginary.4

III. Why appropriate the ritual?

The Ji family uses an eight-row dance formation. Confucius objects not merely to a larger performance, but to a lower position displaying a higher one.

Two things become visible together. The norm is challenged, and the challenger still wants the status conveyed by its form. If ritual were wholly empty, why choose this particular dance?5

Royal audiences and meetings require similar discrimination. Connections can be refused, rearranged, and borrowed without every audience throughout the period having stopped. Kuiqiu, Jiantu, and Heyang show different efforts to manage a continuing relation between standing and action.

The Analects describes rites, music, and military expeditions issuing from the king, lords, ministers, and eventually their dependents. Its ten, five, and three generations warn of the difficulty of retaining the power in question. They are not timings for three completed downward transfers, and cannot be read as measurements of historical acceleration.5

There is nevertheless a substantial insight here. People entrusted with work acquire resources that make the work possible. Those resources can also make them less dependent on whoever entrusted them. Rank specifies who ought to decide; it does not ensure that practical decisions remain there.

IV. Duke Zhuang's arrow—and the visit that night

Duke Zhuang of Zheng's story begins within his family. His brother Duan shares the same mother, who favors the younger son. A base at Jing, expanding control, supporters, and preparations give the conflict substance beyond an abstract dispute about seniority.6

Zheng's connection with the royal court is also more than the word “submission.” Offices, exchanged hostages, and the taking of crops give dependence and distrust specific forms. The hostages, especially, are people exposed to the risks of a relationship, not lifeless instruments of credit.

For 707 BCE, the Zuozhuan gives a sequence: the king removes Zheng's ruler from his relevant royal responsibilities; Zheng ceases attending court; the king leads allied forces against him. An arrow wounds the king's shoulder, but he remains able to direct his army. Zhuang refuses further pursuit and sends Ji Zu to inquire after the king that night.2

The arrow challenges the supreme position. The visit preserves a connection. Zhuang's refusal of a particular demand and his continued recognition of standing are not identical choices.

If the story ends with “the arrow killed royal prestige,” the night visit becomes superfluous. Keeping it changes what we understand. You can defeat someone over one matter and still need to inhabit a world in which further dealings with that person remain possible.

V. Chu is not simply inside or outside

Chu's claim to kingship challenges Zhou's language of rank. Yet even the title's development is not a single person's invention. The Shiji describes Xiong Qu giving royal titles to his sons and later withdrawing them, before the better-known claims of Xiong Tong. Position and power are repeatedly tested and contested.7

Xiong Tong seeks an elevation in standing, fails to obtain it, and claims kingship himself. It is tempting to prescribe an easy alternative for the Zhou: why not grant the promotion? But a refused request cannot prove that acceptance would have prevented every subsequent conflict.

More importantly, Chu does not leave all common language behind. The dispute at Shaoling over tribute and King Zhuang's inquiry about the royal tripods argue over obligations and symbols connected with Zhou. A participant can reject an assigned position without rejecting every element of the relationship.8

Chu also experiments with territorial organization. The annexation of Chen into a county arrangement, followed by its restoration after Shen Shushi's objection, supplies an actual sequence rather than an irreversible institutional switch. Governance after conquest still encounters reasons, objections, and decisions.7

“Outside challenger” is thus better understood as a position concerning certain demands, not Chu's timeless essence. It uses, refuses, and transforms common forms in different matters. An order should not automatically interpret refusal of one requirement as refusal of all cooperation.

VI. Guan Zhong: after the work begins to function

The “Qi Discourses” in the Guoyu presents arrangements associated with Guan Zhong in practical terms: where people live, how they cooperate, and how ordinary organization supports military action. Work, neighborhood, marriages, funerals, and mutual assistance enter the same picture.9

Grouping occupations can transmit skills and reduce the difficulty of assembling people at short notice. It can also confine futures to positions already assigned. The text retains a route for appointing suitable people from farming households; this is not adequately described as an absolute prohibition on all mobility.

Core and outlying districts bear different responsibilities. Those differences need explaining rather than conversion into a modern chart in which one group possesses every right and the other possesses none. Familiar categories are not automatically accurate descriptions of unfamiliar relations.

The “Haiwang” chapter of the Guanzi approaches revenue through necessities such as salt and iron. A direct charge visibly demands something from each person. Income drawn through essential goods and their prices can make extraction less conspicuous.10

What becomes less visible in the state's account is nevertheless borne by people who buy and use the goods. Easier collection is not automatically a lighter burden. The institution's improved ability to obtain resources and the resident's improved life require separate judgments.

The Guanzi collection is not simply Guan Zhong's contemporary policy archive. Its arguments cannot all be converted into measures he personally implemented, including complete state control of every stage of salt and iron production and sale. The materials preserve political reasoning at different historical layers.

Nor do these arrangements replace moral relationships entirely with incentives. They use neighbors, trust, and shared life to organize tasks. The Shiji also says Qi continued following Guan Zhong's policies after his death.11 Some practices outlive a founder while other transitions remain fragile. Distinguishing those outcomes lets us see where improvement occurred and where it stopped.

VII. People between ice and water

“Phase change” remains a useful image for this period. Older forms and new distributions of power coexist; changes proceed unevenly; people in different positions seem to inhabit different temperatures.

But the image cannot make their decisions. Agreements are honored or broken, inherited rites adjusted or enforced. One difficulty can lead to cooperation, migration, warfare, or waiting. History is not ice obliged to become a single kind of water at a measured temperature.

Confucius particularly resists the simple role of a man who knows an order is dead and insists on restoring it unchanged. The description of him as acting despite knowing the difficulty comes from a gatekeeper at Shimen, not from Confucius delivering a confession of failure. His different judgments about caps and bowing show selective reasoning about what can change.12

Preservation and change both demand more than approval of the old or the new. What relationship is a practice protecting? Who pays for it? Can the people it affects still be heard?

VIII. Not a blank page for the Warring States

When Zichan has a penal text cast, Shuxiang writes to object and Zichan responds. They dispute how public rules will change the handling of conflict. In another account, Zichan refuses to destroy the village school where people criticize government, treating criticism as something he can learn from.13

These are not completed versions of modern constitutional government. They are specific attempts to change relations between decisions and the people affected. Institutional invention does not wait for an official boundary between historical periods.

The “rectification of names” is similarly more than putting titles back in order. The Analects connects names with speech, affairs, rites and music, punishment, and people's ability to act. A person needs to understand what is being required, not merely guess after authority has decided.14

The division of Jin, too, has several moments: the defeat of the Zhi, royal recognition of Han, Zhao, and Wei in 403 BCE, and Jin's eventual disappearance. Broken ties, practical standing, and formal recognition do not arrive on a single day.15

Spring and Autumn therefore leaves accumulated experience and unresolved demands, not an empty space waiting for new thought. War makes some changes urgent and destroys many lives. It does not guarantee that the arrangement surviving the contest is the best one.

The king remains; decisions have moved. Next we will see competition enter institutional design more directly, sharpening the distance between an order able to win and one in which people ought to be asked to live.
