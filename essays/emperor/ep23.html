清前中期——奏报到了，然后呢

The Early and Middle Qing — After the Report Arrives

Han Qin (秦汉)

仓里有粮，外面有人买不起粮，为什么还要等？

1735年，方苞提出的仓储建议里，就有这样一个问题。粮价上涨，地方若必须等上级定好价格才能开仓平粜，穷人眼前的困难就得先让位于文书的往返。他建议一面开粜，一面报告。1

还有更不起眼的事：南方潮湿，储存比例不能处处照搬；搬粮要脚价，看仓、卖粮的人要吃饭。不能因为账上不愿承认这些费用，便当它们不存在。否则缺口总要有人填，有时就会变成强派给居民的霉粮。

建议被交部议行，不等于各地已经照办。但它让康雍乾的故事有了一个具体入口：增加报告、规章和监督以后，人的处境是否真的能改变？

一、康熙平定之后，事情还没有做完

三藩之乱不能压成三个藩王在同一天作出同一个选择。吴三桂起兵，耿精忠响应，尚可喜与尚之信父子的不同处境，分别经过1673、1674、1676等转折。2

平定也并非皇帝摆脱汉人军事力量的一次胜利。赵良栋、王进宝等绿营将领继续发挥作用，蔡毓荣组织跨省军需。部队怎样进入四川、云南，粮饷怎样跟上，是战争的一部分，不是胜利之后才开始考虑的琐事。

1683年澎湖战事和郑克塽投降之后，台湾留不留、居民怎么办、府县怎样设置，仍有争论与安排。3 军事结果并不自带一份已经落实的治理方案，更不等于全岛所有人已经处于同一种统治关系。

文化工程也有各自的时间。《康熙字典》的序写于1716年，《古今图书集成》到1726年刊行，不能把后者已经完成的样子直接归到康熙在位时。编书、保存知识当然是建设，但与之相伴的用人、经费和选择，也需要继续追问。

南巡时禁止地方供张、科敛的命令，同样不能当作沿途居民没有负担的证明。3 对上面来说，一道禁令可以写得非常清楚；下面的人是否因此少出了一辆车、一笔钱，是另一层事实。

二、雍正增加通道，也没有消灭中间的人

密折让一些消息避开常规流程，直接到达皇帝面前。这是真实变化，但不是所有官员、更不是所有居民，都因此得到同样的入口。密封的匣子仍要有人运送、接收、呈进和发回。4

少经过某些官署，不等于没有中间环节。皇帝亲自写了批语，也只说明事情受到注意，不能保证最初报告准确。几份报告说法一致，有时可能是互相印证，有时也可能是利益相同的人一起把事情说成同一个样子。

军机处的形成也有过程。《清史稿》有关张廷玉的叙述与职官志分别提供1730、1732等节点，不能把筹设、改称与后来的全部职能压成一个瞬间。军机大臣之外，有章京、文书与持续的办事惯例；内阁仍然存在，明发、廷寄也有不同流转方式。4

财政改革更不能只看一个名字。1712年确定以1711年的丁额为基础，不因后来新增人口而加赋；摊丁入亩则改变旧丁银的分担方式，既有先行地区，也有不同比率和例外。它们不是一道命令把所有无地者的一切负担全部免去。1

1724年耗羡归公及养廉安排，把一些原来容易游离于明账之外的收入纳入省级公用和官员补贴。经费得到承认，是重要进步；但它不等于这些钱都变成中央正项，也不等于地方从此没有额外索取。

纳税凭据、公开完欠数、允许持票申诉，以及1733年张廷玉对刑部拘禁和法律引用方式的批评，说明纠正并非只能期待一次偶然的圣明。14 只是有渠道以后，还要问：普通人拿得到凭据吗？有人收他的申诉吗？能否不受报复地走完这条路？

改土归流也应如此看。换成朝廷任命的流官，不保证官员已经住到当地，更不保证没有暴虐。1730年乌蒙事件中，《清史稿》明确记到总兵刘起元的贪虐和随后军事报复。4 新的职位名称，不能替在它下面生活的人预先宣布受益。

三、乾隆的盛世，不能只看汇总数字

1795年约二亿九千七百万的人口记录，首先是当时行政统计的一项结果。它不能与早年登记的纳税“丁”不加区别地连成曲线，再得出摊丁入亩直接引起人口爆炸的结论。5

人口增加、迁移、土地利用与生计压力值得认真研究，却不是一个政策按钮按下后的必然连锁反应。一个家庭住在沿海岛上，有自己的房屋和生产，也不能因为官府地图上那片地方不方便管理，就被当作等待重新安置的空白。

粮仓也一样。额定应有多少，实际存粮多少，账上还剩多少银，银子能不能买回原来的粮，是四个问题。账能对上，不保证灾年开仓时真有粮。1

1781年甘肃冒赈案中，前后报告曾给出不同说法。原先的疑点并没有因为上级作了确认就自动消失，后来还要由新的观察和调查把交易过程翻出来。1782年国泰库款案中，钱沣要求封库彻查，也说明抽看几个包装整齐的银封，与核查整座库不是一回事。6

和珅1799年获罪，当然关系到腐败，却不宜附带流传中那些相当于全国多少年收入的巨大财富数字。没有一致口径的账，不能拿来当已算清的证据。

《四库全书》保存、辑录了许多书，同时禁毁与追罪也在发生。两面都必须保留。钱谦益著述被禁毁，徐述夔案牵连到沈德潜身后，这些不是只对遥远未来有代价的政策，首先伤到了当时的人。6

戴震仍能对把个人意见冒充天理提出尖锐批评，又说明思想没有彻底停止。7 承认仍有人思考，不会减轻压制的责任；看见压制，也不应把尚在工作的头脑一笔删掉。

四、白莲教战争：保护的费用由谁承担

1796年嘉庆即位，乾隆直到1799年去世前仍以太上皇身份参与政务。白莲教起事不能被讲成“好皇帝一离开，参数立刻漂移”。宗教网络、跨省联系和1794年已经发生的查捕，都在战争之前。8

贫困与流动很重要，信仰也不只是贫困外面的一层包装。查捕波及谁，地方怎样索取，哪些人受到威胁，都会改变人们的选择。并非每个贫苦家庭都会起事，也不是每个宗教关系从开始就具有同一个政治目标。

战争中，寨堡、团练与官军共同发挥作用。1798、1799年前后，地方防御与跨省指挥不断调整，不能写成正规军完全消失，民间武装独自接管战争。8

把居民集中到寨堡，可以切断起事军的补给，也可能改变居民自己的生计。墙是谁修的，夜里谁守，粮从哪里来，还能否照常下田？“保护了地方”不是这些问题的完整答案。

结束也有层次。1802、1803已有平定和奖叙，1804又有追剿残部告竣及军费核查。通常用1804作为主要战事的终点，不等于那一年所有家庭同时回到原来的生活。

这些安排与后来地方军事力量的发展存在可比较之处，却没有预先造好十九世纪的湘军、淮军。后人还要重新招募、筹钱、授权和协商。

五、比较制度，也要比较谁能使用制度

大洋另一边，1776年的《独立宣言》、1787年的联邦宪法、1791年批准的权利法案，分属不同阶段。议会、总统与法院的安排，把某些纠正和更换变成有规则可循的事务。9

但不能简写成所有人直接选举所有职位、所有职位定期轮换。原宪法下参议员由州议会选出，联邦法官也不同于定期改选的议员；奴隶制相关条款更提醒我们，权利语言可以与严重排除并存。

有用的比较，不是拿美国的理想图纸对照清朝所有实际弊病。两边都要问：谁能提异议，到哪里提，用什么证据，谁根本进不了那扇门？

1793年马戛尔尼使团到来，也不是中英贸易第一次出现。新通商地点、商人设施、税费与常驻代表等具体要求，发生在已有贸易及争议之中。乾隆拒绝这些要求，不等于停止所有对外贸易。10

清朝也并非不能想象任何双边协商。1689年《尼布楚条约》已有边界安排，以及持有文书的双方人员往来贸易等规定。它不是现代外交制度的完整先声，却足以让“只能朝贡，根本不能谈判”的说法站不住。10

这不替1793年的决定作总辩护，只要求我们把决定本身讲清楚。接受是否一定能避免战争，拒绝是否已经包含1840年的结局，都不能跳过中间数十年的行动来宣布。

六、纠正能否在受到重视之后继续

康雍乾时期的变化不是在同一个控制台上拧几下旋钮。税怎么分、消息怎么传、地方由谁治理，都改变了人与人之间的关系。

问题在于，纠正能否在一次特别关注之后继续？一个官员被查出造假，能否让下一个被错误征收的人更容易申诉？一座仓库账目补齐，是实际补回粮食，还是把欠账转给了不能拒绝的人？

还有，新的知识能不能进入决定？如果检查者只能看别人预先准备的材料，如果一种意见一旦被认定为正理就不许再问，那么更多报告与更勤奋的阅读，也未必足以纠错。

方苞没有要求取消粮仓。他是在说明，潮气、时间和搬运者的饭钱，不能为了维护一条整齐规则而被忽略。承认它们不是对制度的羞辱，而是让制度还能做原来想做的事。

本系列所说的余项，不替任何人写奏折，也不自动打开仓门。是人发现不合适、提出理由、决定是否改变。能不能让承受要求的人带着自己的理由回来，才是改革之后仍须保留的路。

而他的理由值得被听见，并不以能够延长王朝寿命为条件。

A granary contains grain. Outside it, people cannot afford to eat. Why must they wait?

In 1735, Fang Bao raised a version of this question. When prices rose, requiring a county to obtain approval for its sale price before opening public stocks could postpone help until the useful moment had passed. He proposed selling while reporting, rather than making one wait entirely upon the other.1

His other points were equally tangible. Southern humidity made a uniform storage ratio hazardous. Moving grain cost money; people guarding and selling it needed food. Removing those expenses from an acceptable account would not remove them from the work. Someone would absorb the loss, sometimes in the form of spoiled grain forced upon local households.

The proposal was referred for ministerial consideration, not demonstrably implemented everywhere. But it gives the long reigns of Kangxi, Yongzheng and Qianlong a human point of entry. More rules and more information are useful. What happens after the report arrives?

I. Under Kangxi, Victory Left Work to Do

The Revolt of the Three Feudatories was not three identical decisions taken on one day. Wu Sangui's revolt, Geng Jingzhong's participation and the differing positions of Shang Kexi and his son Shang Zhixin involved successive turns in 1673, 1674 and 1676.2

Suppressing it did not eliminate dependence on Han Chinese military forces. Green Standard commanders such as Zhao Liangdong and Wang Jinbao remained important, while Cai Yurong organized supplies across provincial boundaries. An army's entry into Sichuan or Yunnan depended on work that cannot be reduced to the emperor's decision to send it.

The campaign at Penghu and Zheng Keshuang's surrender in 1683 likewise did not settle every subsequent question about Taiwan. Retention, relocation and administrative arrangements still had to be debated. The establishment of a prefecture and counties did not place every island resident in an identical relationship with the government.3

Cultural projects also had their own timelines. The preface to the Kangxi Dictionary dates from 1716; publication of the vast Gujin tushu jicheng belongs to 1726. Both represent substantial intellectual work, but the completed later project cannot simply be placed within Kangxi's reign.

Nor does an order prohibiting requisitions for an imperial tour prove that no household supplied an unwanted cart, meal or payment. A prohibition can be perfectly clear at the center while its practical force remains uncertain elsewhere. The point is not that commands achieved nothing, but that achievement requires a different kind of evidence from intention.

II. Yongzheng's New Channels Still Needed People

Confidential palace memorials allowed certain reports to bypass ordinary routes. They did not give every official, still less every inhabitant, equal access to the emperor. Sealed containers required couriers, receiving staff and procedures for presentation and return.4

Avoiding some intermediaries did not abolish mediation. An imperial annotation showed that a matter had received attention; it could not certify that the account was true. Several reports might corroborate one another, or simply reflect shared interests in presenting the same reassuring picture.

The Grand Council also developed over time. The Qing history's biography of Zhang Tingyu and its institutional account give landmarks in 1730 and 1732. Preparation, establishment and later responsibilities should not be compressed into one founding instant. Councilors worked with clerks, documents and durable practices. The Grand Secretariat remained, with public and confidential instructions following different routes.4

Fiscal changes require similar distinctions. Kangxi's decision in 1712 fixed the registered adult-male levy at a baseline associated with 1711 rather than increasing it with subsequent population growth. Incorporating that old charge into land taxation was a further process, with regional precedents, differing rates and exceptions. It did not instantly release every landless person from every public obligation.1

The regularization of tax surcharges and provision of supplementary official allowances under Yongzheng recognized real administrative expenses. These were provincial public funds, not simply ordinary central revenue under another name. Recognizing a cost could reduce an incentive for informal extraction. It did not prove that extraction had ceased.

There were also procedures for publishing payments and arrears and making complaints with tax receipts. In 1733, Zhang Tingyu criticized detention practices and selective use of legal provisions at the Ministry of Punishments. Correction was not imagined only as an unpredictable personal intervention by a wise ruler.14

But a route on paper still raises practical questions. Can someone obtain the receipt? Will an office accept the complaint? Can the process be pursued without retaliation? Knowing that a procedure exists and knowing how accessible it is are different achievements.

Replacing hereditary local authorities in the southwest with appointed officials, the policy known as gaitu guiliu, also offered no automatic protection. Officials might reside away from the districts they were meant to serve. At Wumeng in 1730, the official history describes abuses by the military commander Liu Qiyuan and subsequent revolt and repression. A new office does not establish a benefit merely by being new.4

III. Qianlong's Prosperity: Look Beyond the Total

The population figure of roughly 297 million for 1795 is an administrative record with a particular counting basis. It cannot simply be joined to earlier figures for taxable adult males to produce a continuous demographic curve. Still less does it establish that one fiscal reform directly caused an inevitable population crisis.5

Growth, migration, land use and pressure on livelihoods matter. They do not form one automatic sequence. Residents of a coastal island already possessed lives and activities before an official found their location administratively inconvenient. A proposal to move them is not the discovery of empty space awaiting order.

A granary's authorized stock, actual grain, recorded silver and capacity to buy replacement grain are likewise different things. Balanced accounts do not guarantee that food will be available when the doors open.1

The Gansu relief fraud exposed in 1781 involved conflicting reports, reassuring confirmations and further observations that prompted investigation. Agreement within an administrative chain had not guaranteed accuracy. During the Guotai treasury investigation in 1782, Qian Feng's insistence on sealing and examining the stores comprehensively challenged the sufficiency of a favorable sample inspection.6

Heshen's prosecution in 1799 belongs in this history of corruption. Popular claims equating his fortune with a spectacular multiple of national annual revenue need not be repeated without comparable accounts. A memorable number is not an audited balance.

The Siku quanshu project preserved and recovered texts. Prohibition, destruction and prosecution occurred alongside that work. The suppression of Qian Qianyi's writings and the Xu Shukui case, which extended to Shen Deqian's posthumous reputation, were not policies whose costs existed only in a distant failure to modernize. They first affected the people living through them.6

Dai Zhen's criticism of elevating personal opinion into unquestionable principle also matters.7 Thought had not simply frozen. Recognizing continued intellectual work does not excuse coercion; recognizing coercion does not require erasing everyone who continued to think.

IV. The White Lotus War: Who Pays for Protection?

Jiaqing received the throne in 1796, but Qianlong continued to participate in government until his death in 1799. The White Lotus uprisings cannot therefore be explained as the immediate collapse of a perfected system once its operator departed. Religious networks, interprovincial connections and arrests recorded in 1794 preceded the open conflict.8

Poverty and mobility belong in the account. Belief was not merely packaging for a social grievance already fully understood. Policing, widening suspicion and demands for provisions could change people's options. Not every poor household became insurgent, and not every religious relationship began with the same political purpose.

Fortified settlements and local militia became important during the war, alongside regular armies and court-appointed commanders. Accounts of 1798–1799 describe changing local arrangements and coordination across provinces. They do not show a state army vanishing while private society took over the entire task.8

Concentrating residents behind fortifications could deny insurgents food and shelter. It could also interfere with those residents' livelihoods. Who built and guarded the walls? Who fed the people inside? Could they still reach their fields? Calling an arrangement protective does not tell us how its burdens were distributed.

The ending came in stages too. Reports of pacification and rewards appeared in 1802 and 1803; further pursuit was declared complete in 1804, when military expenses also came under scrutiny. Using 1804 as the conventional endpoint of the principal operations does not mean every household recovered its former life that year.

There are questions here that later regional armies would encounter again. Those armies were not already fully formed. Their organizers would still have to recruit, finance, obtain authority and negotiate. A resemblance between wars is not a substitute for that history.

V. Comparing Institutions Means Asking Who Can Use Them

Across the world, the American Declaration of Independence of 1776, federal Constitution of 1787 and Bill of Rights ratified in 1791 belonged to separate stages. The allocation of functions among elected bodies, an executive and courts gave certain forms of correction and replacement defined procedures.9

It did not mean every adult directly elected every office or that every officeholder faced periodic replacement. Senators were originally chosen by state legislatures; federal judges held office on a different basis from elected representatives. Provisions accommodating slavery make the coexistence of rights language and severe exclusion impossible to ignore.

A useful comparison does not place an ideal American blueprint beside every actual Qing abuse. Both arrangements must face questions about access: who could object, where, with what evidence, and who was denied entry altogether? Qing complaint procedures should not disappear from the comparison; neither should people excluded or enslaved under the new republic.

Macartney's embassy of 1793 raises another concrete set of questions. British trade with China already existed. Requests concerning additional trading locations, merchant facilities, duties and permanent representation arose within that relationship. Qianlong's refusals did not terminate all foreign trade.10

Qing practice was not confined to a single form of hierarchical submission. The Treaty of Nerchinsk of 1689 contained negotiated boundaries and provisions for documented travelers to trade on either side. This was not a complete modern diplomatic order, but it rules out the claim that bilateral negotiation was literally inconceivable.10

That qualification is not an overall defense of the decisions in 1793. It asks us to explain those decisions in their circumstances. Neither inevitable war after rejection nor assured peace after acceptance can be established by skipping the intervening decades. British requests also represented particular interests, not a neutral instruction delivered by historical progress.

VI. Can Correction Outlive Special Attention?

The Qing changes were not adjustments to a control panel that left all human relationships untouched. Redistributing a tax, opening an information channel and changing local authority could alter lives substantially.

The question is whether correction can outlive the exceptional attention that first makes it possible. Does exposing one dishonest official help the next person facing an erroneous demand? Has a granary's deficit been repaired by replacing grain or by transferring its loss to people unable to refuse?

What knowledge can return to a decision? An inspector limited to prepared samples may receive abundant information and still miss the problem. A view elevated beyond challenge can make more diligent reading insufficient for correction.

Fang Bao did not propose abolishing granaries. He explained why moisture, time and the food needed by workers could not be ignored to preserve an orderly rule. Acknowledging them was not an insult to the institution. It was a way of letting the institution do the thing it was meant to do.

The remainder does not write memorials or open warehouse doors. People notice a mismatch, offer reasons and decide whether to change. Can someone who bears an obligation return with reasons of their own? That is a path reform must keep open after its first success.

Their claim to be heard does not depend on whether listening would extend the life of a dynasty.
