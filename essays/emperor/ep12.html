三国——撑起秩序的人，也会改变秩序

The Three Kingdoms — Those Who Sustain an Order Help Shape It

Han Qin (秦汉)

曹操需要粮食，需要兵，也需要知道谁能办事。刘备、孙权同样如此。问题是，这些东西从来不是放在库房里等人领取的：粮在谁的地上种，兵听谁的话，一个陌生人的本领又由谁来证明？

东汉留下的地方大族和官僚网络，既能帮人重建秩序，也能决定谁被挡在秩序外面。治理者无法完全不依靠它们，却也不能把“得到了几家的支持”当成“已经照顾了所有人的要求”。

我们仍可用限制、绕过、谈判、纳入来比较这一时期的做法。不过，曹操、刘备、孙氏和司马氏不是同时站上起跑线的四个纯粹模型。司马氏先在魏的官署和军队中任职；每一方实际采取的办法，也比一张标签复杂。

一、曹操的“另起炉灶”，仍要有人添柴

曹操家族与宦官曹腾的关系，成为政治攻击的材料。但由此推到“士族都拒绝他，所以他只能独自开路”，就走得太远了。荀彧来自颍川名门，陈群也出自有声望的家族，他们都在曹操的政权中承担重要工作。1

210年的求贤令强调不要因为一个人不符合完美的品行形象就放弃他的才能。可是才能不会自己走到书案前。曹操仍要求手下发现、推荐人选。改变标准，可以松动旧入口，却不等于从此没有介绍人，也不等于人人都有被看见的机会。

屯田同样不是在社会关系之外凭空建出的经济系统。任峻带着亲族和乡里关系参与其中，后来与曹氏联姻。枣祗关于怎样计算耕牛、怎样分配收成的主张，还牵涉开荒者愿不愿意留下来继续种。2

如果把屯田写成“收获全部归国家”，这些实际的安排就消失了。谁出牛、谁出力、遇到坏年景怎样承担，决定的并不只是账面收入，还决定人能否继续合作。

校事制度则提出另一种困难。增设耳目，可以使掌权者听见原来被遮蔽的消息，也可以给告发和滥权开门。高柔对卢洪、赵达的异议，以及曹丕时期关于告发的争论，说明反对意见曾经出现，处理也有先后。3

因此，曹操不是站在所有关系外面、只靠个人判断驱动的一台发动机。他借用关系，也重排关系。新的通道能否被复核，往往比它是不是“新”更重要。

二、刘备的旗帜之下，还得有人运粮

刘备早年贫困，不等于起步时没有任何联系。求学有亲族资助，起兵有商人帮助，后来麋竺以财产和婚姻支持他。复汉的主张当然重要，但它不是能替人吃饭、备马、渡江的万能货币。4

诸葛亮也不是没有家族背景、只凭一腔信仰出现的人。他的父亲、叔父有官职经历；隆中对所谈的地盘、供给、联盟和治理，更说明共同理想必须找到实际的落脚处。5

到了益州，用“外来荆州团队掌实权，本地豪族只配合”来分人，也会分错。法正出自扶风，李严出自南阳，两人此前都已在益州任职。他们的原籍、服务经历和后来掌握的权力，不能用一个“本地人”包起来。蜀科的制定也是多人参与，不是一位圣贤独自写好。6

法正受到刘备倚重，出现过诸葛亮不愿干预的例外；231年李严与军粮有关的文书冲突，则显示朝廷如何通过前后书信核查责任。这里既有规则，也有偏爱、证据和争执，不是道德感召力一项指标。

诸葛亮去世以后，蒋琬没有停止判断。他提出改走水路进兵，面对异议又作调整；费祎对姜维大规模出兵的限制，也是在分配风险和资源。7

这些人不必复制诸葛亮的人格，才能做有意义的事。蜀汉后来的失败需要解释，却不能把它之前几十年的行政和争论都改名为“信仰耗尽后的惯性”。继承者面对的是新问题，不是一场谁更像前任的比赛。

三、孙吴的合作，并不止于“本地人管文、外地人管武”

孙坚本是吴郡富春人，他的军队和追随者却来自多种关系。地方出生、军队构成、当地人的支持，是三回事。陆逊出自江东大族，既办理地方事务，又统率军队，足以打破“军权始终只在外来旧将手中”的分法。8

222年对蜀作战时，陆逊要让资历、背景各异的将领接受指挥。共同效力于孙氏，并不意味着每一次判断都能自然获得信任。合作是在具体行动里被争取的。

253年诸葛恪围攻新城的失利，也不宜简化成“江东豪族只愿防守，集体不肯供应北伐”。目标的选择、将领的反论、暑热和疾病、报病受到怎样的对待，都进入了过程。消息不能如实上达，可能让一项本该改变的决定继续执行。9

吴当然需要与地方强者谈判。但谈判的后果，不能只从一次战争的输赢倒推。某个家族能保存军事和行政经验，也可能让位置越来越难由外人接近。陆抗接续父亲的部队、在移交驻地前修整设施，就让“家族延续”有了具体内容：有人实际保养了后来还要使用的东西。10

孙权晚年的继承危机更提醒我们，裂痕并不是在他死后才突然发生。陆逊为太子上书、请求入见，遭到反复责问，245年去世；250年孙和被废、孙霸被杀，孙权到252年才去世。不能把在位时已经做出的选择，全部归给继任者缺乏威望。

一个人维持的平衡，可能在他仍然掌权时被他自己打破。年龄、声望或长期执政，都不能替他保证下一次决定仍愿意听人说话。

四、司马氏接管魏，不是一整个阶层投了一张同意票

249年高平陵之变，司马懿并非只有一个名门姓氏可用。他曾有军政职务和部队基础。趁曹爽随皇帝出城，他动用太后名义的文书，控制要地，并由高柔、王观等接掌军营。11

桓范劝曹爽另作安排，曹爽最终放弃抵抗，随后被处死。具体的兵力、文件、劝说和保证，都在事件中起作用。这不是“所有士族利益一致，所以自然拥护司马家”的一次集体表决。

把强者纳入自己的政权，确实可能降低某些冲突，却不会使被纳入者从此不再有自己的要求。同一出身的人可以站到两边，同一家族内也可能作出不同选择。

九品中正制的起点在曹丕时期，不能算作司马氏为完成阶层团结而一次发明出来的办法。它怎样从品评人才发展成越来越倚重权势和家世的入口，也需要看历次任用与改变，不能用后来的一句批评代替全程。1

五、晋的困难，不是完全没人指出困难

刘毅批评九品制度时有一句名言：“上品无寒门，下品无势族。”这里说的是人才品评的等级，不是高低官位的一份完整统计；后半句的“势族”，也不宜随手改成泛指士人的“士族”。他的锋芒指向品评者与权势之间的关系。12

卫瓘还提出废除九品及中正，重新安排地方选举。皇帝称善，事情却没有实行。有人提出、掌权者听见、实际发生改变，是三道不同的门。

所以，问题不是一句“同阶层掌权，内部就没有反馈”能够说明的。反馈存在而无力改变规则，也很值得研究：是哪些人有能力让事情停在称赞之后？哪些成本被留给没有参与决定的人？简短史料未必让我们把原因一一补齐，却不该被改写成从来无人反对。

晋的宗王后来有不同的军政职务、资源和联盟。291年的宫廷暴力，与301年前后扩大的战争不是一场连续十六年的同一战斗。南匈奴等人群与魏晋的关系也早已存在，不能说成内战时才忽然从外面进入。13

内战破坏了防御和供给，不取消后来攻击者的选择。一个社会受到多方面伤害，不等于每一种伤害都可以归到一个最初的开关。

六、四种姿态之外，还要问四件事

第一，限制、绕过、谈判、纳入可以同时发生。任用一个大家族的人，并不代表接受他的一切要求；另建一个机构，也不代表它能摆脱关系。标签可以帮助比较，不能替代观察。

第二，效率和韧性要说清楚是谁的、做什么的。命令传得快，未必坏消息也传得快；一个家族保住了地位，未必同一地区的住户就保住了生活。陆抗修好的设施与一家人控制的入口，可能是同一种延续的不同后果。

第三，胜出不等于所有安排都被证明最好。魏、蜀、吴的军事结局，涉及兵力、供给、路线、将领和时机。用最后的版图给每一套制度打总分，会把需要解释的事情当成已经完成的解释。

第四，结束旧局面的人、继任的家族、承担代价的居民，不是同一个观察对象。曹操积累的资源后来由谁使用，是一个问题；百姓交出的粮食有没有换来更安稳的生活，是另一个问题。即使某个家族跨越了几次改朝换代，我们也不能把它的生存写成所有人的收益。

史书容易记住谁取得了天下。没有留下姓名的人，也可能一直在替这个“取得”耕种、运输、修桥和埋葬死者。

七、统一之后，还不能把结论写好

263年蜀亡，魏晋随后更替，280年吴亡。291年的宫廷冲突、311年洛阳陷落、316年长安失守，又是不同的事件。把它们压成“统一十一年就全面归零”，会同时弄丢时间和具体损失。13

一个首都失去，不等于所有地方官署、书籍、技艺和记忆同日消失。南迁的人带走一些东西，留下的人继续使用一些东西，也确实有大量生命和知识无法恢复。下一篇要看这些连接怎样断裂、转移和重建，而不是提前宣布三百年只剩一片空白。

三国比较最值得留下的，不是四种人物类型，而是一个不断回来的难题：你需要别人替秩序工作，就必须面对他们自己的知识、关系和要求。

撑起秩序的人会改变秩序。更难的问题是，那些只承担负担、却不掌握任何入口的人，能不能也对它说一句话。

Cao Cao needed grain, soldiers, and people who knew how to get things done. So did Liu Bei and Sun Quan. None of these resources waited in a neutral storeroom. Grain grew on someone's land. Soldiers trusted particular commanders. An unknown person's abilities usually needed someone else's introduction.

The powerful families and official networks inherited from Han could help rebuild government. They could also decide who never reached its door. Securing the cooperation of a few influential households did not settle the claims of everyone whose labor sustained them.

We can compare efforts to restrain, bypass, negotiate with, or incorporate such networks. But Cao Cao, Liu Bei, the Sun, and the Sima were not four pure models starting the same experiment together. The Sima first worked within Wei. Each participant used more than one approach, often at the same time.

I. Cao Cao's alternative still needed people to operate it

Cao Cao's connection to the eunuch Cao Teng supplied his enemies with material for abuse. It did not make cooperation with established families impossible. Xun Yu came from a distinguished Yingchuan family; Chen Qun also possessed prominent family connections. Both did important work within Cao Cao's government.1

The recruitment proclamation of 210 challenged the assumption that useful ability must come with an impeccable moral reputation. Yet ability did not announce itself on the ruler's desk. Cao Cao asked associates to identify and recommend candidates. Changing the criteria could loosen an entrance without abolishing introductions or making everyone equally visible.

Agricultural colonies likewise did not constitute an economy outside social relationships. Ren Jun brought relatives and local connections into Cao Cao's service and became related to the Cao family by marriage. The discussion associated with Zao Zhi concerned such practical matters as the provision of oxen and the division of crops.2

These details disappear if all the produce is described as simply belonging to the state. Who supplied the animal? Who supplied the labor? Would people remain to cultivate again? The answers concerned continued cooperation, not merely the treasury's total.

Special investigative officers posed a related problem. Additional channels could expose information concealed by established officeholders. They could also reward denunciation and abuse. Gao Rou's objections concerning Lu Hong and Zhao Da, and a later dispute over accusations under Cao Pi, show interventions unfolding over time.3

Cao Cao was therefore not an engine powered entirely by personal judgment outside every network. He used connections while changing how they worked. Whether a new channel could itself be scrutinized mattered at least as much as its novelty.

II. Liu Bei's banner could not transport grain

Liu Bei's early poverty did not mean that he began without relationships. Relatives helped finance his education; merchants assisted his early following; Mi Zhu later supported him with wealth and marriage ties. The claim to restore Han mattered, but it could not feed people or equip a journey by itself.4

Zhuge Liang was not a figure without a family background who materialized out of pure belief. His father and uncle had held office. The famous discussion at Longzhong also concerned territory, supplies, alliances, and government. A common purpose needed material conditions in which to become more than a declaration.5

In Yi province, a neat division between outsiders holding real power and local families receiving minor roles becomes especially misleading. Fa Zheng came from Fufeng; Li Yan came from Nanyang. Both had already served in Yi. Their origins, earlier service, and later responsibilities were different facts. Several people participated in compiling the Shu regulations; law did not emerge from a single sage's mind.6

Fa Zheng's position with Liu Bei allowed exceptions that Zhuge Liang declined to challenge. In 231, Li Yan's conflicting communications about military supplies became evidence used to establish responsibility. Rules operated amid favor, paperwork, argument, and attempts to evade blame. Moral inspiration alone does not describe this government.

After Zhuge Liang died, Jiang Wan continued making choices. He considered advancing by water, encountered objections, and modified his plans. Fei Yi's restrictions on Jiang Wei's larger campaigns likewise concerned resources and exposure to risk.7

Neither man had to reproduce Zhuge Liang's personality to do consequential work. Shu's eventual defeat requires explanation. It does not turn the preceding decades into mere inertia after the supply of faith ran out. Successors faced their own problems, not an examination in resembling their predecessor.

III. Wu's local families were not confined to civilian work

Sun Jian came from Fuchun in Wu Commandery, while his forces drew on several regional relationships. Birthplace, the origins of an army, and support from the people it governed were separate questions. Lu Xun, a member of a major local family, performed administrative and military work. His career alone rules out a rigid division in which local elites governed and outsiders retained all serious military command.8

During the campaign against Shu in 222, Lu Xun had to establish authority among commanders with differing backgrounds and seniority. Serving the same ruler did not make them agree about the next move. Cooperation had to be obtained in the course of action.

Zhuge Ke's unsuccessful siege of Xincheng in 253 also deserves more than the explanation that local families had no interest in northern expansion. The choice of target, commanders' objections, heat, illness, and the handling of reports all mattered. Treating unwelcome reports as excuses could allow a decision to continue after its conditions had changed.9

Wu certainly negotiated with powerful local interests. But the effects of those relationships cannot be read straight off the outcome of a campaign. A family might preserve useful knowledge while making an office harder for outsiders to approach. Lu Kang inherited troops from his father and repaired installations before transferring a posting. Continuity here meant that someone maintained things other people would need.10

Sun Quan's succession crisis, meanwhile, began during his own reign. Lu Xun wrote in support of the crown prince, sought an audience, faced repeated reproaches, and died in 245. Sun He was deposed and Sun Ba killed in 250; Sun Quan died in 252. Decisions taken under the established ruler cannot all be attributed to successors lacking his authority.

A ruler may damage the balance he once maintained while he is still in charge. Longevity and reputation do not guarantee that his next decision will leave room for another voice.

IV. The Sima takeover was not a unanimous class vote

At the coup of 249, Sima Yi had more at his disposal than an eminent surname. He had held military and civil responsibilities and possessed a base in the armed forces. While Cao Shuang accompanied the emperor outside the capital, Sima Yi used documents issued in the dowager's name, secured key positions, and had men including Gao Rou and Wang Guan take over camps.11

Huan Fan urged Cao Shuang to pursue another course. Cao Shuang ultimately abandoned resistance and was later executed. Troops, documents, advice, and assurances were all part of the sequence. “The great families collectively chose their representative” erases precisely the work that made the takeover possible.

Incorporating powerful people can reduce certain conflicts. It does not remove their separate purposes. People with similar backgrounds may take opposing sides; members of one family may make different choices. Cooperation is not the extinction of disagreement.

The nine-rank system also originated under Cao Pi, not as a single Sima invention designed to align the interests of an entire class. How its assessments became increasingly entangled with family power has to be investigated through its operation and changes. A later criticism cannot serve as a transcript of the institution's whole history.1

V. Jin's difficulties were not wholly unreported

Liu Yi's famous attack on the nine ranks complained that humble families were absent from the upper grades and powerful families from the lower. The grades assessed candidates; they were not simply a complete table of offices already occupied. His petition attacked the influence of power over the people doing the assessing.12

Wei Guan proposed abolishing the ranks and their assessors in favor of a different arrangement for local selection. The emperor reportedly approved, yet the change was not implemented. Proposing something, being heard, and bringing about a change were three separate thresholds.

This is not a story adequately captured by saying that shared social origins eliminated all feedback. Feedback that cannot alter a rule is itself worth investigating. Who could leave a proposal suspended after praising it? Who bore the costs of non-action? A brief source may not answer every question, but that gap should not be filled by declaring that nobody objected.

Jin princes later held different commands and civil appointments and assembled changing alliances. The palace violence of 291 and the wider wars around 301 were not one continuous sixteen-year battle. Southern Xiongnu communities had also maintained relationships with Wei and Jin before these conflicts; they did not suddenly appear from outside when the fighting began.13

Civil war damaged defense and supply. It did not cancel the choices and responsibility of later attackers. Several forms of destruction can interact without becoming the automatic effects of one original switch.

VI. Four questions beyond four labels

First, restraint, bypassing, negotiation, and incorporation can coexist. Employing someone from a powerful household need not mean accepting every demand that household makes. Building another agency need not free it from dependencies. The categories help us compare actions; they cannot perform the comparison for us.

Second, efficiency and resilience need an owner and a task. Orders can travel quickly while bad news travels slowly. A family can preserve its position while its neighbors lose their livelihoods. Lu Kang's repaired installations and a family's control of recruitment may be different consequences of the same continuity.

It is therefore not enough to call one government centralized and another distributed. Which decisions are coordinated? Which resources remain usable after a setback? Can a mistaken command be challenged before more people are required to carry it out? A fast decision may be a good one, or simply a mistake executed sooner.

Third, victory does not certify every arrangement of the winner as superior. The military outcomes of Wei, Shu, and Wu involved routes, forces, supplies, commanders, and timing. Assigning an overall institutional score from the final map turns the event we need to explain into its own explanation.

Fourth, the people who end an old situation, the families who succeed them, and the residents who bear the costs are different units of observation. Who later used the resources Cao Cao assembled is one question. Whether the grain taken from households purchased a more secure life for them is another.

A family might survive successive dynastic changes by preserving useful work and adaptable relationships. Its survival does not establish a benefit for everybody. Chronicles readily name those who acquired a realm. People who sowed, transported, repaired, and buried the dead contributed to that acquisition even when no one preserved their names.

VII. Unification is not the final paragraph

Shu fell in 263. The transfer from Wei to Jin followed; Wu fell in 280. The palace crisis of 291, the fall of Luoyang in 311, and the surrender at Chang'an in 316 marked different events and losses. “Eleven years after unification everything collapsed” compresses them into a misleading instant.13

The loss of a capital did not make every local office, book, skill, and memory disappear that day. Migrants carried some things elsewhere. Those who stayed used others. Lives and knowledge were also genuinely lost. The next essay must examine how connections broke, moved, and were remade, rather than assuming three centuries of blank space in advance.

What remains valuable in comparing these four political formations is not a typology of personalities. It is a recurring difficulty. When you need other people to sustain an order, you must deal with their knowledge, relationships, and purposes. They cannot become interchangeable parts simply by receiving an assignment.

Those who sustain an order help shape it. The harder question is whether people who bear its burdens without controlling any of its entrances can also have a say.
