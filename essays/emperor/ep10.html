王莽——名字改了，日子怎么办

Wang Mang — A New Name Does Not Settle an Old Debt

Han Qin (秦汉)

一块田有了新名字，叫王田。田里的庄稼还在长，买田欠的钱还没还，耕作的人等着收成，收租的人等着自己的一份。

名字可以在一道命令里改变，围着它形成的关系却不会一夜改完。

王莽建立的新朝，按通常纪年从公元9年延续到23年。他援引古典，改变土地、货币、市场与官职。新王朝要借很古老的名义重整眼前的生活。问题并不只在古书对不对，而在一个名义落地的时候，谁的旧承诺要改变，谁又能等到新的安排接上。

一、土地上的困难，比新朝更早

西汉末年的土地问题，不是看到“允许买卖”就能解释完的。有人急着还债，有人可以慢慢等；有人一场病就得卖田，有人手里有钱，能够在别人着急时买进。同样一纸交易，双方拥有的时间并不一样。

此前已有改变的提案。师丹提出限田方向，孔光、何武参与拟议，涉及土地与奴婢数量的上限，也考虑过渡期限。随后在丁氏、傅氏、董贤等宫廷关系的影响下搁置。现实并非只有一边积累苦难、一边所有人全无办法；办法存在，也会在权力关系中停住。1

王氏家族的权势同样有起伏。王莽在哀帝时离开中枢，退居封地，不能把成、哀、平三朝写成一条毫无中断的控制线。儒生也不是一群等着同一位复古者来实现共同梦想的人，龚胜拒绝任用，后来还有区博提出不同意见。2

把这些分歧保留下来，改革才有它真正面对的世界：不是一张等人画图的白纸，而是已经有人作过提案、有人受过损失、有人不愿再接受原安排的生活。

二、四十八万人的名字，同意的是什么

《汉书》写王莽读书、照料家人、捐赠财物、结交士人。这些行动与他的声望有关，也与政治关系有关。我们不必先判他全部真诚或全部虚伪，才能讨论这些行为产生了什么作用。2

四十八万七千五百七十二人上书，是一个尤其容易被数字说服的故事。它的语境在公元5年：王莽不受新野田，上书者请求增加对他的赏赐。不是四十八万人投票要求他取代汉朝。3

人数再精确，也不能替换请求的内容。没有采集与代表性的资料，它更不能变成当时所有人的民意比例。

摄政、周公先例、皇帝形式与符命，逐渐进入权力转移。但代理幼主与宣布天命归自己，承担的是不同主张。刘婴还是幼年指定继承者，不能当作成年在位者自由交出权力。刘崇、翟义、刘快等人的反抗，又说明这一过程并非全无争议、人人照制度顺畅接受。4

借来一种正当语言，可以改变局面，却不能把局面中的每个人都变成同意者。

三、古书落地，要变成几种不同的工作

王田诏本身谈兼并、佃租和人身买卖，并不是完全没有看见现实。它把土地称为王田，限制交易；针对男口未满八而田过一井等情况，规定分给族邻，又涉及无田者受田。奴婢改称私属，买卖同受限制。禁止买卖与取消依附身份，仍不是同一回事。5

要实行这些安排，必须知道地在哪里、多少人耕、有没有债、工具种子归谁。地图上画出一份公平，不会自动给新受田的人一套活下去的条件。

五均六筦则包含多种工作：生产与销售的统制、山泽资源的征取、货币权力，以及价格和信贷安排。规定考虑地方、季节与品质，低于平价时也有允许民间交易的空间。赊贷又区别祭祀丧葬和经营产业的需要。它不是一句“官府给所有东西定同一个价”可以概括的。6

币制变化也早于新朝成立，公元7年就已有改动。后来复杂的宝货目录，不等于全国百姓同时使用二十八种硬币；金银、龟贝、钱布等分类，不能照现代钱币的想象全部装进一个钱袋。公元14年的变化又给大钱留下六年并用期，并非每次改币都立即废尽旧钱。7

官名和地名能表达新的管辖与身份，也会增加核对的工作。一封公文上的地方换了名字，旧档案、债务与人员并不会随之搬走。新旧之间的对应，必须有人认真完成。

四、能处罚旧办法，不等于已提供新办法

王田禁令不是毫无执行：追罚交易，本身就是执行。问题是处罚了旧买卖之后，新分配有没有让人获得可耕作的田、可用的资源。禁止一件事的力量，与使另一种生活成为可能的能力，可以相差很远。5

公元12年，区博进言后，土地与奴婢买卖重新获得许可。这是重要的撤回，却不是全部改革同时撤销。变化中仍有听到异议、接受一部分意见的时刻，不能从最终失败倒推从来无人纠正。2

钱可以继续用，也不表示财产保值。大钱原来的名值与新规定的换算不同，储蓄、欠债、待收的账，都因此要重新计算。史料还记人们私用五铢钱，不能把失信直接写成全部交易都回到以物易物。对钱法的处罚，甚至涉及把人变为官奴婢；货币问题最后落到人的身体与关系上。7

官府使用商人的经验，却也可能同商人和地方势力形成逐利关系。《汉书》写空簿和府藏不实，问题正在账目与可支用实物之间。数字写好了，仓库并不会因此多出东西。6

有些古典安排也确实成为了实物。新莽嘉量是一件具体量器，不只是空名；公元11年暂仍用汉律令的命令，又说明旧法没有一夜全部关闭。授爵却迟迟没有土地、收入与人员配上，更让人看见“已经给了名号”与“已经能承担职务”的距离。8

新旧并存并不必然失败，但必须有人处理它们之间的冲突。若只庆祝宣布完成，最辛苦的工作就会落到没有被问过意见的人身上。

五、一次失败，能够替多少东西作结论

王莽失败，是否意味着儒家理想也失败？问题要拆开一点：理想想保护什么，选了什么措施，实施需要什么条件，出了问题以后怎样改。只用一个王朝的结局，把这几层全部判成同一个答案，太省事了。

“三代”通常指夏商周。后世经典里的制度图式，也不等于一份完整保存的西周地籍与操作手册。援引古代可以提出批评，不能省去对现在的查验。

但不能复制过去的整个社会，也不等于任何旧规则都不能再用。币形可以重铸，某项法令可以撤回，某种职务可以恢复；它们各有条件。甚至批评王莽的《汉书·食货志》结语，仍援引古典讨论财政与救济。新亡没有让古典从此只能作修辞、再不能进入政策。1

反过来，大家已经习惯某个旧安排，也不证明应原样保留。有些人的习惯，是因为没有退出的办法。真正要问的，始终是谁得到什么机会，谁承担什么代价，而不是这个名字到底够不够古老。

六、饥饿不会自己组成军队

水灾、旱蝗、军运与疫病，同改革的冲突叠在一起。对外印信和称号的改变，也会影响匈奴等政权自己的关系，引来回应。不能先认定灾害和战争都只是某个哲学错误的背景，再让它们从解释里消失。9

绿林的王匡、王凤，起初在觅食者之间调解争执。人们逐渐聚集，组织发展为武装；公元22年的疫病又使部众分成不同队伍。饥饿提出活下去的需要，调解、约束、追随与决定，才把人连接起来。10

公元23年，更始政权先立刘玄，后有不同力量进取长安，王莽被杀，新朝结束。《汉书》区分杜吴杀莽与公宾就取其首，不能把整个过程压成“一个商人杀了他”。11

赤眉也不是没有任何组织的一片人潮。史书记其口头规则、内部职号，后来接受更始封号，却没有得到相应土地；公元25年又立刘盆子、任徐宣为丞相，进入长安。这比新亡晚了两年。尝试建立秩序与难以维持秩序，不能说成从来不曾尝试。10

秦和新都短命，值得比较，却没有一只可以据此定为十五年的社会闹钟。承受损失的人不会因为代际公式到点，就自动采取同一种行动。

七、恢复汉朝，也在不断作新决定

刘秀公元25年称帝，不等于当天所有战争结束。到36年，成都的战争仍在。刘氏身份提供号召力，却不替他完成联盟、行政与各地的接受。12

他的恢复也不是原样按下重启。公元30年合并许多县、减少官职，40年恢复五铢钱；这些都有新的决定。此前针对部分被掠卖者的人身安排，以及26年援引孔子讨论减省刑法，又让恢复同时包含改变。12

39、40年的度田，仍要面对登记、核实和当地权势。有人知道旧办法怎样运行，确实可能减少学习成本；但派出一个熟悉旧法的官员，不会自动使每一块田、每一种负担如实进入册籍。

旧经验是可用的资源，不是已经做完的工作。它之所以值得保留，是因为还能帮助现在的人；并不是因为只要叫恢复，就不必再解释后果。

八、回去的究竟是什么

王莽和刘秀都借用过去。前者不能因为用了古名就预先判定失败，后者也不能因为恢复汉号就说什么都没改。要用同一个标准问：恢复的是器物、条文、职位，还是设想中的整个世界？每一种恢复，需要谁配合，允许谁提出不合用？

这就是旧地图比喻真正有用的地方。地图可以指出一条曾经走过的路，却不能保证桥还在，也不能决定今天的人必须再走一遍。

一种安排解决问题以后，仍会有它没有接住的要求。它们不会因王朝改名而消失，也不会自动替人决定下一步。下一篇进入东汉，我们继续看已经被接回来的制度，怎样同豪族、宫廷和新的公共要求相处。

田里的庄稼仍在长。对于耕田的人，最重要的不是这块田终于有了一个正确的古名，而是他能否安排明天，以及安排不下去时，有没有地方把话说出来。

A field receives a new name: royal land. Crops are still growing there. Someone owes money for its purchase, someone is doing the work, and someone expects a share of the harvest.

A name can change in a command. The relationships gathered around it cannot all change overnight.

Wang Mang's Xin dynasty conventionally dates from 9 to 23 CE. Appealing to antiquity, he altered land arrangements, money, markets, and official titles. The new dynasty would reorder present life under very old names. The question was not simply whether the ancient texts were right. It was what happened to existing obligations while their proposed replacements were being made possible.

I. The trouble in the fields preceded the new dynasty

Late Western Han land problems cannot be explained simply by pointing to the permission to buy and sell. One owner needs to repay a debt immediately; another can wait. An illness may force a sale, while a buyer has the resources to purchase precisely when somebody else is desperate. The same contract joins parties who possess different amounts of time.

Proposals for change already existed. Shi Dan advocated limits; Kong Guang and He Wu participated in formulating restrictions on land and enslaved persons, including a transition period. The proposals were subsequently shelved amid the influence of the Ding and Fu families and Dong Xian. This was not a world in which distress accumulated while nobody conceived an alternative. Alternatives could be conceived and then halted within relationships of power.1

The Wang family's influence also fluctuated. Wang Mang left the center and returned to his estate during Emperor Ai's reign. Nor did Confucian scholars constitute a single group waiting for an antiquarian savior: Gong Sheng refused appointment, and Ou Bo would later offer contrary advice.2

Reform therefore encountered a world in which people had already proposed, lost, resisted, and adapted. It was not an empty surface waiting for a design.

II. What did nearly half a million names support?

The Hanshu describes Wang Mang studying, caring for relatives, giving away resources, and cultivating scholarly connections. These actions contributed to reputation and political relationships. We do not need to decide that they were entirely sincere or entirely fraudulent before examining what they accomplished.2

The recorded total of 487,572 petitioners is particularly seductive. Such precision seems to speak for itself. Its context, however, is Wang Mang's refusal of the Xinye lands in 5 CE. The petitioners requested additional rewards for him. They were not voting for his replacement of the Han dynasty.3

A precise count cannot change what was being requested. Without information about collection and representation, it cannot become a percentage of the whole population's consent either.

Regency, the Duke of Zhou's example, imperial forms, and signs of Heaven's mandate gradually entered the transfer of power. Acting for a child and announcing that sovereignty belongs to oneself are different claims. Liu Ying was an infant designated successor, not an adult emperor freely relinquishing rule. Resistance associated with Liu Chong, Zhai Yi, and Liu Kuai also prevents us from calling the process an uncontested unfolding of accepted procedure.4

A powerful language of legitimacy could change a situation without turning everyone within it into a consenting participant.

III. Antiquity had to become several kinds of work

The royal-land edict itself discussed concentration of land, rents, and the sale of human beings. It did not simply fail to notice contemporary suffering. It renamed land, restricted transfers, specified conditions involving male household members and holdings beyond a stated area, and provided for redistribution to relatives and neighbors and allocations to the landless. Enslaved persons were renamed private dependents and their sale restricted. Prohibiting sale was not the same as ending the dependent status.5

Implementation required knowing boundaries, cultivators, debts, equipment, and seed. Drawing a fair distribution did not automatically give a new recipient the means of living from the allocation.

The market and revenue arrangements grouped under the Five Equalizations and Six Controls performed different jobs: control of production and sale, claims on natural resources, monetary regulation, and price and credit provisions. Rules distinguished local conditions, seasons, and quality; they also left room for private trading under specified conditions. Credit for funerals and rites differed from credit for productive activity. None of this can be reduced to officials assigning one price to everything.6

Monetary changes began before the dynasty, with changes in 7 CE. Later categories of treasure currency did not amount to twenty-eight kinds of coin simultaneously carried by every household. They included different materials and forms of value. Changes in 14 also allowed a six-year period of concurrent use for an older large coin. Every alteration did not immediately abolish all previous money.7

Changes of place names and offices could express jurisdiction and standing while adding practical work. An old archive, debt, or appointment did not move when a new name appeared on a document. Someone had to connect the records correctly.

IV. Enforcing a prohibition is not providing a replacement life

The royal-land ban was not wholly unenforced: punishing transactions was enforcement. That leaves the separate question of whether redistribution furnished cultivable land and usable resources. The power to stop one practice may greatly exceed the capacity to make another way of living possible.5

After Ou Bo's intervention in 12 CE, transfers of land and enslaved persons were again permitted. This was an important reversal, not the simultaneous abolition of every reform. The final collapse must not make us erase moments when criticism was heard and some advice accepted.2

Allowing old money to circulate did not preserve its previous value. Changes to the reckoning of the large coin affected savings, debts, and sums still to be received. Accounts also describe the private use of five-zhu coins; distrust did not turn every transaction into barter. Punishment for monetary offenses could turn people into state dependents. An apparently abstract question of value reached directly into bodies and relationships.7

Government employed commercial expertise, but officials and traders could form relationships serving their own gain. The Hanshu's accounts of empty records and deficient stores make the problem concrete. Writing a quantity into a ledger did not put the goods in a warehouse.6

Some classical projects did become material things. The surviving Xin measuring vessel is not merely a name. Conversely, instructions in 11 to continue using Han laws show that the old legal system had not been switched off all at once. Grants of rank without the necessary territory, income, or personnel expose another gap: receiving a title was not yet being equipped to fulfill it.8

Old and new can coexist, but their conflicts require attention. If declaring completion replaces that attention, the unfinished work falls to people whose consent has never been sought.

V. How much can one failure decide?

Did Wang Mang's failure discredit Confucian ideals? Several questions have been compressed into one. What was an ideal trying to protect? Which measure was chosen? What did implementation require? What happened when it went wrong? A dynasty's fate cannot answer all four in the same way.

The Three Dynasties conventionally means Xia, Shang, and Zhou. Later classical patterns are not a complete surviving cadastral survey and operating manual for Western Zhou. An appeal to antiquity can provide grounds for criticism without eliminating the need to investigate current conditions.

Yet being unable to reproduce an entire past society does not mean that no old rule can ever be used again. A coin can be recast, a regulation withdrawn, an office restored. Each has conditions of its own. Even the Hanshu's economic conclusion criticizing Wang Mang continues to appeal to classical ideas. His fall did not confine ancient texts forever to rhetoric, excluding them from policy.1

Conversely, familiarity with an existing arrangement does not prove that it should remain unchanged. Some people have adapted because they cannot leave. What matters is who obtains an opportunity and who bears a cost, not merely how old the arrangement's name is.

VI. Hunger does not organize an army by itself

Flooding, drought, locusts, military transport, and epidemic disease interacted with the conflicts of reform. Changes in seals and titles affected relations with the Xiongnu and other powers, which had concerns of their own. Those events cannot be demoted in advance to scenery surrounding a single philosophical mistake.9

Wang Kuang and Wang Feng, associated with the Green Forest forces, initially mediated disputes among people searching for food. Groups gathered and developed into armed forces; an epidemic in 22 led to separate bodies. Hunger supplied a need to survive. Mediation, commitments, constraints, and decisions connected the people experiencing it.10

In 23, the Gengshi regime first established Liu Xuan as emperor; various forces then advanced on Chang'an. Wang Mang was killed and Xin ended. The Hanshu distinguishes Du Wu's killing of Wang from Gongbin Jiu's taking his head. “A merchant killed him” should not stand in for the entire sequence.11

The Red Eyebrows also had more than numbers and distress. Accounts describe oral rules and internal offices, their acceptance of Gengshi titles without corresponding territory, and their later establishment of Liu Penzi as emperor and Xu Xuan as chief minister. Their entry into Chang'an belongs to 25, two years after Xin's fall. Attempts to create order and difficulty sustaining it are not the same as never attempting it.10

Qin and Xin invite comparison because both imperial regimes were short-lived. Their durations do not establish a fifteen-year social timer. People bearing losses do not automatically choose the same action when a generational formula reaches its appointed date.

VII. Restoring Han required new decisions

Liu Xiu's accession in 25 did not end every war. The campaign culminating at Chengdu was still being fought in 36. His Liu ancestry offered a political resource; it did not complete alliances, administration, or local acceptance for him.12

Restoration was not an unchanged restart. Numerous counties were consolidated and offices reduced in 30; five-zhu currency was restored in 40. Measures concerning particular people captured or sold into dependency, and an appeal to Confucius in a proposal for mitigating penalties in 26, likewise put alteration inside the restoration.12

The land investigations of 39 and 40 still faced problems of registration, verification, and local power. Familiarity with old practices could reduce some learning costs. Sending an official who knew those practices did not automatically enter every field and burden truthfully into a register.

Inherited knowledge is a resource, not work already completed. It deserves preservation when it helps people in the present—not because calling something a restoration frees it from explaining its consequences.

VIII. What, exactly, is being brought back?

Both Wang Mang and Liu Xiu invoked the past. The first cannot be condemned in advance simply for using ancient names; the second cannot be described as changing nothing because he restored the Han title. We need the same questions for both. Is the object being restored a tool, a rule, an office, or an imagined world? Whose cooperation does it require? Who may say it no longer fits?

This is where the metaphor of an old map becomes useful. It can show a road once traveled without guaranteeing that the bridge remains—or obliging today's traveler to make the same journey.

Arrangements leave demands unsettled even when they solve real problems. Those demands do not vanish with a new dynasty's name, nor do they make the next choice on anyone's behalf. The Eastern Han essay will follow restored institutions into their relations with powerful families, court politics, and new public claims.

The crops are still growing. For the person working the field, the decisive question is not whether its name has finally become properly ancient. It is whether tomorrow can be arranged, and where to speak when it cannot.
