西周——创设者离开以后

Western Zhou — After the Founders Have Gone

Han Qin (秦汉)

伯禽到鲁国去了，周公留在周的中心。

父亲与儿子可以当面说清的事，从此要隔着路程完成。再往后，连这一次当面说话的人也会不在。地方上的后代凭什么继续承认远处那个中心？中心又怎样知道地方发生了什么？

我们常把西周的回答归在一句话里：周公制礼。四个字很有力量，也容易把许多人的工作压进一个人的名字。分封、宗法、礼乐不是写好以后就能自动运行的程序。它们要组织土地、人群、记忆与日常行动，让一个已经离开的人所建立的关系，还能被活着的人继续使用。

一、不是打完天下才开始建设

武王克商之后，胜利没有把接下来的事情一并安排好。商的旧人、周的亲属、不同地方的力量，仍有各自的位置。武王去世，成王年幼，周公摄政；《史记》把管、蔡与武庚的冲突放在这个开端之中。1

这对“第一代靠血缘天然忠诚”的想象是一个直接提醒。亲属并不需要等到几代以后才会发生分歧。谁有资格主持、谁在替谁做决定，从一开始就可能有不同理解。

周公的工作因此不能只被看作把一场战争留下的空地画成蓝图。他所面对的是已经存在、又发生了裂口的关系。重组需要人、资源、承诺和行动，不只需要设计者的聪明。

创制故事给了我们一个便于记住的中心人物。真正值得追问的，却是这个人物怎样让其他人能够一起做事，以及他不在之后，那些人靠什么继续。

二、把地方交给谁

分封不是只把儿子和兄弟送到地图上的不同位置。伯禽、康叔、召公一系之外，还有太公这样的异姓功臣，也有微子这样的前朝后裔。彼此与中心的关系并不完全相同。1

授予也不只是一个地名。宜侯夨簋的铭文中，土地、物品与人群一同进入授予的叙述。一个地方要能承担任务，需要真实资源，而不只是一句“这里归你”。2

这种安排的长处，是给地方持续经营的理由。人在当地生活，积累了解，组织防务和生产，可以形成远处中心不可能随时拥有的知识。分散责任，不必等于失去共同关系。

张力也从同一处生长。一个人要有资源，才能替中心承担责任；有了资源，他也能提出自己的条件。地方的婚姻、邻居、利益与危险会不断增加，中央授予的身份只是这些关系中的一部分。

而被分配到这幅政治图景中的居民，并不会因为获得一个新的统治者就失去自己的生活。田由谁耕，东西由谁运，战争来时谁离家，这些都不是册命之后可以略去的细节。

三、亲近不能只靠家谱

我们很容易画一条平滑曲线：第一代是兄弟，第二代是堂兄弟，第三代越来越疏，于是忠诚必然递减。家谱这样展开，政治关系却不一定这样变化。

兄弟可能争执，远亲也可能因为长期合作而彼此信任。距离、往来、共同任务、当地联盟，都在改变关系的实际重量。仅凭亲属称谓，算不出一个人会在关键时刻怎样行动。

宗族的持续，本身就需要祭祀、记忆、婚姻、职责等工作。所谓自然的血缘关系，进入政治以后，也需要不断被维系和解释。

我认为西周的制度想象最有分量的地方，就在这里：它尝试让合作不必每次都从私人熟悉开始。人可以先知道自己处于怎样的关系，再逐步学习怎样在其中行动。问题是，这种可传递的关系能否容纳后来发生的变化，而不只要求后来的人重复最初的姿势。

四、礼让位置被看见

谁先进入，谁站在哪里，谁接受什么礼物，谁可以使用怎样的仪式。礼把抽象位置变成了别人能够看见、听见、参与的行动。

这样做可以减少每一次相遇都重新争论身份的成本。人们不必先打一仗，才知道今天怎样坐下来谈。但它也会把不平等做得十分具体：有的人只能站在外面，有的人连提出问题的时机都由别人决定。

礼不是礼貌的总称，也不只是武力的反面。《礼记·乐记》把礼、乐、政、刑放在一起讨论，提示我们认可、行政和惩戒可以共同维持秩序。3 不能因为仪式看起来安静，就以为安静之外没有执行力量。

后来《论语》记季氏使用八佾，孔子愤怒。这个故事发生在西周之后，却清楚展示了仪式形式为何值得争夺。4 如果一种形式完全无用，越级使用它也就不值得费心。对形式的侵犯，可能同时表明它的约束受到挑战、它的象征价值仍在。

因此，真正的问题不是“有礼便没有暴力”，而是共同认可的形式能约束谁，不能约束谁。别人可以指出违礼，不等于他有能力使违礼者改变。

五、一起听音乐的人

《鹿鸣》中有来宾、乐器、饮酒与款待。读到这里，秩序不只是让人站对位置，还想让人愿意共同留在一个场合里。5

这种欢聚可以是真的。不能一看到等级，就把每一次音乐和宴饮都解释成统治者的麻醉。人可能在共同的声音中感到被接纳，可能暂时从孤立的职责里出来，看见别人也在这里。

但共同参与也不等于处境相同。一起唱歌的人，回去之后仍可能承担很不相等的要求。表达得到出口，感受受到安抚，实际条件得到改变，是三件相邻却不同的事。

礼与乐因此各自有工作。礼使差别可见，乐尝试让有差别的人还能共同生活。它们可以帮助关系持续，却不能保证关系中的每一个难处都已经被接住。一个人在宴席上被欢迎，仍可能在下一次决定中没有声音。

六、排好了顺序，还要有人接受

继承规则想解决一个反复出现的问题：不要每次有人去世，整个家族都重新争一遍。

嫡系长幼与母方地位提供排序依据，不等于把所有儿子不加区分地按出生日期排队。《公羊传》的相关说法，正把“长”与“贵”分别放进规则中。6 这样的约束可以让选择更可预期，也意味着能力不能每次都推翻顺序。

然而，规范的表述与实际王系之间并不总是整齐相合。《史记》所记懿王之后是其叔孝王，再由懿王之子夷王接位。1 西周不能被写成幽王以前始终没有例外的一条父子链。

主支与旁支、祭祀责任与现实资源，也不能简单化为每隔一代就自动降一级的楼梯。有人失去中心位置，仍在另一处承担事务；有人保留名分，却越来越缺少使名分有效的条件。

明确的顺序能减少某些争论，却还需要判断这一次是否适用、怎样面对例外、谁能提出异议。规则把选择提前做了一部分，没有把未来全部做完。

七、后来的人能不能改

制度最希望传下去的，往往是创设者认为已经解决的东西。可后来的人遇到的生活，不会只重复创设者的问题。

他们可能只学会外在形式，也可能认真理解以后发现：某个形式已经妨碍了原先要保护的关系。忠实于是有了两种方向——照着做，或者为了原来的理由而改变做法。

孔子谈礼时，并非事事只求复原。他对冠的材料可以随众，对拜礼的位置却有所坚持。《论语》又问，礼难道只是玉帛，乐难道只是钟鼓？4 形式值得重视，但形式为什么值得重视，不能在传承中消失。

这也使后人的位置不必只是衰败。一个继承者可能知道得更少，也可能看见得更多。关键在于他能否说明自己的理由，能否听到被旧办法压住的生活。若所有改变都被算成不忠，礼就越来越难维持它原来希望维持的联系。

八、幽王的结尾，不只是一堆烽火

幽王的故事常被压缩成博美人一笑。他燃起烽火，诸侯受骗；真正的危险来了，再没有人来救。寓言清楚得几乎不需要历史。

《史记》的叙述还包含废后、废太子、申侯与外部力量的行动。《系年》第二章则以另一条战争次序讲述周末的冲突。两种材料让我们看到，继承、母族、联盟与军事行动纠缠在一起，不能全由一次玩笑解释。17

幽王是通常分期中的西周末王，不是整个周王室的末王。前771年、前770年的常用年代标记，帮助我们定位崩溃与东迁，却不意味着各种关系在同一天全部消失。

天命的警告、谏诤的语言并非不存在。难处是，当最需要它们发生作用时，谁能使一个已有力量的人改变决定。规范能够说出错误，不总能阻止错误带走别人的生命。8

所以，这不是一个精巧系统自然老死的故事。人作了选择，有人遭到排除，有人组织联盟，也发生了战争。制度的边界要通过这些行动去看，不能反过来用一条曲线把行动者都擦掉。

九、周留下了什么

西周以后，人们没有停止借周说话。有的人要恢复某些关系，有的人重新安排它们，有的人怀疑过多的规定是不是也在制造困难。

《荀子·礼论》讨论欲望、争夺与分配，礼在这里有一份需要说明的工作。《韩非子·五蠹》强调处境改变之后不能只守旧法。《老子》则让人警惕规范的堆积和过度干预。9 这些不是三个整齐学派各按一个按钮，而是不同方向的人继续追问：共同生活怎样才能成立，又怎样不把人压扁？

周的遗产因此不只是一个延续了多久的王朝。它也留下了用来要求、批评、协商和改变的语言。语言有时替权力装饰，有时又能使被遮住的问题重新出现。

创设者不能替后来所有人生活。最好的传承，也不是使后来的人永远服从他的答案，而是让他们仍能认真处理他没有遇到的问题。

下一篇，天子还在，决定事情的位置却逐渐转移。我们要看的不只是一个名字怎样变空，更是人们为什么还在借这个名字行动。

Bo Qin goes to Lu. His father, the Duke of Zhou, remains at the political center.

What father and son could once discuss face to face must now cross a distance. Eventually both will be gone. Why should descendants in a distant place continue recognizing the center? How will the center know what happens there?

The traditional answer is often compressed into the Duke of Zhou's creation of ritual order. The phrase gives many people's work a memorable name. It can also make that work look like a program which, once written, runs by itself. Land, kinship, rites, music, and succession instead had to organize continuing relationships among living people.

I. Victory did not leave a blank page

King Wu's conquest of Shang did not settle everything that followed. Shang's former supporters, Zhou relatives, and regional powers still occupied different positions. Wu died, King Cheng was young, and the Duke of Zhou acted as regent. The Shiji places conflict involving Guan, Cai, and Wu Geng near this beginning.1

That is an immediate obstacle to the image of a first generation naturally united by blood. Relatives need not wait centuries to disagree about who should act and in whose name.

The Duke's task was not simply to draw a blueprint on ground cleared by war. He faced relationships already present and now fractured. Reorganization required resources, commitments, and acts by people other than the designer.

A founding story gives us a central figure. The institutional question concerns how that figure enables other people to work together—and what will connect them when he is no longer there.

II. Entrusting a place to someone

Enfeoffment was not merely a family tree distributed across a map. Alongside Bo Qin, Kang Shu, and the line associated with the Duke of Shao were the non-royal ally Taigong and the former dynasty's descendant Weizi. Their connections to the center were different.1

A grant also meant more than naming a territory. The inscription on the Yihou Ze vessel places land, objects, and people within an act of bestowal. Responsibilities needed resources, not only an announcement that a place belonged to somebody.2

Local continuity could be valuable. People living in a region accumulate knowledge, organize defense and production, and build relationships a distant center cannot continually reproduce. Distributed responsibility need not mean the absence of common ties.

Its tension comes from the same source. Resources enable someone to act for the center; they also enable that person to bargain with it. Local marriages, neighbors, interests, and dangers acquire weight. The connection conferred from above becomes one relationship among others.

Nor do the inhabitants lose their own lives by being placed within this political map. Who farms, transports goods, and leaves home when war comes? A grant cannot make those questions secondary for the people who bear them.

III. Kinship needs more than a genealogy

It is tempting to draw a smooth decline: brothers become cousins, cousins become distant relations, and loyalty diminishes accordingly. A genealogy may unfold that way. Political attachment does not necessarily follow the same curve.

Brothers can quarrel. Distant relatives can develop trust through repeated cooperation. Travel, shared duties, local alliances, and unequal burdens change the practical weight of a relationship. A kinship term cannot calculate how someone will act at a decisive moment.

Lineage continuity itself requires work: remembering, marrying, sacrificing, dividing responsibilities, explaining obligations. The supposedly natural bond becomes something continually maintained once it organizes public action.

The substantial ambition of the Zhou arrangement, as I read it, was to make cooperation less dependent on starting again with private acquaintance. People could inherit intelligible relationships and learn how to act within them. The open question was whether those relationships could accommodate new circumstances rather than require descendants to repeat the founders' gestures forever.

IV. Making position visible

Who enters first, who stands where, who receives which gift, who performs which ceremony: ritual turns an abstract position into an action others can see and join.

That can reduce the need to contest status at every encounter. People need not fight before discovering how to sit down together. It can also make inequality concrete. Some remain outside; others may speak only at a moment somebody else permits.

Ritual is not simply good manners, nor the opposite of force. The “Record of Music” discusses rites, music, government, and punishment together. Recognition, administration, and coercion can sustain one order.3 A quiet ceremony does not imply an absence of enforcement beyond it.

The later Analects describes Confucius's anger at the Ji family's use of an eight-row dance formation. The episode comes after Western Zhou, but it shows why ceremonial forms could be worth appropriating.4 If a form were entirely meaningless, borrowing its superior rank would offer little advantage. Violation can expose weakened restraint while also revealing continuing symbolic value.

The question is therefore whose conduct a shared form can constrain. Being able to call an act improper is not always being able to make the actor stop.

V. Listening together

The “Deer Call” poem presents guests, instruments, drink, and hospitality. Here order does more than place people correctly. It tries to make a shared occasion desirable.5

The pleasure can be genuine. The presence of hierarchy does not make every musical gathering a calculated sedative. People may experience welcome and connection, discovering that others are present beyond their separate duties.

Yet participation is not equality of circumstance. People who sing together may return to sharply unequal demands. Expressing a difficulty, finding comfort, and changing its conditions are related but distinct accomplishments.

Rites and music thus undertake different work. Rites display differences; music can help people with differences inhabit something together. Both may sustain relationships without answering every difficulty inside them. Someone welcomed at a feast may still have no voice in the next decision.

VI. A sequence still needs acceptance

Succession rules address a recurring danger: every death should not reopen competition across the entire family.

The senior legitimate line and maternal standing provide ordering criteria. This is not simply all sons ranked by birth date regardless of their mothers' positions. The relevant Gongyang passage distinguishes seniority and standing within its normative account.6 Such constraints make selection more predictable while preventing ability from reopening the result on every occasion.

The transmitted royal sequence is not a perfect enactment of the rule. According to the Shiji, King Yi was followed by his uncle King Xiao, and then by Yi's son, another King Yi written with a different Chinese character.1 Western Zhou cannot be described as an uninterrupted father-to-son chain until its last crisis.

Main and collateral lines, ritual duties and actual resources, also do not form a ladder on which every descendant automatically drops one rung. People can lose proximity to the center while holding responsibilities elsewhere; others keep standing while losing the conditions that make it effective.

A clear sequence settles part of the choice in advance. It still needs application, handling of exceptions, and some response to objections. It has not completed the future.

VII. Can successors change what they inherit?

Institutions tend to preserve what their founders believe they have solved. Later people do not encounter only the founders' problems.

Some learn the outward form without its purpose. Others understand the purpose and discover that a particular form now obstructs it. Fidelity can then point in two directions: repeating the act or changing it for the sake of its original reason.

Confucius is not uniformly opposed to adaptation. He accepts a change in a cap's material while resisting a change in the placement of a bow. Elsewhere, the Analects asks whether ritual amounts merely to jade and silk, music merely to bells and drums.4 Forms matter, but their reasons must not disappear in transmission.

This allows successors to be something other than agents of decline. An heir may know less, or see something new. What matters is whether reasons can be explained and lives obstructed by old practices can be heard. If every alteration counts as disloyalty, a form may become less able to sustain the very relationship it was meant to serve.

VIII. King You's ending is more than a beacon fire

King You's story is often reduced to a joke for a woman's amusement: false alarms summon the lords; when danger really arrives, nobody comes. The moral is so clear that history can seem unnecessary.

The Shiji also describes the displacement of a queen and heir, the role of the lord of Shen, and armed forces from outside. The second chapter of the excavated Xinian presents a different sequence of conflict. Succession, maternal relations, alliances, and warfare become entangled; a practical joke cannot carry the entire explanation.17

You is the last king of Western Zhou in the usual periodization, not the final king of the Zhou house. The conventional dates of 771 and 770 BCE help locate collapse and the eastward move. They do not mean that all relationships disappeared together.

Warnings about Heaven's mandate and languages of remonstrance existed. The difficulty was making them effective when a powerful person most needed to change course. A norm can identify a wrong without preventing that wrong from taking other people's lives.8

This is not simply an elegant system dying of old age. People made choices, others were excluded, alliances formed, and war occurred. The limits of an arrangement must be read through those acts, not used as a curve that erases the actors.

IX. What Zhou left

Later thinkers continued speaking through and against Zhou. Some sought to restore relationships, some to reorganize them, and some questioned whether accumulating rules also accumulated difficulties.

Xunzi's account of ritual links desire, conflict, and distribution: order has a job that needs explaining. The Han Feizi stresses the danger of clinging to old measures under changed conditions. The Laozi makes readers suspicious of proliferating prescriptions and intervention.9 These are not three entire traditions pressing three simple buttons. They are different directions of inquiry into how common life can work without flattening the people who inhabit it.

Zhou's legacy therefore includes more than a dynasty's duration. It includes languages for demanding, criticizing, negotiating, and changing. A language can decorate authority and also expose something authority has hidden.

Founders cannot live on behalf of everyone who follows. The best inheritance is not perpetual obedience to their answers, but continued capacity to address problems they never encountered.

Next, the king remains while the places where decisions are made begin to shift. We will ask not only how a title loses power, but why people still act through it.
