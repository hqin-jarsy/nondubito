唐前期——一套好制度，还在等什么

Early Tang — What Good Institutions Still Need

Han Qin (秦汉)

名单上，一个人应该出门服役；另一份名单上，他家应该按时交粮。两项规定都说得通，放到同一户人家的灶台前，却不会因此多出一双手。

我们讲唐朝，容易先想到一个好制度终于成熟的时代：三省分工，法律成文，考试选人，土地与兵役相配合。可制度能把事情连起来，并不等于它能保证连接所需的一切。收成、消息、愿意说话的人，以及听完之后真的肯改的人，都不能从条文里直接长出来。

唐前期最值得重看之处，恰恰在这里。它既不是隋的简单重启，也不是一个完美系统终于被坏人弄坏的故事。

一、有人负责劝谏，不等于坏消息进得来

《贞观政要》记载，627年，唐太宗对王珪谈起官员彼此顾惜情面的毛病：明知道有问题，却担心说出来伤了同僚。这里没有谁正式取消劝谏。一个部门可以照常存在，一件公文可以照常经过它，而应当被指出的问题没有被指出。1

太宗还有一段谈弓的故事。他以为自己懂弓，直到工匠指出木材纹理与弓的好坏之间的关系，才意识到，连天天拿在手里的东西，也有自己不知道的地方。听取不同官员的意见，不只是摆出谦逊姿态，更是承认自己看不到的事确实有人看到了。

魏征曾服务于李建成，后来能向太宗进谏，当然值得注意。不过，面对皇帝说话、递上奏疏、门下省按职掌审议退回文书，是不同的渠道。不能因为有几次著名的纳谏，就说所有渠道从此都安全，所有错误都能自动得到纠正。

626年的宫门流血也不能被写成一份保证书：经历过内斗，所以太宗以后一定知道节制。我们知道权力是怎样转移的，却不能据此替他写出一生的内心独白。到了647年，翠微宫、玉华宫的营建仍在进行；645年的高句丽战事后，也还有648年的军事行动。唐没有简单重复隋的结局，不等于唐没有继续征发。2

同样，考试提供了一条入仕通道，但学习机会仍与家世、财力和学校资格有关。630年的“天可汗”称号，也要放回战争、归附、授官的关系里看，不能用皇帝的血缘直接解释政治包容。3

好制度的第一层条件，不是设计者特别善良，而是受到影响的人有办法把实际情况送进决策。第二层才更难：这些情况送进来之后，能不能改变决定。

二、武则天走过去了，不等于所有人都有路

武则天从皇后、参与政务者、临朝者，走到690年称帝，并不是只换了最高职位的性别，其他一切原封不动。国号、官名、历法、祖庙安排和合法性的说明，都发生了变化。佛教论述也参与了对新权力的解释。4

她开辟进言和自荐渠道，702年又设置武举。对不在原有圈子里的人，这些办法可能是一扇门。但门打开和进去以后能安全说话，是两件事。告密、酷吏和惩罚也存在于同一个政治环境里；697年处死来俊臣，不会使此前受害者的遭遇自动消失。

把这段历史当作一个干净的实验——只改变君主性别，证明制度完全不受性别影响——反而会遮住最重要的差别。一个女人能够登上顶点，不等于普通女子获得了与男子一样的学校资格、考试机会和仕进道路。例外可以松动人们的想象，却不能代替通行的规则。

武周时期也不是没有外患的平稳十五年。696至698年的战争，需要军队、粮食和决策。到705年，张易之、张昌宗兄弟被杀，宫廷行动迫使武则天交出权力。这不是单凭“年老失控”四个字就能说明的过程。

她的故事留下一个比“制度胜过个人”更具体的问题：走过一条险路的人，是否让后来者也有了可走的路？最高处出现过谁，是政治史的一部分；台阶下面的人能否进入，也是。

三、士兵在家吃饭，谁替制度付了钱

均田与府兵常被概括成一个漂亮的配合：给农民土地，让农民种地，农闲出征，国家就不用另养一支昂贵军队。这样的概括容易让成本消失在“自给”两个字里。

田亩规定里有应还授和可继承的区别，也有允许买卖的条件、土地不足的处理办法。规定应该得到多少，不是每户实际得到多少。兵役则涉及装备、口粮、行程和马匹；有家庭的承担，也有公共与集体的投入。按距离安排轮换，本身就承认：离家一百里和离家两千里，不是同一种差事。5

如果服役时间拉长，家中劳力减少，耕作和赋税却还照旧，负担不会因为账上没有写成国家军费就不存在。它只是留在家里，由没出门的人补上。

变化也并非在无人决策的情况下静静发生。713年的方案没有落实；718年有新的选拔，723年有宿卫招募；737至738年，边地长期服役与旧兵遣归又有调整；749年停止相关鱼书的传递，是另一处标记。宫廷宿卫与边境常驻，不能并成一次改制。6

募兵让国家以新的方式承担供给，却不会天然把士兵变成“谁给钱就替谁造反”的人。关系如何变化，还要看任命、训练、驻地、奖惩和朝廷实际能提供什么。安禄山能整合的力量需要解释；郭子仪、李光弼带领的边地军队为什么能支撑唐朝，也同样需要解释。

这里不是农民兵一定忠诚、职业兵一定危险。真正的问题是：一项承诺所依靠的生活条件变了，制度是否及时改写了自己的要求。

四、繁荣的数字，未必回答正在发生的问题

账面数字越整齐，越容易给人一种已经看清全局的感觉。开元、天宝年间，登记人口总数上升。但总人口登记、纳税人口、实际居住人口，是不同的数，不能拿一个代替另一个，更不能一边说登记人数上升，一边直接据此证明税户持续减少。7

宇文融检括户口的记载还提醒我们，考核本身会改变信息。被要求报出“新增”的官员，有可能把本来就在册的户口也算进成绩。问题不在于所有统计都是假的，而在于一个数字要经过什么过程，才成为上级愿意看到的数字。

人员任用也是如此。安禄山到751年兼领三镇，是权力逐步集中的结果，不是所有节度使一经设立就已经独立。李林甫任相，本纪与列传分别记在734、735年，不能再写成736年起任。747年的“野无遗贤”，出自一次特殊选拔无人通过后的表述，也不是从此所有科举和进言同时停止。8

但这些限定并不减轻问题。一个人提出异议需要冒险，其他人就会重新估计开口的代价。737年，一边有刑律与死刑复核的安排，一边有周子谅因进言受罚；749年又有告发李林甫者遭惩。纸上的审慎和具体处置中的威吓，可以同时存在。

所以，盛世不是一个让制度免于复查的形容词。越是报表好看、边疆强盛、朝廷自信的时候，越要追问：有没有某些情况，已经很难用不惹祸的方式报告上来了？

五、断裂不是一天，恢复也不是一道命令

755年叛乱爆发，756年安禄山称帝，唐廷离开长安，肃宗在灵武即位；757年两京收复，洛阳后来又失而复得；到763年史朝义败亡，吐蕃却又一度进入长安。把这些压成“叛乱平定、唐朝恢复”，会漏掉人们实际上经历的反复。9

高仙芝、封常清退守潼关后，边令诚的奏报推动了处决。史书保存了封常清的临终陈述与高仙芝的否认。这些话不能直接替代完整的责任调查，但它们让一个问题变得十分尖锐：关于战场的不同说法，能不能在杀人以前送到决策者面前？

越是紧急，越容易把询问理由当作拖延。然而执行很快，并不等于知道得足够。杀掉传来坏消息的人，也不会因此补上缺兵、失地和粮运的漏洞。

唐能够继续存在，并非只剩空壳在滑行。军队重新组织，地方与中央不断谈判，学校、考试和财政也在改。764年安排学生归学、提供粮食，在交通困难下调整考试；780年两税法又改变征收的依据。神策军从边军发展为宫廷军事力量，也是逐步发生的事，而不是755年忽然出现的整套答案。10

这些措施不等于百姓已经恢复安稳。它们说明，幸存本身包含新的工作。重夺一座城是一件事，让城里的人能够重新过日子，是另一件。

六、记住教训，还要让教训有用

唐前期留下的，不是一条“盛世只能维持几代”的时钟。它让我们看见四种容易被混为一谈的东西。

第一是规则与条件。土地、兵役、赋税可以在文字里彼此配合，但季节、距离和家庭劳力仍需要照顾。第二是例外与通路。某个人做到了一件前所未有的事，并不表示其他人也有相同机会。

第三是记录与理解。一个数、一份捷报、一道控告，都要追问是谁怎样做出来的。第四是理解与行动。即使情况已经有人说清楚，也还需要允许修改的程序，以及不因说出问题就被惩罚的条件。

吴兢在玄宗时期整理贞观政事，说明前人的经验并没有简单消失。书还在，故事也有人讲。但一本谈纳谏的书，不能自己保护正在进谏的人。11

杜甫《兵车行》里，行役、田地、赋税和家人落在同一幅画面中。诗不是户口调查，却能让整齐的制度名称重新有了人的重量。那个被叫去服役的人，不只是兵额；留在家中的人，也不只是负责补上空缺的劳力。

一套制度值得珍惜，不因为它终于把人安排好了，而因为它还愿意听见：有人已经无法照原来的安排生活。所谓继续，不只是把旧办法传下去，也包括让后来的人有权说，这里必须改一改了。

One register says a man must report for military service. Another says his household must deliver grain. Both requirements may be perfectly regular. Together, they do not give the family another pair of hands.

Early Tang government is often remembered as a successful arrangement: divided administrative responsibilities, written law, recruitment examinations, and a connection between landholding and military duty. Yet an arrangement cannot manufacture all the conditions it needs. Harvests must arrive. Information must travel. Someone must be willing to report a difficulty, and someone else must be able to act on it.

The interesting question is not simply why a good system eventually failed. It is how much work was required while it was succeeding—and who was doing that work out of sight.

I. Having advisers is not the same as receiving advice

In a conversation recorded in the Essentials of Government in the Zhenguan Reign, Taizong complained to Wang Gui in 627 about officials protecting one another's feelings. They could recognize a problem and still hesitate to name it. No office had to be abolished for its corrective function to weaken.1

The same collection tells of a bow maker who unsettled Taizong's confidence in his knowledge of bows. Something he had used for years contained distinctions he had not understood. The point extends beyond a ruler's display of humility. Knowledge of governing does not include, automatically, the knowledge of everyone being governed. Rotating audiences with officials could bring unfamiliar circumstances within reach.

Wei Zheng, who had served Taizong's rival Li Jiancheng, became a celebrated remonstrator. But speaking to the emperor, submitting a memorial, and exercising an office's formal power to review documents were different activities. A successful objection through one channel did not guarantee safety through all the others.

Nor does the violence through which Taizong secured succession in 626 give us access to his private motives for the rest of his life. He may have learned from the Sui; that does not mean he refused every costly undertaking. Palace construction continued in 647, and the withdrawal from Koguryŏ in 645 was not the end of military operations there.2

Two other achievements need similar care. Examinations opened a route into office, but access to schooling remained affected by family standing and resources. Recognition as Heavenly Khagan in 630 belonged to a history of warfare, submission and appointment, not to a simple equation between mixed ancestry and political inclusion.3

The practical test is therefore more demanding than “Did the ruler listen?” Could the relevant person get a particular difficulty heard—and could hearing it change what happened next?

II. Wu Zetian crossed a boundary, but not every boundary disappeared

Wu Zetian's movement from empress to participant in government, regent and finally sovereign in 690 was not a controlled experiment in which only the ruler's sex changed. The dynastic name, official nomenclature, calendar, ancestral arrangements and explanations of legitimate rule were altered. Buddhist arguments helped authorize the new position.4

She provided channels for submissions and self-recommendation, and military examinations followed in 702. These could create opportunities outside established circles. They coexisted, however, with denunciation and persecution. An invitation to speak is not the same as dependable protection after speaking. The execution of Lai Junchen in 697 did not cancel what had happened under the machinery he served.

The distinction matters especially for women. A woman reaching the throne demonstrated that a supposedly unthinkable possibility could occur. It did not give ordinary women the same educational qualifications and routes to office as men. An exceptional ascent can change what people imagine without changing the stairs available to everyone else.

Her reign was not a peaceful interval insulated from outside demands. The wars of 696–698 required supplies and decisions. In 705, a palace operation killed the brothers Zhang Yizhi and Zhang Changzong and forced a transfer of power. Neither the achievement nor its ending is explained by reducing Wu to her sex or to an invented account of her psychology.

The broader question is worth keeping: when someone gets through a formidable barrier, what has become easier for the person who comes after them?

III. A soldier maintained at home is not a soldier without a cost

The equal-field arrangements and the fubing military system can sound admirably economical. Households cultivate land, supply service, and support men who become soldiers when required. The treasury appears to be spared the expense of maintaining everyone permanently.

But the expense has not vanished. Some of it remains at the hearth.

Land regulations distinguished different kinds of holding and permitted certain transfers. Their specifications do not establish how much land every household actually received. Military service involved equipment, provisions, travel and horses, with household, collective and public contributions. Rotation according to distance acknowledged that a distant posting was not equivalent to a nearby one.5

A longer absence could leave less labour for cultivation while other obligations continued. Someone at home had to make up the difference. A rule connecting farming with service did not make the farming look after itself.

The transition was not a single, silent collapse without administrative choices. A proposal in 713 was not implemented; there were selections in 718 and recruitment for palace service in 723. Border arrangements changed again in 737–738. The stopping of particular military documents in 749 marks another stage. Central guards and long-term frontier service cannot be compressed into one reform.6

Paid or permanent service also did not automatically turn troops into the commander's private property. Appointment, stationing, training, rewards and actual provision all mattered. An Lushan's concentration of power requires explanation; so does the fact that regional troops under Guo Ziyi and Li Guangbi defended the Tang.

The issue is not whether farmers are naturally loyal and professional soldiers naturally dangerous. It is whether obligations are revised when the lives supporting them have changed.

IV. Prosperity does not tell us whether the reports are good

A rising total can answer one question while concealing another. Registered population figures increased between 732 and 754. That does not, by itself, establish either an increase or a decrease in taxpayers. Registered persons, taxable categories and the population actually living in a place are not interchangeable measures.7

Accounts of Yuwen Rong's registration work also raise a familiar problem: incentives shape what is reported. If success means discovering additional households, existing households may be presented as new discoveries. That does not make every number worthless. It makes the production of the number part of what needs examining.

Appointments accumulated in similarly particular ways. An Lushan held three commands by 751; this does not mean every military governor had been independent from the moment the office existed. Li Linfu's appointment as chief minister is placed in 734 by the annals and 735 by his biography, rather than in 736. The notorious claim that no worthy men remained outside office followed a special recruitment exercise in 747, not the termination of every examination and avenue of criticism.8

Precision does not make the problem less serious. Punishment changes the next person's calculation. Legal review and the punishment of an inconvenient speaker could coexist: measures concerning criminal law in 737 did not protect Zhou Ziliang when he remonstrated. A later attempt to accuse Li Linfu brought danger to the accuser.

A prosperous period therefore needs inspection, not exemption from it. When the court feels successful, are there facts that have become difficult to report without appearing disloyal or incompetent?

V. Rupture takes time; so does recovery

The rebellion began in 755. An Lushan assumed an imperial title in 756, while the Tang court fled Chang'an and Suzong became emperor at Lingwu. Both capitals were recovered in 757, but Luoyang changed hands again. Shi Chaoyi's defeat in 763 did not make the year an uncomplicated restoration: Tibetan forces also entered Chang'an.9

Inside that chronology lies a sharper question about information. After Gao Xianzhi and Feng Changqing withdrew to Tong Pass, reports by Bian Lingcheng contributed to their execution. The histories preserve Feng's final statement and Gao's denial. These are participants' claims, not a complete independent inquiry. But they expose the importance of sequence: could an alternative account of the fighting reach the decision-maker before the men were killed?

An emergency makes speed attractive. Yet a quick execution does not supply missing troops or repair a supply route. Treating an explanation as an excuse can remove precisely the information needed to make the next decision.

Tang survival after the rebellion was not merely inertia. Armies were reorganized; relations with regional powers were negotiated; educational and fiscal arrangements changed. Measures in 764 addressed students' return, grain for their support, and examinations under disrupted conditions. The Two-Tax reform came in 780. The Shence army's development from frontier force to palace power likewise had its own sequence.10

None of these measures proves that ordinary life had recovered. They establish that recovery required new work. Recapturing a city and making it possible to live there again were not the same accomplishment.

VI. A lesson must still be usable

Early Tang history does not provide a clock announcing how many generations a golden age can last. It reveals several distinctions that success encourages people to overlook.

Rules are not their supporting conditions. An arrangement of land, tax and service still depends on seasons, distances and household labour. An exception is not a general route: one extraordinary career does not establish equal access.

A record is not yet understanding. A population total, a victory report or an accusation must be examined as something produced by particular people. And understanding is not yet correction. Someone must have the authority to change the requirement, while the person describing its failure must be able to survive having done so.

Wu Jing's work on the Zhenguan conversations during Xuanzong's reign shows that earlier experience was not simply forgotten. The book was available; exemplary stories could be repeated. But a book praising remonstrance cannot, by itself, protect a remonstrator.11

Du Fu's Ballad of the Army Carts brings service, fields, taxation and family separation into the same picture. Poetry is not a census. It can nevertheless restore the weight that an administrative term removes. The departing man is not merely a place in the military establishment; those left behind are not a reserve of labour waiting to fill his absence.

An institution is worth preserving not because it has finally assigned everyone a place, but because it can still hear that someone cannot live under the assignment. Continuity must include the possibility of saying: this part now needs to change.
