芭蕾与舞蹈

Ballet and Dance

「身体如何建立和打破预测模型」

"How the Body Builds and Breaks Prediction Models"

秦汉 Han Qin | 2026

第一篇在听觉经验中提出"生定展固"，第二篇把它扩展到多通道关系：一个要素趋于稳定时，另一个可能重开期待。到目前为止，这仍是SAE的审美假说，而非证明。

现在要问一个更根本的问题：凿构循环是不是依赖听觉？

如果这组坐标在无音乐或音乐不居主位的作品中仍有解释力，它就可能跨出听觉。但这还不足以推出它是人类认知的基本操作，只能说明框架值得在视觉—动觉经验中继续检验。

舞蹈是合适的检验场：音乐有时与身体并重，有时退居背景，有些作品也在接近沉默中进行。身体动作因而可以成为主要分析对象，但不能把所有舞蹈都说成主动"去除听觉"。

本篇要问的不是如何确认跨模态定律，而是：在哪些作品、观众和表演条件下，这四个坐标仍然有用？

一、身体如何建立预测模型

观看身体运动时，人常会依据姿势、惯性、情境和经验形成下一步期待；这种期待与听觉预测可以比较，却不必被写成始终运行的同一系统。

你看一个人走路，可能预期下一步落点、步幅和速度。熟悉动作往往不引人注意，但文化习惯、运动经验、身体差异和场景都会改变预测的准确度。

但如果那个人突然改变步态——比如毫无预兆地停下来，或者步伐突然变成了舞步——你会立刻注意到。你的预测模型被打破了。

这与音乐期待有结构类比：原有走向被改变。但视觉—动觉经验还涉及共感、重力、身体训练与空间，不能缩成"换一个通道"。

编舞可以建立并改变身体期待，也可以追求持续、仪式、任务、参与或纯粹运动。凿构循环是其中一种批评读法，不是编舞工作的本质定义。

二、芭蕾：程式化中的凿

古典芭蕾高度程式化，包含五个基本脚位、系统化的步伐词汇与不同时期/学派的身体线条要求。训练会提高观众辨认动作关系的能力，却不意味着每个动作都能被精确预测。

这跟京剧的板式、歌剧的咏叹调convention是同一个结构位置：程式化提供了极强的"定"，让"展"的空间看起来很小，但实际上让展的效果极大。

一段古典芭蕾variation可以被读成"生定展固"，但并非天然都具备完整四步：

生：开场的预备姿态和前几个动作建立了这段variation的动作语汇和节奏模式。你的大脑开始预测——这是一段抒情的adagio还是一段快速的allegro？动作的方向、速度、力度在几秒内被你的预测模型捕获。

定：动作语汇被重复和发展。你看到了几个相似的乐句（是的，舞蹈也有"乐句"），你的预测变得越来越稳定。你开始觉得"我知道这段独舞在做什么了"。

展：变奏段落。一个你没预期的技术段落出现了——可能是一串出乎意料的快速旋转，可能是一个突然改变方向的跳跃，可能是一个在你以为动作结束的地方继续延伸的平衡。你的预测模型被打破了。但跟音乐里的"展"一样，这不是随机的——它在一个你能感受到但无法提前计算的逻辑里。

固：最后的pose或回归。动作回到了某种你认识的语汇，但经历了"展"之后，同样的姿态在你眼中的重量不同了。闭合，但闭合的内容更厚。

四个位置由此可以标出。它与贝多芬第五交响曲的比较只在"期待如何稳定和改变"这一层成立，并非把听觉直接换成视觉—动觉。

《天鹅湖》第二幕与第三幕提供一种版本依赖的对照。Odette的抒情线条与Odile更炫目的技术段落常被演员和编舞塑造成质地反差，但不同制作、舞者和观众会强调不同关系。

第三幕著名的三十二个fouettés是一项持续旋转的技术传统，常成为期待与计数的焦点。把它读成"定被推到极致后翻转为展"是一种解释，不是每一圈都在所有观众意料之外的事实。

白天鹅可以被读为construct，黑天鹅可以被读为chisel；叙事象征、音乐、表演传统和技术展示也同样参与。第二幕的经验可能加强第三幕反差，却不是感受第三幕的必要条件。

三、皮娜·鲍什：定→展的相变

如果说古典芭蕾在程式化的框架内做凿，皮娜·鲍什（Pina Bausch）做了一个更激进的操作：她把"定"本身变成了"展"。

重复是鲍什的重要手法之一：日常动作、手势或关系被反复执行，次数和语境因作品而异，不应虚构为固定几十次。

开始时观众也许辨认动作，随后形成pattern；继续重复可能带来无聊，也可能立刻显出性别、劳动、暴力或喜剧关系。不存在所有人共同经过的信息量归零阶段。

当动作继续，疲惫、失误、动作变形以及与他人的关系可能进入前景。

本文把这种经验称为定→展的"相变"：重复不增加新动作，却改变观众关注的关系。它是审美比喻，不是具有固定临界点的生理过程。

预测模型也不一定被自己的精确性"压垮"；更朴素的解释是，注意从动作类别转向执行差异、情感和场景。

这是一个非常独特的凿构操作。在音乐里很少见（虽然某些极简主义音乐，比如Steve Reich的早期作品，有类似的效果）。在舞蹈里，鲍什把它发展成了一种完整的方法论。

《穆勒咖啡馆》（Café Müller）1978年在伍珀塔尔首演，使用Henry Purcell的音乐，舞台与服装由Rolf Borzik设计。作品中闭眼穿行、椅子碰撞与一组拥抱—分离动作被反复重置；本文可以说重复改变了关系的重量，却不能虚构"同一跌倒第三十次"这样的计数。

四、breaking battle：循环的一种压缩读法

从芭蕾的精致和鲍什的极端，转到一个看起来完全不同的形态：街舞battle。

breaking在1970年代纽约Bronx的街区文化中形成，battle回合常较短，但没有所有场景通用的三十秒至一分钟规则。下面是四步的一种可能读法，不是固定比赛公式：

生：Groove。舞者先建立一个基本的律动感——top rock或者简单的步法。你的大脑在几秒内捕获了他的风格和节奏模式。

定：Flow。Groove被发展和确认。你看到了这个舞者的动作语汇，你开始预测他会做什么。

展：Freeze或power move。突然，一切变了。一个你没预期的定格姿势（freeze），或者一个突然的力量爆发动作（windmill, headspin）。你的预测模型被打破了——而且好的b-boy的freeze不只是技术展示，它在一个精确的音乐拍点上出现，跟你对节奏的预期产生碰撞。

固：回到groove。但经历了freeze之后，同样的groove在你眼中的重量变了。闭合。

一个短回合可以容纳四个位置，也可能故意悬置回归、把高潮放在别处。它与贝多芬或鲍什的比较只说明不同尺度都能组织期待。

battle的实时、即兴和对抗性让回应关系格外突出，但这些特征并非其他舞蹈形式绝无仅有。舞者可以引用对手、音乐、评审和cypher共同形成的情境，而不必各自完成一轮固定循环。

这与爵士即兴互动有可比之处，却不构成完全同构。

五、同构对照：《天鹅湖》与街舞battle

一个来自十九世纪俄国帝国剧场传统，一个形成于1970年代纽约Bronx的breaking文化。两者的制度、身体谱系与观看关系差异巨大。

芭蕾有学院化词汇与线条规范；breaking也有toprock、downrock/footwork、freeze和power move等共享词汇与社区标准，不能说它"没有标准化词汇表"。街舞又包含多种不同传统，本文这里只谈breaking。

两者都可以用建立、确认、重开和闭合来分析，但这是一套外加坐标，不是它们实际做着同一操作的证明。

黑天鹅的三十二个fouettés与b-boy/b-girl的freeze可以作解释性类比：持续运动与突然定格都可能改变时间期待。但两者的技术、意义和观众知识并不相同。

这里的同构是受限比较，而非"编码不同、凿完全相同"。

六、异构同效：能剧与皮娜·鲍什

日本能乐与皮娜·鲍什提供另一项比较：程式中的微差与重复中的关系变化，是否都能重开观看？它不是同一余项效果的证明。

能乐是综合剧场，结合唱念、器乐、舞、面具、服装、道具和叙事，并非可抽离为"纯舞蹈"。suriashi、扇法等高度程式化，却因流派、角色和具体演出而有变化。

面具角度会改变光影与表情感受，步速与停顿也能重组时间；但"仰几度就是喜、低几度就是悲、快半拍就是紧张"过于机械。效果取决于面具、角色、舞台光线与观众训练。

能剧的凿靠的是微观偏离在极度精确的construct里产生的放大效果。

鲍什的作品可以用另一条路径来读：日常动作持续重复后，注意从动作类别转向关系和执行差异。本文把它比喻为相变，但不排除微观偏离、表演程式与其他机制同时作用。

两种凿的方式几乎是相反的。能剧是在极度精确的construct里做最小的偏离。鲍什是在极度简单的construct里做最大的重复。

两者都可能让观众感到既有观看方式不足，但留下的问题并不等价。所谓"余项"要由具体演出和观众经验说明，不能预设为同一认知功能。

异构同效在这里仍是比较假说：不同的凿，可能都重开期待，却不保证同一种不可穷尽。

5a、与一位前辈的对话：Laban的动作分析

舞蹈理论领域有一个天然的对话对象：Rudolf Laban（鲁道夫·拉班）。

Laban的思想后来与Irmgard Bartenieff等人的工作共同发展为Laban/Bartenieff Movement Studies。常用四类是身体（Body）、力效（Effort）、形态（Shape）与空间（Space）；Effort又考察重量、时间、空间注意与流动等因素。不能把当代LMA全部简单归为Laban一人一次完成。

这套框架提供跨场景的观察语言，却也有自身谱系与训练制度，不是文化中立的"通用语言"。

LMA不仅列举动作属性，也关注身体组织、动力关系、形态变化和空间意图。本文关心动作序列怎样建立期待，二者分析问题不同，不能用后者宣称补足前者。

把LMA类比为乐理有启发性，也有限：它是观察与实践系统，不只是静态编码。

因此，Laban/Bartenieff研究为本文提供描述资源，却不验证"生定展固"；本文也没有比它"多一个维度"，只是在另一尺度提出可反驳的序列假说。

七、两个边界案例：广播体操与即兴

第一篇比较了重复性流行片段与实验声音；这里把类似问题移到广播体操与即兴舞蹈，但不预先把它们归为退化形态。

广播体操以功能训练和集体同步为目标，程式稳定，但执行中的身体差异、节奏感、共同记忆与场域仍可能产生意义。它不是失败的舞蹈，也不应被判为"零凿"。

用本文坐标说，它常强调建立和确认；这不等于第一次后模型完全固化或没有余项，因为评价尺度本就不同。

即兴舞蹈也不等于全程无pattern。身体习惯、任务规则、空间、与他人的回应及观众对表演者的熟悉都会形成期待；即兴可以构，也可以凿。

因此，跨通道类比不能写成等式：

重复性流行片段与广播体操，都可能强调确认；实验声音与即兴舞蹈，都可能让参照在过程中生成。但各自目的、材料和观看方式不同。

这种相似性只能支持继续比较，不能作为认知系统存在通用操作的证据。

八、反例：技术展示型舞蹈

每篇需要一个具体的反例。

某些竞技或综艺舞蹈把高难度动作连续排列，形成"开场炫技—中段炫技—结尾炫技"的观看感。这不是所有比赛作品的共同结构。

有些观众不会想看第二遍，也有人会为了技术、音乐性或身体细节反复观看。

为什么？因为它的结构是"展-展-展-展"——全程在打破你的预期（"还能做到这个？""还能更难？"），但从来没有建立过稳定的construct。你没有形成过预测模型，所以"打破"只是一连串的技术惊喜，不是凿。

这与即兴舞蹈不是同一种问题。即兴可以建立关系，炫技也可以通过次序、呼吸、风险和音乐形成construct；批评必须指出某个节目究竟在哪里让关系来不及发展。

余项不能被断言为零。"只记住好厉害"是一种可能的观众反应，而非记忆定律。

黑天鹅的三十二个fouettés常被嵌入第三幕的角色、音乐与场面关系中，因此可能不只是孤立技术。但它也一直具有炫技与明星展示功能；同一动作放进集锦不自动成为噪音。

2026年米兰—科尔蒂纳冬奥会女子单人滑冠军Alysa Liu提供了一个更具体、也更适合保留反例可能的案例。她在短节目后列第三，凭自由滑反超夺金，总分226.79分。

Liu十三岁成为当时最年轻的美国全国冠军，早期以阿克塞尔三周跳和四周跳等技术能力闻名；2022年退役、2024年复出后，她的节目表达与滑行关系受到更多关注。

本文可以把她的奥运自由滑读成技术被放进更完整的节奏与情感弧线：难度不只是连续展示，而在段落中改变重量。但这是批评性阅读，不是她获胜的单一原因。

裁判分数由明确技术与节目内容规则产生，对手表现也影响名次；不能说评委和观众因凿构循环同时被说服，更不能把技术分等同construct、节目内容分等同余项。

这个案例真正支持的是较窄的判断：技术与节目结构并非对立，运动员的关系处理会改变观众怎样理解同一类动作。

九、通道转换的假说

回顾本篇建立的东西。

我们在古典芭蕾、皮娜·鲍什的Tanztheater、breaking battle，以及包含舞蹈的能乐综合剧场中检验了凿构循环。它们来自不同制度与历史，不能只用"西方/东方"或"程式化/反程式化"二分。

这些形式差异巨大，四个坐标在其中有时清楚、有时需要修正；能否标出四步本身不能证明它们完整运行。

音乐在芭蕾、breaking、鲍什作品与能乐中的角色各不相同。能乐更是唱念、器乐与舞蹈结合的剧场，不能作为"几乎无音乐的纯身体"证据。

较谨慎的结论是：身体运动即使不由音乐主导，也可能建立、改变和重新闭合期待。

因此，本篇支持的是一项可证伪假说：凿构循环或许不依赖听觉通道。 它尚不是人类认知系统的基本定律，视觉—动觉期待也受文化、训练、身体和场景制约。

第一篇提出听觉模型，第二篇提出跨通道关系，第三篇把问题移到身体；三篇都没有完成跨模态证明。

下一篇，我们进入最后一个检验场：话剧和电影。当叙事成为主通道，所有媒介同时运行时，凿构循环如何多层级并行？为什么有些电影看十遍还有东西，有些看一遍就够？

而且我们将引入一个在前三篇中已经出现但尚未正式定义的概念：伪凿。

Essay I proposed Arise-Settle-Unfold-Fix in auditory experience, and Essay II extended it to relations among channels. At this point the framework remains an SAE aesthetic hypothesis, not a proof.

Now a more fundamental question: does the chisel-construct cycle depend on audition?

If the coordinates retain explanatory force in works without music, or where music is not primary, they may extend beyond audition. That would not yet make them a basic operation of human cognition; it would justify testing them in visual-kinesthetic experience.

Dance is a fitting testing ground. Music may be equal to the body, recede, or approach silence, allowing bodily movement to become the main analytical object. Not every dance should be described as actively "removing audition."

The question is not how to confirm a cross-modal law, but under which works, audiences, and performance conditions the four coordinates remain useful.

I. How the Body Builds Predictive Models

When watching bodies move, people often form expectations from posture, momentum, context, and experience. This can be compared with auditory prediction without treating the two as one system operating perpetually.

You may anticipate where a walker's next step will land, and how large or fast it will be. Familiar movement often escapes notice, but culture, athletic experience, bodily difference, and setting all alter predictive accuracy.

But if that person suddenly changes gait — stopping without warning, or abruptly shifting into a dance step — you notice immediately. Your predictive model has been broken.

There is a structural analogy with musical expectation: an anticipated trajectory changes. Yet visual-kinesthetic experience also involves empathy, gravity, bodily training, and space; it is not simply audition through another channel.

Choreography can establish and alter bodily expectation, but it can also pursue duration, ritual, task, participation, or movement itself. The cycle is one critical reading, not the fundamental definition of a choreographer's work.

II. Ballet: Chisel Within Formalization

Classical ballet is highly formalized, with five basic foot positions, a systematic vocabulary of steps, and requirements for line that vary across periods and schools. Training can sharpen recognition of movement relations without making every action precisely predictable.

This occupies the same structural position as Peking opera's modal frameworks and Western opera's aria conventions: formalization provides extremely strong Settle, making the space for Unfold appear small but in fact making the effect of Unfold extremely large.

A classical ballet variation can be read through Arise-Settle-Unfold-Fix, but not every variation naturally contains a complete four-step structure:

Arise: The preparatory posture and first few movements establish the variation's movement vocabulary and rhythmic pattern. Your brain begins predicting — is this a lyrical adagio or a rapid allegro? Direction, speed, and force are captured by your predictive model within seconds.

Settle: The movement vocabulary is repeated and developed. You see several similar phrases (yes, dance has "phrases" too); your prediction grows increasingly stable. You begin to feel: "I understand what this solo is doing."

Unfold: The variation passage. An unanticipated technical passage appears — perhaps a series of unexpected rapid turns, a sudden directional change in a jump, a balance that continues extending where you thought the movement had ended. Your predictive model is broken. But as with musical Unfold, this is not random — it follows a logic you can sense but cannot compute in advance.

Fix: The final pose or return. Movement returns to a vocabulary you recognize, but having passed through Unfold, the same posture now carries different weight in your perception. Closure, but thicker closure.

The four positions can thus be marked. Comparison with Beethoven's Fifth holds only at the level of how expectation stabilizes and changes; visual-kinesthetic experience is not simply audition with the channel replaced.

Acts II and III of Swan Lake offer a version-dependent contrast. Odette's lyric line and Odile's more spectacular technique are often shaped as opposing qualities, but productions, dancers, and audiences foreground different relations.

The famous thirty-two fouettés in Act III are a tradition of sustained turning and often become a focus of counting and expectation. Reading them as Settle pushed until it flips into Unfold is an interpretation, not a fact that each turn exceeds every viewer's expectation.

The White Swan can be read as construct and the Black Swan as chisel, while narrative symbolism, music, performance tradition, and virtuoso display also participate. Act II may intensify Act III's contrast, but it is not a necessary condition for experiencing it.

III. Pina Bausch: Phase Transition from Settle to Unfold

If classical ballet chisels within the formalized framework, Pina Bausch performed a more radical operation: she turned Settle itself into Unfold.

Repetition is one important Bausch technique: everyday actions, gestures, or relations recur, with frequency and context varying by work rather than following a fixed count.

A viewer may first identify the action and then learn its pattern. Continued repetition may produce boredom, or immediately reveal relations of gender, labor, violence, or comedy. There is no universal interval of zero information.

As movement continues, fatigue, error, deformation, and relations with others may come forward.

This essay calls that experience a "phase transition" from Settle to Unfold: no new action is added, but the relations receiving attention change. The phrase is an aesthetic metaphor, not a physiological process with a fixed threshold.

The predictive model need not be "crushed by its own precision." A simpler account is that attention shifts from the category of action to differences of execution, affect, and situation.

This is a highly distinctive chisel-construct operation. It is rare in music (though certain minimalist works, such as Steve Reich's early pieces, produce a similar effect). In dance, Bausch developed it into a complete methodology.

Café Müller premiered in Wuppertal in 1978, uses music by Henry Purcell, and has set and costume design by Rolf Borzik. Closed-eye passages among chairs and a repeated sequence of embrace and separation continually reset relations. The essay may say that repetition changes their weight, but should not invent a count such as "the same fall on its thirtieth occurrence."

IV. Breaking Battle: One Compressed Reading of the Cycle

From ballet's refinement and Bausch's extremity, turn to an apparently entirely different form: the street dance battle.

Breaking formed within 1970s Bronx neighborhood culture. Battle rounds are often brief, but no rule of thirty seconds to one minute governs every setting. What follows is one possible four-step reading, not a fixed battle formula:

Arise: Groove. The dancer establishes a basic sense of rhythm — top rock or simple footwork. Your brain captures their style and rhythmic pattern within seconds.

Settle: Flow. The groove is developed and confirmed. You see the dancer's movement vocabulary; you begin to predict what they will do.

Unfold: Freeze or power move. Suddenly, everything changes. An unanticipated frozen pose (freeze), or a sudden explosive movement (windmill, headspin). Your predictive model is broken — and a good b-boy's freeze does not merely display technique; it arrives on a precise musical beat, creating a collision with your rhythmic expectation.

Fix: Return to groove. But having passed through the freeze, the same groove now carries different weight. Closure.

A short round can contain all four positions, or suspend return and place emphasis elsewhere. Comparison with Beethoven or Bausch shows only that different scales can organize expectation.

The battle's real-time, improvisatory, adversarial quality makes response especially visible, but those features are not absent from every other dance. Dancers may quote an opponent, music, judges, and cypher context without each completing a fixed individual cycle.

This is comparable with jazz interaction in some respects, not fully isomorphic with it.

V. Isomorphic Comparison: Swan Lake and the Street Dance Battle

One comes from nineteenth-century Russian imperial theatre; the other formed in 1970s Bronx breaking culture. Their institutions, bodily lineages, and viewing relations differ enormously.

Ballet has an academic vocabulary and norms of line. Breaking likewise has shared terms and community standards for toprock, downrock or footwork, freezes, and power moves; it is inaccurate to say it has no standardized vocabulary. Street dance also contains many traditions, while this essay addresses breaking only.

Both can be analyzed through establishment, confirmation, reopening, and closure, but those are externally proposed coordinates, not proof that the forms perform the same operation.

The Black Swan's thirty-two fouettés and a b-boy's or b-girl's freeze offer an interpretive analogy: sustained motion and sudden stillness may alter temporal expectation. Their technique, meaning, and audience knowledge are not the same.

Isomorphism here is a limited comparison, not "different encoding, identical chisel."

VI. Heteromorphic Equivalence: Noh Theater and Pina Bausch

Japanese Noh and Pina Bausch offer another comparison: can micro-difference within convention and relational change through repetition both reopen viewing? This does not prove the same remainder effect.

Noh is integrated theatre, combining chant, instruments, dance, masks, costume, props, and narrative; it cannot be extracted as "pure dance." Suriashi, fan work, and other patterns are highly formalized but vary by school, role, and performance.

Mask angle can alter light and perceived expression, while gait and pause can reorganize time. Yet "a few degrees up is joy, a few down is sorrow, half a beat faster is tension" is too mechanical; effect depends on mask, role, stage light, and audience training.

Noh's chisel relies on micro-deviation amplified by the extreme precision of the construct.

Bausch can be read along another path: as everyday action continues, attention shifts from the category of action to relation and difference of execution. This essay metaphorically calls that a phase transition without excluding micro-deviation, performance convention, or other mechanisms.

The two chisel methods are nearly opposite. Noh: minimal deviation within an extremely precise construct. Bausch: maximal repetition within an extremely simple construct.

Both may leave an audience feeling that an established way of seeing is insufficient, but the questions they leave are not equivalent. Remainder has to be argued from a performance and audience rather than assumed to have one cognitive function.

Heteromorphic equivalence remains a comparative hypothesis: different forms of chisel may reopen expectation without guaranteeing one kind of inexhaustibility.

5a. Dialogue with a Predecessor: Laban Movement Analysis

Dance theory has a natural interlocutor: Rudolf Laban.

Laban's ideas were later developed with the work of Irmgard Bartenieff and others into Laban/Bartenieff Movement Studies. Four common categories are Body, Effort, Shape, and Space; Effort attends to factors including Weight, Time, Space attention, and Flow. Contemporary LMA should not be attributed to Laban as a single completed act.

The framework offers an observational language across settings, but has its own lineage and training institutions; it is not a culturally neutral "universal language."

LMA does more than list properties. It considers bodily organization, dynamic relation, shape change, and spatial intent. This essay asks how movement sequences form expectations; the questions differ, and the latter cannot claim to complete the former.

Analogy with music theory is suggestive but limited: LMA is a system of observation and practice, not merely static encoding.

Laban/Bartenieff studies therefore supplies descriptive resources without validating Arise-Settle-Unfold-Fix. Nor does this essay add "one dimension beyond" it; it proposes a falsifiable sequential hypothesis at another scale.

VII. Two Boundary Cases: Calisthenics and Improvisation

Essay I compared repetitive popular fragments with experimental sound. Here the question moves to calisthenics and improvised dance without classifying either in advance as a degraded form.

Calisthenics aims at functional training and collective synchronization. Its conventions are stable, yet differences of body, rhythm, shared memory, and setting may still carry meaning. It is not failed dance and should not be diagnosed as "zero chisel."

In this essay's coordinates it often emphasizes establishment and confirmation. That does not mean a model is fully fixed after one pass or remainder is absent, because the evaluative purpose differs.

Improvised dance likewise does not mean no pattern at all. Bodily habits, task rules, space, response to others, and audience familiarity create expectations. Improvisation can construct and chisel.

The cross-channel analogy should therefore not be written as an equation:

Repetitive pop fragments and calisthenics may emphasize confirmation; experimental sound and improvised dance may let references emerge in process. Their purposes, materials, and modes of attention remain different.

Such resemblance supports continued comparison, not evidence for a universal cognitive operation.

VIII. Counter-Example: Technique-Display Dance

Each essay requires a concrete counter-example.

Some competition or television dances arrange difficult movements continuously, producing an impression of opening virtuosity, middle virtuosity, and closing virtuosity. This is not the shared structure of all competition work.

Some viewers may not return, while others rewatch for technique, musicality, or bodily detail.

Why? Because its structure is "Unfold-Unfold-Unfold-Unfold" — breaking your expectation throughout ("they can do that?" "even harder?"), but never establishing a stable construct. You never formed a predictive model, so "breaking" is merely a series of technical surprises, not chisel.

This is not the same problem as improvised dance. Improvisation can establish relations, and virtuosity can create construct through sequence, breath, risk, and music. Criticism must identify where a particular program fails to let relations develop.

Remainder cannot be declared zero. Remembering only "that was amazing" is one possible audience response, not a law of memory.

The Black Swan's thirty-two fouettés are often embedded in Act III's relations of character, music, and spectacle and may therefore function as more than isolated technique. Yet they have always carried virtuoso and star-display functions too; the same movement in a compilation does not automatically become noise.

Alysa Liu, the 2026 Milano Cortina Olympic champion in women's singles, offers a more specific case that should remain open to counter-reading.

Liu became, at thirteen, the youngest U.S. women's national champion at the time and was known for triple Axels and quadruple jumps. It would nevertheless be unfair to reduce her early skating to "technique display" or assert that its programs lacked an arc for every audience.

After the 2022 Beijing Olympics she retired for two years, entered university, lived an ordinary life. When she returned in 2024, something had changed.

Her 2026 Olympic free skate can be read as technique embedded within a broader rhythmic and emotional arc. That is a critical interpretation, not the single cause of victory, and the program did not depend on reproducing the same ultra-difficult jumps associated with her early career.

Her public emphasis on performance rather than competition helps explain the reading, but does not prove that she moved from construct-stacking to a complete cycle.

The verifiable result is narrower: third after the short program, Liu won the free skate and Olympic title with a career-best total of 226.79. Judging followed technical and program-component rules, while competitors' performances also affected the result. Technical score cannot be equated with construct, or program components with remainder.

The case suggests that technique and program structure need not be opposed, and that relational handling changes how viewers understand similar movements. It does not validate the cycle.

IX. The Hypothesis of Channel Transfer

Reviewing what this essay has established.

We have tested the cycle in classical ballet, Pina Bausch's Tanztheater, breaking battles, and the integrated theatre of Noh, which includes dance. They arise from different histories and institutions that cannot be reduced to West versus East or formalization versus anti-formalization.

These forms differ greatly, and the four coordinates are clearer in some than others. The fact that four positions can be labeled does not prove that they run completely.

Music plays different roles in ballet, breaking, Bausch, and Noh. Noh is integrated theatre combining chant, instruments, and dance; it cannot serve as evidence for "a body with almost no music."

A more cautious conclusion is that bodily movement can establish, alter, and reclose expectation even when music does not dominate.

The essay therefore supports a falsifiable hypothesis: the chisel-construct cycle may not depend on the auditory channel. It is not yet a basic law of human cognition, and visual-kinesthetic expectation is shaped by culture, training, bodies, and setting.

Essay I proposed an auditory model, Essay II proposed cross-channel relations, and Essay III moved the question to bodies. None has completed a cross-modal proof.

In the next essay, we enter the final testing ground: theater and film. When narrative becomes the primary channel and all media run simultaneously, how does the chisel-construct cycle operate in multi-level parallel? Why can some films be watched ten times and still yield something, while others suffice at one viewing?

And we will formally define a concept that has appeared throughout the first three essays but has not yet been given a name: pseudo-chisel.
