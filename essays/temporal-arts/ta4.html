Chisel and Construct · The Universal Structure of Temporal Arts · Essay IV

话剧与电影

Theatre and Film

「叙事作为时间性凿构的多层形态」

"Narrative as a Multi-Level Form of Temporal Chisel-Construct"

秦汉 Han Qin | 2026

前三篇分别在听觉（音乐）、跨通道（戏曲/歌剧）、动觉（舞蹈）中检验了"生定展固"。每一篇都增加了一个分析层次：第一篇提出基本循环，第二篇引入跨通道凿，第三篇把问题扩展到身体和视觉——这使我们可以继续追问，凿构循环是否依赖某一种感官通道。

现在进入最后一个检验场：话剧和电影。

话剧和电影的特殊性在于两件事。第一，叙事可以成为主通道——"故事"与声音、身体、空间共同承载凿构循环。第二，电影能同时组织叙事、对白、视觉构图、剪辑节奏、音乐和音效；这种多线并行并不使电影高于其他艺术，却让跨层级关系格外清楚。

这意味着电影里可以同时运行多条凿构循环，每条在不同层级上相对独立，又互相影响。这是前三篇尚未集中处理的复杂度。

同时，通道越多，一个新的风险也越大：伪凿。

一、叙事如何运行凿构循环

先在分析上把叙事单独拎出来。话剧当然不只有对白：身体、布景、灯光、声音和观众共处的现场都在工作；但与电影相比，它通常不依靠镜头和剪辑来安排观看，因此更容易看清叙事关系本身怎样变化。

叙事的凿构循环可以与音乐作同构比较，只是材料和观众所学会的规则不同：

生：故事的开端建立了一个"世界"和一组人物关系。观众开始形成期待——这些人物会怎样互动？冲突会往哪个方向走？一个叙事预测模型逐渐出现。

定：情节发展确认了你的模型。人物的行为符合你对他们性格的预期，冲突按照你预想的逻辑推进。你觉得"我理解这个故事在做什么了"。

展：某个瞬间，你的叙事预测模型被打破了。一个你没预期到的转折，一个人物做了一件你以为他不会做的事，一个你以为已经解决的冲突以新的形态重新出现。但跟音乐的"展"一样——这不是随机的，它在一个你事后能理解但事前不能预测的逻辑里。

固：故事的结局。但经历了"展"之后，同样的人物在你心理上的重量不同了。结尾不是回到开头，是带着凿的痕迹闭合。

四幕话剧，凿构循环极其完整。

生：周朴园的大宅，表面的秩序和体面建立了一个"世界"。你形成了一个预测模型——这是一个关于家庭秘密和阶级冲突的故事。

定：前两幕不断确认这个模型。周朴园的威严，繁漪的压抑，四凤的天真。你越来越确定你理解了这些人物。

展：身份关系的暴露。你以为你理解的人物关系全部被重新定义——但曹禺的厉害在于，暴露不是一次性的。每一层暴露都打破你刚刚重建的模型，余项层层叠加。你在第三幕开始觉得"我不知道这个故事会怎样结束"——但你又觉得它必须按照某种你说不出的逻辑结束。

固：最后的毁灭。周家秩序崩塌，多个人物走向灾难；你感到的不是简单的"故事结束了"，而是"这个结束比开始重得多"。闭合携带了前面所有凿的痕迹。

跟《雷雨》做对照。完全不同的文化和戏剧传统，但凿构循环同构。

生：威利·洛曼的日常生活建立了一个关于"美国梦"的预测模型。

定：他的自我欺骗和家庭关系不断确认这个模型——你越来越清楚这个人在做什么。

展：米勒的凿不是靠情节反转，而是靠时间结构。过去和现在的场景交替出现，你对"现实"的预测模型被时间的错乱打破了。你不确定你看到的是记忆还是幻觉还是正在发生的事。

固：威利的死。但这个闭合的重量来自前面所有时间错乱积累的余项——你在结尾感受到的不只是"一个人死了"，而是"一整个关于成功和失败的叙事模型崩塌了"。

两部话剧的编码系统完全不同（中国家庭伦理悲剧 vs 美国社会现实主义），但凿构循环的操作同构。

二、电影：多层级凿构

话剧把叙事、表演和现场空间结合起来；电影则用镜头、剪辑和录制声音重新分配这些关系。

一部电影同时运行着：叙事线（情节的凿构循环）、视觉构图（画面的凿构循环）、剪辑节奏（时间的凿构循环）、音乐/音效（听觉的凿构循环）、表演（身体-情绪的凿构循环）。

每一条线都可以独立运行自己的"生定展固"。但更重要的是，它们可以跨层级凿——一条线在"定"的同时另一条线在"展"，就像第二篇里京剧的唱腔和身段。只不过电影有更多的通道可以做这个操作。

但本文要强调的不只是"通道更多"。通道数量本身不能在电影与歌剧之间建立高下，也不足以说明两种媒介怎样组织经验。

这一篇把前三篇没有单独作为主要分析对象的叙事通道推到前景。叙事期待不只涉及感官层面的"下一个音是什么"或"下一个动作是什么"，还会涉及概念/逻辑层面的"这个人物会做什么""这个故事会怎样结束""这个世界的规则是什么"。戏曲和歌剧当然也有叙事；这里改变的是分析重心，不是宣布话剧和电影独占一种通道。

当概念模型与感官模型发生偏离时——叙事似乎告诉你一件事，画面和声音却提出另一种关系——电影中的多层级凿就格外可见。它并非电影独有：戏剧、歌剧和数字媒介也能让概念与感官错位；电影只是用取景、剪辑和声画关系把这种错位变成了自身的重要能力。

这部电影的叙事线极度简约——人类发现黑石碑，去木星调查，遭遇AI失控，主角穿越某种超越性体验。如果只看情节，预测模型很快就稳定了。

但库布里克在视觉和音乐层级上做了极端的凿。

"星门"序列：叙事因果变得稀薄，视觉通道接管注意——色彩、光线和抽象图形以难以预判的节奏涌来。整部电影把施特劳斯圆舞曲的秩序感与利盖蒂作品的陌生音响并置；"星门"段落本身主要由利盖蒂的音响支撑。这样的选择不断改写观众对"太空电影应该怎样听起来"的期待。

叙事近乎悬置，视觉在"展"，音乐又以另一种方式组织时间。把三条线看成处于不同相位，是本文的一种分析读法：它有助于解释为什么电影结束后，因果概要仍不能吸收全部感受。五十多年持续出现的新解释说明这个余项仍可被重读，却不意味着每位观众都会得到同样经验。

跟库布里克完全相反的策略。

小津的摄影机几乎不移动：机位低，构图克制。剪辑节奏舒缓，场景之间常以建筑、街道、火车或空房间等过渡镜头相接，音乐也被选择性地使用。叙事极度日常——一对老夫妇去东京看望子女，子女们太忙没时间陪他们，他们回家后，妻子去世了。

从表面看，所有通道都在"定"。没有视觉奇观，没有情节反转，没有音乐高潮。

对许多观众而言，这部电影在复看时仍会显出新的关系。

为什么？一种解释是，小津的凿不只发生在单一通道内，而发生在形式与观众期待之间。你也许期待家庭疏离走向情感爆发——电影却保持克制；你期待死亡成为戏剧高潮——死亡及其前后更多通过省略、安静的场景和人物反应获得重量；你期待结尾完成和解——电影留下的却是有限理解与仍未消失的距离。

每一次你的叙事期待被"不发生"打破，就是一次凿。小津的chisel不是"做了什么"，而是"没做什么"。他在你的预测模型中挖了一个洞——那个洞就是余项。你没办法用"看懂了"来填满它，因为那个洞的形状就是"这里本该有东西但没有"。

本文把这种通过缺席制造余项的手法，与皮娜·鲍什通过重复改变观看重心的手法作异构比较：一个减少预期中的事件，一个让动作持续到关系改变。它们可能都把已经稳定的观看方式重新打开；"相变"在这里是审美分析的比喻，不是已经测得的生理临界点。

三、伪凿：制造意外但不改变模型

现在可以正式定义一个在前三篇中已经多次暗示的概念了。

在本文设想的典型情形中，它让你惊一下，却未必让"闭合后的重量"发生变化。预测模型被短暂扰动，随后大体恢复，复看时由意外本身提供的余项也可能迅速减少。

更精确地说，"伪凿"是本文提出的批评性区分，不是神经科学量表。真凿要求观众重组人物、因果或形式关系，新的理解会携带断裂痕迹；伪凿则可能只增加一个可迅速归档的意外，而不迫使更广泛的关系改变。第二遍体验时惊奇通常会减弱，但注意也可能转向表演、节奏或制作细节，不能把它写成信息熵严格归零。

伪凿在电影中尤其容易分析，因为电影有许多通道可以制造"意外"：一个突然的镜头切换，一个音效的jump scare，一个情节的twist，一个视觉特效的奇观。这些都能让你"惊一下"。但惊不等于凿。

真凿打破你的模型之后，你需要重建模型——而重建后的模型比之前更厚，因为它包含了被打破的痕迹。这就是"固"。

伪凿打断注意之后，原有模型大体仍可维持；在本文的词汇里，"固"没有承担足够的重组工作。这不等于作品毫无价值——惊吓、速度、奇观和共同娱乐都可以是正当目的——只说明惊奇与模型重建不是一回事。

因此，有些大制作电影第一次观看依靠未知的twist或奇观，复看时惊奇下降，而twist之外的人物、节奏和画面关系未必要求重新理解。这里说的是一种可能的批评判断，不是预算规模决定艺术深度，也不是所有观众的复看经验都会相同。

四、同构对照：《雷雨》与《推销员之死》

第一节已经展示了这两部话剧的凿构循环。现在正式做同构对照。

两部作品的编码系统完全不同。《雷雨》用中国家庭伦理的框架，人物关系的复杂性靠血缘和阶级的交织来构建，"展"主要靠身份暴露——你以为你知道这些人是谁，你错了。《推销员之死》用美国梦的框架，"展"主要靠时间结构的错乱——你以为你知道什么是现实，你错了。

一个凿的是"人是谁"，另一个凿的是"什么是真"。凿的对象不同，但凿的操作同构：打破你的叙事预测模型，产生不可一次性消化的余项，然后在闭合时让余项的重量沉入结局。

两部话剧都进入了经典剧目。《雷雨》1934年发表后被不同年代反复复排，《推销员之死》自1949年首演后长期处于美国话剧经典之列。本文的解释是：新演员、社会语境和观众经验会把身份、阶级、记忆与成功叙事的不同关系推到前景；这种可重读性可以用"余项"描述，但不是它们经典地位的唯一原因。

五、异构同效：《2001太空漫游》与《东京物语》

如果说《雷雨》和《推销员之死》可以被读成"不同编码做相近操作"，那么库布里克和小津提供了另一组可检验的比较：相反的形式策略，是否可能产生相近的余项效果？

库布里克靠的是极端的感官过载——"星门"序列用视觉和声音的洪流冲垮你的预测模型。小津靠的是极端的感官克制——用"不发生"在你的预测模型中挖洞。

一个以感官过剩制造余项，一个以克制和省略制造余项。方法相反；对愿意复看的观众，两者都可能让既有解释无法一次封闭作品。但它们留下的情感、伦理与历史问题并不相同。

这与第三篇的"能剧 vs 皮娜·鲍什"形成有限的结构呼应——能剧中的细微偏离、鲍什的重复、库布里克的感官洪流、小津的克制，分别在不同传统和媒介中工作。比较只在"怎样重开期待"这一层成立，不能抹平作品的历史差异。

因此，异构同效不是通用性已经得到证明，而是一项比较假说：不同方法或许能迫使观众重组期待；是否产生相近余项，需要具体版本、文化经验和实际观看来检验。

5a、与亚里士多德的对话

叙事的凿构循环有一个必须对话的前辈：亚里士多德的《诗学》。

两千三百年前，亚里士多德分析希腊悲剧，讨论了突转（peripeteia）——行动局势的逆转；发现（anagnorisis）——从无知到知晓的转变；以及净化（katharsis）。净化究竟指情感疏解、澄清还是别的过程，至今仍有争论，不能把一种译法当成定论。

这些概念可以与"生定展固"对话：突转和发现常常重组观众对行动的理解，因此可与"展"比较；悲剧结束后的效应也可与"固"比较。但这种对应是本文的分析映射，不是亚里士多德预先提出了同一模型。

《诗学》确实把情节置于核心，却不只讨论叙事。亚里士多德也把人物、思想、语言、歌唱/音乐和场面列为悲剧要素，并讨论歌队；只是他认为悲剧的力量首先来自行动的组织，而不是单靠舞台奇观。

因此，本文与《诗学》的差别不应写成"单通道对多通道"。更准确地说，本文把注意力放在各要素的相位差：当情节趋于稳定，画面、声音或表演是否仍在重组观看？《2001太空漫游》的"星门"序列正适合提出这个问题，但它不是对亚里士多德的简单反证。

同样，亚里士多德没有说净化只能发生一次，也没有推论第二遍观看应当失效。"余项"是本文另加的概念，用来询问一次观看尚未吸收什么、复看为何会改变注意；它补充的是问题，而不是宣告《诗学》在重复经验上犯了错误。

与第一篇的Hanslick/Meyer、第二篇的Wagner、第三篇的Laban一样，这里做的是受限的理论对话：借旧概念照亮新问题，同时保留概念之间无法互译的部分。

六、反例：好看但不经看

同一位导演的两部作品可以检验这套区分，但以下首先是本文的批评性阅读，不是关于所有观众复看行为的统计结论。

《星际穿越》可以这样读：父女关系建立情感construct（生定），黑洞和时间膨胀改变"救援"的意义（展），最后的"书架场景"把因果和情感重新闭合（固）。汉斯·季默的管风琴配乐又常把情感强度推到叙事解释之前，形成可分析的跨通道相位差。

对认同这条情感线的观众，复看时父女关系与时间/空间设定可能把不同细节推到前景；对另一些观众，这种闭合也可能显得过度说明。余项不是自动发生的。

《信条》则把大量注意力交给时间逆转机制。第一次观看时，理解"发生了什么"可能占据主要精力；复看时，机制熟悉之后，有人会转向动作编排、友谊线或政治含义，也有人仍觉得人物动机没有获得同等厚度。

用本文的语言说，两片可被假设为两种分布：《信条》的主要"展"更集中于概念和动作机制，《星际穿越》则让概念与情感在不同层级运行。这个判断必须允许反例；若观众在《信条》中不断发现新的关系，或觉得《星际穿越》的情感线已完全封闭，分析就应当修正。

因此，伪凿与真凿不是给作品盖章的客观等级，而是要求批评者说明：哪一项旧关系被迫改变？改变是否只对某一类观众、某一遍观看或某一版本成立？

同样，在本文框架里，第一部《钢铁侠》的人物弧线和《美国队长2》的政治惊悚结构可以读出较完整的带痕闭合；这并不证明它们天然比别的流行娱乐高级，只说明人物变化与情节反转之间存在可继续追踪的关系。

随着系列扩展，一些后期漫威电影更依赖可重复的模板：建立英雄状态（生），确认能力和使命（定），反派制造危机（展？），英雄完成任务（固）。这是对部分作品的概括，不适用于每一部影片。

四步看似齐全，批评的问题却是：危机之后，人物、世界规则或观众的理解是否真的改变？

在符合这一诊断的影片里，"展"的位置放入了更多伪凿。大反派的出现确实制造"意外"，但未必改变观众对整个叙事框架的模型——英雄仍大概率获胜，牺牲者可能在后续作品回归，宇宙规则也常被修复到足以延续系列的状态。预测模型被短暂扰动，随后大体恢复。

视觉奇观也要具体判断。有些高潮只增加刺激，有些动作场面会重新组织空间、伦理选择或人物关系；不能因为爆炸密集就预先判定为"纯炫技"。"展-展-展-展"描述的是一种可能失去闭合的形式经验。

因此，这里不能把"后期伪凿增加—观众复看下降"写成未经数据支持的退化曲线，也不能断言制作水准没有变化。可以保留的批评是：工业化系列常要同时维持角色、宇宙规则和下一部入口，这种制度压力可能让局部危机在结尾恢复到可继续运营的状态。它是否牺牲凿构循环，要逐片论证。

七、口述传统：叙事表演的一条谱系

在结束之前，补一个被前面几篇跳过的形态：口述传统。

荷马史诗的吟诵、中国评书、日本落语、西非griot的表演都让叙事成为主线，但它们属于不同的历史、仪式和社会谱系。不能把它们合并成话剧与电影的单一祖先；戏剧、电影还与宗教仪式、宫廷和城市表演、文字、摄影及录音技术等多条谱系相交。

这些传统也不只有"声音+叙事"。语气、节奏、面部与身体动作、扇子或手巾等物件、现场观众和表演空间都可能参与；它们没有电影剪辑，却有自己的视觉和关系通道。

中国评书的"扣子"可以类比为生与定，某些包袱或揭示可以类比为展，收束可以类比为固。但术语与程式因传统和表演者而异，这是一种分析映射，不是评书的普遍技术定律。

日本落语常由一名表演者坐姿演出，以声音、视线、身体方向以及扇子、手巾等简约物件区分人物。它说明的是：舞台资源很少时，局部期待仍可被建立和重开；这并不把落语还原成凿构循环。

这与第三篇"舞蹈在接近沉默中依然成立"形成呼应：减少某些媒介手段，不等于时间关系消失。它支持跨通道比较的可能，却还不足以证明循环在所有文化中保持不变。

八、系列结论：一切时间性艺术的通用结构

四篇写完了。回到开头的问题。

为什么有些旋律听一百遍还有东西？为什么有些戏看十遍还能看到新的东西？为什么有些舞蹈让你觉得"结束之后比开始更重"？为什么有些电影反复看不厌？

本系列给出的，是同一组追问方式：

这个分析框架尝试跨越：

感官通道——听觉、身体、视觉与叙事；文化传统——贝多芬与K-pop、京剧与breaking各有不同规则；历史时期——巴赫、Kendrick Lamar、能乐与库布里克不能被抹平成同一种艺术；媒介复杂度——纯器乐与电影都可以提出期待如何形成和改变的问题。

"建模→确认→模型被打破→带痕重建"是SAE在本系列中提出的美学假说，不是已经证实的人类认知基本定律。文化习得、专业经验、记忆、观看场所、媒介版本和身体状态都会改变模型在哪里形成、是否被打破。

乐理、舞蹈词汇和叙事结构并不是同一操作的简单外壳。它们各自有不可互译的历史和技术。本系列只假设：在这些差异之中，我们仍可比较期待怎样稳定、重开和留下余项。

"生定展固"取自Self-as-an-End框架的生命周期表。在这里，它是阅读时间经验的一组坐标：选择、构建、被凿开、重新闭合。作品是否真的以这种方式作用，必须回到具体观众和具体表演检验。

本系列不讲乐理，讲乐理的道理。

不讲舞蹈技法，讲技法的道理。

不讲叙事理论，讲叙事理论的道理。

共享的不是唯一道理，而是一个问题：形式稳定之后，什么还能迫使我们重新观看？

最后提出一个从框架延伸出的哲学判断：时间性艺术没有预先可见的终点。

这不是由凿构循环"保证"的经验事实。更谨慎的说法是：任何形式被共同体熟悉后，都可能成为新的construct；它可以被继承、变奏、拒绝，也可能在别处继续作为有效传统。

形式稳定后，新的作品可能重开它；重开暴露当前读法未处理的余项；余项也可能推动另一种construct形成。但这个过程不是自动进步，更不要求每一代都比上一代更复杂。

勋伯格挑战了调性中心，后来发展十二音技法，同时仍使用过继承来的形式。约翰·凯奇质疑的是更广泛的作曲、声音、沉默与演出惯例，不是简单地"凿十二音"。梅兰芳在高度编码的戏曲传统中重塑表演可能；皮娜·鲍什则从德国表现主义舞蹈与Tanztheater谱系出发，重组舞蹈和剧场的关系，而不只是"凿古典芭蕾"。

如果"余项"这个概念有用，它意味着任何一次解释都应承认自己的边界：当前闭合没有吸收的东西，可能成为下一次观看或创作的起点。

这不是已经证明的艺术史定律，而是本系列愿意承担反例的一项结构假说。

The first three essays tested Arise-Settle-Unfold-Fix in audition (music), cross-channel (opera / Chinese opera), and kinesthetic (dance) domains. Each essay added an analytical layer: Essay I proposed the basic cycle, Essay II introduced cross-channel chisel, and Essay III extended the question to body and vision — allowing us to ask whether the cycle depends on any one sensory channel.

Now we enter the final testing ground: theatre and film.

Theatre and film are distinctive in two respects. First, narrative can become the primary channel: story works together with sound, body, and space to carry the cycle. Second, film can coordinate narrative, dialogue, visual composition, editing rhythm, music, and sound design at once. That does not place film above other arts, but it makes cross-level relations unusually visible.

This means film can run multiple chisel-construct cycles simultaneously, each at a different level, operating with relative independence yet mutually influencing one another. This is a degree of complexity the first three essays did not treat as a central problem.

At the same time, the more channels available, the greater a new risk becomes: pseudo-chisel.

I. How Narrative Runs the Chisel-Construct Cycle

First isolate narrative analytically. Theatre is never only dialogue: bodies, sets, light, sound, and the shared time of performers and spectators all work. Yet unlike film, it usually does not organize attention through camera position and editing, so changes in narrative relations can be easier to see.

The narrative cycle can be compared isomorphically with the musical cycle, though its materials and learned conventions differ:

Arise: The story's opening establishes a "world" and a set of character relationships. Viewers begin forming expectations — how will these characters interact? Which direction will the conflict take? A narrative predictive model gradually appears.

Settle: Plot development confirms your model. Characters behave consistently with your expectations of their personalities; conflict advances according to the logic you anticipated. You feel: "I understand what this story is doing."

Unfold: At some moment, your narrative predictive model is broken. An unanticipated turn; a character does something you thought they would not do; a conflict you thought resolved reappears in a new form. But as with musical Unfold — this is not random; it follows a logic comprehensible in retrospect but not predictable in advance.

Fix: The story's ending. But having passed through Unfold, the same characters now carry different weight in your psychology. The ending is not a return to the beginning; it is closure carrying the trace of chisel.

A four-act spoken drama with an extremely complete chisel-construct cycle.

Arise: The Zhou mansion; its surface order and respectability establish a "world." You form a predictive model — this is a story about family secrets and class conflict.

Settle: The first two acts continuously confirm this model. Zhou Puyuan's authority, Fanyi's repression, Sifeng's innocence. You grow increasingly certain you understand these characters.

Unfold: The revelation of identity relationships. The character relationships you thought you understood are entirely redefined — but Cao Yu's mastery lies in revelation not being a single event. Each layer of revelation breaks the model you have just rebuilt; remainder accumulates layer upon layer. By Act III you feel: "I do not know how this story will end" — yet you also feel it must end according to some logic you cannot articulate.

Fix: The final destruction. The Zhou household's order collapses and several characters meet catastrophe; what you feel is not simply "the story is over" but "this ending weighs far more than the beginning." Closure carries the trace of every preceding chisel.

In counterpoint with Thunderstorm. Entirely different cultural and theatrical tradition, but isomorphic chisel-construct cycle.

Arise: Willy Loman's daily life establishes a predictive model of the "American Dream."

Settle: His self-deception and family relationships continuously confirm this model — you grow increasingly clear about what this man is doing.

Unfold: Miller's chisel does not rely on plot twist but on temporal structure. Past and present scenes alternate; your predictive model of "reality" is broken by the disorientation of time. You are unsure whether what you see is memory, hallucination, or present event.

Fix: Willy's death. But the weight of this closure comes from all the temporal disorientation that preceded it — what you feel at the end is not merely "a man died" but "an entire narrative model of success and failure has collapsed."

The two plays' encoding systems are entirely different (Chinese family-ethics tragedy vs. American social realism), but their chisel-construct operations are isomorphic.

II. Film: Multi-Level Chisel-Construct

Theatre combines narrative, performance, and live space; film redistributes those relations through framing, editing, and recorded sound.

A film simultaneously runs: narrative line (the chisel-construct cycle of plot), visual composition (the cycle of the image), editing rhythm (the cycle of time), music/sound design (the auditory cycle), performance (the body-emotion cycle).

Each line can independently run its own Arise-Settle-Unfold-Fix. But more importantly, they can chisel across levels — one line in Settle while another is in Unfold, just as in Essay II where Peking opera's singing and body work ran at different phases. Film simply has more channels available for this operation.

But this essay needs to emphasize more than "more channels." Channel count establishes neither a hierarchy between film and opera nor an adequate account of how the two media organize experience.

This essay brings to the foreground a channel that the first three did not isolate as their main object: narrative. Narrative expectation concerns not only sensory questions such as "what is the next sound?" or "what is the next movement?" but conceptual and logical questions such as "what will this character do?", "how will this story end?", and "what are the rules of this world?" Chinese opera and opera of course also tell stories; the change here is analytical focus, not a claim that theatre and film alone possess this channel.

When a conceptual model diverges from a sensory one — narrative seems to say one thing while image and sound propose another relation — multi-level chisel becomes especially visible in film. It is not unique to film: theatre, opera, and digital media can also set concepts against sensations. Framing, editing, and audiovisual relations simply make this one of cinema's central capacities.

This film's narrative line is extremely spare — humanity discovers the monolith, goes to Jupiter to investigate, encounters AI failure, the protagonist undergoes some transcendent experience. Looking only at plot, the predictive model stabilizes quickly.

But Kubrick performs extreme chiseling at the visual and musical levels.

In the "Star Gate" sequence, causal narration thins while color, light, and abstract pattern take over attention at a difficult-to-predict rhythm. Across the film, the ordered familiarity of Strauss waltzes is juxtaposed with Ligeti's unfamiliar sonorities; the Star Gate itself is supported chiefly by Ligeti. These choices repeatedly revise expectations of what a space film should sound like.

Narrative is nearly suspended, vision is in Unfold, and music organizes time in another way. Treating these lines as differently phased is one reading offered here: it helps explain why a causal synopsis does not absorb the whole experience. More than fifty years of new interpretations suggest that the remainder can still be reread, not that every viewer must have the same response.

The precise opposite strategy from Kubrick.

Ozu's camera almost never moves: its position is low and its composition restrained. Editing is unhurried; buildings, streets, trains, and empty rooms often connect scenes, while music is used selectively. The narrative is extremely mundane — an elderly couple visits their children in Tokyo; the children are too busy to spend time with them; the couple returns home, and the wife dies.

On the surface, all channels are in Settle. No visual spectacle, no plot twist, no musical climax.

For many viewers, the film continues to reveal new relations on repeated viewing.

Why? One explanation is that Ozu's chisel occurs not only within a channel but between form and expectation. You may expect family estrangement to culminate in an emotional eruption, yet the film remains restrained; you may expect death to become a dramatic climax, yet death and its aftermath gain weight through ellipsis, quiet scenes, and character reactions; you may expect reconciliation, yet the film leaves limited understanding alongside undiminished distance.

Each time your narrative expectation is broken by "non-occurrence," that is a chisel. Ozu's chisel is not "what he did" but "what he did not do." He carved a hole in your predictive model — and that hole is remainder. You cannot fill it with "having understood," because the hole's shape is precisely "something should have been here but was not."

This essay places Ozu's use of absence in heteromorphic comparison with Pina Bausch's use of repetition: one subtracts expected events, the other prolongs action until its relations change. Both may reopen an established way of seeing. "Phase transition" is an aesthetic metaphor here, not a measured physiological threshold.

III. Pseudo-Chisel: Producing Surprise Without Changing the Model

A concept repeatedly intimated in the first three essays can now be formally defined.

In the typical case proposed here, it startles you but may not alter the weight of closure. A predictive model is briefly perturbed and then largely restores itself; on repetition, the remainder supplied by surprise alone may diminish rapidly.

More precisely, pseudo-chisel is a critical distinction proposed by this essay, not a neuroscientific scale. Genuine chisel asks viewers to reorganize relations among character, cause, or form; the new understanding carries the trace of rupture. Pseudo-chisel may add a quickly filed surprise without forcing broader relations to change. Surprise usually weakens on a second encounter, but attention may move to performance, rhythm, or craft; information entropy cannot be said to become strictly zero.

Pseudo-chisel is especially easy to analyze in film because film has so many channels available for manufacturing "surprise": a sudden cut, a jump scare in the sound design, a plot twist, a visual-effects spectacle. Each can startle you. But startling is not chiseling.

Genuine chisel, after breaking your model, requires you to rebuild — and the rebuilt model is thicker than its predecessor because it incorporates the trace of breaking. This is Fix.

After pseudo-chisel interrupts attention, the prior model remains largely usable; in this essay's vocabulary, Fix has done too little reorganizing. That does not make the work worthless — fright, speed, spectacle, and shared entertainment can all be legitimate aims — but surprise and model revision are not the same thing.

Some large-budget films therefore rely on an unknown twist or spectacle on first viewing; once surprise recedes, the relations among character, rhythm, and image may not demand reinterpretation. This is a possible critical judgment, not a claim that budget determines depth or that every viewer's second encounter will be the same.

IV. Isomorphic Comparison: Thunderstorm and Death of a Salesman

Section I already presented the chisel-construct cycles of both plays. Now the formal isomorphic comparison.

The two works' encoding systems are entirely different. Thunderstorm uses the Chinese family-ethics framework; the complexity of character relationships is built through the interweaving of blood kinship and class. Its Unfold is primarily through identity revelation — you thought you knew who these people were; you were wrong. Death of a Salesman uses the American Dream framework; its Unfold is primarily through temporal-structural disorientation — you thought you knew what was real; you were wrong.

One chisels "who people are"; the other chisels "what is real." The object of chiseling differs, but the operation is isomorphic: break the narrative predictive model, produce remainder that cannot be absorbed in a single pass, then in closure let the weight of remainder sink into the ending.

Both plays entered the repertory. Thunderstorm has been repeatedly revived since its publication in 1934, while Death of a Salesman has remained central to the American theatrical canon since its 1949 premiere. This essay proposes that new actors, social contexts, and audience experience foreground different relations among identity, class, memory, and success. "Remainder" can describe that rereadability, but it is not the sole reason for canonical status.

V. Heteromorphic Equivalence: 2001: A Space Odyssey and Tokyo Story

If Thunderstorm and Death of a Salesman can be read as different encodings performing a comparable operation, Kubrick and Ozu offer another test: can opposing formal strategies produce comparable remainder effects?

Kubrick relies on extreme sensory overload — the "Star Gate" sequence uses a flood of vision and sound to overwhelm your predictive model. Ozu relies on extreme sensory restraint — using "non-occurrence" to carve holes in your predictive model.

One uses sensory excess, the other restraint and omission. The methods oppose each other; for viewers who return, both may prevent one interpretation from closing the work. Yet the emotional, ethical, and historical questions they leave are not identical.

This forms a limited structural echo with Essay III's Noh/Bausch comparison: minute deviation in Noh, repetition in Bausch, sensory flood in Kubrick, and restraint in Ozu work within distinct traditions and media. Comparison holds only at the level of how expectation is reopened; it cannot erase historical difference.

Heteromorphic equivalence is therefore not proof of universality but a comparative hypothesis: different methods may force viewers to reorganize expectation. Whether they produce comparable remainder must be tested against particular versions, cultural experience, and actual viewing.

5a. Dialogue with Aristotle

The narrative chisel-construct cycle has one predecessor with whom dialogue is necessary: Aristotle's Poetics.

Twenty-three hundred years ago, Aristotle analyzed Greek tragedy and discussed peripeteia — a reversal in the action; anagnorisis — a change from ignorance to knowledge; and katharsis. Whether katharsis means emotional purgation, clarification, or another process remains disputed, so no single translation should be treated as settled.

These concepts can enter into dialogue with Arise-Settle-Unfold-Fix. Peripeteia and anagnorisis often reorganize an audience's understanding of the action and can therefore be compared with Unfold; the effect of tragic closure can be compared with Fix. But this is the present essay's analytical mapping, not evidence that Aristotle proposed the same model in advance.

Poetics does place plot at the center, but it does not discuss narrative alone. Aristotle also names character, thought, diction, song or music, and spectacle as elements of tragedy, and he discusses the chorus. His priority is that tragedy's power arises first from the organization of action, rather than from stage spectacle by itself.

The difference between this essay and Poetics should therefore not be described as "single-channel versus multi-channel." More precisely, this essay attends to phase relations among elements: when plot stabilizes, do image, sound, or performance continue to reorganize the viewing? The Star Gate sequence of 2001: A Space Odyssey is well suited to raising that question, but it is not a simple refutation of Aristotle.

Nor did Aristotle say that katharsis can occur only once, or infer that a second viewing should lose all effect. Remainder is an additional concept proposed here to ask what one encounter has not absorbed and why attention may change on repetition. It adds a question rather than declaring Poetics mistaken about repeated experience.

As with Hanslick and Meyer in Essay I, Wagner in Essay II, and Laban in Essay III, this is a limited theoretical dialogue: older concepts illuminate a new question while the parts that cannot be translated into one another remain visible.

VI. Counter-Example: Exciting to Watch, Not Worth Re-Watching

Two films by the same director can test the distinction, but what follows is first of all this essay's critical reading, not a statistical conclusion about every viewer's rewatching behavior.

Interstellar can be read this way: the father-daughter relationship builds an emotional construct (Arise-Settle); the black hole and time dilation alter the meaning of "rescue" (Unfold); and the final bookshelf scene closes causality and emotion again (Fix). Hans Zimmer's organ score often pushes emotional intensity ahead of narrative explanation, creating an analyzable phase difference across channels.

For viewers who accept that emotional line, repetition may foreground different details in the father-daughter relationship and the film's treatment of time and space. For others, the same closure may feel over-explained. Remainder does not occur automatically.

Tenet, by contrast, gives much of the viewer's attention to the mechanism of temporal inversion. On a first encounter, understanding "what is happening" may consume most of one's effort; once the mechanism is familiar, some viewers may turn to choreography, friendship, or politics, while others may still find that character motivation has not acquired comparable weight.

In this essay's terms, the two films can be hypothesized as different distributions: Tenet concentrates its main Unfold in concept and action mechanism, while Interstellar lets concept and emotion operate on different levels. The judgment must allow counterexamples. If a viewer keeps finding new relations in Tenet, or feels that Interstellar's emotional line is already fully closed, the analysis should change.

Pseudo-chisel and genuine chisel are therefore not objective grades stamped onto works. They require the critic to say which prior relation was forced to change, and whether that change holds only for a particular audience, viewing, or version.

Within this framework, the character arc in the first Iron Man and the political-thriller structure of Captain America: The Winter Soldier can be read as relatively complete traced closures. That does not prove them inherently superior to other popular entertainment; it means that character change and plot reversal sustain relations worth following.

As the franchise expanded, some later Marvel films relied more heavily on a repeatable template: establish the hero's state (Arise), confirm abilities and mission (Settle), let a villain create a crisis (Unfold?), and complete the mission (Fix). This is a generalization about some works, not every film in the franchise.

The four positions appear to be filled. The critical question is whether the crisis actually changes the character, the world's rules, or the audience's understanding.

In films that fit this diagnosis, more of the Unfold position is occupied by pseudo-chisel. A villain's appearance does create "surprise," but may not change the audience's model of the narrative framework: the hero remains likely to win, sacrificed characters may return in later works, and the universe's rules are often repaired enough for the franchise to continue. The predictive model is briefly perturbed, then largely restores itself.

Visual spectacle also has to be judged case by case. Some climaxes merely add stimulus; other action scenes reorganize space, ethical choice, or character relations. Dense explosions alone do not establish "pure virtuosity." "Unfold-Unfold-Unfold-Unfold" describes one possible experience of form that has lost closure.

We should not turn "more pseudo-chisel in later installments, less audience rewatching" into an unsupported degradation curve, or assert that production quality did not change. A more defensible criticism is that industrial franchises must preserve characters, world rules, and an entrance to the next installment at once. That institutional pressure may return local crises to an operable status at the end. Whether it sacrifices the chisel-construct cycle has to be argued film by film.

VII. The Oral Tradition: One Lineage of Narrative Performance

Before concluding, a supplementary form skipped in previous essays: the oral tradition.

The recitation of Homeric epic, Chinese pingshu storytelling, Japanese rakugo, and West African griot performance all place narrative in the foreground, but they belong to distinct historical, ritual, and social genealogies. They should not be collapsed into a single ancestor of theatre and film, which also intersect with ritual, court and urban performance, writing, photography, and recording technologies.

Nor do these traditions have only "voice plus narrative." Tone, rhythm, facial and bodily gesture, objects such as a fan or cloth, live audiences, and performance spaces may all participate. They lack film editing but possess visual and relational channels of their own.

A Chinese pingshu storyteller's "hook" (扣子, a suspense setup) can be compared with Arise and Settle; some punch lines or revelations can be compared with Unfold; a gathering-in can be compared with Fix. Terms and procedures vary by tradition and performer, so this is an analytical mapping rather than a universal technical law of pingshu.

Japanese rakugo is often performed by one seated storyteller who distinguishes characters through voice, gaze, bodily orientation, and minimal objects such as a fan and cloth. It suggests that local expectations can still be established and reopened with sparse theatrical resources; it does not reduce rakugo to the chisel-construct cycle.

This echoes Essay III's claim that dance can hold in near-silence: reducing some media resources does not eliminate temporal relations. It supports the possibility of cross-channel comparison, but does not prove that the cycle remains invariant in every culture.

VIII. Series Conclusion: The Universal Structure of All Temporal Arts

Four essays are complete. Return to the opening question.

Why do some melodies still have something after a hundred listens? Why do some plays still reveal new things after ten viewings? Why does some dance make you feel "the ending weighs more than the beginning"? Why can some films be watched repeatedly without exhaustion?

This series offers the same set of questions:

The analytical framework attempts to cross:

Sensory channels — auditory, bodily, visual, and narrative; cultural traditions — Beethoven and K-pop, Peking opera and breaking each have different rules; historical periods — Bach, Kendrick Lamar, Noh, and Kubrick cannot be flattened into one kind of art; media complexity — both pure instrumental music and film can raise the question of how expectation forms and changes.

"Model → confirm → model broken → rebuild carrying a trace" is an aesthetic hypothesis proposed by SAE in this series, not an established basic law of human cognition. Enculturation, expertise, memory, venue, media version, and bodily state all affect where a model forms and whether it is broken.

Music theory, dance vocabulary, and narrative structure are not simple shells for the same operation. Each has histories and techniques that cannot be translated without remainder. The series makes only this hypothesis: amid those differences, we can still compare how expectations stabilize, reopen, and leave something unabsorbed.

Arise-Settle-Unfold-Fix is taken from the Life Cycle Table of the Self-as-an-End framework. Here it is a set of coordinates for reading temporal experience: selection, construction, being chiseled open, and reclosure. Whether a work actually acts this way must be tested against particular audiences and performances.

This series does not discuss music theory. It discusses the rationale behind music theory.

It does not discuss dance technique. It discusses the rationale behind technique.

It does not discuss narrative theory. It discusses the rationale behind narrative theory.

What they share is not one rationale, but one question: once a form stabilizes, what can still force us to look or listen again?

One final philosophical judgment follows from the framework: temporal arts have no predetermined, visible terminus.

This is not an empirical fact "guaranteed" by the chisel-construct cycle. More cautiously, any form familiar to a community may become a new construct: it can be inherited, varied, rejected, or continue elsewhere as a living tradition.

Once a form stabilizes, a new work may reopen it; reopening may expose what the current reading has not handled; that remainder may help another construct form. The process is not automatic progress, nor does it require every generation to be more complex than the last.

Schoenberg challenged tonal centricity and later developed twelve-tone technique while still using inherited forms. John Cage questioned broader conventions of composition, sound, silence, and performance, rather than simply "chiseling twelve-tone music." Mei Lanfang reshaped performance possibilities within highly coded Chinese opera traditions. Pina Bausch emerged from German expressive dance and Tanztheater lineages to reorganize relations between dance and theatre, not merely to "chisel classical ballet."

If remainder is useful as a concept, it means that every interpretation should acknowledge its own boundary: what a present closure has not absorbed may become the starting point of another encounter or creation.

This is not a proven law of art history, but a structural hypothesis that this series leaves open to counterexamples.
