The Chisel–Construct Cycle · Blockchain 19 / 21

Layer 2

Correctness, control, and cash flow are independent axes; the phrase “inherits Ethereum security” can hide their differences.

Han Qin · 6 September 2026 · English rewrite

IN THIS ESSAY

  1. Execution moves, settlement remains
  2. Finality has stages
  3. Security is more than mathematical correctness
  4. Temporary scaffolding needs an exit test

Execution moves, settlement remains

Layer-2 systems process activity away from Ethereum’s base execution and publish data or proofs back to it. This can lower user cost and increase capacity while keeping a route to base-layer settlement. But different rollups use different proof systems, data arrangements, upgrade controls, and withdrawal conditions.

Finality has stages

An optimistic rollup may accept a state first and leave a challenge window before it becomes final. A validity rollup proves a computation before the base layer accepts it, but still relies on circuits, data, and contracts. User interfaces often show a completed transaction long before every relevant form of finality has arrived.

Security is more than mathematical correctness

A proof can establish that a transition followed the specified rule. Admin keys may still upgrade contracts; a centralized sequencer may order or temporarily censor transactions; data availability may affect exit; an operator may capture fees and MEV. These are separate axes, not minor footnotes to one inherited label.

Temporary scaffolding needs an exit test

Central coordination can help a young system ship and recover from bugs. The question is how that power narrows over time and whether users retain an enforceable route out. A rollup becomes more chain-like not when its marketing changes, but when correctness, control, and value extraction can be examined independently.
