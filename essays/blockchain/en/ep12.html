The Chisel–Construct Cycle · Blockchain 12 / 21

NFT

An NFT records control of a token; it does not automatically deliver the image, copyright, server, or community promise.

Han Qin · 6 September 2026 · English rewrite

IN THIS ESSAY

  1. One token, several objects
  2. Ownership is a bundle
  3. Price can describe activity without describing it well
  4. Do not ask the token to be everything

One token, several objects

An NFT can establish which address controls a unique token. The image may live elsewhere; metadata may point through a URL or content-addressed network; copyright may remain with the creator; promised access may depend on a company and server. The screen presents one collectible while the relation is spread across several layers.

Ownership is a bundle

Buying the token is not automatically buying reproduction rights, a trademark, or an enforceable promise that the artwork will remain online. Some projects add licenses; others say little. Marketplaces may display royalties that another marketplace chooses not to enforce. The chain settles token control precisely and leaves the rest to contracts, platforms, and law.

Price can describe activity without describing it well

NFT markets made trades publicly countable, yet wash trading, self-dealing, incentives, and sparse liquidity could make volume and floor price poor descriptions of demand. Transparency of records does not remove the need to interpret who traded, why, and under what subsidy. More visible data can support a more convincing illusion if the categories are weak.

Do not ask the token to be everything

The point is not that NFTs are unreal. A token can be a durable, composable credential with meaningful social use. Confusion begins when one exact layer is allowed to certify every other: possession becomes authorship, market price becomes aesthetic value, and community membership becomes permanent entitlement. Better design names the bundle instead of hiding it.
