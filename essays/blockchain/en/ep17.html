The Chisel–Construct Cycle · Blockchain 17 / 21

FTX

Control permissions, customer shortfall, criminal loss, and bankruptcy distribution became four different ledgers.

Han Qin · 6 September 2026 · English rewrite

A centralized institution behind crypto screens

FTX offered trading, custody, credit, and settlement through accounts users experienced as balances. Behind the interface, a small group controlled systems, permissions, transfers, and the relationship with Alameda Research. Public-chain visibility did not reveal the internal claims customers believed they held.

Nine days exposed several meanings of “missing”

The November 2022 collapse moved rapidly from questions about Alameda’s balance sheet and the FTT token to withdrawals, insolvency, bankruptcy, and criminal investigation. Assets absent from customer accounts, money misappropriated under criminal law, enterprise value destroyed, and claims recognized in bankruptcy were related but not identical quantities.

Law fixes a date so a process can move

Bankruptcy must choose valuation rules and a petition date in order to compare claims and distribute recoveries. When crypto prices later rise, repayment measured in dollars may look complete while a customer compares it with the coins no longer held. The legal construction is necessary for closure; it does not erase the other experience of loss.

Recovery is not retroactive trust

Successful asset recovery and distribution can repair substantial harm. It cannot turn the original custody arrangement into what users thought it was. FTX reminds us that proof-of-reserves, audited liabilities, governance controls, and legal segregation answer different questions. A transparent token does not make the company holding it transparent.
