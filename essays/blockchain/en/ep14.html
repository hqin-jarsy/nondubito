El Salvador

Bitcoin legal tender produced different verdicts in law, wallet adoption, public finance, and everyday use.

Han Qin · 6 September 2026 · English rewrite

IN THIS ESSAY

  1. One policy, several experiments
  2. Success depends on the denominator
  3. Consent matters inside adoption
  4. There is no single national ledger

One policy, several experiments

El Salvador’s 2021 Bitcoin law made the asset legal tender, launched the Chivo wallet, offered an incentive for sign-up, and placed the government in the market as a buyer. These were related but distinct interventions: a legal rule, a payment system, a fiscal position, and an attempt to change daily behavior.

Success depends on the denominator

Wallet downloads, incentive claims, active use, merchant acceptance, remittance share, and the market value of government holdings answer different questions. A person who downloaded Chivo for the bonus is not necessarily a continuing bitcoin user. A rising asset price does not prove lower remittance friction; weak retail use does not by itself settle the investment account.

Consent matters inside adoption

A national policy can expand access while also pressing merchants and citizens toward a tool they did not choose. Later legal changes that narrowed mandatory acceptance illustrate that adoption is not simply the presence of infrastructure. A payment option and an obligation to accept it distribute risk and agency differently.

There is no single national ledger

Public debate searched for one verdict—success or failure—but the experiment generated several accounts. Law can be revised; wallet infrastructure can persist; fiscal gains and losses change with the valuation date; lived use follows trust, usability, connectivity, and need. Honest evaluation keeps these ledgers separate before asking how they interact.
