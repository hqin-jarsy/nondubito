Reading The Moon and the Bonfires

《月亮和篝火》解读

Sep 5, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)English · Simplified · Traditional英文 · 简体 · 繁体

Four essays on a foundling valued first as money and then labor, the village he needs but cannot re-enter, the three daughters of La Mora, and the three radically different meanings of fire.

Pavese’s last novel asks what it means to return when home survives mainly in the memories of people who may die, disperse, or refuse to speak. These readings follow Anguilla’s search for witnesses and the fragile gesture he can still offer a child who resembles him.

Cesare Pavese’s La luna e i falò (The Moon and the Bonfires), published in 1950. Full-book spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Literary criticism
He Is a Sum of Money

An abandoned child becomes first an orphanage subsidy and then farm labor; decades later, money cannot buy the witnesses to the life behind his nickname.

A Person Needs a Village

A village is not property but something that should keep part of you while you are absent; Anguilla returns to discover how little can be kept intact.

The Last Bonfire

四篇文章，从先被当成一笔钱、再被当成一双手的弃儿，到他需要却无法重新进入的村子、莫拉的三个女儿，以及三场意义完全不同的火。

帕韦泽的最后一部长篇追问：当故乡主要活在那些会死亡、离散或沉默的人身上，回来意味着什么。这组文章追踪鳗鱼寻找见证的路，也追踪他还能递给另一个像自己的孩子的那点东西。

切萨雷·帕韦泽《月亮和篝火》（La luna e i falò），一九五〇年出版。中文篇所用书名、人名、地名为作者自译。全书剧透。

他是一笔钱
