How Do You Acknowledge Borrowing from a Disputed Book?

Naming a debt does not cancel originality. Presenting borrowed material as an independent discovery does deprive the reader of a way to judge.

Han Qin · 2026-10-03

Discusses sources and creative choices without revealing crucial rescues, turns or final destinations in the new fiction.

Another book was on the desk

While writing beyond chapter eighty of Dream of the Red Chamber, I had more than the surviving novel and its early commentary beside me. I also read the text commonly called the Guiyou version, a twenty-eight-chapter continuation with a heavily disputed provenance. I borrowed from it. Readers should know that.

The difficulty is not merely whether to mention its title. After extensive revision, a writer can remember the final decision and forget the initial prompt. A scene acquires different participants; an event changes direction. With time, the result begins to feel as though every part originated at the same desk.

That makes it necessary to look backward. Where did the first suggestion come from? It is now very different may accurately describe the rewriting. It does not answer the question of origin.

Do not let a claimed history do the proving

The Guiyou text circulated online from 2008 and subsequently appeared in differing printed and edited forms. Accounts of its date and authorship disagree. The old manuscript claimed behind it has not been made publicly available for examination, and published scholarship largely treats it as a modern forgery. Those difficulties cannot be omitted because the material is useful.

The project reads it as a later re-creation drawing on an earlier draft. That needs a firm qualification: it is the working hypothesis I adopt, not an established finding about the text’s history. A reader can reject it and still inspect every borrowing.

Not adjudicating authenticity in these essays does not mean authenticity is irrelevant. A modern continuation cannot establish Cao Xueqin’s intentions merely by calling itself an old text. Suitability for a new story and authority as historical evidence are different qualifications.

Keeping them apart makes a more straightforward reading possible. A scene may prompt an idea without becoming evidence of a lost original. A detail may fit the first eighty chapters because a later writer read them well. Fit alone cannot prove antiquity.

Borrowing need not mean following

My largest disagreement with the Guiyou version concerns the setting of its tragedy. It places many characters’ destruction amid political collapse, war, and plunder. I wanted to examine something else: a world need not become an apocalypse for ordinary power, transactions, caution, and silence to leave someone with no way out.

This does not diminish tragedy written about war. It distinguishes the questions two works can ask. Once general chaos explains everything, responsibility can dissolve into that chaos too. I want particular actions and relationships to remain visible.

A borrowed plot outline therefore need not carry the borrowed work’s judgment of a person. An adopted encounter need not reach the same conclusion. What was retained and what was reversed need separate entries.

But reversal does not erase the debt. I am responsible for the new direction; the original prompt still has its source. Both statements can remain true.

Suppose it is entirely modern

The new paper tests the matter under an unfavorable assumption for the project: suppose this text provides no evidence for any draft by Cao Xueqin. What would have to change?

Where an element has independent support in the first eighty chapters or the commentary, that support remains. Where a detail comes only from the Guiyou version, it must be identified as borrowing from another continuation, not independent corroboration. Matching or closely matching wording needs acknowledgment or rewriting. A different overall meaning cannot conceal a verbal debt.

The audit does not conclude that there was hardly any borrowing. Much of what was taken was precisely the material of writing: plot outlines, details, scenes, and a smaller number of chapter titles, labels, and phrases. Calling these accidental similarities in order to defend originality would make the explanation less credible, not more.

Original fiction does not emerge from nowhere. Its claim is not that every source has been washed away, but that someone takes responsibility for the new choices and makes the debts visible. Acknowledging a source also does not automatically resolve questions about the use of its wording. Those still require attention, passage by passage.

Leave the account beside the story

A reader’s essay need not reproduce the complete list of borrowings. Anyone wanting the itemized record can follow the paper; anyone wanting the fiction need not first pass an examination in disputed editions. Both readers should at least know which other book was used and what limits govern that use.

The thirty chapters to be released are my original fiction, not Cao Xueqin’s recovered manuscript. They will carry my interpretation of the people, along with mistakes readers may identify. Source research cannot guarantee that the fiction works. Moving fiction cannot exempt its writer from explaining the sources.

So this essay does not end by declaring the matter closed. The record can change as checking continues. An original document not examined cannot be described as seen, and a debt discovered later must be added. Further studies will follow.

The story belongs on the table. The account of its borrowings belongs beside it. Readers may open either first.
