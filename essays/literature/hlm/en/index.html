NON DUBITO · A RED CHAMBER PROJECT

Lives Beyond Their EndingsReturning to Dream of the Red Chamber

We remember Daiyu’s tears, Baoyu’s attachments, and the dispersal of a whole household. But does knowing what happens to someone tell us everything about them? This project begins with the first eighty chapters, returns to their people, and ventures to continue their stories.

The peopleReader essaysResearch · optionalBeyond chapter eighty

First meet the people. Then continue their stories.

The character essays on this site were the project’s starting point: a sustained return to the people, relationships and choices in the novel. Reading led to another question—how might their stories continue? Character readings, the expanding reader-essay series, research and Han Qin’s original thirty-chapter continuation belong to one undertaking. Start with the essays and choose whether to follow the papers; no theoretical vocabulary is required first.

A note on the registers and labels

The groups below retain the arrangement used when these essays were written, so familiar pieces remain easy to find. The nine-register, 108-person scheme and its relational interpretations are this project’s reading framework—not an authenticated reconstruction of Cao Xueqin’s complete roster, nor permanent verdicts on the characters. Later papers explain where the reading develops or changes; our arrangement is not evidence about the original.

02 · READER ESSAYS · AN EXPANDING SERIES

From Reading to Continuing the Story

6 essays so far: begin with people and endings, then explore commentary, composition and borrowing. Read in order or begin with a question. Further essays will follow as the research develops; this is not a closed sequence.

Each essay has Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese and independently written English editions. No paper is required first. These essays discuss the first eighty chapters and the received continuation, but withhold the new fiction’s crucial turns and final destinations.

01

Can an Ending Tell You Who Someone Was?

We remember how Daiyu dies. Before we return to that ending, there is someone waiting for her to teach a lesson.

02

Why Write Beyond Chapter Eighty?

An ending does more than tell us what happened next. It changes how we understand the life that came before.

03

After a Fate Is Written, Can a Life Still Change?

The reader is shown an ending before the character reaches it. Do we keep watching her live, or only wait for the prediction to come true?

04

Must a Continuation Obey the Marginal Notes?

A voice beside the text may have seen chapters we no longer possess. That makes it worth hearing—not merely selecting the remarks that suit us.

05

Why Does a Book Leave More Than One Set of Footprints?

Deleted passages, unfinished sections, and uneven joins deserve attention. They do not automatically tell us who made every mark.

06

How Do You Acknowledge Borrowing from a Disputed Book?

Naming a debt does not cancel originality. Presenting borrowed material as an independent discovery does deprive the reader of a way to judge.

03 · RESEARCH PAPERS · OPTIONAL

A Closer Look at Evidence and Choices

Continuing a story is more than joining one event to the next. Each choice needs reasons. These papers distinguish the novel’s text, its commentary, interpretive inferences and creative decisions, so readers can question the work—and disagree.

The papers contain spoilers for the new fiction

The 4 papers below discuss major events and endings in Han Qin’s original continuation. Skip them for now if you would rather encounter the fiction first. The essays above stand on their own; the papers offer an optional deeper path.

02 · Composition and sources

Traces in the text, scholars’ explanations and this project’s conjectures are kept distinct. Theories of composition are presented without turning them into established history.

03 · Borrowing and provenance

The Guiyou Version: The Public Record, Theories, and What This Project Adopted

What was borrowed, and what was rejected? Creative material is distinguished from historical evidence; a working hypothesis is not an authenticated provenance.

04 · ORIGINAL FICTION · THIRTY NEW CHAPTERS

Beyond Chapter Eighty

Manuscript complete · Revision in progress · Not yet released

Han Qin has completed an original continuation comprising chapters 81–110. The complete thirty-chapter manuscript is now being refined. It will be released after the paper series, and this section will become its chapter-by-chapter reading entrance.

These thirty chapters are original fiction by Han Qin, not a recovered manuscript by Cao Xueqin or an edited compilation of existing continuations. “Interpretive reconstruction” describes the creative method: developing a reading of the first eighty chapters and related materials into a story. It is not a claim to have recovered the lost original.
