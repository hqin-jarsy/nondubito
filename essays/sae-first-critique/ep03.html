为什么叫第一批判

Why Call It a First Critique?

Han Qin (秦汉)

同一个名字，两件工作

“第一批判”首先让人想到康德的《纯粹理性批判》。这不是偶然借名。康德的三大批判把批判理解成一项划界事业：不是先宣布理性无能，而是查明一种能力在哪些条件下合法使用，越过哪里便发生僭越。

SAE 借的是这个动作，不是康德整套答案。康德考察先天认识如何可能、知性范畴如何对经验对象有效、理性为何会把理念误当作可认识对象。《SAE第一批判》考察的则是 SAE 自己的主张怎样取得地位：哪一项可推，哪一项只是公设，哪一项是后验输入，哪一项只能作为极限名使用。

两部书同名，职位不同。康德的第一批判在 SAE 的发生序列里可以被描述为特定层级的先验作业；SAE 的这部第一批判却审计整条 0D—16DD 道路，包括那种层级划分本身。前一句属于 SAE 对康德的定位，后一句属于 SAE 的自审，不能拿前一句给后一句担保。

从疆域到辖域

康德留下了一套精细的边界语言。概念有可以活动的场地，有可被认识的领地，也有能够立法的领域；本体作为极限概念，只负责阻止感性认识无限外推，不向知识添加一个可描述的新对象。

《第一批判》的“辖域”延续这个方向，却把每项主张的地址拆得更细：对象、层级、分支与公设。一个结论可能对这类对象成立，对另一类不成立；可能在某个发生层级有意义，跨层便失去工具；可能依赖经典逻辑分支，更换排中律或矛盾律后不再有着落；也可能直接站在某条可替换公设上。

这样做的目标不是建立一张比康德更宏大的地图，而是防止地图自己忘记图例。每次使用一项判断，都要能回答它在哪个地址取得效力。

翻转不等于胜出

SAE 与康德最明显的差异，在于边界从固定条件转向随作业移动。康德追问主体使经验可能的先天形式；SAE 把认识理解为持续划界，每次取得都同时产生新的余项。边界不是一次划定后永远不动，而在每次凿构中改写。

这可以被称为一次翻转，但翻转不是战胜。若只说“康德认为边界固定，SAE 认为边界生成，所以 SAE 更彻底”，便再次把差异排成未经审查的高下。康德可以反问：若没有相对稳定的条件，何以辨认这是同一个对象、同一种作业、同一条依赖链？移动边界依靠哪些不随每刀一同消散的规则？

《第一批判》对这个反问的回答不是一句口号，而是把那些规则降到公设与分支公设，公开承担。它不声称彻底摆脱条件，而是要求条件不得假装成无条件。

“人是什么”为什么仍在场

康德把“我能知道什么”“我应当做什么”“我可以希望什么”最后汇到“人是什么”。SAE 也不能把第一批判做成纯粹的逻辑清单，因为执行划界、承担公设、决定是否继续、接受他者追问的，都是主体。

但主体不能在这里被预先写成一种固定本质。若先定义“人天然求知”“人必然追求目的”“人最终趋向互认”，后面的发生序列便只是把结论重新演一遍。《第一批判》因此把认知驱力撤掉，把宇宙、主体与对象列为后验输入，并把从结构事实到规范效力的几处关键过渡移交给尚待完成的实践批判。

“人是什么”没有被取消，而被改写成一份不能一次结案的地址记录：主体在什么条件下成为作业者，什么由其承担，什么仍是他者无法替他完成的部分。

承接的方法，拒绝的继承权

称作“第一批判”，意味着 SAE 承认自己属于康德开启的批判传统：哲学不能只建构对象，还要审查自己据以建构的能力、权限与界限。它也承认本体不是可以随意填入内容的秘密仓库，形式工具不能越过自己的合法使用。

但这个名字不给 SAE 继承康德权威的权利。康德的论证必须按康德的地址阅读，SAE 的术语必须靠自己的账结算。两者可以在“极限概念”“判断的权限”“理性幻相”这些局部接口互相照亮，却不能因为共同使用“批判”二字就合成一条谱系证明。

第一批判之“第一”，于是也不是历史排名。它指向一项优先工作：在继续生产更多 SAE 西哲对读以前，先把这些对读不断调用的底层工具摆出来受审。只有边界、凭证与承担先公开，后面的对接才不会把相似当作证明，把翻译当作收编。

One Name, Two Tasks

“First Critique” inevitably recalls Kant's Critique of Pure Reason. The allusion is deliberate. Kant made critique an inquiry into limits: not a general declaration that reason is weak, but an account of the conditions under which a capacity has legitimate use and the point at which it becomes transcendent pretension.

SAE inherits that activity rather than Kant's complete set of answers. Kant asks how synthetic a priori cognition is possible, how categories apply to objects of experience, and why reason mistakes its own ideas for knowable objects. The SAE First Critique asks how SAE's claims acquire status. Which are derivable? Which are postulates? Which depend on posterior inputs? Which can function only as limit-names?

The books share a title while occupying different offices. Within SAE's developmental vocabulary, Kant's first Critique may be located as a kind of transcendental work at a particular level. SAE's First Critique audits the entire 0D–16DD road, including the legitimacy of that very scheme of levels. The first statement is SAE's placement of Kant; the second is SAE's examination of itself. The placement cannot warrant the examination.

From Territory to Jurisdiction

Kant supplied a precise language of boundaries. Concepts have a field in which they operate, a territory that can become an object of knowledge, and a domain in which they legislate. The noumenon is a limiting concept: its negative use restrains sensible cognition from indefinite extension without adding a new knowable object.

The SAE First Critique continues that concern but gives each claim a more granular address: object, level, branch, and postulate. A result may stand for one class of objects but not another. It may be intelligible at one developmental level and lose its tools when projected elsewhere. It may depend on a classical logical branch and lose standing when excluded middle or non-contradiction is replaced. It may rest directly on an avowed, replaceable postulate.

The purpose is not to draw a map grander than Kant's. It is to prevent the map from forgetting its legend. Every use of a judgment should make it possible to ask where that judgment obtained its authority.

Reversal Is Not Victory

The most visible difference is SAE's movement from fixed conditions toward boundaries rewritten through operation. Kant investigates a priori forms that make experience possible. SAE understands cognition as repeated demarcation in which each gain produces a new remainder. A boundary is not settled once for all; it moves with the work of chiseling and construction.

This may be called a reversal, but reversal does not establish superiority. The easy story says that Kant's boundaries are fixed, SAE's are generated, and SAE is therefore more radical. That story merely arranges difference into an unaudited hierarchy. Kant may ask in return: without relatively stable conditions, how do we identify the same object, the same kind of operation, or the same dependency across successive cuts? Which rules do not dissolve whenever a boundary moves?

The First Critique answers not with triumph but with registration. Those rules appear as postulates and branch postulates that must be openly undertaken. SAE does not claim to have escaped conditions. It insists that conditions must not impersonate the unconditional.

Why the Question of the Human Remains

Kant gathers “What can I know?”, “What ought I to do?”, and “What may I hope?” into “What is the human being?” SAE likewise cannot turn critique into a merely logical inventory. A subject performs demarcation, assumes postulates, decides whether to continue, and receives interrogation from others.

Yet the subject cannot be defined in advance by a fixed essence. If human beings are stipulated to seek knowledge, pursue ends, or move inevitably toward mutual recognition, the developmental sequence only stages conclusions already installed at the beginning. The First Critique therefore removes a compulsory drive to cognition, lists universe, subject, and object as posterior inputs, and transfers several crucial passages from structural description to normative validity to a future practical critique.

The question “What is the human being?” is not abolished. It becomes a record that cannot be closed once: under what conditions does a subject count as a performer, what does the subject undertake, and what can no other person perform in the subject's place?

A Method Inherited, an Authority Refused

Calling the work a First Critique places SAE within the critical tradition Kant opened. Philosophy must examine not only what it constructs but also the capacities, permissions, and limits by which construction proceeds. Noumena cannot become secret warehouses filled with whatever a system needs. Formal instruments cannot exceed their legitimate use.

The title grants SAE no inheritance of Kant's authority. Kant's arguments remain answerable at Kant's address; SAE's vocabulary must settle its own account. The two can illuminate one another at local interfaces—limiting concepts, the jurisdiction of judgment, illusions of reason—without their shared word “critique” becoming a genealogical proof.

The “first” in First Critique is therefore not a claim to historical priority. It marks a priority of work. Before more dialogues between SAE and Western philosophy multiply, the tools repeatedly invoked by those dialogues should be made visible and examinable. Only after boundaries, credentials, and undertakings are public can comparison avoid turning resemblance into proof or translation into annexation.
