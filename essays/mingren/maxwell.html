一百零八种人生 · 063 / 108 · 生命之后留下的工作
Great Lives · 063 / 108 · Work That Outlives a Life

麦克斯韦，光

Maxwell, Light

Han Qin (秦汉)

一、两个地方来的数字

一个人研究电和磁，算到最后，却遇到了光。这个故事最迷人的地方，不是天才突然获得启示，而是两个原本分开的问题，竟然给出了相近的数字。实验室里的电学测量，与光在远处往返的速度，开始指向同一件事。

十九世纪六十年代初，麦克斯韦在伦敦国王学院任教。他研究电磁作用怎样传播，利用韦伯和柯尔劳施测得的两种电学单位之间的关系，算出一个接近当时光速测量值的速度。那些数据来自别人的工作，并不是他为了得到光速而临时选出的答案。

两个数并非现代意义上的精确重合。但相近得足以迫使人认真考虑：我们叫作光的东西，会不会本来就是一种电磁扰动？照亮纸面的光，也许与导线、磁针周围发生的事情，服从同一套规律。

这一步把看似分开的领域接了起来。它不是说一块静止的磁铁就是一束光，而是说光可以作为电磁场传播的波来理解。电场、磁场和辐射之间的差别仍在，差别后面却出现了共同的说明。

在1861至1862年的论文里，麦克斯韦仍然借助一种充满空间的机械介质来思考。他想象旋转、弹性和位移。后来物理学放下了那些具体的以太图景，而他找到的重要关系继续发挥作用。结果可以走得比帮助它诞生的想象更远。

不妨在他把数值摆在一起的动作上多停留一会儿：这里是电学，那边是光学。它们为什么如此接近？一个问题问到深处，忽然把另一间房的门也推开了。门后还有什么，接下来要认真去看。

二、还要问具体怎样

詹姆斯·克拉克·麦克斯韦1831年6月13日出生于爱丁堡，幼年在苏格兰的格伦莱尔生活。传记记下他小时候追问东西怎样运作的习惯。给一个大概说明还不够，他还想知道，具体是怎样动起来的。

小孩子问这类问题，大人很容易拿一个名字挡回去：那是机器，那是磁铁，那是因为电。可名字并不等于解释。后来麦克斯韦做的许多事，仍然带着这份不满足——你已经告诉我它叫什么，现在能不能让我知道它怎么回事？

母亲在他八岁时去世。进入爱丁堡的学校以后，他也经历过因举止和装束显得不同而被取笑的日子。早年的成绩之外，这些日子也属于他。好奇心可以继续生长，却不会因此使一个孩子免于被别人的言行伤到。

十四岁时，他研究用线绳画卵形曲线的方法，成果被提交给爱丁堡皇家学会。这里已经有后来那个人的样子：手上的动作与纸上的几何并不分家。一条线绳怎样拉，画出来的曲线就怎样改变。

他先在爱丁堡大学学习，1850年进入剑桥，1854年在数学荣誉考试中取得第二名。排在前面的劳斯，后来也在动力学与稳定性研究中留下重要工作。一张名次表记下了那一年的考试，却没有替这些年轻人排好一生的道路。

他后来还会研究土星环、颜色、气体，写诗，设计装置。好奇心没有把他固定在一种姿态里。有时候需要算，有时候需要动手，有时候得先承认，眼前这个漂亮说法还没有告诉我们事情到底怎样发生。

三、听懂以后，还能继续说

法拉第留给麦克斯韦的，并不是一堆等待数学家来拯救的模糊感觉。那是长年实验、测量、比较和思考形成的工作。力线让空间中的关系变得可以追问，而不是把一切作用都塞进两个物体之间看不见的远距牵引。

光也早已进入法拉第的问题。1845年，他发现磁作用能够改变偏振光通过物质后的偏振方向；1846年，他又讨论射线振动与力线的关系。他已经把门指给后来的人，只是还没有麦克斯韦随后建立的定量理论。

1855至1856年的《论法拉第的力线》，是麦克斯韦认真接过这个问题的早期成果。他使用数学类比，使力线的分布和变化可以被进一步推算。把一种思想写进另一种语言，不只是换词；新的表达会要求原来的想法说得更清楚。

1857年3月，法拉第写信给他，谈到这般强大的数学进入自己的课题时，最初有些吃惊，读下去又得到鼓舞。两个人让不同的能力成为继续交流的理由。年轻人可以说出新的东西，年长者也仍然有话可说；问题在他们之间多走了一程。

麦克斯韦也不是有语言却没有感知的人。他会设计、测量和试验。法拉第更不是没有数学符号便没有理论的人。这段关系有力量，恰恰因为两人都是完整的工作者，并非各自缺半边，等对方来补成一个人。

读过特斯拉、爱迪生和斯宾诺莎，再看这一段交往，会遇到一个朴素的问题：后来的人拿到你的成果，是原样保存，还是据此继续工作？麦克斯韦对法拉第的尊重，包含把问题带到法拉第尚未到达的地方。接过来，也把它往前送一点。

四、那一处没有导线的间隙

想象两块彼此隔开的金属板，分别接上电路。充电时，电荷在板上积累。导线里的变化容易想象，板与板之间却没有普通的导电通路。这段间隙里发生的事，也必须进入说明，不能因为没有一根线经过就被略掉。

用今天的语言说，变化的电场对磁场的关系也要计入；这就是位移电流项的核心意义。它不是偷偷在空隙里安放了一条看不见的金属丝，也不是说一定有导电电荷像在导线中那样穿过两块板之间。

麦克斯韦到达这里的道路，比“为了对称，加上一项”曲折。他从机械模型、介质的弹性与电位移入手。用现代眼光回看，这个修正又让电荷守恒与场的方程相容。好看的对称是一种线索，却不能替代整段论证。

加入这一变化以后，场的扰动可以按方程作为波传播。不是两个无中生有的小家伙互相追逐，而是电与磁的变化受一组共同规律约束。源把能量送进场，扰动传播；方程说明传播的条件与速度。

让人激动的，是结论不再局限于原先拿来解释的装置。你认真处理导线与间隙，理论却开始谈到远方的光。数学在这里不仅整理旧知识，也生成了可以交给实验检验的新问题。

当然，方程中出现一种可能，还不等于自然已经答应。模型可以错，测量可以改，结论可以受适用条件限制。麦克斯韦工作最有生命力的地方，不是替世界封口，而是把问题写到别人能够接着验的程度。

五、后人手里的四行

1864年12月，他的《电磁场的动力学理论》在皇家学会宣读，1865年正式发表。论文中的一般方程用二十个变量、二十个分量方程展开。今天常见的四行矢量形式，并不是他当年落笔的原样。

赫维赛德、赫兹等人后来重新组织了电磁理论的表达；现代矢量语言也有自己的发展史。如今学生写下“麦克斯韦方程”，名字没有错，但那份简洁已经包含后来许多人的工作。一项成果被接续，往往也会被改写。

十九世纪八十年代，赫兹用实验产生并研究电磁波，让理论的预测得到关键支持。那不是替麦克斯韦所有想象一次盖章，更不是替以太的每一种机械模型作证。接受一项成果，也可以把它与已经不再需要的支架分开。

同牛顿比较，统一的意义会更清楚。牛顿让天上的轨道和地上的运动进入共同规律，麦克斯韦把电、磁与光联系起来。统一不是把现象之间的差别说没了，而是解释为什么这些不同现象能够在同一套关系中被理解。

但电磁理论也使旧有的运动观念遇到难题。怎样理解光速，又怎样把不同匀速运动观察者的测量联系起来？1905年，爱因斯坦把相对性原理与真空光速不变作为出发点，重新处理时间和空间。这一步并非麦克斯韦自己已经完成。

牛顿力学也没有因此成为废纸；在合适的速度与条件下，它仍然非常有用。后来者不是掀掉所有地板才能站住，而是知道哪一层在什么地方可靠。麦克斯韦留下了一项足够强的工作，也留下需要别人继续承担的问题。

六、他也把手伸进实验里

如果只给麦克斯韦一支粉笔，就会漏掉他手里的许多东西。颜色转盘、光学装置、测量气体黏滞性的仪器，都属于他的工作。公式没有使他远离材料，反而常常要求他回到材料面前，问它到底是否照预想的方式变化。

1861年的彩色影像演示尤其容易让人看见这一面。摄影师托马斯·萨顿与他合作，透过滤色装置拍摄格子缎带，再把分开的影像投射叠合，让观众看到颜色的重现。这是一项合作演示，不是一个人凭空造出了今天的彩色相机。

当时感光材料对不同颜色的响应有限，演示的具体效果也比后来流传的“第一张彩照”故事复杂。但核心问题清晰而新鲜：颜色的感觉能否经过分析，再用适当比例的光重新组合？观看本身也可以成为实验对象。

凯瑟琳不只是传记中陪伴他的妻子。她参与了颜色与气体实验。记录读数、调整装置、让测量持续进行，这些事共同构成知识。一个人的名字站在论文上，并不表示房间里只有一双手。

研究气体时，麦克斯韦也改变了提问尺度。分子不是全以同一速度运动，与其假装能追踪每一个，不如研究不同速度出现的分布。统计不是放弃严密，而是为大量个体的运动找到另一种可以检验的描述。

后来被称为“麦克斯韦妖”的设想，也把一位能够区分快慢分子的假想小门卫放进气体问题。这是追问热力学规律与微观运动关系的思想实验，不是恶魔故事，更不是他已经推翻第二定律的公告。关于信息与代价的讨论，还会由后人继续。

七、把继续工作的地方造出来

1865年离开国王学院以后，他回到格伦莱尔。这里给了他写作和整理工作的时间，也有庄园、日常生活和与凯瑟琳相处的日子。伦敦时期已经形成的重要电磁理论，并不是等到独居乡下才忽然降临。

后来1873年出版的两卷《电磁通论》，把大量工作组织成别人能够学习、争论和使用的书。写一篇发现，和写一部让后来者能走进去的著作，是不同的劳动。后者需要不断考虑：读者从哪里开始，在哪一步会迷路？

1871年，麦克斯韦成为剑桥第一任卡文迪许实验物理教授。他参与规划并主持建设实验室，1874年正式开放。这里的工作又换了一种形状：从写出关系，变成给别人准备检验关系的房间与器具。

电学标准、准确测量、仪器的安排与学生的训练，都需要耐心。它们没有一次灵光乍现那样适合挂在墙上，却决定别人能不能重复你的结果，能不能发现你的误差，能不能在你的工作之后再走一步。

一个人最重要的留下，有时不只是一条定律，也是一张可供他人使用的桌子。我们可以看见它怎样扩大研究机会，同时承认当时大学仍有真实的准入限制。后来得到延展的公共价值，不等于开端已经对所有人平等开放。

格伦莱尔帮助他写作，剑桥使他能够建设实验条件，通信又把这些地方接起来。独处与合作、理论与操作，都在同一份有限的生命里分占时间。为别人能够继续工作而花掉的时间，也属于这一生做成的事情。

八、四十八岁以后

1879年11月5日，麦克斯韦在剑桥去世，四十八岁。他罹患腹部癌症，后来安葬在格伦莱尔附近的帕顿。一个能描述光怎样走过空间的人，也没有因此获得更多身体可以承受的时间。

传记写到他最后的痛苦，也写到他对生病的凯瑟琳的牵挂。我们可以读见关心，不必把从不抱怨、隐藏症状或独自忍受提升为伟大人物的考核标准。需要照料，并不减损一个人已经做出的工作。

他没能看见赫兹后来的验证，也没能看见电磁理论进入无线通信与相对论的年代。我们今天熟悉的应用，并不是他坐在书桌前已经一件件预演完毕的未来。后人的试验、工程和判断，不能从故事里删掉。

正因为如此，“生命之后留下的工作”才不只是长久留名。它意味着一项成果能够被别人读懂、质疑、改写，甚至拆开使用。作者不在场，别人仍能据此做出原作者没有做过的事。

这不等于人的价值必须靠死后有用来证明。许多没有留下方程的人，也值得被认真对待。麦克斯韦让我们看见的，是一种特别清楚的传递：成果属于他的努力，继续却不必永远属于他的掌控。

光并不是从纸上被制造出来的。纸上的关系，使人对本来就在眼前的光有了新的理解。世界没有因为一个人看懂它才开始存在，但一个人把理解写清楚以后，别人的眼前可以多出一条路。

九、桌面亮起来

最后仍回到这组散文虚构的桥上。法拉第把装置放到桌边，麦克斯韦在旁边摊开纸。这里没有谁必须蹲在下面、谁必须站在上面。两个人都可以坐下，也都可以指着某一处说：这一点，我还没有明白。

法拉第先指向一次变化，麦克斯韦把它写进关系。写到一半，两人又回头看装置。公式不替实验作答，实验也不因为来得早就禁止新的表达。纸与器具之间，问题来回走动。

凯瑟琳把一份读数记录放过来，萨顿带着影像的材料。我们在这场想象里把他们请到桌边，是为了记得：被一个名字概括的成果，常有别人的劳动经过。让名字更完整，不会使麦克斯韦变小。

斯宾诺莎的镜片还包在软布里。波伏瓦挪开镜子，给纸留出地方。契诃夫看见有人累了，先把椅子推过去。大家属于同一幅场景，却不必被同一套方程解释；人的处境还需要另外的语言。

麦克斯韦写下最后一行，停了一会儿，又把前面一处标记改了。一个思想真正交给别人，不必像碑文那样禁止触碰。也可以像工作纸，容许另一只手在旁边添上疑问。

桥上的灯照亮了桌面。不是粉笔创造了光，也不是方程替所有人的人生作出了判决。只是此前看不清的线条，现在更容易辨认了。远处的人，也可以走近来看看。

他把纸往外推一点，没有要求后来者重复他的姿势。你可以把它带去另一间实验室，换一种写法，提出新的检验。需要保留的不是作者永远站在中间，而是那条仍然能让问题继续的路。

他活了四十八年。桌上的工作没有活到这里就停下。光落在纸上；下一位读者低下头，开始读。

I. Numbers from Different Rooms

A man investigates electricity and magnetism, and finds light waiting at the end of a calculation. What makes this remarkable is not a flash of private revelation. Two apparently separate investigations produce strikingly similar numbers. An electrical measurement begins to speak to the speed of something crossing a room.

In the early 1860s, Maxwell was teaching at King's College London and considering how electromagnetic disturbances might travel. Using Weber and Kohlrausch's measurements connecting two systems of electrical units, he obtained a speed close to contemporary measurements of light. The experimental numbers came from other people's work, not from an answer he selected to make his theory succeed.

There was no agreement to the last modern decimal place. There was enough agreement to make a demanding question unavoidable: might light itself be an electromagnetic disturbance? The illumination falling on the paper might belong to the same physical account as what happened around wires and magnets.

The connection did not make a stationary magnet identical with a beam of light. Rather, light could be understood as a wave travelling through an electromagnetic field. Differences between electric fields, magnetic fields and radiation remained, while a shared set of relationships began to explain them.

In his papers of 1861–62, Maxwell still imagined a mechanical medium filling space, with rotations, elastic responses and displacements. Later physics would abandon those particular pictures of an ether while retaining important relationships he had found. A result can travel farther than the scaffolding that first helped someone reach it.

Do not rush past the modest action of setting those numbers beside each other. One came from electricity, the other from optics. Why should they be close? Pursuing a question within one room had opened the door to another. It was an invitation to further work, not the closing declaration of a complete universe.

II. But How Does It Work?

James Clerk Maxwell was born in Edinburgh on 13 June 1831 and spent much of his childhood at Glenlair in Scotland. Early biography remembers a child persistently asking how things worked. A general explanation was not enough. He wanted the particular account of what made this thing move.

Adults often answer such questions with names: a machine, a magnet, electricity. A name can quietly take the place of an explanation. Much of Maxwell's later work retains the child's dissatisfaction: now that you have told me what to call it, can you show me what is actually happening?

His mother died when he was eight. At school in Edinburgh, his unusual manner and clothing made him a target for teasing. We need not confidently declare that he did not care. The picture of the invulnerable prodigy can erase the ordinary hurts that a gifted child is still capable of feeling.

At fourteen he investigated ways of drawing oval curves using string, work presented to the Royal Society of Edinburgh. Here the hand and the geometry already belong together. Change the arrangement of the string and the drawn shape changes with it. Thinking is something that can also take place through a tool.

After studying at Edinburgh University, he entered Cambridge in 1850 and became Second Wrangler in the Mathematical Tripos of 1854. Edward Routh, who came first, would make important contributions to dynamics and stability. An examination list records a year's performance; it cannot arrange the whole lives of the young people whose names appear on it.

Maxwell would investigate Saturn's rings, colour and gases, build apparatus and write poetry. Curiosity did not confine him to one approved posture. Sometimes the work required calculation, sometimes hands, sometimes the admission that an elegant phrase had still failed to explain the thing it purported to describe.

III. Understanding Is Not the End of the Conversation

Faraday did not leave behind a collection of vague feelings awaiting rescue by a mathematician. His work joined experiments, measurements, comparisons and sustained theoretical thought. Lines of force made relationships throughout space available for investigation, rather than locating the whole explanation in distant bodies exerting unexplained attractions upon each other.

Light was already part of that investigation. In 1845 Faraday discovered a magnetic influence on the polarization of light passing through matter; in 1846 he discussed ray vibrations and lines of force. He had pointed towards a connection, though not supplied the quantitative electromagnetic theory that Maxwell would subsequently develop.

On Faraday's Lines of Force, presented in 1855–56, was an early stage of Maxwell's response. Mathematical analogies made the arrangement and changes of force available for further reasoning. Moving a thought into a new language does more than substitute vocabulary: the new expression demands details that the earlier version could leave open.

In March 1857 Faraday wrote of his initial surprise at seeing such powerful mathematics brought to his subject, and of the encouragement he found as he read. This was not simply an older man abdicating. Different abilities had become reasons for a conversation to continue rather than grounds for closing it.

Maxwell himself designed experiments and took measurements. Faraday could reason theoretically without the advanced mathematical training of his younger correspondent. Their relationship matters more when we see two whole workers, rather than two incomplete halves waiting to make one sufficient person. Neither the hands nor the thought belonged exclusively to one side.

After Tesla, Edison and Spinoza, their correspondence brings a familiar question into a new setting. What does someone do with the work you leave: preserve it, or use it to work further? Maxwell's respect included taking Faraday's questions to places Faraday had not yet reached. Receiving something could also mean passing it forward.

IV. The Gap Without a Wire

Picture two separated metal plates connected to a circuit. While the arrangement charges, electric charge accumulates on the plates. Changes in the wires are comparatively easy to imagine. Between the plates there is no ordinary conducting path, but what happens in that gap must also enter the explanation.

In present-day language, the changing electric field contributes to the magnetic-field relationship: the displacement-current term. This does not secretly insert a thin metal wire into the space between the plates. Nor does it require conducting charges to cross that space in the same manner as charges moving along a conductor.

Maxwell's route was more complicated than adding a term because symmetry looked attractive. He worked through mechanical models, the elasticity of a medium and electric displacement. Retrospectively, the correction also makes the field equation consistent with charge conservation. A pleasing symmetry can suggest a direction without replacing the argument or its historical development.

The resulting equations permit disturbances of the field to propagate as waves. These are not two little creatures chasing one another and generating energy from nothing. Electric and magnetic changes obey connected laws. A source supplies energy; a disturbance travels; the theory describes how that propagation can take place.

The excitement is that the answer reaches beyond the apparatus that helped pose the question. Attend carefully to a circuit and a gap, and the theory begins to say something about distant light. Mathematics is not merely filing established facts. It is producing new questions that experiment can be asked to answer.

Possibility within an equation is not nature's agreement in advance. A model can fail, a measurement change, a result depend on limited conditions. The enduring strength here is the precision with which somebody else can take up the work and test it. The question has acquired a public, usable form.

V. Four Lines in Later Hands

A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field was read to the Royal Society in December 1864 and published in 1865. Its general account used twenty component equations with twenty variables. The four vector equations familiar from modern classrooms are not a facsimile of the page Maxwell originally wrote.

Heaviside, Hertz and others later reorganized the presentation of electromagnetic theory; modern vector notation has its own history. A student is right to call the result Maxwell's equations, while the compactness of that expression also carries other people's labour. Continuing a discovery frequently involves changing its working form.

In the 1880s Hertz produced and investigated electromagnetic waves experimentally, giving crucial support to the prediction. That did not validate every detail of Maxwell's mechanical imagery or every proposed ether. A successful theory can be taken forward while being separated from supports that are no longer needed.

Newton offers a useful comparison. His work brought celestial and terrestrial motion under common laws; Maxwell connected electricity, magnetism and light. Unification does not mean declaring every phenomenon identical. It explains how visibly different phenomena can belong to a coherent account, without making their observable differences disappear.

Electromagnetic theory also raised difficulties for existing ideas about motion. How should light's speed be understood, and how should uniformly moving observers compare their measurements? In 1905 Einstein began from the relativity principle and the invariance of light's vacuum speed, reconsidering space and time. Maxwell had not already completed that further step.

Newtonian mechanics did not thereby become waste paper. Within appropriate conditions and speeds it remains extraordinarily useful. A successor need not demolish every earlier floor, but must learn where each is reliable. Maxwell left both a powerful working achievement and problems whose consequences would have to be carried by other people.

VI. He Had Hands Too

Give Maxwell only a piece of chalk and much of his work disappears. Colour discs, optical arrangements and apparatus for investigating gas viscosity also belonged to his practice. Equations repeatedly sent him back to materials, asking whether the world actually behaved in the way a proposed account required.

The colour-image demonstration of 1861 makes that especially visible. Working with Maxwell, the photographer Thomas Sutton photographed a tartan ribbon through different filters. Separate images could then be projected together to reproduce colour. This was a collaborative demonstration of a method, not a modern colour camera invented entire by a solitary man.

The photographic materials had limited sensitivity to different colours, and the demonstration's practical performance was more complicated than the familiar story of a first colour photograph suggests. The underlying question was nevertheless precise: could a colour appearance be analysed and reconstructed by suitably combining light? Seeing itself had become something to investigate experimentally.

Katherine belongs in this working picture, not merely beside it as the wife who offered comfort. She participated in colour and gas experiments. Reading instruments, adjusting arrangements and sustaining a measurement are part of making knowledge. A paper associated with one famous name need not have been made in a room containing only one pair of hands.

With gases, Maxwell also changed the scale of the question. Molecules do not all have one speed. Instead of pretending to follow every individual, a researcher can investigate how speeds are distributed across a population. Statistical description need not abandon rigour; it makes a different, testable account of many moving individuals possible.

His imagined molecular gatekeeper, later called Maxwell's demon, pressed the relation between microscopic motion and thermodynamic regularity. It was a thought experiment, not a supernatural visitor or an announcement that the second law had been disproved. Later thinkers would continue the questions about information, physical operations and their costs, adding work he had not himself done.

VII. Building Somewhere to Continue

After leaving King's College in 1865, Maxwell returned to Glenlair. There was time for writing and organizing research, alongside the estate, everyday concerns and life with Katherine. The important electromagnetic theory developed during his London years did not suddenly descend on an isolated thinker only after his retreat to the countryside.

The two-volume Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism, published in 1873, arranged a large body of work into something other people could study, challenge and use. Reporting a discovery and making a book that later readers can enter are different kinds of labour. The second asks where someone will begin, and where they may get lost.

In 1871 Maxwell became Cambridge's first Cavendish Professor of Experimental Physics. He helped plan and oversaw the construction of the laboratory, which opened in 1874. The work changed shape again: from writing relationships to preparing rooms and instruments in which other people could investigate those relationships for themselves.

Electrical standards, accurate measurement, the placement of instruments and the training of students all required patience. They are less easily commemorated than a sudden insight. Yet they determine whether someone can reproduce a result, find an error, or take one more step after the person whose discovery began the work.

What a person leaves may include a usable bench as well as a law of nature. We can recognize how the laboratory enlarged research opportunities while acknowledging the real restrictions on university access in its period. An institution's later public value does not mean it began with equal entry for everyone.

There is no need to turn rural quiet and university organization into a choice between incompatible lives. Glenlair supported writing; Cambridge offered the means to build experimental conditions; correspondence connected places. Solitude and collaboration, theory and practical work, divided the hours of one limited human life between them.

VIII. After Forty-Eight

Maxwell died in Cambridge on 5 November 1879, aged forty-eight, with abdominal cancer. He was buried at Parton, near Glenlair. Being able to describe how light crosses space had given him no exemption from the amount of time his body could endure, or from the need for other people's care.

Biographical accounts describe suffering near the end and his concern for Katherine, who was also unwell. We can attend to that concern without making silence about pain, concealed symptoms or solitary endurance entrance requirements for greatness. Needing help does not diminish the work a person has already done.

He did not see Hertz's later experiments, or the subsequent age of wireless communication and relativity. Familiar modern applications were not a future he had already rehearsed in every detail at his desk. Other people's experiments, engineering choices and judgment belong to the history, rather than merely decorating his fulfilment.

Work that outlives a life means more than a name lasting on a plaque. It means something others can understand, dispute, rewrite and sometimes take apart for use. The author need not remain present for someone else to do something with it that the author never did.

This is not a test of human worth based on posthumous usefulness. People who leave no equations also deserve serious attention. Maxwell makes one particular kind of transmission unusually visible: the achievement belongs to his effort, while its continuation need not remain under his permanent ownership or control.

Light was not manufactured by the marks on the page. Those marks helped people understand light that had already been falling before their eyes. The world did not wait for a physicist to comprehend it before existing. A clear account could nevertheless open a new route through it for another person.

IX. A Lit Working Table

Return finally to the invented bridge of these essays. Faraday sets his apparatus beside the table; Maxwell spreads a sheet of paper nearby. Neither must remain crouching below while the other stands above. Both may sit down, and both may point to something they have not yet understood.

Faraday indicates a change. Maxwell writes a relationship, then pauses, and they look back at the apparatus together. Equations do not supply the experiment's answer for it. An experiment's priority does not forbid a new description. Between paper and instrument, the question travels back and forth.

Katherine brings a record of readings; Sutton brings materials for the images. We invite them to this imagined table to remember that work remembered under one name often passed through other people's hands. Making the company more visible does not make Maxwell smaller. It makes the work more fully visible.

Spinoza's lens is still wrapped in its soft cloth. Beauvoir moves her mirror to make room for the papers. Chekhov notices somebody tiring and brings a chair. Everyone inhabits this scene without becoming an illustration of the same equations. Human circumstances still call for other kinds of language.

Maxwell finishes a line, waits, then changes a mark earlier on the page. Work genuinely offered to another person need not resemble an inscription that forbids touching. It can look like a working sheet, with room in the margin for someone else's question or an alteration not yet imagined.

The bridge's lamp lights the tabletop. Chalk has not created light, and no equation has passed judgment on everybody's life. Lines that were difficult to see are simply easier to distinguish. Somebody standing farther away can now come closer to the table and examine them.

Maxwell pushes the paper a little outward. There is no requirement that the next person reproduce his posture. Take it to another laboratory, give it another notation, devise another test. What needs to continue is not the author's position at the centre, but the possibility of carrying the question further.

He lived for forty-eight years. The work on the table did not stop at the end of that life. Light falls across the page. The next reader bends down and begins to read.
