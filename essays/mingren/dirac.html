狄拉克：方程比观察先到

Dirac: The Equation Arrived First

Han Qin (秦汉)

一、最安静的人

物理学家的故事里，有一个不列入国际单位制的单位：一个狄拉克，等于每小时一个词。

这是同事间流传的玩笑，不是一份逐小时记录。它记住了一个人的少言，也容易让后来的人误以为，已经用一个数字说完了他。

我们从这个玩笑进去，但不把门关在身后。

有些话可以暂时不说，纸上却不能因此少写一个条件。别人需要看懂你用了什么，怎样从这里走到那里，哪里仍然过不去。狄拉克留下来的，不只有沉默，还有允许别人逐行追问的论文。

1928年1月2日，《电子的量子理论》送达期刊；2月1日刊出。方程来到读者面前时，它带来的问题还远没有结束。

论文面对的电子并不陌生。已经积累起来的光谱问题、自旋研究、量子理论与狭义相对论，挤在同一张桌上。不是自然什么也没给，他便从空白中取出了一个世界。是已有的东西还不能一起安放。

方程出来了。解释没有同时完成。

负能量解仍是问题；后来有了空穴理论，也有把空穴认作质子的尝试。到1931年，他明确提出一种与电子质量相同、带相反电荷的新粒子：反电子。

1932年，安德森在云雾室实验中取得了正电子的证据；1933年的论文把照片、装置与辨认理由一起交了出来。那不是一句“拍到了”，而是一项需要排除其他解释的实验工作。

方程的确先到了。

但它不是先于一切经验到达。它先于那项新的发现；其间还有解释的改变、别人的质疑和另一间实验室里的劳动。

四年不是一个人在纸上写下答案后，世界慢慢走来盖章的空白。

二、美比正确更重要？

1963年，狄拉克写过一句很强的话：与让方程立即贴合实验相比，他把方程中的美放在更优先的位置。他举的例子涉及薛定谔早先尝试中的困难，以及后来对自旋的理解。这是一种关于研究怎样前进的主张，不是一张宣布美永远正确的成绩单。

它最有力量的地方，也许恰好是它没有保险。

已经知道正确的东西，不需要你冒险喜欢。困难在于尚未知道的时候：这一条值得继续吗？一个尚有障碍的结构，是否比一份贴得严丝合缝、却没有展开余地的答案更值得追下去？

狄拉克寻找的，不是把现成方程排得漂亮一点。他要把相对论要求与量子理论的演化方式一起放进结构。用今天常见的记号，一个自由电子的方程可以写成：

iℏ ∂ψ/∂t = (c α·p + βmc²)ψ

这里的动量是算符，ψ不是一个普通的单分量数。时间的一阶形式、相对论要求以及系数必须满足的代数关系共同限定了选择。在他使用的构造里，四乘四矩阵与四分量波函数由这些共同要求出现；不是仅仅说一句“我要线性”，自然便送来四个格子。

漂亮，并不等于少。

有时它意味着多出的部分也不能随手剪掉。你喜欢这个构造的一半，它却要求你交代另一半。方程的吸引力在这里变成了负担：不是“我想要，所以它存在”，而是“我已经接受了这些条件，便不能只领取满意的结果”。

历史学家克拉格记述，狄拉克1956年访问莫斯科时，曾在黑板上留下关于物理定律与数学美的题句。粉笔写下的，是一种研究者愿意押上时间的信念。

即使粉笔字被擦去，这个选择仍然在那里：当一个结构很美，却没有照顾你已有的解释，你愿不愿意修改解释？当它终于与可靠证据冲突，你又愿不愿意修改结构？

美可以是指南针。

指南针不能替你抵达。

三、裂缝在何处

读到这里，很容易把三个人排成一条直线：孔德提供事实，波普尔检验，狄拉克先看见美。可他们没有分工得这样整齐。

孔德在《教程》第一讲里，已经承认理论与观察彼此需要。没有一个先提出的问题，连什么值得观察都难以确定。

波普尔也没有要求猜想只能从已经看见的东西中摘取。他区分科学划界与意义判断，并不因此宣布诗、数学或形而上学全无意义。一个理论具有数学上的理由，也不使它关于自然的主张自动退出猜想与检验的问题。

所以，狄拉克不是一个从他们绝对不准存在的地方闯进来的人。

真正值得停下来的，是两个“必须”之间的距离。

一个必须是：接受这些前提，遵循这些运算，结果不能任意改变。另一个必须是：自然一定如此。前者可以在数学中获得证明；要把它交给后者，还需要知道，这些前提和解释是不是用对了地方。

推导可以比提出它的人更不迁就人。应用却不会因为推导严密，就自动得到世界的签字。

“方程比观察先到”，因而也不能直接改写成“先验知识得到了后验认证”。早于一次检验，是时间关系；康德意义上的先验，涉及认识根据。使用了多年实验与理论训练的人，可以先提出一个新答案，但他并没有因此从全部经验之外出发。

这并不削弱美。它给美一个不必假装全知的位置。

美可以参与发现：让人看出一种关系值得追，感觉几个条件之间的牵连不应被轻易拆散，愿意在尚无现成答案时继续工作。这种辨认不是装饰，也不是检验结果的替代品。

用本系列的语言说，知，是能够交代已经走出的几步；不知，是不把尚未完成的解释填成空无；认，是主体决定怎样承担眼前的问题。

不是方程替他选择。是他选择让自己写下的方程，也有机会反过来纠正自己。

四、他和爱因斯坦

在1963年那篇回顾中，他认真描述相对论怎样改变物理学使用的概念与数学形式。对他而言，继承不是往旧表格里再填几个数字；有时连什么可以当作表格，都需要重新考虑。他也没有把爱因斯坦的每一项要求都变成不可质疑的遗嘱，包括怎样看待量子理论，以及数学形式应当怎样服务物理问题。

这里有一种不必抹平分歧的接近。

一个人给你留下的，不只是几个可以接着代入的公式。他可能改变你所能提出的问题，使原来不可思议的改写，成为值得认真尝试的工作。你后来不同意他，并不等于那份改变从未发生。

反过来，受过影响也不要求终身站在同一个答案里。

继承不是把判断一并交出去。狄拉克带着自己的经历进入物理学，也得在每一个具体问题前，决定接过什么、改动什么、暂时留下什么。

狄拉克与爱因斯坦之间的这条线，可以留在一个更小、也更实在的位置：一个人留下的问题，进入另一个人的工作；进入之后，没有取得免受质问的权力。

五、沉默中的工作

沉默不是证明方法。

方程也没有绕过语言。它是一种高度明确、能够被转写、计算和争论的表达。四分量怎么来，系数满足什么关系，负能量状态如何解释，都不是把耳朵贴在纸上就能得到的回答。

“听方程说话”，适合作为比喻。它说的是：你不能只听见你想听的。

当推导带来麻烦，研究者可以重算，检查假设，寻找新的解释，也可以暂时放下。每一种都是行动，不是等一个神谕穿过寂静。

1931年的论文开头，狄拉克谈到的数学进展，恰恰不只是在固定规则中多算几步。他容许新的想法改变理论所使用的基础。寻找答案，有时还会改写原来允许哪些答案出现的安排。

因此，负号不是永远只能通向一扇预先开好的门。

你选择不把困难抹去，并不等于它已经替你指出唯一的路。余项留下继续发问的位置；继续、转向或停下的动力，仍然来自主体的判断与选择。

沉默可以把这个尚未决定的时刻留出来。

不急着用一个熟悉名称盖住陌生结构。不急着因为别人正在等，就给出一种自己也不能承担的确定。先让问题以问题的样子待一会儿，然后把能交代的部分写下来。

写下来，别人便能进来。

他们不必具有你的性格，也不必分享你对美的全部感觉。他们可以从某一行开始，指出一个错误。这个时刻，沉默没有遭到破坏；它所守住的工作，得到了继续。

六、后来的人

从这里往后，屈原、麦克林托克与波兰尼会从不同方向进入这个系列。

不是三个人轮流替狄拉克作证。

屈原的诗让我们遇见一种不能用实验成功来兑换的美。《离骚》不是尚未找到检测仪器的粒子预言。读者需要面对它怎样说话、怎样组织经验，自己又在什么地方受到触动。诗中的事实与解释当然可以被质疑；但诗不是先通过物理学考试，才获准作为诗存在。

我们也不能因为诗与方程都可能美，就说两种美承担同一种证明责任。狄拉克对电子作出了物理主张，这份责任不会因他也有审美判断而减轻。

麦克林托克1950年的论文，从玉米第九号染色体短臂的实验讲起：种植的材料里，原本少见的现象开始反复出现。她带到实验面前的，既有问题，也有多年与这些材料相处的经验。

有些感觉长在长期观察里。它可能使人注意到原来当作背景的变化，改变下一次怎样种、怎样比较、怎样追问。但注意到不等于已经知道原因，更不是在显微镜里看见一个贴着“转座子”标签的小东西。

波兰尼给我们另一个入口：能够明说的规则，未必穷尽正在进行的认识。

一个人骑车时，身体对倾斜的调整，不必每次先变成一句完整的口令。可是，会骑车不等于已经正确解释了所有相关力学；感觉方程值得追，也不等于方程已经得到了物理证明。

这些区别不把默会的部分赶出去。它们使它有机会留下，而不必冒充另一种东西。

三条路没有在这里合并成一条。诗的声音、实验中的辨认、尚未完全说出的技能，分别向我们提出问题：你所承认的认识，是否只剩下已经容易列成表格的那部分？

不能明说，并不自动更深。

能够明说，也不意味着已经说尽。

七、那个负号

回到纸上。

对自由粒子，一个熟悉的能量关系写成：

E = ±√(|p|²c² + m²c⁴)

麻烦不是“负数在物理学中一律非法”。能量可以相对于选定的零点为负。这里更具体的困难是：怎样理解与正能量分支并存、向下没有下界的那一支，以及为什么电子不会不断落入更低的状态。

1928年的论文并没有平静地替每个解安排好住所。有些解一度被拒斥，问题也被明确留了下来。后来重新处理它，不需要我们把早年的犹豫改写成从来没有犹豫。

空穴理论是一种尝试：设想负能量电子态已经被填满，少掉一个电子的空穴，表现出正能量与正电荷。狄拉克起先希望把它认作已知的质子。可是，同质量的要求不能迁就质子与电子的巨大质量差；关于湮灭过程的批评，又使稳定物质的解释出现困难。外尔的质量论证与塔姆、奥本海默等人的相关批评，不是同一项证明的几个可互换姓名。1931年的狄拉克自己把这些问题分别写了出来。

反电子的提出，因而包含放弃一种原先想保住的解释。

数学约束把他带到这里。别人的异议也在其中。纸上看似独自完成的一步，留下了共同工作的痕迹。

再看安德森的照片。1932年8月2日取得的那幅径迹，穿过六毫米厚的铅板。粒子穿板后损失能量，弯曲程度改变，这帮助判断运动方向；方向与磁场合在一起，才能辨认电荷的符号。径迹的长度等信息，还用来排除它只是质子的解释。最初的质量限制并非今天那样精确，论证却已经不只是猜一个正号。

照相机留下了痕迹。实验者使痕迹成为可以争论的证据。

理论与实验在这里相遇，不是一个发命令，另一个终于服从。

四分量的说法也需要留下条件。自由粒子在给定动量下的解，可以按正负能量分支与自旋分成相应的模式；不能因此把任意表示中的前两个分量永久贴作“电子”，后两个永久贴作“正电子”。分量、解与粒子解释，不是同一张分类表。

在现代量子场论中，正电子是相对于真空具有正能量的激发，不是实验室里游荡的负能量电子。反粒子的观念也不意味着把同一个电子方程无条件地套到所有粒子身上。有些粒子还是自己的反粒子，例如中性π介子；“镜子另一边必定另有一个不同的自己”，只是过于整齐的图画。

1955年的反质子发现、1956年的反中子工作，扩展了这条历史，却不能倒写成1928年方程已把一切做完。狄拉克在1931年、1933年继续讨论反质子，也承认把电子理论直接用于质子仍有困难。后来的实验需要自己的装置、判据与推论。

方程里有他起初没有看清的后果。那些符号是他写下的，却还需要回来重读。

排除法也在这里得到一个更严格的位置。若候选确已穷尽、前提成立、排除有效，剩下的答案才能被锁定。若候选表本来就不全，或者共同前提用错了，排除几个旧解释并不保证新解释必然是自然的真相。

数学结构没有绕过推理。人正是经由推理，逐步认出它不肯迁就自己的地方。

那个负号没有替他承担判断。

它使一项已经作出的判断，不能轻易结束。

八、桥头

狄拉克走来时，几乎没有声音。他在栏杆旁停下，拿出笔和一张纸。

波普尔先开口：“你的方程也可能错吗？”

狄拉克没有立即回答。

他把纸折平，才说：“推导可以检查。起点也可以。”

孔德翻着日历，手指停在牛顿所在的月份附近。他没有把笔抢过去。纸上出现了日历上尚未安排的新问题；他看了一会儿，也没有立即翻到下一页。

康德从稍远处走来。

“先于经验，”他问，“先于哪一次？”

狄拉克抬头看了他一眼，又把目光放回纸上。这一回，沉默没有被任何人宣布为赞成。

笔尖移动，一个负号留在纸上。

纸很轻。孔德伸手挡了一下风，没有替他按住那一行。

波普尔等着能被追问的下一步。康德没有把方程收进自己的范畴表。狄拉克也没有把美递给他们，当作不准提问的通行证。

桥面有极轻的嗡鸣。

方程可以先到。

它不能替世界回答。

写方程的人，还在那里。

I. The Quietest Man

There is a unit in the stories physicists tell that you will not find in the international system of measurement. One dirac: one word per hour.

It is a joke remembered about a colleague, not the result of recording and counting every hour of his life. It preserves something about his reticence. It also tempts us to believe that a single number has finished the job of describing a person.

We can enter through that joke without closing the door behind us.

A spoken answer may be postponed. A condition in a proof cannot simply be left out. Other people must be able to see what you used, how you moved from one step to another, and where you could not yet proceed. What Dirac left was not only silence. He left papers that other people could question line by line.

The Quantum Theory of the Electron reached the journal on 2 January 1928 and appeared on 1 February. When the equation reached its readers, the questions it brought were far from settled.

The electron in that paper was already the object of extensive investigation. Spectroscopy, work on spin, quantum theory and special relativity crowded the desk. Dirac did not pull a world out of a blank sheet because nature had supplied him with nothing. What had already been supplied could not yet be made to fit together.

An equation emerged. Its interpretation did not arrive complete beside it.

Negative-energy solutions remained troublesome. There would be a hole theory and an attempt to identify the holes with protons. In 1931 Dirac explicitly proposed a new particle with the electron's mass and the opposite electric charge: the anti-electron.

In 1932 Carl Anderson obtained experimental evidence for the positron in a cloud chamber. His 1933 paper presented not just a photograph but an apparatus and reasons for identifying what the photograph showed. Other explanations had to be considered and excluded.

The equation did arrive first.

Not before all experience. Before this new discovery. Between the two lay revised interpretations, other people's objections and work in another laboratory.

Four years were not an empty interval in which the world made its leisurely way to a desk to stamp a mathematician's answer correct.

II. Beauty Matters More Than Being Right?

In 1963 Dirac gave mathematical beauty a striking priority over immediate agreement with experiment. His example concerned difficulties in Schrödinger's earlier work and the later understanding of spin. He was making a claim about how research might advance, not issuing a certificate that beautiful ideas never fail.

Perhaps the claim matters most precisely where it offers no insurance.

You need not take a risk in admiring something already known to be right. The difficult choice comes earlier. Is this worth pursuing? Might a structure with an unresolved difficulty lead further than an account that fits everything neatly but offers nowhere else to go?

Dirac was not trying to arrange an existing equation more prettily. He sought a structure that would meet relativistic requirements while retaining the form of evolution required by quantum theory. In familiar modern notation, the free-electron equation can be written:

iℏ ∂ψ/∂t = (c α·p + βmc²)ψ

Momentum here is an operator, and ψ is not an ordinary one-component number. The first-order treatment of time, the demands of relativity and the algebraic relations imposed on the coefficients constrain the construction together. In Dirac's construction they lead to four-by-four matrices and a four-component wave function. Merely asking for linearity in time does not summon four compartments out of nature.

Beautiful does not necessarily mean less.

Sometimes it means being unable to cut away what seems surplus. You like one half of a construction, but it requires you to account for the other half as well. Attraction becomes an obligation: not “I want it, therefore it exists,” but “Having accepted these conditions, I cannot collect only the consequences that please me.”

The historian Helge Kragh records that Dirac wrote about physical laws and mathematical beauty on a Moscow blackboard in 1956. In chalk, a researcher set down a conviction on which he was willing to spend his time.

Even if the chalk were erased, the choice would remain. When a structure is beautiful but does not accommodate your interpretation, will you revise the interpretation? When it conflicts with dependable evidence, will you also be prepared to revise the structure?

Beauty can be a compass.

A compass cannot arrive on your behalf.

III. Where Is the Crack?

It is tempting to arrange the three men in a line: Comte supplies facts, Popper tests, Dirac sees beauty first. Their work does not divide so neatly.

Comte’s opening lecture already recognizes the reciprocal need for theory and observation. Without a question to begin with, even what deserves observation is difficult to determine.

Nor does Popper require a conjecture to be extracted from what has already been seen. His demarcation of science is not a declaration that poetry, mathematics and metaphysics are meaningless. Having mathematical reasons for a theory does not automatically remove its claims about nature from questions of conjecture and testing.

Dirac is therefore not an intruder from a territory that both men have categorically forbidden to exist.

The place worth stopping is the distance between two uses of “must.”

One means that, given these premises and valid operations, the result cannot be altered at will. The other means that nature must be this way. The first can be established mathematically. To carry it across to the second, we also need to know whether the premises and their interpretation apply.

A derivation may be less accommodating than the person who devised it. Its application does not acquire the world's signature merely because the derivation is rigorous.

For the same reason, “before observation” cannot simply be renamed “a priori knowledge confirmed a posteriori.” Being earlier than a particular test is a relation in time. The Kantian question of the a priori concerns grounds of knowledge. Someone formed by years of experiment and theoretical work may propose a new answer before it is tested without thereby stepping outside all experience.

This does not diminish beauty. It gives beauty a place where it need not pretend to know everything.

Beauty can participate in discovery. A relation becomes worth pursuing; a connection between requirements seems too important to sever casually; someone chooses to keep working where no ready answer is available. Such recognition is not decoration. Neither is it a substitute for the outcome of an investigation.

In this series' terms, knowing means being able to account for steps already taken. Not-knowing means refusing to replace an unfinished explanation with a declaration that nothing remains. Acknowledgment means the subject deciding how to take responsibility for the question encountered.

The equation does not make that decision for him. He decides to let what he has written become capable of correcting him.

IV. Dirac and Einstein

In his 1963 account, relativity changes the concepts and mathematical forms available to physics. Inheritance is not simply a matter of entering more numbers in an old table. Sometimes what can serve as a table must itself be reconsidered. Yet Dirac does not turn every requirement associated with Einstein into an untouchable bequest, whether in the interpretation of quantum theory or in deciding how mathematical form should serve a physical problem.

Here is a kind of proximity that does not require agreement.

What someone leaves you may be more than a set of formulas ready for further substitution. That person may change the questions you can ask. A revision that once seemed inconceivable becomes work worth attempting. Your later disagreement does not mean that this change never occurred.

Conversely, having been influenced does not commit you to remaining inside the same answer for the rest of your life.

Inheritance does not mean handing over one’s judgment. Dirac brought his own history into physics. At each particular problem he still had to decide what to take up, what to change and what to leave unresolved for the time being.

The connection between Dirac and Einstein can be left in a smaller, firmer place: one person's questions enter another person's work. In entering, they do not acquire immunity from being questioned themselves.

V. Work Inside the Silence

Silence is not a method of proof.

And equations do not bypass language. They are a particularly explicit form of expression, available for transcription, calculation and dispute. Why four components? Which algebraic relations? What interpretation of the negative-energy states? No one can obtain those answers merely by putting an ear to the paper.

“Listening to the equation” works as a metaphor. It means that you cannot listen only for what you want to hear.

When a derivation makes trouble, a researcher can calculate again, examine assumptions, seek another interpretation or set the matter aside. Each is an action, not the reception of an oracle through stillness.

At the beginning of his 1931 paper, Dirac describes mathematical advance as more than further calculation within fixed rules. New ideas can change the foundations being used. The search for an answer may alter the arrangement that originally determined which answers could appear.

The minus sign does not, therefore, always point toward one door opened in advance.

Choosing not to erase a difficulty does not mean that the difficulty has already chosen the only path for you. A remainder leaves room for another question. The impetus to continue, change direction or stop belongs to the subject's judgment and choice.

Silence may preserve a moment in which that choice is not yet made.

Do not rush to cover an unfamiliar structure with a familiar name. Do not promise a certainty you cannot answer for merely because someone is waiting. Let the question remain a question for a while. Then write down what you can account for.

Once it is written, someone else can enter.

That person need not share your temperament or everything you find beautiful. They can begin at a particular line and identify an error. Silence has not been violated at that moment. The work for which it made room has been allowed to continue.

VI. The People Still to Come

Qu Yuan, Barbara McClintock and Michael Polanyi will enter this series from different directions.

Not as three successive witnesses called to confirm Dirac.

Qu Yuan's poetry brings us before a beauty that cannot be redeemed for experimental success. Li Sao is not a particle prediction still awaiting the right detector. Its reader has to encounter a voice, a way of organizing experience, and the places where that encounter matters to them. Claims about the poem, including factual ones, remain open to challenge. But the poem does not first have to pass a physics examination to be permitted to exist as poetry.

Nor does the beauty of both a poem and an equation assign them identical evidential responsibilities. Dirac makes a physical claim about the electron. Having aesthetic judgment does not release him from that claim's obligations.

McClintock’s 1950 paper begins with experiments involving the short arm of maize chromosome 9. A previously rare phenomenon began recurring in the experimental cultures. She brought both a question and years of experience with the material to the experiment.

Some kinds of feeling grow inside prolonged observation. They may make a change previously treated as background worth following; they may alter the next planting, comparison or question. Noticing is not yet knowing the cause. Nor is it seeing an object under a microscope already wearing a label that says “transposable element.”

Polanyi offers another entrance: the rules that can be stated may not exhaust the knowing already at work.

A cyclist need not convert every adjustment of balance into a complete verbal command before making it. Yet riding well does not mean correctly explaining all the mechanics involved. Sensing that an equation deserves further work does not mean that its physical interpretation has already been established.

These distinctions do not expel the tacit part. They allow it to remain without impersonating something else.

The three roads do not merge here. A poetic voice, discernment in an experiment, a skill not fully articulated: each asks whether the knowing we recognize has been reduced to whatever can already be put easily into a table.

What cannot be said is not automatically deeper.

What can be said has not necessarily said everything.

VII. The Minus Sign

Back to the paper.

For a free particle, a familiar energy relation reads:

E = ±√(|p|²c² + m²c⁴)

The difficulty is not that negative numbers are universally forbidden in physics. Energy can be negative relative to a chosen zero. The specific problem here involves a branch extending downward without a lower bound, alongside the positive branch, and why electrons do not continually fall into lower states.

The 1928 paper does not calmly provide a home for every solution. Some were initially rejected, and an unresolved problem was acknowledged. Returning to it later does not require us to rewrite the earlier hesitation as unwavering certainty.

Hole theory was an attempt at an answer. Suppose negative-energy electron states are filled: a missing electron would behave as a hole with positive energy and positive charge. Dirac initially hoped to identify that hole with the known proton. But the equality of masses could not accommodate the enormous difference between proton and electron. Criticism concerning annihilation introduced further difficulties for stable matter. Weyl's mass argument and the work of Tamm, Oppenheimer and others on related problems are not interchangeable names attached to one proof. Dirac distinguished these issues in his own 1931 account.

The proposal of the anti-electron thus includes the abandonment of an interpretation he had wanted to retain.

Mathematical constraints brought him here. Other people’s objections helped too. A step that looks solitary on the page bears the marks of shared work.

Look again at Anderson's photograph, taken on 2 August 1932. The track crosses a six-millimetre lead plate. Energy loss in crossing changes the curvature, helping establish the direction of travel. Direction, together with the magnetic field, permits the sign of the charge to be determined. Information including the length of the track helps exclude an ordinary proton. The original mass bound was not a modern precision measurement, but the argument was already much more than guessing a plus sign.

A camera preserved a trace. Experimental reasoning made the trace into evidence that could be disputed.

Theory and experiment meet here. One is not giving orders while the other finally submits.

The four-component description also needs its conditions. For a free particle of specified momentum, solutions can be organized by energy branch and spin. That does not make the first two components in every representation permanently “electrons” and the other two permanently “positrons.” Components, solutions and particle interpretations are not the same classification.

In modern quantum field theory, a positron is a positive-energy excitation relative to the vacuum, not a negative-energy electron wandering through a laboratory. Nor does the antiparticle concept mean that the identical electron equation applies without qualification to every particle. Some particles are their own antiparticles—the neutral pion, for example. A mirror that must always contain a separate other self is too tidy a picture.

The antiproton discovery in 1955 and antineutron work in 1956 extended the history; they cannot be retroactively packed into a claim that the 1928 equation had already completed it. Dirac continued discussing antiprotons in 1931 and 1933 and acknowledged difficulties in directly applying the electron theory to protons. Later experiments needed their own apparatus, criteria and inferences.

The equation contained consequences he had not initially seen. He had written those symbols himself. He still had to return and read them again.

Elimination, too, finds a more exact place. An answer can be pinned down if the candidates really are exhaustive, the premises hold and the eliminations are valid. An incomplete list or a mistaken common assumption changes the situation. Excluding several old interpretations cannot by itself guarantee that the new one is nature's truth.

Mathematical structure has not bypassed reasoning. Through reasoning, people gradually recognize the places where it refuses to accommodate them.

The minus sign did not take responsibility for his judgment.

It made a judgment already undertaken harder to bring to an easy close.

VIII. The Bridgehead

Dirac arrives almost without sound. He stops beside the railing and takes out a pen and a sheet of paper.

Popper speaks first. “Could your equation be wrong too?”

Dirac does not immediately answer.

He flattens the paper. “The derivation can be checked. So can the starting point.”

Comte turns the pages of his calendar. His finger rests near Newton's month. He does not take the pen. A question not yet assigned a place in the calendar appears on the sheet. He looks at it for a while without immediately turning another page.

Kant approaches from a little further away.

“Before experience,” he asks. “Before which experience?”

Dirac looks up at him and then down at the paper. This time no one declares the silence to be assent.

The pen moves. A minus sign remains on the sheet.

The paper is light. Comte raises a hand against the wind without pinning down the line Dirac has written.

Popper waits for the next step that can be questioned. Kant does not file the equation inside his own table of categories. Dirac does not hand them beauty as a pass forbidding questions.

A very faint humming comes from the bridge.

The equation can arrive first.

It cannot answer for the world.

The person writing it is still there.
