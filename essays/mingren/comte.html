孔德：把缝隙灌满水泥的人

Comte: The Man Who Grouted the Cracks

Han Qin (秦汉)

一、那本日历

1849年，奥古斯特·孔德出版了一本日历。1852年，已经出了第四版。

它把一年分成十三个月，每月四周，每周七天。十三乘二十八，三百六十四。余下的一天另作纪念日，闰年再加一天，不给它们安排星期名称。日、周、月，各有自己的位置。人的记忆也可以如此安排吗？

月份的名字不是一月、二月，而是摩西、荷马、亚里士多德这样的名字。圣保罗也在里面。孔德没有把宗教人物全赶出去；他改变的是纪念他们的理由：不再请他们担保超自然的真理，而是把他们放进他所编排的人类往事。诗人、思想者、科学家与宗教人物，一起进入时间的格子。

有些名字占一个月，有些占一周，有些占一天。一个人在这张表上有多大，要由排表的人决定。

这张表主要面向西方历史。孔德把对具体人物的纪念看成一项过渡安排，也希望日历改革逐渐获得接受，而不是由世俗权力强行推广。连这位排表的人，也为表格留下了临时的位置。

所以，问题不是一个人竟然制作日历。

我们也正在编排一百零八种人生。每放进一个名字，就有无数名字留在外面。一本日历可以帮助记忆，一个系列可以邀请相遇。只要没有把选出的名单说成人类应当成为的全部。

问题在更远的地方：一个承认这份名单只是过渡的人，为什么又相信，人类认识世界的道路已经有了最终的形式？

日历还可以翻页。

规定人类怎样长大的那一页，还可以改吗？

二、他在堵什么

孔德1798年出生于蒙彼利埃，1814年进入巴黎综合理工学院。1817年开始与圣西蒙共事，1824年分道扬镳。早在1822年，他已经把科学工作与社会、道德生活的重新组织连在一起。这个计划并不是晚年突然多出来的附录。

他没有获得自己期望的教授席位。综合理工学院的辅导教师、入学考试考官，是他做过、后来又失去的工作。朋友与支持者的资助帮助他继续写下去。想替知识安排一个稳定位置的人，自己的生活却屡次失去支点。

1830年至1842年，六卷《实证哲学教程》陆续出版。十二年。书从数学走向天文学、物理学、化学、生物学与社会学，试图把知识的各个部分连成一幅可以理解的图。

图上最有名的路线，是三阶段法则。

在孔德的叙述中，人先以神意和超自然行动者解释世界，再用抽象实体或本质替代它们，最后转向现象之间可以研究的关系与规律。这是他提出的人类思想发展学说，不是每一种文化已经共同签字确认的年表。他也不认为各门知识在同一天进入同一阶段。

他主要要求放下的是第一因、目的因与绝对本质，不是禁止科学再问任何一种“为什么”。解释雷雨怎样形成，与追问宇宙为什么非如此不可，不会因为都用了一个“为什么”，就自动变成同一类问题。

这项限制有力量。遇见不能回答的问题，承认方法的边界，比编造一个听起来完整的答案诚实。

然而，孔德不只说：我们目前只能从这里工作。

他还把实证状态称为理性发展的最终状态。与此同时，他又说，我们的知识始终相对于人的构造、处境与历史；甚至有些实在可能永远超出我们的认识。

这就不是“他以为自己已经知道一切”那么简单了。

他允许地图继续修改，却相信制图的最高方向已经确定。

缝隙还在。我们要问的是：有没有一种缝隙，被预先取消了作为问题的资格？

三、知识只有一种合法形式

《教程》第一讲已经承认，观察需要理论引导，理论又必须面对事实。没有某种联系，事实甚至不能被组织成观察。1844年的《论实证精神》也没有赶走想象力；它反对的是想象不再对现实负责，不是思想只能等待资料到齐。

他的科学不是仓库。

仓库之外仍然有一道门槛。《论实证精神》第十二节提出：不能归结为某个特殊或一般事实的命题，不具有真实、可理解的意义。这道门槛不只要求一句话拿出根据。它还决定，什么样的话才有资格被听见。

张力正在这里。

知识有限，知识相对，理论可以变化；可什么样的话有资格成为知识，却似乎已经获得最后的安排。

一个方法怎样检查自己划出的辖域？它是否允许别人提出：问题也许不在答案太少，而在门槛本身？这并不自动证明孔德自相矛盾。他可以把三阶段法则辩护为历史研究，把实证原则辩护为经过比较的方法选择。辩护仍然需要展开，不能由“最终”两个字替它完成。

本系列曾用三幅图来记住三种危险：柏拉图的墙，亚里士多德的地板，孔德的水泥。这是我们的构图，不是三部哲学史的缩印。图画一旦不许人物反驳它，也会成为它正在批评的东西。

孔德排列六门基本科学，并不是说社会学只需把物理公式放大，也不是宣布所有未知都已填完。越复杂的对象，需要越具体的方法；《教程》还警告，不要指望把全部现象强行归入一条总规律。

同样，他对内省的批评针对的是一个特定难题：思考中的心智能否同时把自己当作观察对象？我们可以质疑他的处理，不能据此判他没有反省能力。研究思想的历史与方法，本来就是他工作的一部分。

因此，水泥不是事实，不是分类，也不是理论。

水泥是某一套安排从“这样工作有用”，滑向“除此之外，再没有需要认真提出的问题”的那一刻。

这个比喻只在这一刻成立。

四、克洛蒂尔德

1826年，孔德经历严重的健康危机并接受治疗。1842年，他与1825年结婚的卡罗琳·马桑分居。他试着重新安排人类的共同生活，自己的日子也在变化之中。

1844年，他遇见克洛蒂尔德·德沃。后来一年多的交往，在他的生活中留下了很深的痕迹。1846年4月5日，她去世。

但先不要急着让她成为孔德的圣像。

她写作，发表过《露西》。她希望有自己的工作和生活。往来书信中，她要求友谊，也明确拒绝过孔德的恋爱与共同生活安排。她不是一个只有“被爱”这一项动作的人。

孔德的感情可以是真诚的。她的边界也可以同样真实。

承认其中一件事，不必取消另一件。

她去世后，孔德对她的纪念进入了自己的思想与仪式。他后来的《实证政治体系》四卷在1851至1854年出版，《实证主义教理问答》出版于1852年。人类宗教试图把思想、情感、行动与共同生活组织在一起。

这份哀悼进入了一个早已开始的计划。孔德一直关心情感如何参与道德与社会生活；克洛蒂尔德的死，使这些问题有了一个他再也不能当面询问的人。

在另一段关系里，穆勒逐渐与他拉开距离。1841年至1846年末的通信，留下了关于女性角色、资助关系与思想方向的分歧。到1865年，穆勒又用一部专书回看孔德。亲近没有使分歧消失，分歧也没有使对方变成一个无须再读的人。

真正使这段故事停不下来的，是另一个问题。

纪念一个人，何时开始替她说话？把她放到一套体系最明亮的位置，会不会也让她原来不同意的地方，越来越难被听见？

一个人可以进入别人的思想。

她留下的那封拒绝，也应当一起进去。

五、模式匹配的天花板

“模式匹配”在这里指一种被抽出来的方法：从既有资料中辨认关系，改善模型，获得更可靠的预测。它可以做很多事。这不是孔德脑内活动的诊断书，也不是把他的全部哲学压缩成某一种机器。

困难是：一个系统可以不断提高给定问题的成绩，却不曾重新问过，为什么只做这一道题。

模型错了，可以修模型。题目选错了呢？把所有反对意见都归成“资料不足”，也许会越修越牢固地回到原来的题目。

沿本系列的Self-as-an-End读法，需要追问的不只是认知、更多认知与认知方向，还包括这个方向能否被质问。余项指向尚未被一套构造说尽的部分，为继续发问留下位置；它不是一个替主体选择方向、逼人行动的发动机。动力仍在人的判断与选择。

孔德并非没有认识到理论需要修正。我们真正向他提出的，是同一个更窄的问题：假如三阶段法则本身成了那道被规定好的题目呢？“最终状态”是否也要面对可能改变它的质问？

把“先验”与“后验”放进来，还需要小心。一个猜想先于某次实验提出，不因此就是康德意义上的先验知识。它可能来自以前的经验、数学工作、类比，甚至错误。时间上的先后，不能直接替代认识论上的根据。

下一篇的波普尔会把另一件事推到前面：理论愿意冒什么失败的风险？一个说法无论发生什么都能解释过去，与一个提前说出什么情况会使它遇到困难的说法，不是一回事。

但可证伪性是科学划界问题，不是把科学之外的思想一律宣布无意义。一次不符预期的观察，也需要检查仪器、辅助假设与观察本身，不是自动处决理论的按钮。

这里没有“猜想万能，事实退位”。猜想要接受检验，检验也要接受检查。

门可以有。问题在于，门上的牌子能否被重新读、重新问。

六、他做对了一件事

孔德留下的一个重要要求是：不要让解释只是给未知重新起名字。

叫出一个本质，并不等于说明它怎样起作用。画出一个体系，也不能使体系免于面对它声称说明的世界。

这个要求不是由孔德独自发明的。他自己就把工作放在培根、笛卡尔、伽利略等人的接续中。现代科学也没有等待某一个人先清空地面，才获得站立的资格。

可接续仍是一种工作。把分散的学科连起来，让专门知识不至于忘记别的知识，把观察与预见接在一起，这些事情不因我们质问他的最终方向就应一笔勾销。

狄拉克的方程可以帮助我们看清另一面。在1933年的诺贝尔演讲中，他回顾理论如何遇到困难，又怎样引向关于正电子的新后果。不是一堆资料自己从抽屉里长出了答案，也不是方程一旦美丽，现实就必须照办。理论走到尚待检验之处；实验决定相关预言能否站住。

麦克林托克则让人看见观察里面的工作。她在1983年的演讲中回顾，自己怎样从长期的玉米实验出发，注意到原来问题没有预料的变化，随后沿着它继续实验。看见异常并不是结束。要知道它值得追踪，还要重新安排工作。

凯勒为她写的传记，题为《对有机体的感觉》。这个题目里有漫长的时间：种植、比较、等待，直到别人当作背景的地方，开始向她显出差别。

波兰尼谈默会认识时，把明说的规则不能穷尽实际判断这件事提了出来。知道怎么做，不总能写成一份别人照着勾选就一定成功的清单。

这不是给随意的感觉发通行证。一个念头怎样来到，与我们有什么理由接受它，是相关却不能互相代替的问题。手艺可以超出操作说明，实验却仍可能说明这一次做错了。

至于屈原，诗让读者听见一种不能被指标替代的经验，不等于诗句因此变成新的物理定律。这是本系列准备展开的另一条阅读道路，不需要假装它与实验室争夺同一张考卷。

我们不是为了保留缝隙，就把地板拆掉。

要能站住，也要能发现自己站在什么上面。

七、三种封闭

现在回到墙、地板与水泥。

一幅图把答案放在远处，仿佛那里已经完成。一幅图把眼前的东西分好位置，仿佛位置就是东西的全部。第三幅图先规定什么样的问题算成熟，剩下的问题便不再需要回答。

这是三种可供检查的封闭，不是给三位哲学家颁发的终身判词。柏拉图、亚里士多德与孔德都比这三件道具复杂。我们也会把自己的道具用得太顺手，以至于再看不见人。

后面的名字，不是来排一条谁比谁更高的楼梯。

波普尔带来猜想愿意承受的风险。狄拉克带来理论走到观测前面的那一步。屈原让我们问，认识是否只剩可以交卷的命题。费希特让提问者自己进入问题。麦克林托克让熟练的眼睛跟着意外重新工作。西蒙娜·薇依让我们停下来问，注意一个人时，有多少自己的需要盖在了上面。龙树让概念之间看似独立的支点接受追问。波兰尼则把明说不尽的实际判断带回来。

这些是本系列将分别展开的会面，不是一条已经证实的影响链，更不是八个人共同签署的宣言。他们之间也会互相反对。

不能把“会互相反对”当作需要消除的噪声。只挑他们能证明我们的话，那不过是另一本排好了的日历。

孔德对这本系列的用处，也正在于他不是一块可以随手敲碎的薄墙。他关于理论、想象、相对知识和学科差异的论述，会反过来纠正我们对他的概括。承认这些，才有资格继续问那两个字：最终。

一条道路可以走得很远。

走得远，是否就获得了替所有道路宣布终点的权利？

这个问题也朝着Self-as-an-End回来。

八、桥头

孔德到了桥头。

他带着日历。

伽利略和牛顿站在桥上。桥下的水声里，还有莎士比亚。日历里熟悉的名字，已经到了格子外面。孔德低头看一眼纸，再抬头。

一个名字占多少格，原来并不告诉你，这个人会向哪个方向走。

水声没有按照格子的宽度缩小。伽利略没有站到日期指定的位置。牛顿靠着栏杆，两手空着。

亚里士多德看见他的日历，笑了一下。分类确实可以帮助人开始谈话。没有人要把纸夺走。

康德站得稍远。孔德没有立刻走过去。这里没有一句替两个世纪取消分歧的握手词。

他从口袋里取出那张画像。

克洛蒂尔德。

在日历上，他可以为一个名字安排位置。在记忆里，他也可以反复回到同一天。但她曾经写下的意愿，不会因为后来有了纪念，便变成另一种意愿。

他看得久了一点。

没有人替他解释这多出来的一点时间。

风吹过来，日历翻了一页。

孔德伸手把它按住。

按得很用力。

I. The Calendar

In 1849, Auguste Comte published a calendar. By 1852 it had reached its fourth edition.

Thirteen months. Four weeks to a month. Seven days to a week. That made 364 days. An additional commemorative day stood outside the weekly cycle; in a leap year there would be two. Days, weeks, months: each had a place. Could human memory be arranged as neatly?

The months were named not January and February but Moses, Homer, Aristotle. Saint Paul was there too. Comte had not cleared the calendar of religious figures. He changed the grounds on which they were remembered: no longer as guarantors of supernatural truth, but as participants in the human past he was arranging. Poets, thinkers, scientists and religious figures entered the same architecture of time.

Some names received a month, others a week, others a day. How much room a person occupied depended on the person drawing up the table.

The calendar principally concerned Western history. Comte described the commemoration of particular people as transitional and envisaged gradual acceptance of the reform, not its imposition by temporal authority. Even its maker left this arrangement a provisional place.

Making a calendar is not, by itself, the problem.

We are arranging 108 lives. Every name admitted leaves innumerable names outside. A calendar can help us remember; a series can invite encounters. Neither need claim that the chosen company exhausts what humanity can be.

The difficulty lies further along. Why did someone who regarded this particular arrangement as transitional also regard the positive approach to knowledge as the final form of intellectual development?

The calendar can turn another page.

Can the page that tells humanity how to grow up be rewritten?

II. What He Was Sealing

Born in Montpellier in 1798, Comte entered the École polytechnique in 1814. He began working with Saint-Simon in 1817; they separated in 1824. As early as 1822, he was linking scientific work to the reorganization of social and moral life. That ambition was not an appendix unexpectedly added in old age.

He never obtained the professorial chair he wanted. At the École polytechnique he worked as a tutor and an admissions examiner, then lost those posts. Friends and supporters helped him keep writing. The man seeking a stable arrangement for knowledge repeatedly lost the supports of his own daily life.

The six volumes of the Course of Positive Philosophy appeared between 1830 and 1842. Twelve years. From mathematics through astronomy, physics, chemistry and biology to sociology, they tried to draw an intelligible map of the relations among kinds of knowledge.

Its most famous route was the law of three stages.

In Comte's account, supernatural agents first explain the world. Abstract entities and essences then take their place. Finally, inquiry turns to relations and laws among phenomena. This is his thesis about intellectual development, not a chronology jointly ratified by every culture. Nor did he think every science crossed the same threshold on the same day.

What he chiefly set aside were first causes, final causes and absolute essences. He did not forbid every scientific question beginning with “why.” Explaining how a storm develops and asking why the universe must exist in precisely this form do not become the same inquiry merely because the same word introduces them.

There is strength in that restraint. Acknowledging a method's limits is more honest than inventing an explanation that merely sounds complete.

But Comte did not only say: this is where we can work for now.

He called the positive state the final condition of rational development. At the same time, he insisted that knowledge remains relative to human constitution, situation and history. Some realities might remain beyond our knowledge altogether.

The difficulty is therefore not that he thought he knew everything.

He allowed the map to change while treating its governing orientation as settled.

The cracks remained. The question is whether some were denied, in advance, the standing of questions.

III. One Legitimate Form

The opening lecture of the Course already acknowledges that observation needs theoretical guidance, while theory must answer to facts. Without some connecting ideas, facts could not even be organized into observations. The 1844 Discourse on the Positive Spirit does not expel imagination either. It objects to imagination ceasing to answer to reality, not to thought starting work before all the evidence is in.

His science is not a warehouse.

Outside the warehouse there is still a threshold. Section 12 of the Discourse on the Positive Spirit denies real, intelligible meaning to a proposition that cannot be reduced to a particular or general fact. The threshold does more than ask a statement for its grounds. It decides which statements qualify to be heard.

Here is the tension.

Knowledge is limited and relative. Theories can change. Yet the eligibility of statements for knowledge appears to have received a final arrangement.

How does a method examine its own jurisdiction? Can it hear the objection that the difficulty may lie not in too few answers but in the threshold itself? This does not automatically convict Comte of contradiction. He can defend the three-stage law as historical research and positive principles as a comparative choice of method. But that defence has to be made. The word “final” cannot make it for him.

This series has used three images for three dangers: Plato's wall, Aristotle's floor, Comte's grout. They are our composition, not miniature editions of three histories of philosophy. Once an image refuses to let its subject answer back, it can become what it was meant to criticize.

Comte's arrangement of six fundamental sciences does not say that sociology is physics enlarged. Nor does it announce that every unknown has been filled in. More complex objects demand more specific methods. The Course also warns against forcing every phenomenon under a single universal law.

Likewise, his objection to introspection addresses a particular problem: can an intellect engaged in thinking simultaneously observe itself thinking? We can question his response without declaring him incapable of reflection. Investigating the history and methods of thought was part of his work.

The grout, then, is not facts. It is not classification. It is not theory.

It is the moment an arrangement slips from “working this way is useful” to “outside this arrangement there are no questions worth taking seriously.”

Only at that moment does the image hold.

IV. Clotilde

In 1826 Comte suffered a serious health crisis and received treatment. In 1842 he separated from Caroline Massin, whom he had married in 1825. As he tried to reorganize humanity’s common life, his own life was changing too.

In 1844 he met Clotilde de Vaux. Their closer relationship over the following period left a deep mark on his life. She died on 5 April 1846.

Before she becomes his icon, let her be a person.

She wrote and published Lucie. She wanted work and a life of her own. In their correspondence she asked for friendship and rejected his proposals for a romantic relationship and a shared domestic life. Being loved was not her only possible action.

Comte's affection could be sincere. Her boundary could be equally real.

Accepting either fact need not cancel the other.

After her death, his commemoration of her entered his thought and ritual. The four volumes of the System of Positive Polity appeared in 1851–1854; the Positivist Catechism in 1852. The Religion of Humanity sought to bring thought, feeling, action and common life into an organized relationship.

The mourning entered a project already under way. Comte had long been concerned with feeling’s place in moral and social life. Clotilde’s death gave those questions the presence of someone he could no longer ask in person.

In another relationship, Mill gradually drew away. Their correspondence, from 1841 to late 1846, records disagreements over women’s roles, financial support and the direction of Comte’s thought. In 1865 Mill returned to him in a book. Proximity had not erased disagreement; disagreement had not made Comte someone no longer worth reading.

What keeps this story open is a different question.

When does remembering someone begin to mean speaking for her? Can placing her in the brightest position in a system make the places where she disagreed harder to hear?

A person may enter another person's thought.

The letter in which she said no should enter with her.

V. The Ceiling of Pattern Matching

“Pattern matching” here names an isolated procedure: finding relations in available material, refining a model and improving predictions. It can accomplish a great deal. It is not a diagnosis of Comte's mental activity, nor a reduction of his philosophy to a particular machine.

The difficulty is that a system can keep improving its performance on an assigned problem without ever asking why it is doing only that problem.

A faulty model can be repaired. What about a badly chosen question? Treat every objection as insufficient data, and each repair may return you more securely to the same assignment.

In this series' Self-as-an-End reading, the issue includes cognition, further cognition and cognitive direction, but also the exposure of that direction to questioning. Remainder names what a construction has not exhausted. It leaves a place from which inquiry can continue; it is not an engine that selects a direction or compels action on a subject's behalf. Judgment and choice remain with the person.

Comte was not unaware that theories require correction. Our narrower question concerns the three-stage law itself. What happens if that law becomes the assigned problem? Must the “final state” also face questions capable of changing it?

Introducing “a priori” and “a posteriori” requires another distinction. A conjecture proposed before a particular experiment is not, for that reason, a priori knowledge in Kant's sense. It may come from earlier experience, mathematics, analogy or error. Temporal precedence cannot substitute for an account of epistemic grounds.

Popper, in the next essay, will put another question in the foreground: what risk of failure will a theory accept? A claim that can accommodate anything after it happens differs from one that states beforehand what would put it in difficulty.

But falsifiability is a criterion of scientific demarcation, not a declaration that everything outside science is meaningless. Nor is an unexpected observation an automatic execution button for a theory. Instruments, auxiliary assumptions and the observation itself may require examination.

Conjecture does not become sovereign while evidence retires. Conjectures face tests; tests themselves face scrutiny.

A door can be necessary. The sign on it must remain open to reading and questioning.

VI. What He Got Right

One important demand remains: an explanation should do more than give the unknown a new name.

Naming an essence does not explain how anything works. Drawing a system does not release it from answering to the world it claims to describe.

Comte did not invent that demand alone. He placed his work in succession to Bacon, Descartes and Galileo, among others. Modern science did not wait for a single person to clear a floor before it acquired somewhere to stand.

Still, carrying work forward is work. Connecting scattered sciences, preventing specialized knowledge from forgetting other knowledge, joining observation to foresight: questioning Comte's final destination need not erase these efforts.

Dirac's equations offer another angle. In his 1933 Nobel lecture he described theoretical difficulties and the route to new consequences concerning the positron. The answer did not simply grow out of a drawerful of data. Nor did the beauty of an equation oblige reality to comply. Theory reached into territory awaiting tests; experiment determined whether the relevant predictions could stand.

McClintock brings into view the work within observation. In her 1983 lecture she recalled how long-running maize experiments led her to changes not anticipated by the original question, and how she followed them with further experiments. Seeing the anomaly was not the end. Recognizing that it deserved pursuit meant rearranging the work.

Keller called her biography A Feeling for the Organism. There is a long stretch of time inside that title: planting, comparing, waiting, until differences emerge where another observer might see only background.

Polanyi's account of tacit knowing brings forward something explicit rules do not exhaust: practical judgment. Knowing how is not always convertible into a checklist that guarantees success to anyone who follows it.

This is no licence for arbitrary feeling. How an idea arrives and what reasons we have to accept it are related questions, not interchangeable ones. Craft can exceed the instructions; an experiment can still show that this particular attempt was mistaken.

Qu Yuan belongs to another path this series will explore. A poem can make an experience audible that a metric does not replace. It does not thereby become a new law of physics. We need not pretend that poetry and the laboratory are competing to complete the same examination paper.

Preserving cracks does not require demolishing the floor.

We need somewhere to stand—and the ability to discover what we are standing on.

VII. Three Closures

Return to wall, floor and grout.

One picture places the answer in the distance, as though it were already complete. Another assigns everything a position, as though its position exhausted the thing. A third first defines which questions count as mature; those left over no longer require an answer.

These are three closures to investigate, not life sentences passed on three philosophers. Plato, Aristotle and Comte are more complicated than these props. Our own familiarity with a prop can also make the person behind it disappear.

The names ahead do not form a staircase on which one thinker stands above another.

Popper brings the risk a conjecture is prepared to bear. Dirac brings the step theory takes ahead of observation. Qu Yuan asks whether knowing has been reduced to propositions that can be submitted for marking. Fichte brings the questioner into the question. McClintock lets a trained eye follow the unexpected into new work. Simone Weil asks how much of our own need overlays our attention to someone else. Nāgārjuna puts seemingly independent conceptual supports in question. Polanyi returns us to judgments that cannot be fully stated.

These are proposed encounters for this series, not an established chain of influence or a declaration signed by all eight. They can disagree with one another.

Their disagreements are not noise to be removed. Selecting only what confirms us would amount to arranging another calendar in advance.

Comte matters here precisely because he is not a thin wall we can casually knock down. His discussions of theory, imagination, relative knowledge and differences between sciences can correct our account of him. Only after acknowledging that correction can we return to the word “final.”

A road may go a long way.

Does going a long way confer the right to announce the destination of every road?

That question returns to Self-as-an-End as well.

VIII. The Bridgehead

Comte arrives at the bridgehead.

He has brought the calendar.

Galileo and Newton stand on the bridge. Shakespeare is still in the sound of the water below. Familiar names have moved beyond the calendar’s squares. Comte looks down at the paper, then up.

Knowing how many squares a name occupies turns out not to tell him which way the person will walk.

The water has not narrowed its voice to fit a square. Galileo has not taken the position assigned by a date. Newton leans against the railing, his hands empty.

Aristotle sees the calendar and smiles a little. Classification really can help a conversation begin. Nobody tries to take the paper away.

Kant stands further off. Comte does not approach him immediately. There is no handshake formula here that cancels disagreements across the centuries.

He takes the portrait from his pocket.

Clotilde.

On a calendar he can give a name a position. In memory he can return again and again to the same day. But a wish she once expressed will not become a different wish because commemoration follows it.

He looks a little longer.

Nobody explains that additional interval for him.

The wind catches the calendar and turns a page.

Comte puts his hand on it.

He presses hard.
