爱因斯坦，上帝不掷骰子

Einstein, God Does Not Play Dice

Han Qin (秦汉) · March 2026

一、专利局

1905年。爱因斯坦26岁。他不是教授。他是伯尔尼专利局的三级技术审查员。

这一年，他发表了四篇重要论文。后来，人们把这一年称为“奇迹年”。

第一篇提出光量子的设想，用一份一份的能量解释光电效应。光的波动现象没有因此消失，但连续的波已不足以说尽光。他后来获得1921年度诺贝尔物理学奖，获奖理由特别指出的，是光电效应定律。

第二篇研究布朗运动——悬浮在液体中的小颗粒不停抖动，背后是看不见的分子热运动。这让原子、分子不再只是一种方便的假设，而有了可以测量的后果。

第三篇是狭义相对论——在惯性参考系之间，真空光速不变，时间间隔和长度却不再各自绝对。我们原以为所有人共用的那只钟，第一次显出了裂缝。

第四篇把质量和能量连在一起。后来写成E=mc²的关系说，一个静止物体也有与其质量相应的能量。看似分属两栏的东西，原来不是互不相干。

四篇论文。一个专利局审查员。一年。

这不是朝牛顿的一座房子凿四个相同的洞。光量子触到了经典电磁学的边界；布朗运动把分子带进了可检验的世界；相对论重问空间和时间；质能关系重问物质的账本。他凿的是几处被当成地基、不再追问的地方。

不是在既有答案上修修补补。是把问题重新打开。

二、牛顿的绝对时空

牛顿的世界像这样：空间是固定的舞台，时间是匀速走动的钟。物体在舞台上移动，钟在背后均匀地走。舞台不参与剧情，钟不因谁的遭遇而快慢。

这个框架支撑了两百多年的物理学。行星、苹果、潮汐，都进入同一套计算。它是人类最成功的构之一。

康德则把空间和时间追问为经验何以可能的先天形式。那不是简单地把牛顿的外在容器搬进人的头脑；但在那个思想世界里，时空也已深到不像一项可以重新试验的假设。

爱因斯坦把问题重新放到了钟与尺之间。

彼此匀速运动的观察者，不一定同意两件事是否同时发生；他们比较钟和尺，会得到不同的时间间隔和长度。这不是谁看错了，也不是宇宙暗藏一个真正静止的人。参考系变了，测量的关系跟着变。

这种差别不是纸上的游戏。GPS必须处理运动和引力对钟的不同影响——既用到狭义相对论，也用到广义相对论。手机找到街角的路，背后有一只不再绝对的钟。

十年后，1915年，他又走了一步。

广义相对论把引力写成时空几何。物质和能量参与决定时空的曲率，时空的几何又规定自由落体怎样行进。舞台不再只是容纳演员，它也进入剧情。

在这种描述里，地球绕太阳，不必再想成一根看不见的绳子把它拉住。它沿着太阳周围弯曲时空中的自然路径前行。自由下落的苹果，也沿着这样的测地线走。

不是把一切力取消。是把引力换了一种语言。

这是物理学里一次极深的凿：看似必须由“拉力”解释的东西，可以由几何来解释；看似独立于世界的舞台，成为世界的一部分。

牛顿没有因此变成废墟。在低速、弱引力的范围内，他的计算仍然好用。但那座房子不再等于整个世界。

三、构

爱因斯坦不只凿。他也构。

广义相对论不只说“牛顿不够”。它给出场方程，把时空几何与物质、能量联系起来。几行符号，承担了一个新世界的重量。

这个世界允许引力波，也允许黑洞。1916年爱因斯坦研究了引力波；2015年，LIGO第一次直接探测到来自双黑洞并合的波纹。在理论预言之后近一个世纪，一次极轻的颤动抵达仪器。

方程还允许膨胀的宇宙。弗里德曼和勒梅特走出了这条路，后来的星系观测使它有了经验支撑；1929年哈勃的工作是其中一块重要的石头。爱因斯坦起初偏爱静态宇宙。他写出的构，已经能走到作者没有先走到的地方。

水星轨道、光的偏折、钟的快慢、引力波：这套理论经受了一次又一次严厉检验。不是每一个可能的预言都已验证，也不是疑问已经用尽。它的成功仍然惊人。

它优雅，也精确。在引力的许多领域，它是我们手里最好的描述。

康德追问经验的条件，构成三大批判。爱因斯坦追问钟、尺与引力，构成相对论。这里可以看见同一种动作：凿开继承的秩序，再承担建造的工作。

但哲学论证不是物理实验。两个人可以在这篇文章里相照，不能由一方的成败替另一方盖章。

也都可以继续被追问。本系列曾问康德：自然与自由之间，生命被放在哪里？在爱因斯坦这里，问题变成：当强引力和量子现象不能再分开处理时，这座时空的构还需要什么？

门外站着的，不只是反对者。还有他自己帮助带进来的量子。

四、上帝不掷骰子

二十世纪物理学常被画成两根柱子：广义相对论处理引力，量子理论处理物质和辐射的量子行为。“大”和“小”是入门的路标，不是两块永不相交的领地。

真正棘手的地方，是两者都不能退场的时候。黑洞深处、宇宙极早期，我们还没有一套得到充分验证的量子引力理论。

广义相对论使用连续的时空。量子力学包含离散的某些测量结果和概率，却并不因此宣布时空必定由颗粒砌成。两根柱子的张力，比“光滑对颗粒”更深。

而爱因斯坦与量子力学的争论，也不只是两座理论争地盘。他是量子论的开创者之一；他承认它的成功，却追问它是否已经说尽物理实在。

一个粒子可以处于叠加态。叠加不是“没有状态”；是这个状态未必给你所问的每一种量都预先指定一个确定结果。通常的量子规则给出各种结果的概率。

算得准，是否就已经解释到底？

1926年，他写信给玻恩，用一句后来变得极有名的话表达疑虑：“上帝不掷骰子。”那不是神学实验，而是他对自然秩序的一种信念。

1935年，他与波多尔斯基、罗森把问题写成EPR论文：若能不扰动远处系统而确定地预言某个量，理论是否应承认相应的实在？量子描述是否遗漏了什么？

争论的核心是完备性、物理实在，以及相隔遥远的系统怎样保持各自的独立。不是爱因斯坦看见概率就关上眼睛。

1964年，贝尔把一部分争论变成了可以检验的不等式。在局域性、测量选择与隐变量统计独立等条件下，一类隐变量解释受到明确限制；量子理论却预言可以越过这个界限。

实验一次又一次越过了它。受打击的是这些条件能够同时成立的那幅世界图景。爱因斯坦所珍爱的局域实在观，遇到了他没有活着看到的严厉挑战。

但“所有隐变量都不存在”，不是这个结果。玻姆式理论保留决定性的演化，付出的代价是非局域性。骰子不是一个实验便能替全部解释投完的。

爱因斯坦没有赢得他希望的答案。他的问题却没有白问。今天对纠缠的研究，仍从这道他坚持不让人绕过去的门经过。

“上帝掷骰子”可以是一句漂亮的回话。

它不能代替说明：我们检验了什么，又放弃了什么。

五、凿向自己的构？

爱因斯坦凿开了绝对时空，把引力重新写成几何。他当然是二十世纪最伟大的凿的人之一。

那么，凿子转向自己时呢？

广义相对论在适当的初值条件和适用范围内，给出确定性的演化。它容纳的秩序，与通常量子理论中单次测量的概率性，很不一样。

爱因斯坦更愿意寻找一个深层的、统一的描述，而不把现有的概率规则当作终点。这里确有一种紧张：开过世界的人，也会忠于自己相信世界应有的样子。

可是，他并没有把广义相对论奉为完成了的最后一句。他知道它不足以包办物质和量子，晚年的统一场研究本身就说明，他仍在寻找另一座构。

问题于是比“能凿别人，不能凿自己”更难。什么时候，忠于一条线索是在坚持研究？什么时候，它开始替尚未到来的证据决定答案？

在普林斯顿，哥德尔常和他一起散步。两个人说着德语，走过研究所与住宅之间的路。哥德尔1931年的不完备性定理，此时早已发表；后来他也认真研究过相对论中的宇宙。

那条定理有清楚的边界：足够表达算术、可有效公理化的一致形式系统，不可能决定其中所有算术命题。它没有证明广义相对论必然遗漏量子随机性，也没有判定哪一种物理解释正确。

散步的朋友手里，没有一张能够结束这场物理争论的数学判决书。

但两个人走在同一条路上，仍让人想问：建造一个世界，与知道自己的构不能包揽一切，能不能同时发生？这是这篇文章借来的问题，不是定理替我们说出的话。

凿向自己的构，不必等于立即放弃它。也可能是继续工作，却不给它预先签发“世界只能如此”的通行证。

六、他和这个系列的每个人

把前面的身影放到一起。这不是一场给伟人排名的审判，而是本系列留下的几种姿势。

苏格拉底的追问也回到自己：不知道，不只是用来刺破别人的话。

孔子说“知我者其天乎”。一个没有把自己的一生变成现成答案的人，仍把工作交给明天。

老子写下可说之道不是恒常之道。传说里，他随后出了关；连留下的文字，也不该被误认成道本身。

庄子的混沌被凿开七窍而死。那则寓言使我们看见：使一切变得可分辨的好意，也可能杀死它想帮助的东西。

康德划出知识的界限，让物自体不被当成经验中的一个对象。他的构有边界，边界也仍可追问。

尼采把许多价值凿到根部。他后来患病；疾病不能充当哲学的报应，也不能替思想作证。

王阳明把追问带回良知与行动之间。向内凿，并不意味着从此不再面对别人。

释迦牟尼留下过河的筏喻：教导用来渡河，不是用来背在身上炫耀。放下执取，不是烧掉一切教导。

耶稣被钉上十字架。在上一篇的文学图景里，他在桥上，受伤而仍不把他人只当作工具；那不是要求受害者必须原谅施害者的命令。

哥德尔为一类形式系统证明了严格的界限。他后来在严重的健康危机中死去；数学并没有把这个人推向死亡。

而爱因斯坦拆开了物理学的一座大构，又亲手建立另一座。他没有停止寻找，却始终不愿轻易放下某些关于实在与秩序的要求。

他不是这里唯一一个有所执着的人。没有一个人因为留下一句智慧的话，就从此免于执着。

我愿意把这篇文章的问题，放在美上。

场方程太优雅了。原来分散的现象，竟能在同一幅几何里相遇。一个人亲手造出这样的东西，继续相信它会通向更深的统一，不难理解。

美能引路。也可能挡路。

我们可以借苏格拉底的追问、筏喻的放手、庄子的寓言，练习不把工具当成终点。但不能因此声称他们从无牵挂，只有爱因斯坦被困住。

“上帝不掷骰子”里，有物理判断，也有哲学信念；我们还可以听见对秩序的审美忠诚。但那最后一层，是本文的阅读，不是对他内心的诊断。

美的构能不能留下尚未理解的地方，而仍然是美的？不急着把缺口封住，也不把每个缺口都赞美成真理——这比喊一句“接受不完备”更难。

哥德尔没有证明美必须有洞。量子力学也没有证明庄子的寓言。它们在这里彼此照亮，并不彼此提供证据。

普林斯顿的两个人继续散步。我们知道他们留下的工作，不知道每一次沉默里各自在想什么。可以让那条路保持开放。

1955年4月18日，爱因斯坦在普林斯顿去世。传记记载，他没有选择进一步手术延长生命，认为自己的工作已经做过，可以走了。那份告别的从容，不需要被当成任何理论的最后证明。

人会离开。构还在，继续接受比作者更长久的追问。

上帝究竟掷不掷骰子？这一次，让问号留下。

I. The Patent Office

1905. Einstein was twenty-six. Not a professor: a technical expert, third class, at the patent office in Bern.

That year he published the four papers later gathered under the name annus mirabilis, the miracle year.

The first proposed light quanta, discrete portions of energy, and used them to explain the photoelectric effect. Light's wave phenomena did not disappear; a continuous wave was no longer the whole account. When Einstein received the Nobel Prize for 1921, its citation singled out the law of the photoelectric effect.

The second investigated Brownian motion: tiny particles suspended in a liquid keep twitching, driven by the thermal motion of molecules too small to see. Atoms and molecules acquired measurable consequences. They were more than a convenient supposition.

The third was special relativity. Between inertial frames, the speed of light in vacuum remains the same, while durations and lengths are no longer separately absolute. The single clock we thought everyone shared began to crack.

The fourth connected mass with energy. The relation later written E=mc² says that even a body at rest has energy corresponding to its mass. Two apparently separate columns in nature's ledger belonged together.

Four papers. One patent examiner. One year.

These were not four identical holes drilled into Newton's house. Light quanta tested the reach of classical electromagnetism; Brownian motion made molecules experimentally accessible; relativity reopened space and time; mass and energy forced a new accounting of matter. He struck at several places that had come to be treated as foundations beyond questioning.

Not merely improving inherited answers. Opening the questions again.

II. Newton's Absolute Space and Time

Newton's world looks like this: space is a fixed stage, time a uniformly ticking clock. Bodies move on the stage while the clock runs evenly behind them. The stage takes no part in the drama. No one's adventures change the clock.

This framework supported physics for more than two centuries. Planets, apples, tides: one system could calculate them all. It was among humanity's most successful constructions.

Kant asked a different question, treating space and time as a priori forms that make experience possible. He was not simply moving Newton's external containers inside the human head. Yet in that intellectual world, too, space and time lay deeper than an ordinary hypothesis one might put back on the experimental table.

Einstein returned the question to clocks and measuring rods.

Observers in uniform motion relative to one another need not agree that two events are simultaneous. They obtain different durations and lengths when comparing their measurements. Neither is merely mistaken; there is no secretly privileged observer truly at rest. Change the frame, and the relations among measurements change.

This is not a game played on paper. GPS must account for the different effects of motion and gravity on clocks, using both special and general relativity. The phone that finds your street corner depends on a clock that is no longer absolute.

Ten years later, in 1915, Einstein went further.

General relativity gives gravity a geometric language. Matter and energy help determine spacetime's curvature; that geometry governs the paths of freely falling bodies. The stage no longer merely contains the actors. It joins the drama.

In this description, the Earth need not be imagined tethered to the Sun by an invisible pulling cord. It follows a natural path through the curved spacetime around the Sun. An apple in free fall follows such a geodesic too.

Not the abolition of every force. A different language for gravity.

This was an extraordinarily deep cut into physics. What seemed to require a pull could be understood through geometry. The stage apparently independent of the world became part of it.

Newton was not reduced to rubble. At low speeds and in weak gravitational fields his calculations remain useful. But his house was no longer the whole world.

III. Construction

Einstein did not only carve away. He built.

General relativity does more than announce that Newton is insufficient. Its field equations connect spacetime geometry with matter and energy. A few lines of symbols carry the weight of a new world.

That world permits gravitational waves and black holes. Einstein investigated gravitational waves in 1916; in 2015 LIGO directly detected them for the first time, arriving from a merger of two black holes. Nearly a century after the theoretical prediction, a very faint tremor reached an instrument.

The equations also permit an expanding universe. Friedmann and Lemaître opened that route; galaxy observations later gave it empirical support, with Hubble's 1929 work an important part of the story. Einstein had initially preferred a static cosmos. His construction could travel where its author had not first ventured.

Mercury's orbit, the bending of light, the rates of clocks, gravitational waves: the theory has survived repeated and demanding tests. Not every possible prediction has been checked. Not every question has been exhausted. Its success is still astonishing.

Elegant and precise, it remains our best description across a great range of gravitational phenomena.

Kant questioned the conditions of experience and built the three Critiques. Einstein questioned clocks, rods and gravity and built relativity. We can recognize a shared movement here: open the inherited order, then shoulder the work of construction.

But a philosophical argument is not a physics experiment. These two figures may illuminate one another in an essay; neither can certify the other's achievement.

Both remain open to questions. This series asked Kant where life belongs between nature and freedom. Here the question becomes: when strong gravity and quantum phenomena can no longer be treated separately, what more does this construction of spacetime require?

Outside the door stand not only opponents, but the quantum Einstein himself helped usher in.

IV. God Does Not Play Dice

Twentieth-century physics is often drawn as two pillars: general relativity for gravity, quantum theory for the quantum behavior of matter and radiation. “Large” and “small” are introductory signposts, not territories that never meet.

The difficult places are those where neither can leave the room: deep inside black holes, in the very early universe. We still lack a fully verified theory of quantum gravity.

General relativity uses continuous spacetime. Quantum mechanics includes discrete outcomes for certain measurements and gives probabilities; this does not declare that spacetime must be built of grains. The tension runs deeper than smoothness against particles.

Nor was Einstein's disagreement with quantum mechanics simply a territorial quarrel between theories. He was one of quantum theory's founders. He acknowledged its success and asked whether it had said everything about physical reality.

A particle can occupy a superposition. A superposition is not the absence of a state; it is a state that need not assign a definite result in advance to every quantity we might ask about. The usual quantum rules give probabilities for outcomes.

Does calculating correctly mean we have explained all the way down?

In a 1926 letter to Born, Einstein expressed his doubt in words later condensed into the famous phrase “God does not play dice.” This was no theological experiment, but a conviction about nature's order.

In 1935 Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen sharpened the question in the EPR paper. If we can predict a quantity with certainty without disturbing a distant system, should the theory acknowledge a corresponding physical reality? Was something missing from the quantum description?

Completeness, physical reality, the independence of distant systems: these were at issue. Not a man closing his eyes at the sight of a probability.

In 1964 Bell turned part of the dispute into testable inequalities. Under conditions including locality and statistical independence between measurement choices and hidden variables, a class of hidden-variable explanations faces definite limits. Quantum theory predicts correlations beyond them.

Experiments have repeatedly crossed those limits. The challenged picture is one in which those conditions can all hold together. Einstein's cherished conception of local physical reality met a severe test he did not live to see.

But this does not mean that every hidden variable is ruled out. Bohmian theories preserve deterministic evolution at the cost of nonlocality. One experiment cannot throw the dice for every interpretation.

Einstein did not obtain the answer he hoped for. His question was not wasted. Today's research on entanglement still passes through the doorway he refused to let others walk around.

“God plays dice” makes an elegant reply.

It cannot replace saying what was tested, and what had to give way.

V. Turning the Chisel Toward One's Own Work?

Einstein opened absolute space and time and rewrote gravity as geometry. He was certainly one of the twentieth century's great carvers.

What happens when the chisel turns toward one's own construction?

With appropriate initial conditions and within its domain, general relativity gives deterministic evolution. Its order differs markedly from the probabilities for individual measurements in the usual quantum account.

Einstein preferred to seek a deeper, unified description rather than treat the existing probability rules as the final word. There is a real tension here. Someone who opens the world can also remain loyal to what he believes the world ought to be like.

Yet he did not enthrone general relativity as a completed last sentence. He knew it could not account for all matter and quantum phenomena. His later search for a unified field theory shows that he was still looking for another construction.

The question is harder than “he could challenge others but not himself.” When does loyalty to a clue sustain research? When does it begin deciding the answer before the evidence arrives?

In Princeton, Gödel often walked with him. The two spoke German along the roads between the Institute and their homes. Gödel's incompleteness theorem of 1931 had long been published; he later also studied relativistic cosmology in earnest.

That theorem has precise boundaries. A consistent, effectively axiomatized formal system strong enough to express arithmetic cannot decide every arithmetic proposition in its language. It does not prove that general relativity must leave quantum randomness out, or settle which interpretation of physics is right.

His walking companion carried no mathematical verdict that could end this dispute.

Yet seeing the two men on the same road makes one ask whether building a world and knowing that one's construction cannot encompass everything can happen together. That is the question this essay borrows, not a sentence the theorem pronounces for us.

Turning the chisel toward one's own work need not mean abandoning it at once. It can mean continuing to work without granting it advance permission to declare that the world can only be this way.

VI. Einstein and Everyone Else in This Series

Bring the earlier figures together. Not a court ranking great people, but a gathering of postures this series has left behind.

Socrates' questions return to himself. Not knowing is more than a weapon for puncturing someone else's claims.

Confucius wonders whether Heaven alone knows him. A life that has not become a ready-made answer still hands its work to tomorrow.

Laozi writes that the way that can be spoken is not the constant way. In the legend, he then leaves through the pass. Even the words he leaves behind must not be mistaken for the way itself.

Zhuangzi's Hundun dies after seven openings are bored into him. The fable lets us see how the wish to make everything distinct may kill what it means to help.

Kant marks the limits of knowledge, refusing to make the thing-in-itself one more object of experience. His construction has a boundary; that boundary remains open to inquiry.

Nietzsche digs down to the roots of values. He later becomes ill. Illness is neither philosophy's punishment nor evidence on its behalf.

Wang Yangming brings questioning back to innate knowing and action. Turning inward does not release one from facing other people.

Shakyamuni leaves the image of the raft: a teaching is for crossing, not carrying on one's back as a trophy. Releasing attachment does not mean burning every teaching.

Jesus is crucified. In the preceding essay's literary image he is on a bridge, wounded yet still refusing to treat others merely as instruments. That is not an order requiring victims to forgive those who harm them.

Gödel proves precise limits for a class of formal systems. He later dies during a grave health crisis. Mathematics did not drive him to his death.

And Einstein opens one great construction of physics and builds another. He does not stop searching, yet remains reluctant to relinquish certain demands about reality and order.

He is not the only person here with attachments. Leaving a wise saying behind never exempted anyone from holding on.

I want to place this essay's question in beauty.

The field equations are extraordinarily elegant. Phenomena once scattered across separate accounts meet within one geometry. It is understandable that someone who made such a thing would keep trusting it to lead toward a deeper unity.

Beauty can guide. It can also stand in the way.

Socratic inquiry, the letting go of the raft, Zhuangzi's fables: we may borrow these to practice not making a tool our final destination. We cannot conclude that those figures had no attachments, and Einstein alone was trapped.

“God does not play dice” contains a physical judgment and a philosophical conviction. We may also hear an aesthetic loyalty to order. That last register is this essay's reading, not a diagnosis of his inner life.

Can a beautiful construction leave room for what is not yet understood and remain beautiful? Neither sealing the gap in haste nor praising every gap as truth: that is harder than announcing “accept incompleteness.”

Gödel did not prove that beauty must have holes. Quantum mechanics did not prove Zhuangzi's fable. Here they illuminate one another; they do not supply one another with evidence.

The two men in Princeton keep walking. We know the work they left, not what occupied every silence. We can leave the road open.

On April 18, 1955, Einstein died in Princeton. His biographers recount that he declined further surgery to extend his life, feeling he had done his work and could go. The composure of that farewell need not serve as a final proof of any theory.

A person leaves. The construction remains, answering questions for longer than its maker could live.

Does God play dice? This time, let the question mark remain.
