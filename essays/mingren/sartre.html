萨特，他人即地狱

Sartre, Hell Is Other People

Han Qin (秦汉) · March 2026

一、门开过

一个房间，三个人，没有镜子，灯一直亮着。《禁闭》1944年搬上舞台，地狱看起来比想象中更像一间客厅。

加尔森想让另一个人相信，他不是懦夫。伊内斯不肯把那张证明给他。埃斯泰尔也需要别人的目光，而三个人想从彼此那里得到的东西，恰好使他们无法相安无事。

最值得记住的，不只是房门关着。门曾经打开，加尔森却没有走。他还需要伊内斯改变判断；身体能离开，那个向别人索取最后裁决的自己却留下了。

“他人即地狱”于是比“别人很烦”更难受。你把谁当作唯一有权给你定案的人，谁就可能把你困在那里。剧中人的过往伤害也没有因为他们痛苦就被取消。

萨特后来明确解释过，这句话不是说一切人际关系必然有毒，而是说扭曲的关系会变成地狱。他人的存在并不自动剥夺我们的自由。

因此，这篇文章不从“只剩孤独才安全”开始。它从一个更熟悉的瞬间开始：门明明开着，你却还在等某个人说，你终于可以做你自己。

二、一棵树的根

在1938年的《恶心》中，洛根丁面对树根，感到名字与解释突然失去了平常的安稳。说出“树根”，好像还没有触到眼前这团顽固存在的东西。

我们习惯把东西放进用途：这是长椅，那是道路，树是公园里的树。现在，分类还在，存在却多出来了。它不是因为洛根丁的说明才在这里，也没有欠他一个必须如此的理由。

这种体验被写成恶心。它不是对植物没有生物学功能的发现，也不是告诉读者，出现身体不适就获得了某种哲学资格。小说把一个通常被语言遮住的困惑推到了眼前。

洛根丁继而看见自己：我也在这里，可没有一份预先写好的说明书，保证我为什么非得存在。偶然性使他不安，却不能直接推出他的生命没有价值。

萨特的无神论拒绝一个事先设计好每个人用途的神圣工匠。这是他提出的哲学立场，不是散文作者替所有读者宣布，关于上帝和目的的争论已经结束。

没有被安排用途，与不值得活，是两句话。如果一本书拆掉了第一句话里的保证，我们不必顺手把第二句话当作它留下的唯一答案。

三、裁纸刀以外

1945年巴黎的演讲，次年以《存在主义是一种人道主义》出版。萨特用一把裁纸刀解释那句著名的“存在先于本质”。

制造裁纸刀以前，工匠知道它用来做什么。用途、工艺、设计，先于桌上这件成品。用同样的方法理解人，就是假设他出生前已经有一份必须完成的产品规格。

萨特反对的，正是把活着的人压成这份规格。一个人的身份不能只靠“我天生就是这样”结账；他怎样行动、怎样对待他人，也在形成他会成为什么人。

不过，新生儿不是从社会、身体与关系之外掉进世界的一张白纸。没有预先确定的完整人生，不等于没有继承而来的条件，也不等于一切能力都可以凭意志现做。

过去的行为不能撤销，未来也不必被过去永远封死。承认我曾经逃避，不等于取得“我就是懦夫，所以只能逃避”的永久许可证；改变又不是一句声明就已完成。

这句话最有力量的地方，也许在那个不肯盖上的印章：你需要回答自己做过什么，但别人和你自己都不能只凭一个称号，宣布你此后再没有事情可做。

四、你不能替自己退出选择

“被判为自由”，听起来像一场惩罚。它的尖锐处在于，我们没有先选择来到世界，却已经在世界里，必须回应某些事情。拖着不答，有时也会造成后果。

萨特的语言常常把责任推得很远，远到令人喘不过气。认真读他，可以感到这股压力，也可以追问：怎样不让责任变成对困境中人的苛责？

他区分处境与超出既有处境的计划。贫穷、疾病、战争和他人的强制不是幻觉；人不能靠决定自由，便穿过锁住的门。自由不是全能，更不等于人人拥有同样的选项。

演讲里那个青年，在照顾母亲与参加战斗之间犹豫。两边都有理由，也都牵涉具体的人。去请谁指点，本身已经包含了对某种回答的倾向；一条现成格言不能替他把代价取消。

这个例子不是赞美谁都不必咨询，而是提醒咨询以后仍要由人承担行动。反过来，承认一个人还能行动，也不意味着他应为别人加在身上的压迫负责。

没有一种诚实需要把受害者先说成有罪，才能开始谈自由。我们需要的，是在真实限制中找出可做的下一步，而不是站在限制之外责问别人为什么不飞。

五、服务员不是一张名牌

《存在与虚无》里，咖啡馆服务员的动作过于周到，仿佛每一抬手都在表演“标准服务员”。萨特借这个例子讨论自欺，法语叫mauvaise foi，常译“自欺”或“坏信仰”。

问题不是认真工作就不真实，更不是哲学家坐着看一眼，便有权判断某个劳动者的内心。那是一个被安排来显示结构的例子，不是一份对所有服务员的诊断。

自欺的一种方式，是把角色当作全部：因为我是这样的人，所以别的事与我无关。职务替我下命令，性格替我免责，名牌替我回答。活着的人仿佛退到一件物品后面。

另一种方式恰好相反：我总能重新开始，所以过去做过什么都不算，承诺和身体也不能要求我什么。只承认可能、不承认事实，同样可以是一种逃跑。

“我就是这样”与“那完全不算我”，都可能把最该面对的事情推出门外。诚实不是选择其中一句，而是既不否认已经发生的事，也不把它变成永远不改的理由。

用这个眼光看奥古斯丁的“但不要现在”，可以提出一个问题，却不能替萨特给奥古斯丁作出历史诊断。延迟有时是逃避，有时是准备，分辨它需要了解一段生活，不只需要一条漂亮概念。

六、凌晨三点以后

把萨特放在克尔凯郭尔旁边，我们会认出那种不安：道理都知道了，可是下一步仍没有人替我走。

不过，萨特不是把基督教从克尔凯郭尔身上减掉后剩下的版本。他也从胡塞尔、海德格尔和现象学中发展自己的问题；两人的思想路径不能由一个减法说明。

克尔凯郭尔的信仰不是买到一张从此不再焦虑的保险，萨特的无神论也不是宣布世界里只剩一个孤立的我。没有上帝来替你签字，不等于没有朋友、历史、身体和他人。

在《方法问题》中，萨特认真讨论克尔凯郭尔与黑格尔。他一方面保留不能被概念轻易消解的痛苦，另一方面也不肯把客观关系和历史条件当作无关的布景。

真正的难处于是更接近凌晨过后的早晨。你要把昨夜的决定带进工作、关系和别人的生活，承受它不再只属于你的那一部分。

信仰与无神论仍然分开。两位作者也各自有未解决的难题。但读者不必在一幅“一个被上帝接住、一个永远坠落”的漫画里，替他们决定谁更勇敢。

七、排队的人

到了1960年的《辩证理性批判》，萨特追问的不再只是一个人怎样面对选择，还包括：许多人为什么会一起活在某种关系里？

等公共汽车的队伍里，每个人都想回家，却可能只是被同一班车排成彼此可替换的位置。共同处境并不自动等于共同活动；要改变它，需要的也不只是各自在心里作一次决定。

匮乏、劳动、制度和历史进入自由的讨论。他试图让个人行动与集体实践同处一个解释中，同时批评把人过快归入阶级标签、便以为已经解释完他的办法。

这项工作有争议，也没有完成；第二卷后来以身后稿出版。未完成可以提示困难，却不能单凭书没写完，就证明个人自由与社会分析原则上绝不相容。

政治上的萨特也需要被读者追问。他反对殖民与压迫，同时对某些革命事业和政权的判断留下严重争议。知识分子的良好目的，不能替他支持的手段自动免责。

最值得带走的，或许是一种双重要求：不能用环境把人全部解释掉，也不能用个人选择把环境全部解释掉。两句话之间的工作，比任何一句单独喊得响都难。

八、另一个人不是偶然道具

萨特与波伏瓦1929年相识，持续相伴与思想交流，直到1980年他去世。他们没有结婚，以“必要的爱”与“偶然的爱”来安排彼此及其他关系。

不按既定婚姻模式生活，可以是认真选择。但“我们自行约定”不等于“这份约定自然公正”，更不等于每一个被卷入的人，都在同样有利的位置上同意了它。

信件、回忆和其他关系中的叙述，留下了嫉妒、痛苦与不对等。不是只问这两位著名思想家能否维持关系，还要问被他们称作“偶然”的人，能不能说不，能不能被认真对待。

持续五十一年，说明联系深，不自动证明模式优越。婚姻并不必然吞没人，非婚姻也不自动使人自由；关切应落在具体的尊重、诚实和退出的可能。

波伏瓦更不是替萨特的自由作见证的附录。她有自己的哲学、作品和判断，也会修改他的问题。下一篇属于她，不只是因为他们相爱，而是因为她自己有话要说。

反对把人变成工具，最难的考试常在亲密关系里。你越能讲出关于自由的道理，就越不能让那个与你有关的人，只负责证明你的理论活得很好。

九、还可以重新开始

1980年4月15日，萨特在巴黎去世，七十四岁。送葬人群数以万计，涌向蒙帕纳斯。人数表现了影响，却不能替一种哲学表决真伪。

晚年视力严重衰退，阅读与写作变得困难，他仍通过谈话和口述工作。他没有把所有问题解决完。衰老留下的依赖，也使“一个人独自作主”的形象显出不足。

在这个系列想象的桥头，他戴着眼镜，带着烟斗，听见别人说话。我们不用再把每一道目光都写成一项判决。

苏格拉底可以问一个问题，而不是给他贴“自以为知道一切”的标签。克尔凯郭尔可以与他意见不同，而不必站在信仰的门口把他永远拒之门外。

萨特仍会感到不安。别人的看法有力量，它们既可能帮助理解，也可能把人钉住。桥头没有魔法，可以让这种风险消失。

但一个人可以听完后回应，承认错误，拒绝不公的定义，也给对方同样的机会。另一个人不只是困住我的目光，也可能是使我重新看见自己的声音。

这不是宣布《禁闭》已经获得一个幸福结局。剧中的人仍留在那里；这里是我们有意另写的相遇。

门开过。还活着的时候，我们可以问：这一次，要不要不再索取那张终身判决书，走出去，与别人继续做一点事？

注释

《禁闭》里门确实打开，加尔森选择留下；“他人即地狱”的解释同时参考萨特后来的澄清。树根属于《恶心》的文学场景，裁纸刀和青年选择出自1945年演讲、1946年出版文本。服务员是《存在与虚无》的哲学例子，不能用来远程判断具体劳动者。自由不等于能力无限；自欺也不只包括接受角色，还包括否认既有事实与承诺。

《方法问题》与《辩证理性批判》中的社会分析不因未完成就被判作不可能；政治立场及亲密关系仍接受伦理审视。波伏瓦拥有独立思想，其他关系中的人也不是验证二人自由的道具。比较克尔凯郭尔、奥古斯丁以及桥头相遇属于本文的阅读和文学想象，不冒充萨特原作；末节不改写《禁闭》的结局。

I. The Door Has Opened

A room, three people, no mirrors, the lights always on. When No Exit reached the stage in 1944, hell looked disconcertingly like a drawing room.

Garcin wants somebody else to confirm that he is not a coward. Inès refuses to issue the certificate. Estelle needs another's attention too, and what each demands from the others makes coexistence a form of torment.

The crucial detail is not simply a locked door. The door opens, and Garcin does not leave. He still needs Inès to change her verdict. His body could depart; the self waiting for final certification remains.

Hell is other people is therefore more troubling than an observation that company can be irritating. Give somebody exclusive power to settle who you are and you may find yourself imprisoned by the decision. The characters' distress does not erase the injuries they themselves have caused.

Sartre later explicitly rejected the idea that the line meant all human relationships were poisonous. Distorted relationships can become hell. Another person's existence does not automatically cancel our freedom.

We begin, then, not with solitude as the only safe solution, but with a familiar pause: the door is open, and you are still waiting for somebody to grant permission to be yourself.

II. A Tree Root

In Nausea, published in 1938, Roquentin confronts a tree root and finds that naming and explanation have lost their usual reassurance. Saying root no longer seems to contain the stubborn existence before him.

We ordinarily place things within uses: a bench, a path, a tree belonging to the park. The descriptions remain available, but existence exceeds their familiar fit. The root is not there because he has explained it, and owes him no guarantee that it had to be there.

The novel renders this disturbance as nausea. It is not a discovery that roots lack biological functions, nor a claim that physical distress confers philosophical insight. A usually covered-over question has become uncomfortably present.

Roquentin sees himself in the disturbance. He exists without a prior instruction manual making his particular presence necessary. Contingency troubles him, but it does not logically establish that his life is worthless.

Sartre's atheism rejects a divine craftsman assigning each human being an intended use. This is a philosophical position he argues from, not the essayist's announcement that every reader's questions about God and purpose have already been settled.

Not designed for a predetermined function and not worth living are different judgments. Removing the first guarantee need not make the second judgment the only thing left to say.

III. Something Other Than a Paper Knife

The Paris lecture of 1945 appeared the following year as Existentialism Is a Humanism. A paper knife introduces its famous formula: existence precedes essence.

Before making the knife, a craftsperson knows its intended use. Design, purpose, and method precede the finished object. To approach a person in the same way is to imagine a specification written before birth that the product must fulfil.

Sartre resists reducing someone alive to that specification. An identity cannot settle its accounts by saying I was born this way. Actions and the treatment of other people also participate in what that person becomes.

Yet an infant does not arrive from outside bodies, societies, and relationships as a blank sheet. The absence of a completely predetermined life is not the absence of inherited conditions. Abilities cannot all be summoned into being by a declaration of will.

Past conduct cannot be cancelled, and the future need not be sealed by it. Acknowledging an evasion does not issue a permanent licence to evade again because I am a coward. Changing requires more than announcing a replacement identity.

The force of the formula may lie in the stamp it refuses to apply. You must answer for what you have done; neither you nor somebody else may use a single label to announce that nothing further remains possible.

IV. Unable to Opt Out

Condemned to be free sounds punitive. Its edge is that we did not first choose to arrive in the world, yet find ourselves already having to respond. Postponing a response can itself have consequences.

Sartre sometimes presses responsibility so far that the reader struggles to breathe. Taking him seriously means feeling that pressure and asking how to prevent responsibility from becoming cruelty toward somebody in difficulty.

He distinguishes our given situation from the projects through which we go beyond what is given. Poverty, illness, war, and coercion are real. Deciding to be free does not carry a person through a locked door. Freedom is neither omnipotence nor an equal distribution of available options.

His lecture describes a young man torn between caring for his mother and joining the struggle against occupation. Both courses have reasons; both concern particular people. Even selecting an adviser leans toward one kind of response. No ready-made maxim makes the competing costs vanish.

The example does not prohibit consultation. It asks who acts after the advice has been heard. Conversely, acknowledging a person's capacity to act does not make that person responsible for oppression imposed by others.

Honesty need not begin by finding the injured party guilty. We need to locate the next feasible action within actual limits, rather than stand outside those limits demanding to know why another person does not simply fly.

V. More Than a Name Badge

In Being and Nothingness a café waiter moves with conspicuous precision, as if performing the complete idea of a waiter. Sartre uses the scene to explore mauvaise foi: bad faith, a kind of self-deception.

Conscientious work is not thereby inauthentic. Nor does a philosopher watching a worker acquire reliable access to that worker's inner life. The scene is a constructed illustration, not a diagnosis covering an occupation.

One form of bad faith lets the role become everything. Since I am this sort of person, nothing else concerns me. My office gives the orders, my temperament supplies the excuse, my badge gives the answer. A living person retreats behind a thing.

The opposite escape is available too: because I can always begin again, nothing I have done counts; promises and bodily circumstances may make no claim on me. Recognising only possibility while disowning facts can be another evasion.

That is just who I am and that was not really me can both push the decisive issue out of the room. Honesty requires acknowledging what happened without converting it into a reason never to change.

Read Augustine's not yet through this lens and a question emerges, not a historical diagnosis delivered by Sartre. Delay may be evasion or preparation. Distinguishing them requires knowledge of a life, not simply possession of an elegant concept.

VI. After Three in the Morning

Beside Kierkegaard, Sartre presents a recognisable anxiety: the reasons are understood, but nobody has taken the next step for me.

He is not, however, what remains when Christianity is subtracted from Kierkegaard. Husserl, Heidegger, and phenomenology also shape his questions. A subtraction cannot explain the route of either thinker.

Kierkegaardian faith is not insurance against all further anguish; Sartrean atheism does not leave a solitary ego inhabiting an empty universe. Without God to sign the decision, friends, bodies, history, and other people have not ceased to exist.

In Search for a Method Sartre engages both Kierkegaard and Hegel. He preserves suffering that a concept cannot merely dissolve, while refusing to demote objective relationships and historical conditions to irrelevant scenery.

The difficulty begins to resemble the morning after the anxious night. A decision must enter work and relationships. You must encounter the part of its consequences that no longer belongs to you alone.

Faith and atheism remain different, and both thinkers leave difficult questions. A reader need not award courage through a cartoon in which God catches one man while the other falls forever.

VII. People Waiting for a Bus

By the Critique of Dialectical Reason in 1960, Sartre is investigating more than the solitary encounter with choice. How do many people come to inhabit a particular relationship together?

Those waiting for a bus each want to get home, yet the same transport arrangement can place them in interchangeable positions. A shared situation does not automatically become common action. Changing it requires more than a private decision inside each person's head.

Scarcity, labour, institutions, and history enter the account of freedom. Sartre tries to think individual action and collective practice together, while resisting the assumption that assigning someone a class label completes the explanation of a life.

The project is contested and unfinished; a second volume later appeared from posthumous materials. Its incompletion reveals difficulty. It does not by itself prove that personal freedom and social analysis are inherently incompatible.

Sartre's political commitments also require scrutiny. His opposition to colonialism and oppression coexists with deeply disputed judgments about revolutionary causes and regimes. An intellectual's emancipatory intention cannot automatically acquit the means he supports.

A double demand remains useful: do not explain the person away through circumstances, and do not explain circumstances away through personal choice. Working between these demands is harder than making either slogan louder.

VIII. Nobody Is a Contingent Prop

Sartre and Simone de Beauvoir met in 1929 and maintained an enduring personal and intellectual relationship until his death in 1980. They did not marry; necessary love and contingent loves were their terms for arranging their bond alongside others.

Departing from a conventional marriage can be a serious choice. We agreed to it does not establish that every aspect was fair, however, or that everyone drawn into the arrangement could consent from an equally secure position.

Letters, memoirs, and accounts from other relationships preserve jealousy, suffering, and inequality. The question is not only whether two famous thinkers could keep their partnership going. Could those designated contingent refuse, and have their lives treated seriously?

Fifty-one years testifies to a durable bond, not the superiority of a model. Marriage need not swallow a person; its absence does not guarantee freedom. Respect, honesty, and the possibility of leaving matter in the concrete relationship.

Beauvoir is not an appendix certifying that Sartre lived freely. She has her own philosophy, books, and judgments, and can transform his questions. The next essay belongs to her because she has something to say, not merely because they loved each other.

Resistance to treating people as instruments faces a demanding examination in intimacy. The more persuasively you speak about freedom, the less entitled you are to ask someone close to you merely to demonstrate how well your theory works.

IX. Still Possible to Begin

Sartre died in Paris on 15 April 1980, aged seventy-four. Tens of thousands joined the funeral procession toward Montparnasse. A crowd measures influence; it cannot vote a philosophy true.

Severe visual impairment complicated his later reading and writing, but conversation and dictation continued. The questions were not all settled. Dependence in old age also unsettles the portrait of a person governing life entirely alone.

At the imaginary bridge of this series, he wears glasses and carries a pipe, listening to the others. We need not turn every look directed toward him into a verdict.

Socrates can ask a question without branding him a man who thinks he knows everything. Kierkegaard can disagree without standing at faith's entrance and permanently barring the way.

Uneasiness remains. Other people's judgments have power: they can help us understand and can fix us in place. No magic at the bridge removes the risk.

Someone can listen and answer, acknowledge a wrong, refuse an unfair definition, and extend that same possibility to the speaker. Another person may be more than the gaze that imprisons me: a voice through which I see myself anew.

This does not give No Exit a happy ending. Its characters remain in their room. Here we have deliberately imagined a different encounter.

The door has opened. While alive, we can ask whether this time we might stop demanding a lifelong verdict, go outside, and do something further with other people.

Notes

The door in No Exit opens and Garcin remains; the reading also follows Sartre’s later clarification of the line about others. The root belongs to Nausea; the paper knife and student appear in the 1945 lecture published in 1946. The waiter is a philosophical illustration in Being and Nothingness, not a diagnosis of actual workers. Freedom is not unlimited power; bad faith can deny facts and commitments as well as reduce someone to a role.

Search for a Method and the Critique’s social inquiry are not declared impossible merely because the project remained unfinished. Political judgments and intimate arrangements still invite ethical scrutiny. Beauvoir has independent thought; others are not props validating the couple’s freedom. The comparisons with Kierkegaard and Augustine and the bridge scene are this essay’s interpretation and invention, not Sartre’s text; the ending does not revise No Exit.
