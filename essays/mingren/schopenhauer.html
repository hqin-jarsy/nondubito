叔本华，底下有东西

Schopenhauer, There Is Something Underneath

Han Qin (秦汉) · March 2026

一、选在同一个钟点

1820年，三十二岁的叔本华取得在柏林大学授课的资格。他把自己的课排在黑格尔主要课程的同一时段。这不像一个怕没人注意的新讲师会做的安排，更像一封写进课表里的挑战书。

结果，听众寥寥。不是一个人也没来，更不能顺着这幅冷清画面，把他后来的任教尝试全部抹掉。几年后，他还回到柏林谋求讲课的机会，只是始终没有建立起成功的大学教学生涯。

我们可以想象那种难堪：你确信自己有重要的话，别人却走向另一间教室。不过，想象不等于已经读懂他的全部动机。不能把他往后对黑格尔的攻击，都诊断成那一天受挫的回声。

攻击确实尖刻，也持续很久。但音量不是理由。一个人不受欢迎，不能证明他错了；一个人自称被时代误解，也不能因此证明他比时代更清醒。

值得继续读的，是骂声后面那个问题：如果我们替世界搭起一个壮观、合理的说明，人的饥饿、争夺和痛苦，是否就真的被说明完了？

叔本华不肯轻易点头。他怀疑，在我们拿得出理由之前，已经有某种东西在推着我们走。那间冷清教室里也不例外。他想让人不再被欲望牵着走，可他自己很想被听见。

二、世界怎样来到眼前

他的主要著作《作为意志和表象的世界》，在1818年底至1819年初问世，扉页标的是1819年。最初销路冷落，声誉来得很慢。它不是一本一出生就拥有后来地位的书。

开篇那句话很响亮：“世界是我的表象。”容易被听成：一切都是我瞎想的。可他不是说闭上眼，桌子便任我取消；他问的是，世界怎样作为一个可以被认识的世界，来到我们面前。

有被看见的东西，也有看见它的人。空间、时间和因果关系，让我们把经验理解为一件件彼此有关的事。我们无法先跳出所有认识条件，再从世界外面核对自己的认识。

这里接着康德，却不是把康德缩成一句“帘子后面不知道，别问了”。康德划分的是我们能够怎样认识、哪些要求越出了认识的权限；他没有禁止人继续思考。

叔本华仍然觉得，这样还不够。我们不只是望着世界的眼睛，还活在世界里面。也许有一个地方，我们并不只是从外部观看。

那个地方不是遥远的天国，是自己的身体。你端起水杯时，手不只是视野里一个移动的物体。这个动作，也是你正在做的事。

三、手伸出去的时候

从外面看，手臂抬起，手指合拢，杯子离开桌面。从里面说，你正在伸手喝水。叔本华认为，这不是两件由因果链勉强接起来的事，而是同一个行动以不同方式被知道。

所以，不能把他的意思讲成一项神经科学实验：人还没来得及想，肌肉就先收缩了。他说的不是念头和动作谁早几毫秒，而是我们对自身的经验，并非只有旁观这一条路。

他把这种从内部认识的意愿叫作“意志”，然后走出很大的一步：以此理解自然的内在性质。植物生长、动物觅食、自然力的作用，都被纳入他的解释。这一步是形而上学的推展，不是摸到自己的脉搏，就已经测出了宇宙的底层。

这里的意志，也不只是一个人下定决心的毅力。个别行动可以有目标；他所说的世界意志，却没有一个最后完成便从此安顿下来的总目标。它不断趋向、不断争取，不等于背后有位理性的总设计师。

用一头饥饿的兽来比喻它，很有力量。但兽是我们的比喻，不是世界下面真的躲着一个生物。读者可以被这种诊断击中，仍然追问：从我的欲求到万物的本性，这个跨越站得住吗？

叔本华使人难以轻轻放过的，不只是他的答案，也是那份经验：有时我们说得头头是道，理由还没有说完，身体和情绪已经表明，我们究竟想要什么。

四、下一件想要的东西

拿到盼了很久的东西，有时确实会高兴。叔本华盯着的，却是高兴过后那个很快回来的声音：然后呢？

没有得到时，缺少让人难受；得到了，满足未必长久。新的目标又来，或者暂时什么都不缺，反而无聊起来。他用钟摆写这种来回：一边是痛苦，一边是无聊。

今天的读者不难找到自己的例子。等一个回复，争一次认可，买到一件以为会改变生活的东西。等待结束了，等待的本领却还在，很快替自己找到新的对象。

这个比喻抓住了人的一种处境，却不必成为对每一段人生的总判决。照顾一个人、学会一门手艺、一起度过一个普通下午，其中的价值，不能只因它不会带来永久满足就被取消。

没有永恒的满意，不等于所有满意都是假的；没有欲望的终点，也不等于每一种欲望都只能使人受害。我的保留在这里：诊断可以很深，仍然可能把它照亮的那一面，说成了人的全部。

前一篇的休谟会暂时离开书桌，去吃饭、下双陆棋，再回来思考。叔本华则追问，饭后重新响起的那个“还想要”怎么办。两个人不是一个看穿世界、一个拒绝承认；他们把注意力放在了不同的难处。

五、不只是暂时忘记

叔本华的回答，先把我们带进艺术。听一段音乐，望着一幅画，忽然有一会儿不再问：这对我有什么用，我能从中拿走什么？

那不是单纯让自己开心。他设想一种不再由个人利害指挥的观看。艺术使我们暂时从追逐中抽身，所看的东西不再只是满足欲望的工具。音乐在他的理论中地位尤其特殊，被理解为对意志的直接表达，而不只是描摹某个外在对象。

这可以和西田几多郎放在一起读，却不能说两者结构完全相同。叔本华仍以纯粹认识主体及其对象来说明审美；西田的问题另有出发处。相近的体验，不能自动兑换成同一套学说。

音乐结束，事情还得处理，欲望也可能回来。艺术没有把一生永久修好。但一次不为占有而看的经验，仍然可能让人知道，自己并非每时每刻都只能追逐。

而他的伦理不只剩下欣赏艺术。还有同情：别人的痛，不必先变成我的损失，才值得我停下来。他把这种不以自身利益为唯一中心的回应，放在道德的重要位置，也把关切伸向动物。

设想有人在你面前摔倒。你伸手，不只是因为以后想得到回报，也不必先把宇宙的道理想完。这里说的是他的伦理思路，不是证明一切帮助都毫无复杂动机。但它让“痛苦”不再只是我自己的问题。

他还谈到更彻底的禁欲和意志的否定。这不是买一本书，学一句“不想要了”，就能完成的心理技巧；也不是一句要求受苦者停止生活的命令。他试图描述欲求不再主宰人的转变，是否说通，可以继续争论。

因此，不能把他写成只会望着怪兽叹气的人。艺术中的暂歇、对别人的同情、对解脱的追求，在他那里并不相同。尤其第二件事，会把我们从自己的苦带回别人面前。

六、不能让痛苦自动有道理

现在回到黑格尔。把他写成“正反合，一步比一步好”，再让叔本华一句话拆掉，这场争论未免赢得太容易了。黑格尔的辩证法不是一张三格流程图，也不是每场灾难都值得欢迎。

但他们之间确实有很深的分歧。黑格尔赋予历史中的理性与自由发展重要地位；叔本华不相信，历史换了布景，就意味着人从欲求和苦难里不断向上走。

一场战争结束，另一代人又被动员；一个人终于得到权力，又开始怕失去。他让我们留意，变化中的重复，不要仅凭故事一直向后写，就以为生活一定向上升。

我愿意站在他的这个提醒旁边：不能对受苦的人说，你的痛正好是历史必须支付的费用，然后把账合上。一个大解释即使能安排痛苦的位置，也未必回答了那个正在痛的人。

但拒绝自动进步，不等于必须否认所有真实的改善。少一次羞辱，多一个可以申诉的地方，一项减少伤害的制度，即使没有把人类永久治好，也不因此毫无意义。

我们不需要在“所有苦难都有道理”和“一切努力都没有价值”之间二选一。两种说法都太容易替当事人把话说完。历史是否前进要看具体发生了什么，人的痛苦也不能只用来给某个体系作证。

七、地下室不是同一间

读到“理智常在为欲求效劳”，会想到弗洛伊德。这是有意义的联想。我们不总是先弄清理由，再决定想要什么；有时想要早已在那里，解释后来才赶到。

不过，叔本华的世界意志，不等于弗洛伊德的无意识。前者是对世界本性的说明，后者在精神分析里涉及冲突、压抑、梦和症状等问题。不能把一个词换成另一个词，就宣布他们其实说的是同一件事。

影响关系也不能写得太直。弗洛伊德在1925年的自述中承认思想上的相近，同时说自己很晚才读叔本华，不愿把那些相近都归因于阅读。这是他的自我报告，不是思想传播史从此结案，但至少不是一封简单的师承证明。

“地下室”可以留下，作为本文的比喻：意识里拿得出的说明，不一定穷尽正在推动我们的东西。却不能因此断言，理性主体完全是假的，每个理由都只是事后编造。

两个人也不是一个只看、一个保证能治。叔本华有伦理和解脱问题；精神分析的解释与疗效也需要各自接受检验，不能因为比喻里装了一盏灯，就宣布地下室已经照得通明。

更贴近日常的问法可能是：我说这是为了你好时，有没有也在保护自己的控制感？看见动机复杂，不必让人放弃行动。它可以使行动多一层检查：别把自己还没认清的欲望，先交给别人承担。

八、书页与生活之间

叔本华认真阅读印度思想，包括经译本接触的《奥义书》，也持续关注佛教。在当时的欧洲哲学环境里，这很重要。不过，把他封为第一个认真读东方思想的西方哲学家，没有必要，也经不起那样简单的排序。

更不能把佛教当作他的意志论换了一套名字。佛教关于渴爱、苦及其止息的讨论，与他的思想可以相互照亮，却不因此拥有同一个形而上学基础。“色”也不是“世界都是幻象”的另一个译名。

早期佛教的中道明确拒绝沉溺感官享乐和自我折磨两个极端。修行不是把所有愿望一律压死。求知、照顾别人、认真练习，不能仅因其中有意愿，就一概与使人困住的渴爱等同起来。

那叔本华自己过得怎样？他没有按自己描绘的圣者道路生活。晚年在法兰克福写作、散步、听音乐，也在意舒适和名声。一个研究欲望的人，仍然有欲望。这让理论与生活的距离变得可见。

还有更不能轻放的事：他在柏林与一位女邻居发生肢体冲突，后来被判长期支付款项。传记里的一桩纠纷，对受伤的人却不是谈资。不能因为哲学家的脾气很有故事性，就把伤害说成可爱的怪癖，更不能拿洞见替他免责。

可是，他没有做到，并不能证明谁都做不到；他过得舒服，也不等于别人受苦是假的。更不能据此宣布他比佛陀诚实。把一个人的失败自动变成理论胜利，会使理论永远没有接受反驳的机会。

我更愿意留下一个不替他开脱、也不替他封圣的结论：看见痛苦很深，和对眼前的人好一点，仍是两件需要分别去做的事。他写过同情，所以这份要求也应当落到他自己身上。

九、狗没有往下看

1860年9月21日，叔本华在法兰克福去世，七十二岁。这时他已经得到迟来的声誉。1851年出版《附录与补遗》时，他六十三岁，不是七十多岁才突然第一次被看见。

后来的读者越来越多，进入哲学、文学和音乐各个领域。尼采、瓦格纳等人的回应，使他的书有了他不可能亲自安排的后续。但“全欧洲都知道他”仍然只是夸张，不能拿来填满早年的冷清。

他身边有贵宾犬。狗跟着他，进入他有规律的散步和独居生活。它不需要先懂他的哲学，才成为生活的一部分。一个思想家愿意照顾动物，也不能自动抵消他与人相处时的问题。

以下是这套系列的桥头想象，不是史料里的场面。叔本华来到桥上，先低头看桥面下面，仿佛那里有什么一直在动。

他提醒大家：别只看自己写得漂亮的理由。争胜、占有、害怕失去，也许比理由更早把方向定好了。桥上的人可以听进去，不必因此同意万物都已被“意志”一个词解释完。

休谟把双陆棋放在一旁，问他要不要坐下。叔本华没有马上答。他还想看清下面的东西，还想告诉那些忙着向前走的人，脚下并不那么安稳。

狗却没有往下看。它看着他。

在这个由我想象的停顿里，问题小了，也近了。不是世界有没有最后的出口，而是身边这个依赖他的生命，现在需要什么。水，散步，还是有人把目光从远处收回来？

看见底下有东西，是他的力量。但不管底下是什么，眼前的生命并不因此变得次要。叔本华不必先战胜整个意志，才低下手来，回应身边的等待。

I. The Same Hour

In 1820, the thirty-two-year-old Schopenhauer qualified to lecture at the University of Berlin. He scheduled his course against Hegel's principal lectures. It was less a cautious newcomer's arrangement than a challenge written into the timetable.

The audience was small, not nonexistent. Nor did the disappointment end all his attempts at university teaching. He returned to Berlin several years later and tried again, without establishing the academic career he had hoped for.

We can imagine the embarrassment: you are certain you have something important to say, and the students choose another room. But imagining a feeling is not discovering every motive. His later attacks on Hegel cannot all be diagnosed as echoes of that one humiliation.

Those attacks were fierce and persistent. Their volume does not settle their merits. Unpopularity does not prove a thinker wrong; feeling misunderstood does not certify that he understands more than everyone else. Both shortcuts let us avoid reading.

Behind the invective lies a question worth staying with. Once we have constructed a magnificent, intelligible account of the world, have we really accounted for its hunger, struggle and pain? Where does the explanation leave the person who is suffering?

Schopenhauer suspects that something is already pushing us before our reasons arrive. That suspicion applies inside his own sparsely attended classroom. He wants us to stop being driven by wanting. He also very much wants to be heard.

II. How a World Appears

His principal work, The World as Will and Representation, appeared around the turn of 1818–1819, with 1819 on its title page. Sales were disappointing. Its eventual reputation should not be projected backward onto the young author's desk.

Its opening sentence announces that the world is my representation. That does not simply mean everything is imaginary, or that I can abolish the table by shutting my eyes. It asks how a world becomes available to a knowing subject in the first place.

There are things perceived and someone perceiving them. Space, time and causality allow experience to appear as connected objects and events. We cannot first leave every condition of knowing behind, then inspect our knowledge from a lookout beyond the world.

Here Schopenhauer follows Kant, though Kant's position is not adequately rendered as a curtain with a notice saying do not ask. Kant investigates the scope and limits of possible knowledge. He does not prohibit thought from continuing at the boundary.

Schopenhauer still wants another route. We are not merely eyes surveying a scene. We belong to what we survey. Perhaps there is one place where our access is not exclusively that of an observer looking from outside.

That place is the body. When you lift a glass, the hand is not simply another moving object in your field of vision. Its movement is also something you are doing. The difference is ordinary enough to overlook.

III. Reaching for the Glass

Seen from outside, an arm rises, fingers close, a glass leaves the table. Experienced from within, you are reaching to drink. Schopenhauer treats these not as two events joined by a causal link but as one act known in different ways.

This is not a neuroscience experiment demonstrating that muscles move milliseconds before a conscious decision. His question concerns our twofold acquaintance with embodied action, not a race between a thought and a nerve signal. The distinction matters to the route he takes next.

He calls what is known inwardly will, then makes an enormous extension: this becomes his key to the inner nature of the world. Growing plants, feeding animals and natural forces enter the same interpretation. The extension is metaphysics, not a measurement obtained by taking one's pulse.

Will here does not mean individual determination or admirable perseverance. Particular acts can have purposes; the underlying will has no final goal whose achievement would bring striving permanently to rest. It is not a rational designer planning the whole.

A hungry beast is a vivid image for it. But the beast is our metaphor, not an organism Schopenhauer discovered beneath the universe. We can recognise the force of the description and still question the move from our own willing to the nature of everything.

What makes him difficult to dismiss is also an experience we recognise: sometimes, while we are still delivering a beautifully ordered explanation, our bodies and emotions have already disclosed what we want. The explanation has not invented the desire.

IV. The Next Thing

Getting something long desired can make us genuinely happy. Schopenhauer listens for the voice that follows too soon: and now? The possession arrives, but the machinery of wanting has not agreed to retire.

Before attainment there is lack; afterward, satisfaction may not last. Another object presents itself, or nothing seems missing and boredom takes over. His pendulum swings between pain and boredom, each promising relief from the other without providing lasting rest.

A reader today can supply the details: waiting for a reply, competing for recognition, buying something that seems about to transform life. One wait ends. The capacity to wait anxiously remains, ready to recruit a new object.

The image captures a predicament. It need not become a verdict on every human activity. Caring for someone, learning a craft, spending an ordinary afternoon together: their value cannot be cancelled merely because they do not produce permanent satisfaction.

A pleasure that ends is not thereby counterfeit. Nor does the absence of a final destination for desire make every desire destructive. This is where I hesitate: a penetrating diagnosis can still mistake the part it illuminates for the entirety of a life.

In the preceding essay, Hume leaves his desk for dinner and backgammon, then returns to inquiry. Schopenhauer asks what to do with the wanting that returns after dinner. They need not be cast as the man who sees and the man who refuses to see. Their attention rests on different difficulties.

V. More Than Forgetting for a While

His first answer takes us into art. Listening to music or looking at a painting, we may briefly stop asking what use this has for us, what advantage we can extract, what we can take away as our own.

The point is not simply to feel good. He envisages attention no longer governed by individual interests. What we contemplate ceases to be merely an instrument of desire. Music has a distinctive place in this account: he understands it as an expression of will itself, not just a depiction of external objects.

This can be read beside Nishida, but resemblance is not identity. Schopenhauer still describes aesthetic contemplation through a pure knowing subject and its object; Nishida's inquiry begins elsewhere. Similar experiences do not automatically yield interchangeable philosophical explanations.

The music ends. Work remains, and wanting may return. Art has not permanently repaired a life. Nevertheless, a moment of looking without possessing can disclose that pursuit is not the only relation to things available to us.

His ethics also contains something an account limited to art and renunciation would miss: compassion. Another's pain need not first become my loss before it matters. He gives this response beyond exclusive self-interest a central moral place, extending concern to animals as well.

Imagine someone falling in front of you. You reach out without first calculating future repayment or completing a theory of the universe. This illustrates his ethical concern; it does not prove that every helpful act has unmixed motives. Pain has become more than a problem about me.

He also describes ascetic renunciation and the denial of will. These are not a quick technique acquired by repeating that one wants nothing, and this essay does not offer them as instructions to stop living. Whether his account makes sense remains a serious philosophical question.

So he is not merely a spectator sighing at a monster. Aesthetic respite, compassion and the aspiration to release are distinct concerns. The second, especially, brings us back from contemplating our own distress to attending to someone else's.

VI. Pain Is Not Automatically Justified

Return to Hegel. If we reduce him to thesis, antithesis, synthesis, with everything improving at each step, Schopenhauer wins much too cheaply. Hegel's dialectic is not a three-box diagram, nor a recommendation to welcome every catastrophe.

Their disagreement is nevertheless profound. Hegel gives the historical development of reason and freedom a central place. Schopenhauer distrusts the idea that a change of historical scenery necessarily means an ascent out of wanting and suffering.

One war ends; another generation is mobilised. A person acquires power and begins fearing its loss. Schopenhauer asks us to notice repetition within change, instead of assuming that because the story continues its characters must be getting somewhere better.

I want to stand beside this warning. We cannot tell someone in pain that their suffering is history's necessary expenditure, then close the accounts. Even an explanation that locates suffering within a whole may fail to answer the person enduring it.

Yet rejecting automatic progress does not require denying every improvement. Less humiliation, an opportunity to appeal, an institution that reduces harm: these matter even if they do not cure humanity once and for all. Incompleteness does not make them nothing.

We need not choose between all suffering is justified and no effort has value. Both finish the sentence for the people involved. Historical improvement needs particular evidence; someone's pain should not become merely a witness called to vindicate a system.

VII. Not the Same Basement

The thought that intellect often serves wanting brings Freud to mind. The association is useful. We do not invariably establish our reasons and only then discover our desires. Sometimes the desire has been waiting while its explanation is still on the way.

But Schopenhauer's world-will is not Freud's unconscious. One belongs to an account of reality's inner nature; the other figures in psychoanalytic questions about conflict, repression, dreams and symptoms. Swapping the labels does not make the projects identical.

The line of influence is less straightforward than a simple inheritance. In his 1925 autobiographical account, Freud acknowledged similarities while saying he read Schopenhauer late and did not attribute the parallels to that reading. This is Freud's report about himself, not the last word on intellectual transmission.

We can keep the basement as this essay's metaphor. The explanation available to consciousness need not exhaust what moves us. That does not establish that rational agency is wholly fictitious or that every reason is invented after the event.

Nor is the contrast simply that one watches while the other reliably cures. Schopenhauer has ethical and emancipatory ambitions; psychoanalytic explanations and therapeutic claims require their own examination. Putting a lamp in a metaphorical basement does not establish how much we can see.

A smaller question may be more useful: when I insist I am doing this for your good, am I also protecting my own control? Complex motives need not paralyse action. They can make us check whether someone else is being asked to carry a desire we have not acknowledged.

VIII. Between the Page and the Life

Schopenhauer took Indian thought seriously, reading the Upanishads through translation and continuing to engage with Buddhism. This mattered within his European philosophical setting. It does not require the untenable accolade of being the first Western philosopher ever to take Eastern thought seriously.

Buddhism is not his philosophy of will under different names. Its treatment of craving, suffering and cessation can illuminate his concerns without sharing the same metaphysical foundation. Rūpa, material form, is not another word for the claim that the whole world is an illusion.

The early Buddhist middle way rejects both sensual indulgence and self-torment. Practice is not the indiscriminate suppression of every aspiration. Learning, caring and making an effort cannot simply be equated with binding craving because they involve wanting something.

How, meanwhile, did Schopenhauer live? He did not follow the saintly path he described. In Frankfurt he wrote, walked and listened to music, caring also about comfort and recognition. A philosopher of desire continued to desire. The distance between teaching and living remains visible.

There is a graver matter: a physical altercation with a female neighbour in Berlin led to a long-term payment obligation. An incident in a biography was an injury in someone's life. A philosopher's temper must not turn harm into an endearing eccentricity, and insight offers no exemption from responsibility.

Yet his failure to practise an ideal does not demonstrate that nobody could practise it. His comfort does not make another person's suffering unreal. Nor does it prove him more honest than the Buddha. Turning every personal failure into theoretical victory would make the theory impossible to challenge.

I prefer a conclusion that neither canonises nor excuses him. Seeing suffering clearly and treating the person in front of you better remain separate tasks. He wrote about compassion. Its demands must also be allowed to reach its author.

IX. The Dog Is Looking Elsewhere

Schopenhauer died in Frankfurt on 21 September 1860, aged seventy-two. Recognition had finally come. When Parerga and Paralipomena appeared in 1851, he was sixty-three, not a man already in his seventies receiving his first public notice.

His readership spread into philosophy, literature and music. Responses by Nietzsche, Wagner and others gave his books futures he could not arrange. That does not make it literally true that all Europe knew his name. Late recognition cannot retroactively populate every empty seat.

He had poodles. A dog entered his walks and his solitary routines without needing to understand his philosophy first. Affection for animals belongs in the portrait; it does not automatically cancel difficulties or harm in his dealings with people.

Now we enter this series' imagined bridge scene, not a historical record. Schopenhauer arrives and immediately looks underneath, as if something down there has never stopped moving. He is less interested in the view than in what supports it.

He warns the others not to admire only their well-phrased reasons. Rivalry, possessiveness and fear of loss may have set the direction earlier. They can hear the warning without accepting that the single word will has now explained everything.

Hume sets out his backgammon board and offers a seat. Schopenhauer does not answer at once. He still wants to see beneath the surface, still wants those walking confidently forward to notice that the footing is less secure than they think.

The dog does not look beneath the bridge. It looks at him.

In this pause, which I have imagined, the question becomes smaller and nearer. Not whether the world has a final exit, but what this dependent creature needs now. Water, a walk, or someone's attention returning from a great distance?

Seeing something underneath is his strength. Whatever lies there, the life beside him does not become secondary. He need not first conquer the whole of will before lowering his hand and answering that waiting presence.
