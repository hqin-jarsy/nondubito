第四篇 Essay IV

共凿正和

Mutual Chiseling, Positive-Sum

Han Qin (秦汉) · 2026

设想这样一个法庭：原告是做错了事的人，被告是受了伤害的人。

这不是颠倒黑白的戏谑。这是道德律第四篇提出的机构设计，有其严格的逻辑基础：在真正以立法主体方式运作的共同体里，当一个人无意中伤害了另一个人，自我判决已经在当事人内部发生了。受害方不需要控诉，加害方不需要被外部宣判有罪——加害方自己已经宣判了。这个法庭要做的，是判决之后的事情：补偿的协调。

于是加害方成为原告，自我起诉。受害方成为被告，对出席有完全的自由——可以完全不出现，可以只是观察，可以充分参与。陪审团不裁决罪责，只做事实层面的观察陈述。最关键的是，有一个"公被诉人"的机构位置：任何受害方不接受的补偿，流向公被诉人，进入公共资金池。这个设计的结构之美在于：受害方只要选择完全不出席，程序就在没有受害方参与的情况下完成——加害方自我起诉，全部补偿流向公共，受害方不需要承受任何重演。在这个程序里，借助它伤害受害方，从结构上讲是不可能的。

这个设计里有一处容易被忽略的语法精度，值得单独说清楚：痛感展示，不是受害方的动作，是加害方陈述的一部分。加害方作为立法主体自我起诉，陈述包含两层，缺一不可——承认自己的过错："我做了X"；陈述造成的伤害："X给你带来了Y的伤害"。后一层，才是"痛感展示"——它不是受害方站出来指控，而是加害方主动把自己观察到的、造成于对方的伤害说出来，这本身就是把对方当作目的的具体动作。控告，则是完全不同的语法：控告预设一个外部权威能够裁定有罪与否，把识认层面的事件转成对抗。在这套道德庭程序里，没有人做控告——加害方不需要控告自己，因为自我判决已经发生；受害方如果试图控告加害方，这个动作本身，就已经把事件重新对抗化了，就已经是退出15DD（自我立法主体）运作的具体显现。这个区分是整个程序设计的守门人：一旦你分不清"我说出了你受的伤"和"我指控你有罪"，程序的全部结构之美就无从谈起。这也是为什么公被诉人这个位置，不只是一个技术性的兜底安排，而是整套防滥用设计的脊梁。任何想要恶意利用这套程序的人，面对的都是同一堵墙：程序能导向的最坏结果，是赔偿全部流入公共资金池——利用它，得不到任何东西。把"程序被滥用时的最坏结果"设计成制度上干净的结果，等于提前堵死了所有"通过程序谋取不正当利益"的路径，不需要逐一预判和拦截每一种可能的滥用手法。

从道德庭再往深处走，到第五篇，问的是一个更基本的问题：为什么两个立法主体真诚地互相凿磨，结果是正和而不是零和？

通常的直觉是，争论是零和的，甚至负和的——你的观点赢了，我的就输了。但这个直觉假设了一件事：被争论的对象是一个有限的资源池，双方在争夺它。第五篇的回答是：不是这样的。被争论的对象——无论是一个哲学问题、一个具体事件的诠释、还是一个审美判断——是物自体，是任何有限的言说都无法穷尽的东西。你凿出一个面，我凿出另一个面，我们的互动又凿出第三个面——这第三个面是我们任何一个人单独都无法凿出的。而物自体本身，余项始终存在。更精细的凿磨，不是使余项缩小，而是使边界的维度提升，就像越精密测量的海岸线越长。

这也解释了14DD（自目的主体）法庭真正的代价是什么。14DD处理余项的方式是消除：表述留有余项，就意味着表述不完整，意味着争议没有解决，需要外部权威下判决，把悬而未决的部分强行关闭。但这份"关闭"是压抑加位移，不是真正的消除——败诉一方未被承认的陈述、被压下去的立法主体性，都没有真的消失，只是被移到了制度看不见的角落，作为势能积累下来，在某个不可预知的时刻，以怨恨、系统性的不公感、或者更长期的制度侵蚀释放出来。法庭看似成功地收束了案子，代价是把余项变成一颗定时的应力炸弹。15DD的道德庭走的是另一条路：尊重余项，而不是试图消灭它。承认表述留有余项，就是承认自己的理解有极限，承认对方的视角有对方独有的价值，承认这场对话不会是最终定论——这份承认本身，就是把对方当作目的的具体动作。余项不需要被清空，只需要有地方安放：公被诉人接住了那些不被接受的补偿，道德庭的开放性接住了那些不被认领的余项。没有强制关闭，也就没有被位移进阴暗面、等待某天爆炸的应力。

第二个层面：主体性是活动，不是资源。资源遵循守恒：你多一点，我少一点，零和是结构性保证。活动遵循共振：两个人一起唱歌，没有耗尽对方的歌唱能力，反而共同振荡产生了更大的系统能量。立法主体性——言说、回应、内省、判断——都是活动的动词，不是名词。凿磨使它更活跃，不使用使它退化。一个长期不言说自身观点的自我，逐渐失去言说的能力。

两者合在一起：两个15DD主体真诚互凿，操作的对象是物自体（不可穷尽），驱动的是主体性活动（活动共振不耗竭）。两个人都变得更活跃，不是一个赢一个输。这是本体论层面的正和，不是分配了什么好处，而是两人的立法主体性都变得更充沛。孔子见了老子之后说：鸟，我知道它能飞；鱼，我知道它能游；龙，吾不能知。今日见老子，其犹龙乎。这不是谦辞，而是识认——他看到了老子的言说有一个余项，是他自己的框架无法穷尽的。互凿正和的历史切片，完整保存在记录里。

孔子和老子的相遇留下的是各自返程、继续沿着自己的路径发展的分流式正和。历史里还留着另一种收束形态的记录：1401年，佛罗伦萨，洗礼堂青铜门设计竞赛。二十四岁的布鲁内莱斯基和十五岁的多纳泰罗都参加了，最后败给了吉贝尔蒂——布鲁内莱斯基为此痛苦了很久，但两人都承认吉贝尔蒂的方案确实更好，没有去质疑评委的判断。此后布鲁内莱斯基转向建筑，多纳泰罗留在雕塑，各自的艺术立法走向不同的方向，但两人的友谊和相互凿磨持续了一辈子——一四零二年到一四零四年，两人结伴去罗马，一起挖掘、研究古典建筑与雕塑的遗迹，当地人叫他们"寻宝人"。大约一四一零年，多纳泰罗雕成一具木刻的基督像，把基督塑造成一个有血有肉、情感强烈的受苦之人。布鲁内莱斯基看过之后说了一句流传下来的评价："你雕的是个农民，不是基督。"这不是否定，是说出了自己对基督形象的不同理解——他没有争辩，也没有要求多纳泰罗改。他自己回家，用自己的理解另雕了一具基督像：理想化的形体，古典的比例，神性的庄严；然后请多纳泰罗吃饭，把自己的作品随意地摆在显眼的地方。多纳泰罗进门看见，怔住了，留下了同样流传下来的一句回应："雕基督的天分给了你，雕农民的天分给了我。"这不是让步，是对彼此专长的确认——他没有收回自己的作品，也没有修改自己的雕像，只是承认了对方立起来的那一面，是自己无法替代的。两人后来一起在卢卡合作过具体项目，彼此的手法也持续互相渗透：布鲁内莱斯基发现的线性透视，影响了多纳泰罗极浅浮雕里表现纵深的方式；多纳泰罗的写实主义，也渗进了布鲁内莱斯基后期建筑里雕塑与结构的融合。瓦萨里在《艺苑名人传》里专门记下了这段友谊。两人后来并列被写进艺术史，作为文艺复兴早期的奠基者——谁也没有把谁比下去。

Imagine a court in which the person who committed the harm is the plaintiff, and the person who was harmed is the defendant.

This is not a conceptual joke. It is the institutional design proposed by Paper Four of the Moral Law series, and it has strict logical foundations. In a genuine community of 15DD legislative subjects (self-legislating subjects), when one person inadvertently harms another, internal self-judgment has already occurred. The perpetrator has already judged themselves. What the court handles is not guilt-determination but what comes after: coordination of compensation.

So the perpetrator becomes the plaintiff — prosecuting themselves. The victim becomes the defendant — with full freedom over their attendance. They may not attend at all. They may observe only. They may fully participate. The jury does not adjudicate guilt; it makes factual observations only. And there is a crucial position called the Public Defendant: any compensation the victim does not accept flows to this collective institutional position, into a public fund. The structural elegance of this design is that if the victim chooses complete non-attendance, the procedure concludes without them — the perpetrator self-prosecutes, all compensation flows to the public fund, and the victim bears nothing. Using this procedure to harm the victim is, structurally, impossible.

There is a precise grammatical distinction embedded in this design that is easy to miss and worth drawing out on its own: pain-displaying is not the victim's action. It belongs to the perpetrator's own statement. A perpetrator, self-prosecuting as a legislative subject, must include two inseparable layers — acknowledging the wrong ("I did X") and stating the harm it caused ("X caused you Y harm"). That second layer is what pain-displaying actually is — not the victim stepping forward to accuse, but the perpetrator voluntarily naming the harm they themselves observed they caused the other person. That act, by itself, is already a concrete instance of taking the other as an end. Accusation is a completely different grammar. It presupposes an external authority capable of ruling on guilt, and it converts a recognition-level event into an adversarial one. In this procedure, no one performs accusation: the perpetrator has no need to accuse themselves, since self-judgment has already occurred; and if the victim attempts to accuse the perpetrator, that very act re-adversarializes the event — which is itself the concrete form that exiting 15DD operation takes. This distinction is the gatekeeper for the entire design. Lose the difference between "I am naming the harm you suffered" and "I am charging you with guilt," and the whole structural elegance of the procedure has nothing left to stand on. This is also why the Public Defendant position is not merely a technical fallback, but the backbone of the entire abuse-prevention design. Anyone attempting to exploit the procedure runs into the same wall: the worst outcome the procedure can produce is that all compensation flows into the public pool — abuse yields nothing to the person attempting it. Designing the worst-case outcome of the procedure to be institutionally clean closes off every route to illegitimate gain through the procedure in advance, without needing to anticipate and block each possible form of abuse one by one.

Moving deeper — from the moral court to Paper Five — the question becomes more fundamental: why is genuine mutual chiseling between two legislative subjects structurally positive-sum rather than zero-sum?

The usual intuition is that argument is zero-sum or worse — your view wins; mine loses. But this intuition smuggles in an assumption: that what is being argued about is a finite pool of content that the two parties are dividing. Paper Five's answer is that this assumption is false. The object of genuine mutual engagement — a philosophical question, an interpretation of an event, an aesthetic judgment — is the thing-in-itself, something no finite articulation can exhaust. You chisel out one face; I chisel out another; what emerges from our interaction is a third face that neither of us could have articulated alone. And the thing-in-itself as a whole? The remainder always persists. More precise engagement does not shrink the remainder — it elevates the dimension of the boundary, exactly like how more precisely measured coastlines are longer, not shorter.

This also explains what the real cost of a 14DD (self-purposing subject) court actually is. 14DD's way of handling remainder is to eliminate it: if an articulation still leaves a remainder, that means the articulation is incomplete, which means the dispute is unresolved, which requires an external authority to hand down a verdict and force the open question closed. But that closure is repression plus displacement, not real elimination. The losing party's unrecognized account, their suppressed legislative subjectivity — none of it actually vanishes. It is simply moved to a part of the institution that stays invisible, where it accumulates as potential energy, ready to be released at some unpredictable moment as resentment, a diffuse sense of systemic unfairness, or slow institutional erosion. The court appears to have successfully closed the case; the price is turning the remainder into a stress bomb with no fixed fuse. The 15DD moral court takes a different path: respecting the remainder rather than trying to eliminate it. To acknowledge that an articulation still leaves a remainder is to acknowledge that your own understanding has limits, that the other's perspective carries value uniquely their own, that this exchange will not be the final word — and that acknowledgment is itself a concrete act of taking the other as an end. The remainder does not need to be emptied out. It only needs somewhere to go: the Public Defendant absorbs the compensation no one will accept, and the court's own openness absorbs the remainder no one claims. Nothing is forced shut, so nothing gets displaced into the dark to wait for the day it detonates.

The other layer: subjectivity is an activity, not a resource. Resources obey conservation — if you have more, I have less, and zero-sum is structurally guaranteed. Activities obey coherent resonance — two people singing together do not deplete each other's capacity to sing; they jointly produce greater system energy. Legislative subjectivity — articulating, responding, doing internal review, judging — is all activity verbs, not nouns. Chiseling makes it more active. Disuse makes it atrophy. A self that has long stopped articulating its own view gradually loses the capacity to articulate.

Putting both together: two 15DD subjects genuinely chiseling each other are operating on an inexhaustible object (the thing-in-itself) with activity-mode subjectivity (which resonates rather than depletes). Both become more active as legislative subjects. Neither wins; neither loses — both develop. Confucius, after meeting Laozi, said: "Birds — I know they can fly. Fish — I know they can swim. The dragon — I cannot know it. Today I saw Laozi. He is like a dragon." This is not flattery. It is recognition — Confucius saw that Laozi's articulation had a remainder his own framework could not exhaust. A complete historical instance of mutual chiseling, preserved in the record.

Confucius and Laozi's meeting left behind a divergent form of positive-sum — each returning to develop along their own path. History preserves another shape this closure can take. Florence, 1401: the design competition for the baptistery's bronze doors. Brunelleschi, twenty-four, and Donatello, fifteen, both entered — and both lost to Lorenzo Ghiberti. Brunelleschi was bitterly disappointed, but both men agreed Ghiberti's design really was the better one, and neither contested the judges' call. Afterward, Brunelleschi turned to architecture and Donatello stayed with sculpture — their artistic legislation diverging in direction — but their friendship and their mutual chiseling continued for the rest of their lives. Between 1402 and 1404, the two traveled together to Rome to dig through and study the ruins of classical architecture and sculpture; locals called them treasure hunters. Around 1410, Donatello finished a wooden crucifix, carving Christ as a real, suffering, emotionally raw human being. Brunelleschi, on seeing it, offered the line that survives to this day: "You have carved a peasant, not a Christ." This was not a dismissal — it was Brunelleschi naming his own, different understanding of what Christ's figure should be. He did not argue the point or ask Donatello to change the work. He went home and carved his own Christ from his own understanding instead: idealized form, classical proportion, divine dignity — then invited Donatello to dinner and left the carving casually in plain sight. Donatello walked in, saw it, and stopped short. His reply survives too: "To you it is given to do Christs, and to me, peasants." This was not concession. It was recognition of each other's specialization — he never withdrew his own work, never revised his own carving; he simply acknowledged that what Brunelleschi had made held something he himself could not have replaced. The two later collaborated on specific projects in Lucca, and their techniques kept bleeding into each other's work: Brunelleschi's discovery of linear perspective shaped how Donatello rendered depth in his extremely shallow relief carving; Donatello's naturalism found its way into the sculptural integration of Brunelleschi's later architecture. Vasari, in his Lives of the Artists, recorded their friendship specifically. History now reads the two of them side by side, as founding figures of the early Renaissance — neither eclipsing the other.
