两个同名的构

Two Constructs with the Same Name

Han Qin (秦汉)

一 同一个名字，两种比赛

一场以破世界纪录为目标的一万米，和一场奥运会一万米决赛，叫同一个名字，进同一张世界纪录表。

可是这两场比赛，几乎在每一个可以控制的变量上都不一样。

破纪录那一场，赛事组织者可以合法地雇佣配速员，可以预先设定每一圈的目标速度，可以使用获批的节奏灯，可以挑一条快的跑道，一个低的海拔，一个凉爽的时段，还可以在名次奖金之外单独设一笔时间奖或者纪录奖。运动员要做的，是尽可能长时间地待在挡风的，节奏稳定的位置上，把力气全部用在逼近那个预定的时间上。

锦标赛那一场，通常没有受雇的兔子。运动员要考虑位置、对手的能力、被包夹的风险，也要为最后冲刺保留储备；在设有预赛和半决赛的距离上，还会有晋级与多轮疲劳，而现代全球锦标赛一万米通常直接进行决赛。世界田径描述锦标赛一万米时也提到，运动员常先保存体力，最后几圈决胜。最合理的策略，经常不是从第一圈便把平均速度拉到最高，而是等待合适的提速时机。

这是两种常见而系统性不同的组织方式。一边更重视时间，一边更重视击败对手；实际提供的配速与信息条件也可能不同。它们可以使运动员选择不同的跑法，但并不因此各自适用一套完全相反的帮助规则。差别发生在同一个合法项目之内，也并不是每场比赛都整齐落在两端。

而世界田径，在它的现行规则里，没有把这两种比赛分成两个项目。

一万米仍是一万米。规则当然区分世界锦标赛、奥运会与其他层级的赛事，也规定比赛是否合法、哪些帮助允许、成绩怎样取得纪录资格。但它没有因为一场以追时间为主、另一场以争名次为主，就为同一距离设立两套独立的世界纪录项目。这里讨论的不命名，是没有分设纪录类别，不是规则从未给锦标赛一个名字。

这就是这一篇要讲的东西。有两个构，它们在实际运行中系统地不同，而它们共用一个名字，共用一张纪录表。构自己在官方的平台上承认它们不同，却在正式的规则里没有据此把它们分设为两个世界纪录项目，然后用一张统一的纪录表，把两者重新合并。

前面第十七篇讲的是，构备好了闭合的手段而选择不用。这一篇讲的是一件相邻的事:构看得见一个差异，有能力命名这个差异，却选择了不命名。

这个不命名，需要说准。官方介绍已经承认两种比赛常有不同节奏和策略，所以不能简单说机构没有注意到差别。但这些描述也不能替代一份明确讨论过分设纪录类别、随后决定拒绝的会议记录。本篇能看到的是结果：差异被描述了，却没有因此成为两套纪录项目。看见差异，与赋予差异独立的法律类别，是两件事。

二 这个论题要成立，得先收紧一个说法

在往下走之前，必须先把一个太强的说法收紧，否则整篇会站不住。

太强的说法是:纪录赛和锦标赛的成绩根本不能比较，它们是两项不同的运动。

这个说法过头了。规则和纪录制度，恰恰把它们当作同一个项目的，可以比较的时间。而且锦标赛也完全可能跑得极快，甚至创造世界纪录;纪录赛里也仍然包含真实的竞争。

2024年巴黎奥运会男子一千五百米就是一个明显的反例。那场比赛没有商业配速员，可是英格布里格森主动高速领跑，把整场比赛的节奏拉了起来，霍克以三分二十七秒六五夺冠，只比世界纪录慢一点六五秒。一场没有雇兔子的锦标赛决赛，被领跑的运动员从内部跑成了近乎纪录赛的样子。所以纪录赛和锦标赛之间，不是一道不可逾越的墙。

那么这个论题真正能站住的版本是什么。

它不是两个成绩根本不能比，而是:在同一个项目的名下，规则容许两种系统性不同的成绩生成机制存在;因此，如果直接把一个计时成绩，当作对同一种竞技能力的无条件测量，就会丢失赛事结构这一个关键的变量。

这个版本要弱一些，也硬得多。生理和赛事的数据支持生成机制不同这一半;而世界田径的规则制度，明确反对法律上不可比这另一半，因为只要都符合规则，两者仍然被计入同一个纪录体系。所以准确的说法不是两项不同的运动，而是:一个法律项目之下，两种高度稳定，目标函数不同的赛事生产结构。

守住这个收紧的版本，这一篇才不会滑向一个证据不支持的断言。下面所有的材料，都是在支持这个收紧的版本，而不是那个太强的版本。

这个收紧，其实划出了这一篇和一种常见的抱怨之间的界线。那种抱怨是，现在的纪录都是靠兔子和灯光和快鞋堆出来的，不算真本事。这一篇不是这个意思。这一篇不评判纪录赛的成绩算不算真本事，那些成绩是运动员真实跑出来的，兔子挡了风，人还是得自己迈腿。这一篇要说的，是一个更冷静，也更结构性的东西:当两种系统性不同的比赛共用一张表的时候，那张表上的一个数字，单看它自己，已经不能告诉你它是在哪一种结构里跑出来的了。这不是在贬低任何一个成绩，是在指出那张表丢掉了一个变量。

三 配速员是参赛者，不是场外服务

要理解这两个构，得先看清配速员在规则里到底是什么身份。而这个身份，和直觉不太一样。

现行的帮助规则，禁止的配速是这样几种:不参加同一场比赛的人提供的配速，已经被套圈，即将被套圈的运动员提供的配速，以及未经允许的技术装置提供的配速。

看清这条界线划在哪里。它划的不是你是不是在为别人配速。它划的是，你是不是这场比赛的合法参赛者，你是不是从一个正常的竞赛位置提供帮助。

道路赛的规则把这一点说得更直白。它直接规定，受雇于赛事组织者的配速员，是真正的参赛者，并要求他们和其他精英选手一起从枪响起跑。

所以一个合法的配速员，不是站在赛道外面提供服务的工作人员。他是一个法律身份上的参赛者，同时是一个经济功能上的配速员。他报名进入起跑名单，如果他跑完全程，原则上可以取得正式成绩，甚至获胜。世界田径的档案里就有本来担任配速员，最后却赢得了比赛的实例。

这个身份设计，是这两个构能够共用一张纪录表的制度基础。因为配速员在法律上是参赛者，所以一场有配速员的破纪录赛，在规则上仍然是一场正常的比赛，它的成绩就可以进入正式纪录。构没有把兔子当作赛外的工具，它把兔子变成了赛内的参赛者。于是那些由兔子帮助跑出的成绩，在法律上和没有兔子的成绩，没有区别。

这个身份安排，是共用纪录体系的一个重要条件。假如配速被列为禁止的外部帮助，相关成绩可能直接失去纪录资格；若另设辅助类别，才可能分开记账，两者都不是自动结果。现行规则把合规配速员放在参赛者中，同时限制套圈、轮换和场外介入。合法配速于是可以成为比赛内部的构成，而不是一出现就把赛事改成另一个纪录项目。

不合法的三种配速，反过来定义了这个身份的边界。赛外人员进来配速，不行，因为他不是参赛者。被套圈的人回来充当新鲜的配速员，不行，因为他制造了一种超出正常竞争的持续帮助。不在例外里的技术设备，不行。历史上真正敏感的，从来不是大家事先商量好了速度，而是能不能通过轮换，通过不断投入新鲜的跑者，或者通过外部的设备，制造出超过同场正常竞争的，持续的辅助。

四 班尼斯特那场，被否决的是前一年

配速员这个身份的微妙，在班尼斯特跑进四分钟这件事上，暴露得最清楚。而这件事，有一个流传很广的错误版本。

先说真实发生的。1954年5月6日，在牛津的伊夫利路，布拉舍负责早段领跑，查塔威随后接过节奏，班尼斯特最终独自完成最后的攻势，跑出三分五十九秒四。这场比赛被安排在牛津大学对业余田径协会的正式比赛框架里，不是一个只给班尼斯特单独计时的训练测试。

流传的错误版本是:1954年这个成绩跑出来之后，官方正式调查了它是不是一场真实的比赛，经过听证之后才批准。

这个版本不被材料支持。它高度可能是把另一件事，移植到了1954年。

真正遭到英国官方以非真实比赛为由否决的，是1953年，不是1954年。

1953年6月27日，在莫茨帕克，班尼斯特尝试跑进四分钟，跑出四分零二秒。那一次，麦克米伦先配速，而布拉舍采取了一个特别的安排:他先慢跑，让班尼斯特把自己套圈，然后再以比较新鲜的状态，重新回来给班尼斯特领跑。英国业余田径理事会随后拒绝承认这个成绩为英国纪录，核心的理由，就是这个项目不构成规则意义上的真实比赛。

这个1953年的先例，解释了为什么1954年的组织形式格外谨慎。1954年，两名帮手从枪响起就是同一场合法比赛里的运动员，没有再采用那个被套圈后以新鲜状态回来的办法。

所以这里必须把指控和裁决分开。指控是，布拉舍和查塔威事先承担的是服务班尼斯特的角色，未必以自己获胜为首要目的。裁决是，没有找到1954年英国理事会作出比赛不真实的正式否决，三分五十九秒四反而进入了正式的世界纪录序列。

而当事人自己的证词，恰好点破了这里面的张力。查塔威在第一人称的回忆里说，1954年之后，理事会没有胆量再去质疑他和布拉舍是不是真正的竞争者。他随后非常坦率地承认，从真实的取胜意图来说，他们当然不是。

这句话极其关键。主观目的上，查塔威和布拉舍就是配速，他们没打算赢。可是在法律的处理上，这个成绩没有遭到1953年那样的否决。一个名义上的参赛者，如果从一开始就没有赢得比赛的意图，那么真正的参赛者这个说法，就出现了语义上的张力。查塔威的坦白，恰好把这个张力暴露在了阳光下。

这里还要留一个诚实的空缺。1954年成绩被认可，可以确认；本篇尚未取得它获批的完整书面理由。两年的一个可见区别，是1954年没有再采用1953年那种让布拉舍被套圈后接上领跑的安排，而不是只有1954年的帮手才从同一场比赛的枪响开始。这个差别为理解认可结果提供了线索，却不能冒充已经找到的逐条裁决理由。

五 从雇一只兔子，到造一套生产系统

班尼斯特那个年代，配速还是几个朋友帮忙。而1990年代以来最明显的变化，不是配速员突然被发明，是破纪录这件事，被商业巡回赛制度化了。

1998年启动的黄金联赛，把奥斯陆，罗马，摩纳哥，苏黎世，布鲁塞尔，柏林这些一日赛，组织进了一个高奖金，电视化，精英化的连续商业体系。世界田径自己的黄金联赛纪录档案里，就列着一些在这些商业赛里创造的世界纪录，比如埃尔格鲁伊1998年在罗马跑出的一千五百米三分二十六秒零零。

这种纪录型的商业赛，有一套典型的组织模块，可以归纳出来。目标时间预先确定;从枪响起就在同一场比赛中的配速员，先后承担不同阶段的节奏;主将尽可能长时间地待在挡风，节奏稳定的位置;赛事选择低海拔，快跑道或者平坦的道路;根据天气预报选择更合适的起跑时段;在名次奖金之外，设置时间或者纪录的奖励。这些要素不是每一场都同时出现，但在精英一日赛和快速马拉松里反复出现。

这里要防止一个太强的说法。有人说，1990年代的破纪录赛都有一笔统一的世界纪录奖金，比如固定的五万美元。这个说法不能成立为一个统一的制度事实。奖金有好几层:系列的总奖金，本站的名次奖金，出场费，时间奖金，以及赛事或者赞助商另外单设的纪录奖。后面这几项，经常由赛事或者合同单独决定，而历史上的合同又未必公开。可以确认各种破纪录奖金，时间奖金确实存在，但不能由个别赛事，反推出有一张统一的世界纪录现金价目表。

然后是节奏灯。这个系统在2017年前后开发，2018年6月1日第一次在比赛里使用，2020年得到了世界田径的全面规则许可。在标准的四百米跑道内侧，布设大约四百个灯点，大约一米一个，软件按照目标时间驱动灯光向前推进。于是运动员可以把一张抽象的圈速表，变成一条持续可见的，在跑道上移动的空间位置。他不再需要自己在脑子里换算还差多少，他只要跟着那道光跑。

这道光做的事情，值得和前面第八篇那扇门对照一下。那里，泡沫垫这个物质条件，开启了背越式的可能。这里，节奏灯这个技术，开启的是另一种东西:它把运动员从一项一直属于他的任务里解放了出来，那就是自己判断速度。在节奏灯之前，一个追纪录的运动员，得靠自己的身体感觉，配速员的领跑和报圈的反馈，拼凑出自己现在跑得够不够快。这是一项技能，一项关于自我感知的技能。而节奏灯把这项技能，变成了跟着一道光这件简单的事。运动员省下的，不是体力，是一部分判断。这一部分判断该不该被外部的装置替代，恰恰是下一节那个争论的核心。

关于这道光，有一个流行的说法要拆掉:它是规则漏洞里的非法电子兔子。不是。世界田径的帮助规则，在禁止技术装置配速的同时，专门把表示比赛进程时间，包括相关纪录节奏的电子灯光，列为允许的例外。所以今天的法律结构，不是技术配速一律禁止，而是原则禁止，加上对特定的可视时间系统的明示豁免。

还有一个说法要收紧：这种灯光设备永远不能出现在锦标赛。2025年欧洲室内锦标赛确实引入了Wavelight，但欧洲田径明确区分了用途：预赛使用部分赛段的参考灯，决赛在选手接近相关纪录节奏时显示纪录灯，并非照搬钻石联赛为运动员持续设定目标速度的直接配速。这说明设备可以进入锦标赛，却不能据此说两种比赛的灯光帮助完全相同。具体用途与赛事实施，仍然重要。

六 那道光该不该算外部帮助

节奏灯该不该被允许，是一个双方论据都很清楚的争论，而世界田径最终选了其中一边。这一节把两边摆出来，不做裁定。

主张允许的一方说，这道光只是一个所有参赛者都看得见的进程时间信息，相当于把大屏幕，钟表和报圈的信息，搬到了跑道上，空间化了。它不推运动员，也不拉运动员，不为某一个运动员单独提供机械能量，而且规则透明，对所有人一视同仁。世界田径最终采取的，就是这个法律立场。

主张限制的一方说，一个持续可见的视觉目标，可以不断地消除运动员自己判断速度的误差，把一件传统上需要运动员，配速员和计时反馈共同完成的事情，也就是节奏控制，自动化了。有分析特别指出，靠着节奏灯跑出的一些纪录，圈速的离散度极小，几乎没有波动，而这使得纪录的历史可比性成了一个问题。因为过去的纪录保持者，是要自己承担一部分判断速度的任务的，而现在这部分任务被外包给了一道光。

这个争论不能只靠一个实验了结：实验可以估计特定条件下节奏灯的影响，却不能独自决定什么帮助应当允许。至于某一次世界纪录，鞋、天气、配速、身体状态同时在场，不能把它重新跑成其他条件完全一样的对照。我们可以用研究和模型提出有条件的估计，不能从最终时间直接拆出灯光贡献了几秒。精确归因的困难，不等于这个问题原则上不能研究。

这里值得注意的，是这个争论的真正性质。它不是在争这道光有没有帮助，帮助显然是有的。它争的是，这种帮助，是不是仍然属于这项运动内部应当接受的那种竞赛关系。这和配速员的争论，是同一个问题的两个版本。

七 同一个人，不同的比赛

现在看那张把两个构合并起来的纪录表，具体差异到底有多大。

女子五千米是最干净的一个例子。切贝特2025年那个纪录，五个一千米分段，最大的极差只有大约七点二二秒，而最快的那一段，恰好是最后一千米。也就是说，它不是一个机械节拍器那样从头到尾完全同速的东西，它是一个高度稳定的主体，加上一个有意识的末段加速。这正符合研究者说的纪录型长跑那种两头略快，中间平稳的结构。

把它和锦标赛比。同样是切贝特，2025年东京世锦赛女子五千米夺冠，成绩是十四分五十四秒三六，而她那个世界纪录是十三分五十八秒零六。她夺冠那场的最后四百米大约五十七秒六，而全场平均每四百米大约七十一点五五秒，末段比全场平均快了将近百分之二十。

这是同一个人在同一年留下的鲜明对照：纪录环境十三分五十八，锦标赛夺冠十四分五十四，相差约五十六秒。它减少了把差别全归给不同运动员的理由，却没有控制天气、赛程、状态和对手。目标与战术显然值得放进解释，但不能仅凭这两个时间，就证明五十六秒中每一种因素各占多少。

女子一万米又提供了同人的对照，但不是同一年。切贝特在2024年5月25日的尤金跑出二十八分五十四秒一四，那场比赛既以世界纪录为目标，也兼作肯尼亚奥运选拔。2025年东京世锦赛，她以三十分三十七秒六一夺冠，两次相差约一百零三秒。目标、配速和比赛展开值得比较，但还应同时记住年份、天气和身体状态的差别，不能把全部差额直接记在赛事结构名下。

但这里必须防止把差异绝对化。不能说纪录赛就是匀速，锦标赛就是慢跑加最后四百米冲刺。前面那个巴黎2024男子一千五百米就是反例。有些锦标赛，因为夺冠热门不愿意把胜负交给最后的冲刺，会主动把节奏推到接近个人极限。反过来，世界纪录也常常表现为开局稍快，主体稳定，末段再快的那种起伏，而不是数学上的常速。学术上更准确的说法是，两类比赛的分段分布概率不同，而不是两个互不重叠的类型。

这些差异，并没有因此变成两套纪录类别。切贝特二十八分五十四和一场锦标赛跑出的合法一万米，仍进入同一套项目体系。官方资料可以保留日期、场地和赛事名称，让人继续追查背景；但单看排序中的时间，它不会自动展开配速、战术与目标的差别。赛事结构不是在现实或档案里消失了，而是没有成为这张表独立分账的依据。

用同一个人的成绩来比较，是为了少跨一道差异，而不是宣称其他差异都消失了。五千米两场在2025年，一万米纪录和东京夺冠则隔着2024与2025两个赛季。同一个人的能力与状态也会变，天气和对手更不会因为名字相同就固定。这样的对照不能给赛事结构单独估价，却提醒我们，时间差不能只用一个脱离比赛目的的“能力”概念解释。读懂数字，还要读它怎样被跑出来。

八 锦标赛决赛，几乎不破纪录

如果纪录赛和锦标赛真的是两种系统性不同的构，那么应该能看到一个后果:锦标赛决赛很少创造世界纪录。而历史的频率，恰好支持这一点。

先把统计口径说清楚。这里的全球成年室外锦标赛，指奥运会与世界田径锦标赛；只数男女一千五百米、五千米、一万米六个项目的决赛，不数预赛和半决赛。奥运会男项从1912年数至2024年，女项从各自正式入奥数至2024年；世锦赛从各项正式入项数至2025年东京这一届。把今天回溯确认的世界纪录进程与这些决赛日期交叉核对，可以确认七场决赛创造了进程表中所列的世界纪录。

这是编辑根据官方历届结果重建的计数：奥运会一百一十场，世锦赛一百一十五场，共二百二十五场；七场约占百分之三点一一。奥运会女一千五百米、五千米、一万米分别从1972、1996、1988年起；世锦赛男三项与女一千五百米从1983年、女五千米从1995年、女一万米从1987年起。男一千五百米自1912年数，并非它参加奥运的全部历史。七场按今天回溯的进程统计，不等于当时曾获批准的总数：科勒迈宁1912年奥运万米曾先获批为世界纪录，后来布安那次更快成绩的日期更正至1911年，科勒迈宁的成绩便不再列入进程。

在同一截止时间与回溯口径下，这七场全部来自奥运会。1983年至2025年的世界田径锦标赛，在这六个项目各自正式入项后的决赛里，没有一场进入这份世界纪录名单。自1983年现代世锦赛开办以来，名单中唯一的全球锦标赛决赛，是2016年里约艾亚娜的女子一万米；那也是奥运会。

这个约百分之三，不能单独证明某一种比赛机制压制了纪录。年代、天气、当时纪录的难度、训练和装备都会影响概率；轮次、激励与赛程本来也是赛事结构的一部分，不能又算作与结构无关的因素。这样的统计适合提示一个现象，若要估计原因，还需要比较口径一致的赛事，并处理这些共同变化的条件。

它能直接告诉我们的，是在上述项目和时期的锦标赛决赛里，世界纪录确实少见。若要进一步说，专门纪录赛的单场破纪录率高出多少，还需要纪录赛的明确定义与分母，不能拿这一个比例直接相比。两类比赛的组织目标与策略差别，已有具体案例可看；这项历史统计为问题增加背景，却不独自完成因果和频率的全部比较。

九 别的运动，给了不同的答案

田径拒绝命名这个差异，而别的运动面对同一个问题，给出了不同的答案。把它们放在一起，能看清田径这个选择的位置。

游泳，是一个只成立一半的类比。

游泳确实有纪录导向的分段和锦标赛决赛的分段的差别。世界游泳的纪录，可以在符合规则的不同正式赛事里创造，不要求一定是奥运或者世锦赛决赛。而世锦赛决赛也可能被主动游成纪录的节奏。所以游泳也有纪录目标和冠军目标不同这件事。

但游泳不能直接等同于田径的配速员问题。因为每一个游泳运动员在独立的泳道里，你不能雇一个人在他前面一米的地方，持续地为他挡水流，报节奏。游泳的那另一套账，主要是由预赛，半决赛，决赛的多轮疲劳，由战术的观察，由纪录的目标造成的，而不是由合法的受雇兔子构成的。所以游泳是纪录目标与冠军目标不同的一个好类比，却不是配速员制度的一个同构的案例。

自行车，则给出了田径没有采用的另一种答案:把两套构，直接命名为不同的项目。

在自行车的规则里，公路大组赛就是一个项目，位置，车队的协作，风阻，突围，冲刺，正是这个项目的内容。个人计时赛是另一个项目，车手按间隔单独出发，以最低的时间决定成绩。这两个项目，不会因为距离相近，就被塞进一张谁骑得最快的单一纪录逻辑里。它们是两个名字，两套账。

而自行车对外部帮助的处理，尤其有启发。因为车辆即使跟在车手后方，过近时也可能带来空气动力收益，自行车的管理机构，把个人计时赛的车手与后方车辆的最低距离，提高到了二十五米，以减少跟车气流对成绩的影响。这里，制度承认的，是一个和节奏灯，配速员完全同源的问题:一个外部的信息或者物体，从什么时候开始，不再只是帮助比赛进行，而是开始改变那个要被测量的竞技能力。

用自行车支持田径也应分账，是规范主张，不是历史必然。自行车把不同竞赛方式设为不同项目，田径则长期把世界纪录、奥运冠军和战术较量放在同一项目中。保留这种连续性，是维持合并的一种可理解理由，但本篇没有因此取得完整的制度动机。分组也不必抹掉旧纪录；怎样另设类别、保留既往成绩，仍有多种设计。

这个对照仍有分量。自行车说明，为不同竞赛方式分设项目是可行的；田径则保留了不同的组织传统。班尼斯特三分五十九秒四属于一英里的纪录史，不是今天一千五百米纪录的直接前一段，两者必须分清。在各自项目内部，旧成绩与今天的合法成绩继续比较，是合并制度的真实好处。是否值得进一步标注赛事结构，可以讨论，却不能只凭现状断言机构曾明确选择以模糊交换连续性。

十 一个被拒绝命名的差异

把这一篇收拢起来。

有两个构。一个纪录构:赛事组织者可以合法地雇佣同场的配速员，可以设定目标圈速，可以使用获批的节奏灯，可以选择有利的跑道，气候和时段，经济激励可以直接和时间或者纪录挂钩。一个锦标赛构:通常没有受雇的兔子，运动员面对直接对手与夺冠目标，在设有晋级轮的距离上还要面对多轮赛程，最合理的策略经常是保留能量，改变位置，等待末段提速。

这两个构，生成成绩的机制系统性地不同。世界田径自己在官方的平台上，反复描述过这种不同。它会说锦标赛没有配速员，会说纪录尝试通常是有配速的比赛，它心里清楚这是两种不同的运行逻辑。

但它没有据此分设两套世界纪录项目。不同组织条件下的合法时间，仍然进入同一个项目的纪录体系。描述差异，与把差异变成独立的纪录资格，是不同的制度动作。

这就是这一篇的余项，而它的形态，又和前面所有的不一样。

前面的余项，有的是构装不下的，有的是构算不清的，有的是构够不到的，有的是构主动预留的。这一篇的余项，是那个真实存在、却没有被单列为纪录类别的差异本身。赛事结构，是一个系统性地影响成绩的变量。构看得见它，构在官方平台上描述过它，构甚至有能力命名它，自行车就命名了。而它没有成为独立的纪录类别，单看表里的时间，也不会展开这个差异。

所以这个差异留在了数字之外。它参与塑造二十八分五十四和三十分三十七那样的比赛，却没有作为独立类别进入纪录排序。表能比较合法时间，但若读者把这个时间直接读成不受情境影响的能力，就会漏掉成绩的生成条件。问题不在表一定作出了这种宣称，而在一个清楚的数字，很容易被我们读成过于完整的答案。

这是构处理差异的另一种方式。它可以承认差异存在，也在官方介绍中描述它，却不把每一种差异都变成独立项目或纪录类别。这样做不必等于否认，也不必等于唯一合理的设计。我们可以继续问：共用一个名字和一张表，保存了什么，又把什么交给读者在表外辨认？

这里更值得看的是三层关系。第一层，纪录资格没有按追时间与争名次分成两个项目。第二层，赛事组织让配速、信息、赛程与策略呈现不同组合，不是每场都一样。第三层，合法时间在同一项目里继续排序。这三层可以同时成立，却不能彼此替代：纪录分类不是比赛组织的全部，成绩排序也不是成绩来历的全部。

规则按项目分类，赛事组织产生不同的具体条件，表则比较符合规则的时间。它们之间有张力，却不必是一个逻辑矛盾。真正值得保留的提醒是：同一把尺可以合法比较两次成绩，但这不意味着两次成绩的全部条件都已经相同，更不意味着关于能力与战术的判断到此为止。

这个构能雇一个人为你挡风，能布四百个灯点为你标出节奏，能挑一个凉爽的夜晚，一条最快的跑道，能把这一切帮助都写进合法的规则里，让你跑出的时间进入正式的纪录。它也能在另一场比赛里，撤掉所有这些帮助，让你独自面对对手，疲劳和位置。官方介绍已经描述过，这两种比赛常有不同的组织方式和策略。可是当它记账的时候，它把这两种比赛跑出的时间，写进了同一行。它没有为那个差异另设一个纪录类别，于是那个差异，就悄悄地留在了每一个不能被这张表解释的秒数里。

1. The Same Name, Two Kinds of Race

A 10,000-metre race aimed at breaking a world record and an Olympic 10,000-metre final bear the same name and enter the same world-record table.

Yet the two differ in almost every controllable variable.

In the record attempt, organizers can lawfully hire pacemakers, set target speeds for each lap in advance, use approved pacing lights, and choose a fast track, low altitude and a cool time of day. They can also offer a time or record bonus separate from prize money for placings. The athlete's task is to remain as long as possible in a sheltered position at a steady pace and devote every ounce of effort to approaching the predetermined time.

In the championship race there are usually no hired pacemakers. Athletes consider position, opponents, the risk of being boxed in and reserves for a final sprint. Events with heats and semifinals add qualification and accumulated fatigue; the 10,000 metres at modern global championships is normally a straight final. World Athletics likewise describes championship 10,000-metre racing as often conserving energy before the closing laps. The best strategy is frequently not the highest possible average speed from the first lap, but finding the right moment to accelerate.

These are two common, systematically different ways of organizing a race. One emphasizes time, the other defeating opponents; the pacing and information actually supplied may differ too. They can encourage different strategies without necessarily operating under opposite assistance rules. The distinction lies within one lawful event, and not every race falls neatly at either end.

Yet World Athletics' current rules do not divide them into two events.

A 10,000 metres remains a 10,000 metres. Rules do distinguish World Championships, Olympic Games and other levels of competition, and regulate legality, assistance and record eligibility. But emphasizing a time in one race and a placing in another does not create two separate world-record events at the same distance. “Not naming” here means not establishing separate record categories, not never giving championships a name.

That is this essay's subject. Two constructs differ systematically in actual operation while sharing one name and one record table. The construct itself acknowledges the difference on its official platforms, yet does not thereby establish separate world-record events in its formal rules, then reunites them through one uniform record table.

Essay 17 concerned a construct that had prepared the means of closure and chose not to use them. This essay concerns something adjacent: a construct sees a difference, has the capacity to name it, and chooses not to.

“Not naming” needs precision. Official descriptions recognize different pacing and strategies, so the distinction is not simply unnoticed. Yet those descriptions do not establish a meeting at which separate record categories were considered and rejected. What this essay can observe is the outcome: the difference is described without thereby becoming two record events. Recognizing a difference and giving it a separate legal category are different acts.

2. For the Argument to Hold, One Claim Must First Be Narrowed

Before proceeding, a claim that is too strong must be narrowed, or the entire essay will lack a footing.

The overstrong claim is that record-race and championship performances are fundamentally incomparable, that they are two different sports.

That goes too far. The rules and record system expressly treat them as comparable times in the same event. Championships can also be extremely fast, even producing world records, while record attempts still contain real competition.

The men's 1,500 metres at the Paris Olympics in 2024 supplies an obvious counterexample. There was no commercial pacemaker, but Ingebrigtsen voluntarily led at high speed and raised the pace of the entire race. Hocker won in 3:27.65, only 1.65 seconds outside the world record. A championship final with no hired pacemaker was transformed from within, by its leading athlete, into something approaching a record race. There is no impassable wall between the two.

What, then, is the version of the argument that genuinely holds?

Not that two performances cannot be compared at all, but this: under the name of one event, the rules allow two systematically different mechanisms of performance production. Directly treating a recorded time as an unconditional measurement of the same competitive ability therefore loses a crucial variable: the structure of the competition.

This version is weaker and much firmer. Physiological and competition data support the claim that the production mechanisms differ. World Athletics' regulatory system explicitly contradicts the claim of legal incomparability, because both remain in the same record system as long as they satisfy the rules. The accurate formulation is therefore not two different sports, but two highly stable structures for producing competition, with different objective functions, under a single legally defined event.

Only by holding to this narrower version can the essay avoid slipping into a claim unsupported by the evidence. All the material below supports the narrowed version, not the stronger one.

This narrowing also draws a boundary between the essay and a familiar complaint: that today's records are assembled from pacemakers, lights and fast shoes, and do not count as real ability. That is not the point here. This essay does not judge whether record-race performances count as genuine ability. Athletes really run those times. A pacemaker may shelter them from the wind, but they still have to move their own legs. The point is cooler and more structural: when two systematically different races share a table, a number in that table, considered alone, no longer tells us the structure in which it was produced. This does not belittle any performance. It identifies a variable the table has discarded.

3. A Pacemaker Is a Competitor, Not an Outside Service

To understand these two constructs, we must first see exactly what status a pacemaker has under the rules. That status is not quite what intuition suggests.

The current assistance rules prohibit pacing of several kinds: pacing supplied by people not participating in the same race; pacing supplied by athletes who have been or are about to be lapped; and pacing supplied by unauthorized technical devices.

Notice where the boundary falls. It does not ask whether you are pacing someone else. It asks whether you are a lawful participant in this race and whether you provide help from a normal competitive position.

The road-racing rules put it more explicitly. They directly stipulate that pacemakers employed by organizers are bona fide competitors and require them to start at the gun with the other elite athletes.

A lawful pacemaker is therefore not a staff member standing outside the course to provide a service. Legally, the pacemaker is a competitor; economically, a pacemaker. They enter the start list and, if they complete the distance, may in principle obtain an official result or even win. World Athletics' archives contain examples of athletes hired to pace who ultimately won the race.

This design of status is the institutional basis that allows the two constructs to share a record table. Because a pacemaker is legally a competitor, a paced record attempt remains a normal race under the rules, and its performance can enter the official records. The construct has not treated the pacemaker as an external tool. It has made the pacemaker an internal competitor. Performances achieved with pacing assistance are consequently no different in law from performances achieved without it.

That status is an important condition for a shared record system. If pacing were prohibited outside assistance, the performance might simply lose record eligibility. Separate accounting would require a further choice to create an assisted category; neither outcome is automatic. Existing rules place lawful pacemakers among competitors while restricting lapping, rotation and outside intervention. Legal pacing can thus be part of the race without automatically creating a different record event.

The three unlawful kinds of pacing define the boundaries of this status in reverse. An outsider cannot enter and pace because they are not a competitor. A lapped athlete cannot return as a fresh pacemaker because that creates continuing assistance beyond normal competition. Technical devices outside the exceptions are prohibited. Historically, the sensitive issue has never been simply that everyone agreed on a pace beforehand. It has been whether rotation, the continual introduction of fresh runners, or outside equipment can manufacture sustained assistance beyond the normal competition of the same field.

4. In Bannister's Story, the Rejected Race Was the Year Before

The subtleties of the pacemaker's status are clearest in Bannister's sub-four-minute mile. A widely circulated version of that story is wrong.

First, what actually happened. On 6 May 1954 at Iffley Road in Oxford, Brasher led the early stages, Chataway then took over the pace, and Bannister finally completed the last assault alone, running 3:59.4. The race was arranged within the framework of an official Oxford University versus Amateur Athletic Association meeting. It was not a training test timed solely for Bannister.

The mistaken version says that after the 1954 performance, officials formally investigated whether it had been a genuine race and approved it only after a hearing.

The materials do not support that version. It very likely transplants another incident into 1954.

The performance that British authorities actually rejected as not a bona fide race occurred in 1953, not 1954.

On 27 June 1953 at Motspur Park, Bannister attempted to break four minutes and ran 4:02.0. McMillen paced first, while Brasher followed a special arrangement: he jogged slowly, let Bannister lap him, and then returned in a relatively fresh state to lead Bannister again. The British Amateur Athletic Board subsequently refused to recognize the performance as a British record. Its central reason was that the event did not constitute a bona fide competition within the meaning of the rules.

That 1953 precedent explains why the organization of the 1954 race was especially careful. In 1954, both helpers were athletes in the same lawful race from the gun. The device of returning fresh after being lapped was not repeated.

Allegation and ruling must therefore be separated here. The allegation is that Brasher and Chataway had agreed beforehand to serve Bannister and did not necessarily regard winning themselves as their primary aim. The ruling is that no formal 1954 rejection by the British board on grounds of a non-genuine race has been found. On the contrary, 3:59.4 entered the official world-record progression.

The participants' own testimony exposes the tension. In a first-person recollection, Chataway said that after 1954 the board did not have the nerve to question again whether he and Brasher were genuine competitors. He then admitted with great frankness that, in terms of an actual intention to win, of course they were not.

That statement is crucial. In subjective purpose, Chataway and Brasher were pacemakers. They had no intention of winning. In legal treatment, however, the performance was not rejected as the 1953 one had been. If a nominal competitor has no intention of winning from the outset, the expression “bona fide competitor” acquires a semantic tension. Chataway's candour brings that tension into the open.

An honest gap remains. Recognition of the 1954 performance is established, but this essay has not obtained the complete written reasons. One observable difference is that 1954 did not repeat the 1953 arrangement in which Brasher let himself be lapped before taking up the pace. It is not that only the 1954 helpers started in the same race from the gun. That difference helps interpret the outcome without substituting for a recovered statement of the grounds.

5. From Hiring a Pacemaker to Building a Production System

In Bannister's era, pacing still involved a few friends helping out. Since the 1990s, the most conspicuous change has not been the sudden invention of pacemakers. It has been the institutionalization of record-breaking through the commercial meeting circuit.

Launched in 1998, the Golden League organized one-day meetings in Oslo, Rome, Monaco, Zurich, Brussels and Berlin into a continuous commercial system with large prizes, television coverage and elite fields. World Athletics' own Golden League record archive lists world records achieved at these meetings, including El Guerrouj's 3:26.00 for 1,500 metres in Rome in 1998.

A characteristic set of organizational modules can be identified in this record-oriented commercial racing. The target time is set in advance. Pacemakers who start in the same race from the gun take successive portions of the pace. The principal athlete remains as long as possible in a sheltered position at a stable speed. Organizers select low altitude and a fast track or flat road course, choose a more suitable start time according to the weather forecast, and add time or record rewards to prizes for placings. Not every element appears in every race, but they recur in elite one-day meetings and fast marathons.

One overstrong claim needs guarding against. Some say that record attempts in the 1990s all offered a uniform world-record bonus, such as a fixed US$50,000. That cannot be sustained as a general institutional fact. Rewards have several layers: the series purse, the individual meeting's place prizes, appearance fees, time bonuses, and record bonuses separately offered by organizers or sponsors. The latter items are often determined individually by organizers or contracts, and historical contracts are not necessarily public. The existence of various record and time bonuses can be confirmed. Individual events do not establish a uniform cash price list for world records.

Then came pacing lights. Developed around 2017, the system was first used in competition on 1 June 2018 and received full permission under World Athletics rules in 2020. Approximately 400 lights are installed along the inside of a standard 400-metre track, roughly one per metre. Software advances the lights according to a target time. An abstract lap-time schedule becomes a continuously visible spatial position moving around the track. The athlete no longer needs to calculate mentally how far behind the target they are. They need only follow the light.

What that light does deserves comparison with the door opened in Essay 8. There, the material condition of foam landing mats opened the possibility of the Fosbury flop. Here, pacing-light technology opens something else: it releases athletes from a task that had always belonged to them, judging their own speed. Before pacing lights, an athlete pursuing a record had to assemble bodily sensation, the pacemaker's lead and lap-time feedback to determine whether the pace was fast enough. This was a skill, a skill of self-perception. Pacing lights turn it into the simple act of following a light. What the athlete saves is not physical effort, but part of the work of judgment. Whether an external device should replace that judgment is precisely the core of the next section's debate.

One popular claim about the light must be dismantled: that it is an illegal electronic pacemaker exploiting a loophole. It is not. While prohibiting pacing by technical devices, World Athletics' assistance rules expressly list electronic lights indicating elapsed race time, including the pace of a relevant record, as a permitted exception. The present legal structure is therefore not a blanket ban on technological pacing. It is a general prohibition accompanied by an express exemption for specified visual timing systems.

Another claim needs narrowing: that this lighting equipment can never appear at championships. The 2025 European Indoors did introduce Wavelight, but European Athletics distinguished its role: guide lights during part of the heats and record displays when finalists approached relevant record pace, not the Diamond League's direct aid continuously setting an athlete's target speed. Equipment can enter a championship without the assistance being identical. Its actual use and event implementation still matter.

6. Should the Light Count as Outside Assistance?

Whether pacing lights should be allowed is a dispute with clear arguments on both sides, and World Athletics ultimately chose one. This section lays out both without adjudicating between them.

Those favouring permission say that the light merely supplies elapsed-time information visible to every competitor, equivalent to moving information from screens, clocks and lap calls onto the track and giving it a spatial form. It neither pushes nor pulls athletes, supplies no mechanical energy to any individual, and operates under transparent rules that treat everyone alike. This is the legal position World Athletics ultimately adopted.

Those favouring restriction say that a continuously visible target continually removes errors in athletes' own pacing judgment. It automates pace control, something traditionally accomplished jointly by the athlete, pacemakers and timing feedback. Some analyses particularly note that certain records achieved with pacing lights have extremely small lap-time dispersion, almost no fluctuation, raising a question about historical comparability. Earlier record-holders had to bear part of the task of judging speed themselves. Now that part is outsourced to a light.

No single experiment settles the whole dispute. Experiments can estimate the effect of pacing lights under specified conditions, but cannot alone decide what assistance should be allowed. A particular world record cannot be rerun with shoes, weather, pacing and bodily condition otherwise identical. Studies and models may yield conditional estimates; the finishing time alone cannot tell us how many seconds came from the lights. Difficulty in precise attribution is not impossibility in principle.

What matters here is the dispute's real nature. It is not about whether the light helps; it plainly does. It is about whether that kind of help still belongs among the competitive relationships that the sport ought to accept internally. This and the pacemaker dispute are two versions of the same question.

7. One Athlete, Different Races

Now consider the record table that combines the two constructs. How large are the actual differences?

The women's 5,000 metres offers one of the cleanest examples. Across the five 1,000-metre splits of Chebet's 2025 record, the total range was only about 7.22 seconds, and the fastest split was the final kilometre. It was therefore not a mechanical metronome maintaining exactly the same speed throughout. It combined a highly stable main portion with a deliberate late acceleration. This fits the structure researchers describe for record-oriented distance racing: slightly faster at the two ends, steady in the middle.

Compare it with a championship. The same Chebet won the women's 5,000 metres at the 2025 Tokyo World Championships in 14:54.36, while her world record was 13:58.06. Her last 400 metres in the championship win took approximately 57.6 seconds, against an average of about 71.55 seconds per 400 metres over the whole race. The closing split took almost 20 percent less time than the overall per-lap average.

The same athlete in the same year makes a vivid comparison: 13:58 in a record setting, 14:54 to win a championship, about 56 seconds apart. This reduces the temptation to attribute everything to different runners, but does not control weather, scheduling, form or opponents. Objectives and tactics belong in the explanation. Two times alone do not establish each factor's share of those 56 seconds.

The women's 10,000 metres supplies another within-athlete comparison, but not in the same year. Chebet ran 28:54.14 in Eugene on 25 May 2024, in a race designed as a record attempt that also served as Kenya's Olympic selection race. She won the 2025 Tokyo world title in 30:37.61, about 103 seconds slower. Objectives, pacing and the race's development deserve comparison, alongside differences in year, weather and form. The entire gap cannot simply be booked to competition structure.

But the differences must not be made absolute. We cannot say that record races are always even-paced while championships are jogging followed by a final 400-metre sprint. The Paris 2024 men's 1,500 metres is the counterexample already discussed. In some championships, a favourite unwilling to leave the outcome to a closing sprint deliberately pushes the pace close to personal limits. Conversely, world records often show a slightly faster opening, a stable main section and another acceleration at the end, rather than mathematical constancy. Academically, the more accurate formulation is that the probability distributions of splits differ between the two kinds of race, not that they form two non-overlapping types.

Those differences do not create two record categories. Chebet's 28:54 and a legal championship 10,000 metres still enter the same event system. Official material can preserve dates, venues and meeting names through which context can be traced. The ranked time alone, however, does not unfold differences in pacing, tactics and objectives. Competition structure has not vanished from reality or the archives; it has not become the basis for separate accounting.

Comparing one athlete removes one layer of difference, not every other one. The 5,000-metre races occurred in 2025; the 10,000-metre record and Tokyo victory span the 2024 and 2025 seasons. The same person's ability and form can change, and neither weather nor opponents become fixed by the athlete's name. These comparisons cannot price competition structure independently. They do remind us not to explain times solely through an ability detached from the race's purpose. Understanding the number requires asking how it was run.

8. Championship Finals Almost Never Break Records

If record races and championships really are two systematically different constructs, one consequence should be visible: championship finals rarely produce world records. Historical frequencies support that expectation.

First define the scope. Global senior outdoor championships here means the Olympic Games and the World Athletics Championships. Count finals only in the men’s and women’s 1,500, 5,000 and 10,000 metres, excluding heats and semifinals. For the Olympics, the men’s count runs from 1912 through 2024; each women’s event starts with its official Olympic debut and runs through 2024. World Championship counts start with each event’s official introduction and end at Tokyo 2025. Cross-checking those finals against the retrospectively accepted world-record progressions identifies seven finals that produced records listed in those progressions.

This is an editorial reconstruction from official historical results: 110 Olympic finals and 115 World Championship finals, 225 in all; seven is about 3.11 percent. The Olympic women’s 1,500, 5,000 and 10,000 metres start in 1972, 1996 and 1988 respectively. At Worlds, the men’s three events and women’s 1,500 start in 1983, women’s 5,000 in 1995 and women’s 10,000 in 1987. Counting the men’s Olympic 1,500 from 1912 does not cover its entire Olympic history. The seven follow today’s retrospective progressions, not every historical ratification: Kolehmainen’s 1912 Olympic 10,000 metres was initially ratified as a world record, but Bouin’s faster performance was later redated to 1911, leaving Kolehmainen’s mark out of the progression.

Within the same cutoff and retrospective definition, all seven came at the Olympics. From 1983 through 2025, no World Championship final in these six events, counted from each event’s official introduction, enters this world-record list. Since the modern World Championships began in 1983, the list’s only global championship final is Ayana’s women’s 10,000 metres at Rio 2016, itself an Olympic race.

About three percent cannot by itself prove that a particular competition mechanism suppresses records. Era, weather, the standing record, training and equipment all affect probability. Rounds, incentives and scheduling are themselves parts of competition structure, not factors wholly unrelated to it. The statistic can identify a pattern; estimating causes requires comparable races and attention to these changing conditions.

What it directly tells us is that world records were rare in the championship finals within this stated scope. To say how much higher the per-race rate is in dedicated record attempts, we also need a definition and denominator for those attempts. This one proportion does not supply that comparison. Cases already illustrate differences in objectives and strategy. The historical count adds context without completing every causal or frequency comparison.

9. Other Sports Have Given Different Answers

Athletics declines to name this difference. Other sports have faced the same problem and given different answers. Setting them alongside one another reveals the position of athletics' choice.

Swimming supplies an analogy that holds only halfway.

There is indeed a difference between record-oriented splits and championship-final splits in swimming. World Aquatics records can be set in different official competitions that comply with the rules; they need not occur in Olympic or World Championship finals. A World Championship final can also be deliberately swum at record pace. Swimming therefore also has the distinction between the goal of a record and the goal of a championship.

But swimming cannot be equated directly with athletics' pacemaker issue. Each swimmer occupies a separate lane. One cannot hire someone to swim a metre ahead continuously, sheltering the swimmer from water resistance and calling the pace. Swimming's other ledger arises chiefly from fatigue over heats, semifinals and finals, tactical observation and record objectives, rather than a lawful system of hired pacemakers. Swimming is therefore a good analogy for the difference between record and championship objectives, but not a structurally identical example of the pacemaker institution.

Cycling gives another answer that athletics has not adopted: name the two constructs directly as separate events.

In cycling's rules, the mass-start road race is one event. Position, team cooperation, aerodynamic resistance, breakaways and sprints are its substance. The individual time trial is another. Riders start alone at intervals, and the shortest time determines the result. Similar distances do not force these two events into one record logic asking who rode fastest. They have two names and two ledgers.

Cycling's treatment of outside assistance is particularly instructive. Because proximity to vehicles can produce aerodynamic gains, the governing body increased the minimum distance between an individual time-trial rider and the following vehicle to 25 metres, reducing the effect of the vehicle's airflow on performance. Here the institution recognizes a question with exactly the same roots as pacing lights and pacemakers: when does an external source of information or an object cease merely to facilitate competition and begin to alter the competitive ability being measured?

Cycling can support a proposal for separate ledgers in athletics, not a historical necessity. Cycling defines different racing forms as separate events; athletics has long combined world records, Olympic titles and tactical contests within one event. Preserving that continuity is an intelligible reason for combination, not a complete institutional motive established here. Nor would new categories have to erase old records. Several designs could preserve past performances while adding classifications.

The contrast still matters. Cycling shows that separate events for different racing forms are feasible, while athletics retains a different organizational tradition. Bannister's 3:59.4 belongs to mile-record history, not directly to the 1,500-metre progression; the two must remain distinct. Within each event, continued comparison between old and new legal performances is a real benefit of combination. Whether competition structure deserves further annotation remains debatable. The present system alone does not establish an explicit institutional bargain trading clarity for continuity.

10. A Difference Denied a Name

Let us draw the essay together.

There are two constructs. A record construct: organizers can lawfully hire pacemakers participating in the same race, set target lap speeds, use approved pacing lights, select favourable tracks, weather and times of day, and link financial incentives directly to times or records. A championship construct: usually there are no hired pacemakers; athletes face direct opponents and the goal of a title, with qualifying rounds added in events that have them; the most rational strategy is often to conserve energy, change position and wait for a late acceleration.

The two constructs generate performances through systematically different mechanisms. World Athletics itself repeatedly describes that difference on its official platforms. It says that championships have no pacemakers and that record attempts are usually paced races. It knows perfectly well that these are two different operational logics.

It has not thereby established two world-record events. Legal times produced under different organizing conditions still enter one event's record system. Describing a difference and giving it independent record status are different institutional acts.

That is this essay's remainder, whose form again differs from all its predecessors.

Earlier remainders included what the construct could not contain, could not calculate, could not reach, or deliberately reserved. Here the remainder is the difference itself, real but not classified separately for records. Competition structure systematically affects performance. The construct sees it, describes it on official platforms, and even has the capacity to name it, as cycling has done. It has not become a separate record category, and a listed time alone does not unfold it.

The difference therefore remains beyond the number. It helps shape races such as the 28:54 and the 30:37 without becoming an independent category in record ranking. The table can compare legal times. If we read a time as ability unaffected by context, however, we lose the conditions that produced it. The table need not explicitly claim that; a clear number can tempt us to treat it as too complete an answer.

This is another way of handling difference. A construct may recognize and describe it without turning every difference into a separate event or record category. That need be neither denial nor the only reasonable design. We can still ask what one name and one table preserve, and what readers must recognize beyond the table.

The three layers are worth distinguishing. First, record eligibility does not split the event into time-seeking and placing-seeking categories. Second, organizers produce different combinations of pacing, information, scheduling and strategy; not every race is the same. Third, legal times remain ranked within one event. All three can hold without replacing one another. Record classification is not a complete description of organization, nor is ranking a complete account of how a result arose.

Rules classify events, organizers produce particular conditions, and tables compare lawful times. There is tension between these layers, not necessarily a logical contradiction. The point worth retaining is that one measure can lawfully compare two performances without making all their conditions identical or ending judgment about ability and tactics.

This construct can hire someone to shelter you from the wind, install 400 lights to mark your pace, choose a cool evening and the fastest track, and write all this assistance into lawful rules so that your time enters the official records. In another race, it can remove all those aids and leave you to face opponents, fatigue and position alone. Official descriptions already recognize that these races often differ in organization and strategy. Yet when it keeps the accounts, it writes the times produced by both on the same line. It has created no separate record category for that difference. The difference therefore remains quietly in every second that the table cannot explain.

The ruler has not yet measured accurately. It is still measuring.
