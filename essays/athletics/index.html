凿构周期律：田径

The Chisel–Construct Cycle: Athletics

田径把世界压进一套看似最干净的数字：秒、米、分数、名次。可每一个数字都要先经过名单、资格、器械、场地、分类、证据与规则。二十三篇文章沿着这条链条追问：构如何生产可比性，又把什么留在尺子之外。

系列分为四组：纪录与度量（01—07）、技术与边界（08—12）、身体与分类（13—16）、闭合、责任与极限（17—23）。中文据完整原稿整理；繁体为离线阅读层；英文按英语读者的论述习惯独立重写。

Han Qin (秦汉) · 2026 · 二十三篇 · 中文简繁与英文
第 01 篇
只有名字，没有数字

古代奥林匹亚留下了一条延续千年的胜者名单，却没有留下成绩。不是数字散失了，而是那套制度从未打算生产数字。

第 02 篇
纪录的发明

报纸、三块表、统一量线与认证机构如何把一次比赛结果变成可累积、可追认的纪录。纪录不是数字，而是一整套核对装置。

第 03 篇
谁有资格被记入

业余资格、吉姆·索普与帕沃·努尔米说明：纪录先要给人开门，才会给数字开门；而门槛的来源、效力与纠正各走各的时间。

第 04 篇
哪些身体可以被度量

1928 年女子 800 米不是女性“跑不了”的证明，而是制度如何把观看、资格与纪录分进不同房间的样本。

第 05 篇
四个变量同时到场

比蒙的 8.90 米同时属于运动员、海拔、风与测量装置。纪录要求一个数，却无法把共同到场的原因逐项拆开。

第 06 篇
零点二四秒

手计时与全自动计时怎样被一条 0.24 秒的接缝缝进同一张表；它不是自然换算，而是新制度扶正时留下的历史补丁。

第 07 篇
把不可通约的东西通约

十项全能计分表把十种不同能力压成一个总分；每次重写都在重新分配什么身体、什么技术更值钱。

第 08 篇
技术长在规则没说的地方

背越式不是规则预先设计的进步；它从规则没有规定姿势、而泡沫垫突然允许背向落地的缝里长出来。

第 09 篇
技术不收敛

铅球的滑步与旋转共存七十年，说明同一规则、同一目标、同一纪录线并不会自动收敛成唯一最优技术。

第 10 篇
构在被越过时才发现边界

前空翻式跳远被禁，玻璃纤维撑竿被接纳。两个相似创新得到相反结论，真正的分界不是增益大小，而是项目定义。

第 11 篇
为保住可比性销毁旧账

霍恩的 104.80 米没有被“抹掉”，而是随标枪规格改变被封进旧账。为保住可比性，制度必须承认连续性已经断裂。

第 12 篇
同一个难题两种结论

超级跑鞋与聚氨酯泳衣都改变了成绩，田径和游泳却都保留既有纪录。规则线可以截断未来，却未必追溯重写过去。

第 13 篇
构安放不了的身体

皮斯托瑞斯案不是简单回答义肢有没有优势，而是暴露：当参照身体不存在时，制度如何分配不确定性与举证责任。

第 14 篇
必须分类才能运行

女子组资格标准六十年间反复更换；2025 年世界田联改采 SRY 检测，进一步显出分类不是发现自然边界，而是选择制度边界。

第 15 篇
叙事也是一种构

肯尼亚长跑与牙买加短跑的集中是真实的；把集中压成民族身体的天赋故事，却会把选择、制度与未测量的人一起抹掉。

第 16 篇
把生理常数写成定义

0.100 秒起跑阈值把经验假设写成“抢跑”的定义；艾伦的 0.099 不是不存在，而是被规则定义为不能存在。

第 17 篇
构备好了闭合手段而人不用

巴尔希姆与坦贝里共享金牌不是改写规则，而是使用规则预留的“不闭合”出口。制度有能力继续分胜负，也可以授权人不分。

第 18 篇
两个同名的构

纪录赛与锦标赛都叫赛跑，却生产不同对象：一个优化终点数字，一个分配当场名次。配速系统把这个差别推到台前。

第 19 篇
同一距离两套账

波士顿马拉松的赛道可以量得准确，却不满足世界纪录的地理条件。同一个 42.195 公里因此同时属于赛事史与纪录史。

第 20 篇
别问

反兴奋剂的严格责任不以主观故意为成立条件，但责任成立与处罚轻重仍是两层。制度不问内心，也不能假装没有个人。

第 21 篇
账本保存着自己无法背书的条目

一些可疑的历史纪录仍在册上，不是制度确信它们清白，而是集体证据、统计异常与个案证明之间不能互相冒充。

第 22 篇
构为余项发明类别

中立个人运动员与难民奥运代表队不是天然身份，而是制度为国家资格闸门装不下的人发明的不同通道。

第 23 篇
一个人到底能多快多高多远

从四分钟一英里到“女子何时超过男子”，极限预测反复把纪录曲线误当成人。尺子能追踪构生产出的数字，却够不到主体的最后刻度。

The Chisel–Construct Cycle · A Sixth Field

The Chisel–Construct Cycle: Athletics

《凿构周期律：田径》

Athletics appears to compress the world into exceptionally clean numbers: seconds, metres, points, and places. Yet every number must pass through eligibility, equipment, venue, classification, evidence, and rule. These twenty-three essays ask how a construct produces comparability—and what it must leave beyond the ruler.

The series moves through four arcs: records and measurement (01–07), technique and boundaries (08–12), bodies and classification (13–16), then closure, responsibility, and human limits (17–23). The English essays are independent reader-facing rewrites of the complete Chinese manuscripts.

Han Qin (秦汉) · 2026 · Twenty-three essays · English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese
Essay 01
Names Without Numbers

Ancient Olympia preserved a line of victors but no performances. The numbers were not merely lost; the institution had never been designed to produce them.

Essay 02
The Invention of the Record

Newspapers, stopwatches, measuring lines, and governing bodies turned performances into records. A record is not a number but an apparatus for checking one.

Essay 03
Who Gets to Enter the Book

Amateur eligibility, Jim Thorpe, and Paavo Nurmi show that a number enters only after a person is admitted—and that rules, their stories, and their corrections run on different clocks.

Essay 04
Which Bodies May Be Measured

The 1928 women's 800 metres did not prove women incapable. It revealed how an institution can separate participation, eligibility, and record recognition into different rooms.

Essay 05
Four Variables Arrive Together

Beamon's 8.90 metres belonged at once to athlete, altitude, wind, and measuring apparatus. The ledger demands one number but cannot apportion the causes that produced it.

Essay 06
The Missing 0.24 Seconds

A 0.24-second convention stitched hand timing to automatic timing. It was not a law of nature but a historical seam created when one system displaced another.

Essay 07
Making the Incommensurable Commensurable

The decathlon table compresses ten unlike capacities into one total. Every revision quietly reallocates which bodies and techniques are worth more.

Essay 08
Technique Grows Where Rules Are Silent

The Fosbury Flop was not progress designed by rule. It grew in the gap between rules that did not prescribe style and foam pits that finally made a backward landing possible.

Essay 09
Technique Does Not Converge

Glide and rotational shot-put techniques have coexisted for decades. One rule, one target, and one record line do not guarantee convergence on a single optimum.

Essay 10
A Construct Finds Its Boundary Only When Crossed

The front-somersault long jump was banned while fiberglass poles were accepted. The dividing line was not the size of the advantage but the event's unstated definition.

Essay 11
Destroying the Old Ledger to Preserve Comparability

Hohn's 104.80 metres was not erased; it was sealed into an old ledger when the javelin changed. Preserving comparability required admitting that continuity had broken.

Essay 12
One Problem, Two Conclusions

Super shoes and polyurethane swimsuits changed performance, yet athletics and swimming retained existing records. A rule can close the future without retrospectively rewriting the past.

Essay 13
The Body the Construct Cannot Place

The Pistorius case did not simply decide whether prostheses conferred advantage. It exposed how a system allocates uncertainty when the reference body does not exist.

Essay 14
A System Must Classify to Operate

Female-category eligibility criteria have repeatedly changed; World Athletics' 2025 turn to SRY testing makes clear that classification selects an institutional boundary rather than discovering a natural line.

Essay 15
Narrative Is Also a Construct

Kenyan distance running and Jamaican sprinting are real concentrations. Turning them into stories of national bodies erases selection, institutions, and all the people never measured.

Essay 16
Writing a Physiological Constant into a Definition

The 0.100-second start threshold turned an empirical assumption into the definition of a false start. Allen's 0.099 was real, but the rule defined it as impossible to credit.

Essay 17
The Construct Offered Closure; the Athletes Declined

Barshim and Tamberi did not rewrite the rules by sharing gold. They used an authorized exit from closure: the system could separate them, but allowed them not to ask it to.

Essay 18
Two Constructs with the Same Name

Record races and championship races share a name but produce different objects: one optimizes a finishing number, the other allocates places on the day. Pacing exposes the difference.

Essay 19
One Distance, Two Ledgers

Boston can be an accurately measured marathon yet fail world-record geography. The same 42.195 kilometres therefore belongs to race history and record history in different ways.

Essay 20
Do Not Ask What Was Intended

Anti-doping strict liability does not require proof of intent, but violation and sanction remain separate questions. The system brackets the inner life without making the person disappear.

Essay 21
The Ledger Keeps Entries It Cannot Vouch For

Some suspect historical records remain because institutional evidence, statistical anomaly, and proof against an individual entry are not interchangeable.

Essay 22
The Construct Invents a Category for the Remainder

Individual Neutral Athletes and the Refugee Olympic Team are not natural identities. They are distinct pathways invented for people the nationality gate cannot otherwise place.

Essay 23
How Fast, High, and Far Can a Person Go?

From the four-minute mile to predictions that women will overtake men, limit forecasts repeatedly mistake record curves for persons. The ruler tracks numbers produced by a system, not the final measure of a subject.
