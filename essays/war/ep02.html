第二篇：上兵伐谋之上

Essay 2: Above the Highest Use of Arms

Han Qin (秦汉)

《孙子兵法·谋攻篇》里有一句被引用了两千多年的话：

"上兵伐谋，其次伐交，其次伐兵，其下攻城。"

它通常被当作战争智慧的顶点来读——最高明的用兵是打掉对方的谋划，最下策才是硬攻城池。这个读法没有错，但它把一条序列的第一级当成了最后一级。

上兵伐谋不是顶点。它上面还有四级。

而比这更要紧的是另一件事：战争根本不在这条序列里。

一、五级

这五级不是"五种兵"，是碰撞被处理的五种方式。每往上一级，那个叫"兵"的动作就少一点，那个叫涵育的东西就多占一点位置。

上兵伐谋。孙子的位置。在谋的层面处理碰撞，伐的对象是对方的谋、对方的计划、对方的布局。双方仍然互相承认为主体，兵作为智慧层面的较量存在。这已经是了不起的一步——它把碰撞从蛮力那一层提了上来。

超兵伐辩。两个已经在"人是目的"这件事上一致的主体，相互诘问、相互撼动、相互让对方显露。这里的"伐"不是击败，是让显露发生。辩的终点不是谁赢，是两个主体各自更清楚地显露了自己。

中世纪大学把这件事做成了制度。阿奎那写《神学大全》的体例是：每一个问题先以最强的形式陈述反对自己的意见，然后才回应。他不是在给自己找最弱的对手，是在给自己找最强的。公开的 disputatio 也一样——一个论题被当众攻打，主持者必须现场应对所有反驳。这种"伐"的目的不是消灭对方的论点，是把双方的位置都逼到清楚。

极兵伐思。这是序列的主语转换点，也是整条序列里最难的一级。伐思伐的不是对方的思，是自己的思——问的不是"怎么赢他"，是"为什么我发展得还不够，让这个碰撞对他来说变得没有意义？"

焦点从对面移回了自己。对面从"处理对象"变成了"反思镜"。

马可·奥勒留在多瑙河前线的营帐里写《沉思录》。他手上握着当时世界上最强的军事机器，正在打一场拖了多年的边境战争，而他在纸上写下的几乎全是对自己的诘问——今天我哪里做得不对，我为什么会被这件事激怒，我凭什么认为我的判断是对的。位置与动作的反差正是这一级的样子：手里有全部的兵，眼睛却收了回来。

玄兵不伐，走为先。"伐"这个动作在这里被取消。走为先不是打不过就跑的实用主义，是识别出这个碰撞不是自己该进入的场域，然后走回自己的道。

门诺派与阿米什人四百年的连续迁徙是一个长尺度的示范——从瑞士山谷到阿尔萨斯，到普鲁士，到俄国的草原，再到北美和南美。每一次遇到征兵、遇到强制、遇到不能接受的要求，他们的回应既不是打，也不是屈服，是走。走的代价极大：每一次都要重新开垦土地、重新建立生计、重新学一种语言。但他们要保的东西保住了。这不是软弱——四百年不打仗而没有消失，需要的韧性比打一场仗大得多。

圣者不兵，隐于宇。主语在这里彻底改变。这不是"某种更高级的兵"，是从兵的序列里走出来的人。圣者不需要避免兵，是兵对他的状态不相干。隐于宇不是躲起来，是不以需要被看见的方式存在。

这一级我举不出历史实例。而这本身就是它的定义带来的后果——一种不以需要被看见的方式存在的状态，不会在史料里留下可以指认的痕迹。凡是能被举出来的例子，大概都还不在这一级。举不出来不是资料的缺失，是这一级的性质。

二、序列在数什么

表面上，这条序列在数"兵这个动作的逐级消解"。它真正在数的是另一件事——涵育占据的结构空间在逐级变大。

伐谋里，涵育通过智慧的较量展开，双方在谋的层面互相磨出更深的判断力。伐辩里，涵育通过相互显露展开。伐思里，涵育通过自我反思展开——那种反思本身就是深化的动作。不伐里，涵育通过走开展开，让它在正确的场域里继续。不兵里，涵育已经充满了主体的整个生活，兵没有位置可以出现。

还有一种同样重要的数法：这条序列在一级一级地重新定义"对面"。

伐谋和伐辩里，对面是处理对象——眼睛在对方身上。从伐思开始，对面是反思镜：对方想和我碰撞，那是对方的事；我没有发展到让碰撞对他变得无意义，这是我的事。责任收回到了自己这边。到不伐，对面是场域的一部分，识别之后走开。到不兵，对面是互相砥砺的伙伴——问题不再是"与谁碰撞"，是"与谁一起生长"。

两种数法说的是同一件事的两面。

三、尽头不是没有碰撞

这里有一处必须说清楚，否则整条序列会被读成一种逃避主义。

序列的尽头不是没有碰撞，是不做不必要的碰撞。

两个主体之间真实的顶撞、真实的相互磨，是涵育的必要条件。没有这种碰撞，主体会萎缩成自我封闭，涵育会停滞。圣者隐于宇，不是回到孤立的自我循环里——他仍然在互相砥砺之中，只是砥砺的形态不再是战、不再是诡、不再是竞争。

去掉的是不必要的碰撞，不是碰撞本身。

四、那战争在哪里

现在可以回答开头那句话了。

战争不在这五级里。

五级是涵育在碰撞情境下的不同展开方式；战争是涵育被打断之后的修复手段。它不是序列里某个光荣的位置，是序列之外、为了让涵育恢复而不得不启用的兜底。

这个区别决定了战争和这条序列的关系是保护者，不是替代者。战争不替代序列上行，战争让序列上行在被打断的时候有机会恢复。当一个主体的涵育被压到五级里任何一级都启动不了——因为涵育本身已经无法继续——战争才作为兜底出现，目的是让它恢复到可以重新上行的状态。

上一篇说过战争和法在这套框架里同构。现在这个同构可以说得更完整：两者都在序列之外，都是保护性的机制，都不是涵育本身。法处理常态碰撞，让日常冲突不至于打断涵育；战争处理极端态碰撞，让涵育在已经被打断时还有一条恢复的路。

五、战争的开始永远是防守

还剩最后一件事要立下来，它决定了后面五篇讨论的对象范围。

战争的起源结构是不对称的。

一种系统性目的——国家利益、意识形态的实现、某种"应当如此"的文明形态的推广——它的内在运作方式就是扩张。它的目标不在当前边界内就完成，它总是需要更多：更多的资源、更多的服从者、更多的扩散空间。这不是某个具体系统的"坏"，是这种结构类型的内在逻辑：一个系统性目的不扩张就无法维持自己。

它扩张不动的时候也不会真的停下来，它蛰伏——继续做意识形态动员、军事建设、对内压制，等扩张的条件重新出现。所以它的"和平"是扩张的暂停，不是扩张意图的消失。

而且对外扩张和对内压制是同一件事的两面。要扩张，就必须把自己内部的人变成执行单元，因为完整的主体可能拒绝被动员、可能质疑扩张的合理性。所以一个系统性地压制自己人民的结构，向外扩张只是时间问题；一个正在向外扩张的结构，也必定在对内压制。

另一边，一个真正把"人是目的"作为运作方式的主体，没有扩张的必然性。它的"目的"是让每一个主体作为目的存在——这件事在结构上不需要通过压制别人来实现；恰恰相反，压制别人会直接违反它自己，因为被压制的那个也是主体。它就在自己所在的位置生长，不需要靠扩张来维持自己。

两层合起来，战争的真正结构就清楚了：开始永远是扩张方的进攻，防守方的反抗是防守，不是进攻。

这也划定了后面几篇的对象。两个扩张型系统之间的碰撞——历史上大部分战争的样子——不在射程内：双方都不以"人是目的"为起点做判断，这套判准对它们不起作用，那些事由外交、条约、力量均衡去处理。两个已经在"人是目的"上一致的主体之间也不需要战争：它们的碰撞在伐辩那一级就处理完了，因为它们之间没有目的冲突，只有实现路径的差异。

换个角度可以说得更硬。从涵育的视角看：两个扩张型系统碰撞时，涵育不在场，所以我们不想讨论；两个真正承认"人是目的"的主体碰撞时，涵育正在健康展开，所以我们不用讨论。剩下唯一不得不讨论的只有一种情境——涵育已经在场，正面临被吞噬的威胁，而它自己手里没有"压制"这个工具可用。

这是整个系列唯一的"不得不"。

六、反抗方的结构性难题

最后留一个悬念，它会贯穿后面五篇。

防守方的反抗是防守，但反抗本身带来一个麻烦：反抗需要组织、需要动员、需要战时纪律、需要集中决策。而这些要求在结构上，和它正在反抗的那种系统化运作方式非常像。

于是防守方有一个风险——它可能在反抗一台机器的过程中，把自己造成另一台一样的机器。

后面要立的四条定理、三种结束形态、反玉碎的判准，都不只是用来约束进攻方的。它们更是用来约束反抗方自己不要滑向它所反抗的那种结构。

因为如果那件事真的发生了，那么即使打赢了，世界上也只是少了一个防守者，多了一个压制者。

下一篇

既然战争是兜底，那它的目标是什么？

答案比想象中反直觉——不是打赢。

One line from Sunzi's Art of War has been quoted for twenty-three centuries:

"The highest use of arms attacks the opponent's strategy; next, his alliances; next, his troops; the lowest is to assault walled cities."

It is usually read as the summit of war wisdom — the finest generalship dismantles the other side's plan, and storming a city is the last resort. That reading is not wrong. But it mistakes the first rung of a sequence for the last.

Attacking strategy is not the summit. There are four levels above it.

And something matters more than that: war is not on this sequence at all.

1. Five Levels

These five are not five kinds of soldiering. They are five ways a collision can be handled. With each step up, the action called "arms" shrinks and the thing called cultivation takes up more room.

The highest use of arms attacks strategy (shang bing fa mou). Sunzi's position. The collision is handled at the level of planning, and what gets attacked is the opponent's design, his calculation, his disposition. Both sides still recognize each other as subjects; arms exist as a contest of intelligence. This is already a considerable achievement — it lifts collision off the level of brute force.

Arms transcended attack through disputation (chao bing fa bian). Two subjects who already agree that persons are ends interrogate each other, unsettle each other, and make each other disclose. Here "attack" does not mean defeat; it means making disclosure happen. The endpoint of a disputation is not who won, but that both parties have shown themselves more clearly.

The medieval universities turned this into an institution. Aquinas structured the Summa so that each question first states the objections against his own position in their strongest form, and only then answers them. He was not looking for the weakest opponent; he was looking for the strongest. Public disputatio worked the same way — a thesis was attacked in the open, and the respondent had to meet every objection live. The aim of that kind of attack is not to destroy the other's argument. It is to force both positions into clarity.

Arms at the limit attack one's own thought (ji bing fa si). This is where the subject of the sentence changes, and it is the hardest rung on the whole sequence. What gets attacked is not the opponent's thinking but one's own. The question is no longer "how do I beat him" but "why have I not developed far enough that this collision would become meaningless to him?"

The focus moves from across the field back to oneself. The opponent stops being an object to be handled and becomes a mirror for reflection.

Marcus Aurelius wrote the Meditations in a tent on the Danube frontier. He held the strongest military machine in the world and was fighting a border war that had dragged on for years, and what he wrote on the page is almost entirely interrogation of himself — where was I wrong today, why did that provoke me, on what basis do I assume my judgment is sound. The contrast between position and action is exactly what this rung looks like: every instrument of force in hand, and the eyes turned inward.

Arms grown mysterious do not attack; walking away comes first (xuan bing bu fa). Here the act of attacking is cancelled outright. Walking away is not the pragmatism of fleeing a fight you cannot win. It is recognizing that this collision is not a field you should have entered, and returning to your own path.

Four centuries of Mennonite and Amish migration is a long-scale demonstration — from Swiss valleys to Alsace, to Prussia, to the Russian steppe, and on to North and South America. Each time they met conscription, coercion, or a demand they could not accept, their answer was neither to fight nor to submit. It was to move. The cost was enormous: clearing new land, rebuilding a livelihood, learning another language, over and over. But what they were protecting survived. This is not weakness. Four hundred years of not fighting without disappearing takes more resilience than fighting a war does.

The sage has no arms and is hidden within the cosmos (sheng zhe bu bing). Here the subject changes completely. This is not a higher grade of soldiering; it is a person who has stepped out of the sequence of arms altogether. The sage does not need to avoid arms — arms are simply irrelevant to the state he is in. Being hidden is not concealment. It is existing in a way that does not require being seen.

I have no historical example for this rung. That absence follows from its own definition: a mode of existence that does not require being seen leaves no identifiable trace in the record. Any example that can be produced probably belongs to a lower rung. The gap is not missing evidence. It is the nature of the level.

2. What the Sequence Is Counting

On the surface, the sequence counts the stepwise dissolution of the act called "arms." What it actually counts is something else: the structural room cultivation occupies, expanding rung by rung.

At the strategy level, cultivation unfolds through a contest of intelligence — both sides sharpen their judgment against each other. At disputation, it unfolds through mutual disclosure. At self-thought, it unfolds through reflection, and that reflection is itself an act of deepening. At non-attack, it unfolds by walking away, so that growth can continue in the right field. At no-arms, cultivation has filled the subject's entire life and there is no position left for arms to occupy.

There is a second, equally important way to count: the sequence redefines "the other side," one rung at a time.

At strategy and disputation, the other side is an object to be handled — the eyes are on him. From self-thought onward, the other side becomes a mirror: that he wants a collision is his affair; that I have not developed far enough to make the collision pointless to him is mine. Responsibility comes back to my side of the field. At non-attack, the other side is part of a field to be recognized and left. At no-arms, the other side is a partner in mutual sharpening — the question is no longer whom I collide with, but whom I grow alongside.

Both countings describe two faces of the same thing.

3. The End of the Sequence Is Not the End of Collision

One point has to be settled here, or the whole sequence reads as a doctrine of avoidance.

The end of the sequence is not the absence of collision. It is the absence of unnecessary collision.

Real friction between subjects, real grinding against each other, is a necessary condition of cultivation. Without it, a subject shrinks into self-enclosure and growth stalls. The sage hidden within the cosmos has not retreated into an isolated loop of self — he is still in mutual sharpening. Only the form has changed: no longer war, no longer deceit, no longer competition.

What is removed is unnecessary collision, not collision itself.

4. So Where Is War

Now the opening line can be answered.

War is not on these five rungs.

The five are the different ways cultivation unfolds inside a collision. War is what repairs cultivation after it has been broken. It is not some honorable slot on the sequence; it sits outside it, a backstop invoked only because cultivation needs a route back.

This distinction settles the relationship between war and the sequence: war is a protector, not a substitute. It does not replace the upward movement; it gives that movement a chance to resume after an interruption. When a subject's cultivation has been pressed so far that none of the five rungs can even be engaged — because cultivation itself can no longer continue — war appears as the backstop, and its purpose is to restore the conditions under which the climb can start again.

The previous essay noted that war and law hold isomorphic positions in this framework. The isomorphism can now be stated in full: both sit outside the sequence, both are protective mechanisms, and neither is cultivation itself. Law handles ordinary collisions so that everyday conflict does not break cultivation. War handles extreme collisions so that cultivation has a route back once it has already broken.

5. A War Always Begins as Defense

One last thing has to be established, because it fixes the scope of the five essays that follow.

The origin structure of war is asymmetric.

A systemic purpose — national interest, the realization of an ideology, the propagation of some civilizational form that "ought" to prevail — operates by expanding. Its goal is never completed inside the current border; it always requires more: more resources, more compliance, more room to spread. This is not the wickedness of any particular system. It is the internal logic of that structural type. A systemic purpose that stops expanding cannot sustain itself.

When expansion is blocked, it does not actually stop. It goes dormant — continuing ideological mobilization, military buildup, internal suppression — and waits for conditions to return. Its "peace" is a pause in expansion, not the disappearance of the intent.

External expansion and internal suppression are also two faces of one thing. To expand, a system must turn its own people into executing units, because complete subjects might refuse mobilization or question whether the expansion makes sense. So a structure that systematically suppresses its own population will expand outward eventually; and a structure currently expanding outward is necessarily suppressing internally.

On the other side, a subject that genuinely operates by treating persons as ends has no necessity of expansion. Its purpose is that every subject exist as an end — and structurally that cannot be achieved by suppressing others. The opposite: suppressing others violates it directly, since the one suppressed is also a subject. It grows where it stands, and does not need expansion to sustain itself.

Put the two layers together and the real structure of war becomes clear: it always begins with the expanding side attacking, and the defending side's resistance is defense, not attack.

This also fixes the scope of what follows. Collisions between two expansionist systems — which is what most historical wars look like — fall outside the range. Neither side reasons from persons-as-ends, so these standards do not apply to them; those matters are handled by diplomacy, treaty, and the balance of power. Nor do two subjects who already agree that persons are ends need war: their collision is resolved at the level of disputation, because they have no conflict of purpose, only differences in how to realize the same one.

From cultivation's angle, this can be said more sharply. When two expansionist systems collide, cultivation is not present in the field, so we have no wish to discuss it. When two subjects who truly recognize persons as ends collide, cultivation is unfolding healthily, so there is no need to discuss it. Exactly one situation remains — cultivation is present, it faces being swallowed, and it has no instrument of suppression available to it.

That is the only "cannot-not" in this entire series.

6. The Defender's Structural Problem

One thread is left hanging here, and it runs through the five essays ahead.

The defender's resistance is defense — but resistance itself creates a difficulty. Resistance needs organization, mobilization, wartime discipline, centralized decision. Structurally, those requirements look a great deal like the systematized machinery being resisted.

So the defender runs a risk: in the course of resisting one machine, it may build itself into another one just like it.

The four theorems, the three ending forms, and the standard against self-annihilation — none of these exist only to restrain the attacker. They exist just as much to keep the resisting side from sliding into the structure it set out to resist.

Because if that happens, then even a military victory leaves the world with one fewer defender and one more suppressor.
