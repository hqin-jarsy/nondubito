第四篇：谋、阴谋、阳谋、诡

Essay 4: Strategy, Scheme, Open Play, and Deceit

Han Qin (秦汉)

"兵者，诡道也。"

这是《孙子兵法》开篇不久的一句话，也是中文世界里关于战争最广为流传的一个判断。它通常被理解成：打仗这件事，本质上就是骗。

这一篇要做的事是把"兵"这一级拆开。即使已经落到了兜底，兵里面也不是铁板一块——它有四个完全不同的结构位置：谋、阴谋、阳谋、诡。

四者的分流不是道德选择的分流。是它们和涵育的关系完全不同。

一、谋

谋是智慧、计算、策略、布局。双方在碰撞中用谋是正常的，而且用谋反而让伤害变小——因为碰撞被提到了智慧较量的层面，谁看得更远、谁判断更准、谁准备更充分，由这些真实能力决定结果。

谋不压制己方的主体性，也不靠欺骗对方来运作。它是透明的智慧较量。

这就是"上兵伐谋"的分量所在：把碰撞导向谋这一级，比停留在蛮力层面值得。谋在这套判准下完全合法。

二、阴谋

阴谋通过隐瞒真实意图、制造假象、让对方误判来获得优势。声东击西、虚张声势、诱敌深入都在这一级。阴谋依赖的是"对方不知道我的真实打算"。

短期常常奏效。但它有一个结构弱点：一旦被识破，失败的不只是这一次——对方从此会对你的一切姿态保持永久警惕。未来所有的合作、协议、信号都要被打一个折扣。

1870年的埃姆斯密电是这个结构的教科书。普鲁士首相收到一份关于国王与法国大使会面的电报，他把文字重新编辑了一遍——没有捏造任何事实，只是删削，让语气从平和变成了近乎羞辱——然后把这个版本发给报界。法国在几天内宣战。就短期效果而言，这是一次极其成功的操作。

但这类操作的长期账单是：此后几十年里，欧洲每一个外交对手在读这个国家的每一份声明时，都会先问一句"这次又删了什么"。信任一旦被这样花掉，就不能再花第二次。阴谋的短期收益，抵不过长期的信任崩塌。

阴谋不是被完全禁止的技术，但它是"一般"的技术——它的长期结构不如阳谋稳固，合法性也不如谋清楚。

三、阳谋

阳谋的做法是：把话摆在明面上，而且对方还不能不跟。

对方完全知道我要做什么、我的底线在哪、我的下一步是什么。但对方没有办法阻止——因为我所处的结构位置（资源、时间、地理、真实能力、正当性）让这件事对我有利，对方即使全部预见了，也没有可用的反制。

阳谋的力量不来自信息差。它来自结构位势差本身。

公元前127年的推恩令是最干净的一次演示。中央政府颁布的政令是：诸侯王的封地，从此不再只由嫡长子继承，而要分给所有的儿子。

没有一个字是隐瞒的。所有人都看得出这是削藩——几代之后，任何一块封地都会被分成小到不足为患的碎片。但没有一个诸侯王能拒绝，因为拒绝意味着要当着自己所有儿子的面说"你们不配分"。政令把压力从中央与诸侯之间，转移到了诸侯与他自己儿子之间。

这就是阳谋：透明，而且正因为透明才有效。

这让阳谋在这套判准下成为一种涵育友好的战争技术。双方都在完全清醒的状态下参与碰撞，双方的主体性都没有被取消，结果由结构决定而不是由蒙蔽决定。对方是在清醒中被结构说服的，不是在黑暗中被信息差打败的。

阳谋还有一个更深的性质：时间站在它这一边。

真实的结构不需要维护——真实和它自己是自动一致的。虚假需要持续维护：每一个新的陈述必须和旧的陈述对得上，每一次外部验证都是一个被识破的风险点，而知道内情的人只会越来越多。所以阳谋方的成本随时间下降，靠隐瞒运作的一方成本随时间单调上升。

阳谋不在短期赢。阳谋在时间里赢。

"大诡若诚"这句话真正指向的，正是这里。诡推到极致，反而走出了诡——因为诡推到极致要让自己看起来真诚，而真诚推到极致就变成了透明；一旦透明，力量的来源就必须从被包装的表象转移到结构本身。如果结构本身没有优势，透明只会暴露弱点；如果结构本身有优势，透明就变成了阳谋。

阳谋不是某种超级诡。它是诡这个轴消失之后剩下的东西——结构优势的自然显现。

四、诡：要害不在骗对方

这里必须先立一条防误读的定义，因为"诡"在日常语言里太容易被读成广义的"战争欺骗"。

战场上一次性的战术隐蔽——埋伏、佯攻、伪装行军路线——不是这里说的"诡"。那些属于谋或阴谋的范畴。

这里锁定的"诡"是更深的一层：为了维持某种系统性的欺骗结构，战争方必须让己方内部的人不知道真相。

这是关键的一步，请慢读一遍——

诡的要害不在于它欺骗了对方。欺骗对方的那个叫阴谋。

诡的要害在于：为了让这套操作运转下去，战争方必须对己方的士兵、己方的民众、己方的内部隐瞒真相。它让己方人员成为"不需要知道全貌的执行单元"。

这就是诡的结构定义。它把己方的主体性降格成了工具。

所以诡不合法的根本原因不是它伤害了对方，是它把己方内部变成了它本来要反对的那种结构。每一次使用诡，都压制一次己方的主体性，打断一次己方的涵育。

1914年之前的欧洲同盟体系是这一层的清楚展示。当时几个大国之间存在一整套秘密条约和军事协定——参战义务、动员时间表、援助承诺，其中相当一部分内容对本国议会和民众是不公开的。也就是说，几千万人被绑在一套他们不知道内容的承诺上；等到危机来临，动员表开始运转，很多人是在已经无法回头的时候，才知道自己站在了什么位置上。

这个体系的运转，必须以本国国民不知情为条件。这就是诡。

五、诡的四个位置

把诡放在四个不同的结构位置上看一遍，它的性质会更清楚。

两个扩张型系统之间。双方都可以用诡，也都在用。双方也都付出己方涵育被打断的代价。这就是大部分历史战争的样貌——两边都在骗自己人，两边都在结构上受损，谁也没有走到更高的位置。

两个真正承认"人是目的"的主体之间。诡既用不了也用不上。它们的碰撞形态是辩，而辩要求双方相互显露；一个对自己人隐瞒的主体，连自己的立场都不完整地拥有，根本没法参与辩。

防守反抗的一方。这是最要紧的一个位置：它不能用诡——不是它选择不用，是它的结构让诡启动不了。

后面第五篇会立下一条定理：战争方不得不接受追问。而诡要求压制内部追问。两者不可共存。防守方的士兵、民众、异议者可以追问决策，而指挥官压不住这些追问——因为压住的那一刻，他就已经从自己所在的位置上跌了下去。

还有更深的一层。防守方的结构性目标不是消灭对方，是让对方作为主体真正站起来。压制方之所以在压制，正是因为它自己被系统目的工具化了——它内部的人同样是被压制的主体。防守方真正的工作是让那些人有机会从工具化里解放出来。而这件事不可能通过诡完成：诡预设对方是可以被欺骗的客体，解放的工作预设对方可以作为主体重新站立。两个预设互相排斥。

压制的一方。诡的主要使用者，而且它是诡的原生地。系统性的扩张在结构上就依赖内外两重欺骗：对内让被工具化的人不知道全貌，对外让被压制方和第三方误判自己的性质。

但诡对它自己的账单同样真实，而且有三笔。

第一笔，内部涵育被彻底打断。长期处在被欺骗、被压制状态里的人，涵育无法在他们身上发生。

第二笔，短寿。维护成本随时间单调递增，最终会超过扩张的收益，系统在内部矛盾里自行崩溃。大征服型的结构常常在数代之内衰败或分裂——这不是巧合。

第三笔最有意思。它的士兵和民众会看到：对面不用诡，对面允许内部追问，对面的运作是透明的。然后一个念头会浮起来——"人家那边怎么不干这个？我们真的是对的那一边吗？"

这种自我怀疑在压制方内部会慢慢积累。而这个自我怀疑本身，就是涵育最深层的工作。一个人从内部识别出"诡是结构劣势的标志"，就是在识别自己需要从工具化里走出来。

所以诡不是压制方的长期优势。它是压制方的慢性自毁。

六、诡锁死了整条序列

最后一个观察，它比前面所有的都更硬。

诡不只是在兵这一级里有结构问题，它还锁死了整条上行序列。

一个习惯对己方隐瞒的主体，走不到任何一个上行层级。

走不到伐辩，因为辩要求诚——包括对内的诚。走不到伐思，因为伐思要求把眼睛从对方身上移回自己身上，而诡训练的恰恰是持续盯着对方、同时对内隐藏。走不到不伐，因为不伐要求识别场域然后走开，而诡让人习惯留在场域里做操作。走不到不兵，因为不兵要求站在"人是目的"里——包括己方人员也是目的——而诡站在"己方人员可以被当作工具使用"里。

所以"兵者诡道也"这句话在这套判准下的真实后果是：把整个碰撞处理的可能空间，从五级上行的全谱压缩到最低的一级。

这才是拒绝诡的最深理由。不是诡不好用——它作为技术确实有效，至少短期有效。是诡把一个文明锁死在了兵的最低级里，让所有上行的通道都关上了。

七、必须给孙子的credit

这一篇对孙子不是道德评价，是结构观察。而且必须在这里给他一次完整的承认。

孙子在两千三百年前能把谋和诡分开，这件事本身就已经在朝"人是目的"的方向走了。

他立下"上兵伐谋、其次伐交、其次伐兵、其下攻城"这个层级，意思是：兵不全是一回事。兵里面有高低、有优劣、有不同的结构位置。把碰撞导向谋比导向攻城更值得——这个区分的建立本身就是一种结构自觉，它说明孙子已经感受到，"兵"不是一个同质的工具箱，是一个有内在层级的场域。

同样，他把"兵者诡道也"说出来的时候，并没有美化诡，也没有把诡推为战争的最高形态。相反，在他自己立下的层级里，伐谋在伐兵之上，谋在诡之上。他在他可用的语言里，已经在说"伐谋比诡更好"这件事了。

在他所处的位置上做到这一步，已经非常深。它意味着孙子已经感受到：战争这件事不是所有技术都等价的，有些技术比另一些更值得追求，而最高的战争技术是不用打的那一种。这些感受在这套框架的语言里可以说成"朝向相互承认"、"朝向涵育的保全"、"朝向序列上行"——但孙子没有这些语言可用。他用他手上有的，把这件事说到了他能说到的极致。

他看到了谋和诡的差别。这个系列能做的，只是把这个差别的结构理由说清楚：谋不压制主体，诡压制主体；阳谋让对方作为清醒的主体参与，诡让己方作为工具被使用；谋的力量来自智慧的真实较量，诡的力量来自信息差；时间站在谋和阳谋这一边，时间在诡的反面。

这些理由孙子说不出来，但他感受到了。两千三百年前能感受到这件事的人，本身就在他那一层里撑着涵育的方向。今天能在新的语言里把它说出来，是站在他的肩膀上——而这个接续本身，就是涵育的一种方式。

下一篇

定位做完了，技术分流也做完了。接下来是这个系列最硬的部分：四条约束。

它们全都以同一个词起头——不得不。

"War is the way of deception."

The line appears early in Sunzi's Art of War, and it is probably the single most widely circulated judgment about war in the Chinese language. It is usually taken to mean: fighting is, at bottom, lying.

This essay takes the level of arms apart. Even once things have fallen to the backstop, arms are not uniform. There are four entirely different structural positions inside them: strategy (mou), scheming (yin-mou), open play (yang-mou), and deceit (gui).

What separates them is not a moral choice. It is that each stands in a completely different relation to cultivation.

1. Strategy

Strategy is intelligence, calculation, planning, disposition. Using it in a collision is normal, and using it actually reduces harm — because the collision is raised to the level of a contest of judgment, where the outcome is settled by who sees further, judges more accurately, and prepares more thoroughly.

Strategy does not suppress your own side's subjectivity, and it does not run on deceiving the other side. It is a transparent contest of intelligence.

That is what gives "the highest use of arms attacks strategy" its weight: steering a collision to this level is worth more than leaving it at the level of force. Strategy is fully legitimate under these standards.

2. Scheming

Scheming gains advantage by concealing real intent, manufacturing appearances, and inducing misjudgment. Feints, bluffs, drawing an enemy in — all of it lives here. Scheming depends on the other side not knowing what you actually intend.

It often works in the short run. But it has a structural weakness: once it is seen through, what fails is not just this one operation. From then on the other side treats every posture you adopt as suspect. All future cooperation, agreements, and signals get discounted.

The Ems Dispatch of 1870 is the textbook case. The Prussian chancellor received a telegram reporting a meeting between his king and the French ambassador. He re-edited the text — fabricating nothing, only cutting, so that a mild account read as something close to an insult — and released that version to the press. France declared war within days. As a short-term operation it was extraordinarily effective.

The long-term bill on operations like that reads differently. For decades afterward, every diplomatic counterpart in Europe read every statement from that state while first asking: what was cut this time? Trust spent that way cannot be spent twice. Scheming's short-term yield does not cover its long-term collapse of credibility.

Scheming is not categorically forbidden here, but it is the mediocre technique. Its long-term structure is less stable than open play, and its legitimacy less clear than strategy's.

3. Open Play

Open play works like this: put everything on the table, and the other side still cannot refuse.

They know exactly what I intend, where my limits are, what my next move is. And they cannot stop it — because my structural position (resources, time, geography, real capability, standing) makes the move favorable to me, and foreseeing it in full gives them no usable counter.

Open play's force does not come from an information gap. It comes from the differential in structural position itself.

The Chinese imperial edict of 127 BCE known as the Order of Extended Grace is the cleanest demonstration. The decree announced that a vassal king's fief would no longer pass intact to his eldest son, but be divided among all his sons.

Not one word was hidden. Everyone could see this was a policy for dissolving the vassal states — within a few generations, any fief would be split into fragments too small to threaten anything. And not a single vassal king could refuse, because refusing meant telling all of his own sons, to their faces, that they did not deserve a share. The decree moved the pressure off the axis between the center and the kings, and onto the axis between each king and his own children.

That is open play: transparent, and effective precisely because it is transparent.

This makes open play a cultivation-friendly technique under these standards. Both sides participate fully awake, neither side's subjectivity is cancelled, and the outcome is decided by structure rather than by blindness. The other party is persuaded by the structure while lucid, not defeated in the dark by an information gap.

Open play has a deeper property too: time is on its side.

A real structure requires no maintenance — the truth is automatically consistent with itself. Falsehood requires continuous maintenance: every new statement must square with the old ones, every external check is a point at which it can break, and the number of people who know the inside keeps growing. So the cost to the open player falls over time, while the cost to anyone operating on concealment rises monotonically.

Open play does not win in the short run. It wins in time.

There is an old line: great deceit resembles sincerity. What it actually points at is exactly this. Push deceit to its limit and it walks out of deceit altogether — because deceit at the limit has to look sincere, and sincerity at the limit becomes transparency. And once you are transparent, the source of your force has to move from the packaging to the structure itself. If the structure holds no advantage, transparency only exposes weakness. If it does, transparency becomes open play.

Open play is not a superior grade of deceit. It is what remains once the axis of deceit disappears — structural advantage simply showing itself.

4. Deceit: The Crux Is Not Fooling the Enemy

A definition has to be fixed first, because "deceit" reads far too easily as a general term for wartime lying.

One-off tactical concealment on a battlefield — an ambush, a feint, a disguised line of march — is not what is meant here. Those belong to strategy or scheming.

What is meant here is a deeper layer: in order to sustain a systematic structure of deception, a warring party must keep its own people from knowing the truth.

This is the crucial step, and it is worth reading twice.

Deceit's crux is not that it fools the enemy. Fooling the enemy is scheming.

Deceit's crux is that, for the operation to keep running, the warring party must conceal the truth from its own soldiers, its own population, its own interior. It turns its own people into executing units that do not need to know the whole picture.

That is the structural definition of deceit. It demotes your own side's subjectivity into instrumentality.

So the fundamental reason deceit is illegitimate is not the harm it does to the enemy. It is that it turns your own interior into the very structure you set out to oppose. Every use of it suppresses your own subjectivity once more and interrupts your own cultivation once more.

The European alliance system before 1914 shows this layer plainly. A web of secret treaties and military conventions ran between the great powers — obligations to enter a war, mobilization timetables, promises of support — and a substantial part of it was not disclosed to national parliaments or populations. Tens of millions of people were bound to commitments whose contents they did not know. When the crisis came and the timetables began to run, many learned what position they were standing in only after there was no way back.

That system could only function on the condition that its own citizens did not know. That is deceit.

5. Deceit in Four Positions

Run deceit through four different structural positions and its nature comes into focus.

Between two expansionist systems. Both can use deceit, and both do. Both also pay the price of having their own cultivation interrupted. This is what most historical wars look like — both sides lying to their own people, both structurally damaged, neither having climbed anywhere.

Between two subjects who genuinely recognize persons as ends. Deceit is neither usable nor useful. Their collisions take the form of disputation, and disputation requires mutual disclosure. A party that conceals from its own people does not even fully possess its own position, and cannot participate in disputation at all.

The defending, resisting side. This is the most important position: it cannot use deceit — not by choice, but because its structure will not let deceit start.

The next essay establishes a theorem: a warring party cannot not accept questioning. Deceit requires suppressing internal questioning. The two cannot coexist. The defender's soldiers, citizens, and dissenters can question decisions, and the commander cannot shut those questions down — because the moment he does, he has already fallen out of the position he occupies.

There is a deeper layer. The defender's structural goal is not to destroy the other side but to let the other side stand up as subjects. The suppressing party suppresses precisely because it has been instrumentalized by its own systemic purpose — the people inside it are suppressed subjects too. The defender's real work is to give those people a chance to come out of that instrumentalization. And that work cannot be done through deceit: deceit presupposes the other as an object that can be fooled, while liberation presupposes the other as a subject who can stand again. The two presuppositions exclude each other.

The suppressing side. The principal user of deceit, and its native ground. Systematic expansion structurally depends on deception running in two directions: inward, so the instrumentalized do not see the whole picture; outward, so the suppressed and third parties misread what it is.

But the bill deceit runs up against its own user is equally real, and it comes in three parts.

First, internal cultivation is broken outright. People held for long periods in deception and suppression are people in whom cultivation cannot occur.

Second, a short life. Maintenance costs rise monotonically until they exceed the returns from expansion, and the system collapses in its own internal contradictions. Large conquest structures frequently decay or fracture within a few generations. That is not a coincidence.

Third, and most interesting. Its soldiers and citizens notice that the other side does not use deceit, that the other side permits internal questioning, that the other side's operation is transparent. And a thought surfaces: why don't they have to do this? Are we actually the side that is right?

That self-doubt accumulates slowly inside the suppressing party. And the self-doubt is itself cultivation's deepest work. A person who recognizes from the inside that deceit is the mark of a structural disadvantage is recognizing that he needs to come out of his own instrumentalization.

So deceit is not a long-term advantage for the suppressing side. It is that side's chronic self-destruction.

6. Deceit Locks Down the Whole Sequence

One last observation, and it is harder than everything above.

Deceit is not merely a structural problem at the level of arms. It locks down the entire upward sequence.

A subject in the habit of concealing from its own people cannot reach any level above.

Not disputation, because disputation requires honesty — including honesty inward. Not self-thought, because that requires moving the eyes off the opponent and back onto oneself, while deceit trains exactly the opposite reflex: stay fixed on the opponent while hiding from your own. Not non-attack, because that requires recognizing a field and leaving it, while deceit habituates you to staying in the field and operating. Not no-arms, because that requires standing inside persons-as-ends — including your own people as ends — while deceit stands inside "our own people may be used as instruments."

So the real consequence of "war is the way of deception," under these standards, is this: it compresses the entire possible space of collision-handling from a five-level spectrum down to the lowest rung.

That is the deepest reason to refuse deceit. Not that it fails to work — as a technique it is genuinely effective, at least in the short run. It is that deceit locks a civilization into the lowest level of arms and closes every channel upward.

7. The Credit Sunzi Is Owed

None of this is a moral evaluation of Sunzi. It is a structural observation. And he is owed full acknowledgment here.

That Sunzi could separate strategy from deceit twenty-three centuries ago is itself movement in the direction of persons-as-ends.

His hierarchy — attack strategy, then alliances, then troops, and last of all walled cities — means that arms are not all one thing. There are higher and lower positions inside arms. Steering a collision toward strategy is worth more than steering it toward a siege. Drawing that distinction at all is a form of structural self-awareness: it shows Sunzi already felt that "arms" is not a homogeneous toolbox but a field with internal levels.

Likewise, when he wrote that war is the way of deception, he did not glamorize deceit or promote it as war's highest form. On the contrary, within his own hierarchy, attacking strategy stands above attacking troops, and strategy stands above deceit. In the language available to him, he was already saying that attacking strategy is better than deceit.

Getting that far from where he stood is remarkably deep. It means Sunzi already sensed that not all techniques of war are equivalent, that some are more worth pursuing than others, and that the highest technique is the one that does not require fighting. In this framework's language those intuitions would be called orientation toward mutual recognition, toward preserving cultivation, toward climbing the sequence — but none of that vocabulary existed for him. He used what he had, and pushed it as far as it would go.

He saw the difference between strategy and deceit. All this series can do is state the structural reason for that difference: strategy does not suppress subjects, deceit does; open play lets the other participate as a lucid subject, deceit uses one's own as instruments; strategy's force comes from a real contest of intelligence, deceit's from an information gap; time stands with strategy and open play, and stands against deceit.

Sunzi could not have said those reasons. He felt them. Someone who could feel that twenty-three centuries ago was holding cultivation's direction open at his own level. That we can now state it in a later vocabulary is standing on his shoulders — and that continuity is itself one of the ways cultivation happens.

The locating work is done, and so is the technical taxonomy. What comes next is the hardest part of this series: four constraints.

All four begin with the same words — cannot not.
