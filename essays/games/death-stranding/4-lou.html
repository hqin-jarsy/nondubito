Essay IV of IV · Death Stranding死亡搁浅：绳、桥与被留下的人 · IV / IV

Lou

路易斯

Sep 4, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)Independent English edition中文原作 · 简 / 繁

Edition note: An independent English rewriting of the Chinese argument for English-language readers. Plot claims have been checked and interpretive claims are marked as such.

Years ago, Clifford Unger ran down a corridor carrying an infant. He was trying to escape the facility that had turned his son into an experiment.

He did not make it out. The gunfire struck both father and child.

The child was Sam.

An early sacrifice

Sam was at the center of the early Bridge Baby experiments, intended as a sacrifice for the developing chiral network. Cliff tried to rescue him; both received fatal wounds.

Amelie found the dead infant on the Beach and returned him to life. Sam became a repatriate. By upsetting the balance between living and dead, this rescue triggered the wider Death Stranding that engulfed the world.

The chronology needs care. BT incidents and early voidouts, including the Manhattan disaster, had already occurred. Sam’s repatriation was not the first supernatural death or explosion. It transformed a breach into a pervasive condition.

His life is therefore born inside an account that cannot be neatly settled. He was saved from a program that treated an infant as a component; the person who saved him had also authorized that program and accidentally shot him. Rescue, exploitation, guilt, and power occupy the same act.

An unpayable account is not a blank one

Measured only by consequences, returning Sam to life helped produce catastrophe. Seen only from the Beach, Amelie faced a baby sacrificed by an adult project and mortally wounded by her own hand.

The game refuses an easy calculation. It also refuses to say that loving intent erases responsibility. Amelie later exploits Sam’s trust and sends him to build the very network that can accelerate the Last Stranding. The original rescue does not absolve her manipulation. Her later manipulation does not turn the infant into someone who ought to have remained dead.

Some choices have consequences too extended to total in advance. That does not make every choice equivalent. One question remains available even when the full balance sheet does not: has the life immediately before us been made into a replaceable part of someone else’s plan?

An embrace

At the final Beach, Death Stranding offers the outline of a conventional showdown. Sam has a gun. Amelie appears to be the final adversary. The action that matters, however, is an embrace.

For Sam, this is not sentimental decoration. He lives with aphenphosmphobia, an intense aversion to touch. He can connect cargo terminals, data archives, and an entire nation while keeping himself outside the connection.

The embrace neither proves that humanity will survive nor excuses what Amelie has done. It refuses to compress her into an object that must be eliminated, while also refusing the ending she has chosen for everyone else. Sam treats her as someone who can still be addressed, opposed, and held responsible.

Amelie isolates her Beach and postpones extinction. The rain that follows is ordinary rain. The end has not been abolished. It has simply not been imposed today.

The last delivery

After Sam returns, BB-28 shows no signs of life and is declared a failed unit. His order is familiar: carry the pod to the incinerator.

This is not a special punishment for becoming self-aware. It is the ordinary logic of the BB system. Equipment that can no longer perform is disposed of.

But Sam has already been calling the baby Lou. He has soothed Lou, depended on Lou, and repeatedly exposed Lou to his own memories. Naming and attachment—the two gestures discouraged by procedure—did not suddenly occur in the final scene. The final order makes it impossible for Sam to pretend that they changed nothing.

For the first time, he refuses to complete a delivery.

From “it” to “her”

Sam opens the pod, removes Lou, and desperately tries to revive her. She eventually breathes.

The game does not supply a complete technical explanation for that revival. It does not establish a rule that any BB can survive if someone simply opens the pod. Treating the scene as an experiment that disproves official medicine would make its choice smaller.

Sam does not disobey because he knows a miracle will occur. He first refuses to treat Lou as defective equipment; only afterward does he see her return to life.

Nor is “Lou” a name invented at this instant. It is the name by which Sam has already acknowledged a person throughout the journey. The ending reveals that Lou is a girl, later known in full as Louise. A name does not create her life. It prevents Sam from hiding his responsibility behind a serial number.

No total victory, but a real refusal

The ending does not announce that the Bridge Baby system has been abolished. It does not purify the chiral network or rescue every infant built into it. Sam saves one child.

That is not a systemic triumph. Neither is it merely private sentiment.

Institutions survive not only through commands but through countless operators who continue to perceive a life as a category, a unit, or a function. Sam cannot rewrite the institution in that moment. He can stop performing its final required action.

The entire game has asked him to deliver things to the destinations chosen for them. His last assignment is to deliver failed equipment to the fire.

He does not deliver her.

He carries Lou out of the pod and into the ordinary rain. The world is not repaired. But one person is no longer being transported toward an ending somebody else selected for her.

Based on Kojima Productions' 2019 Death Stranding and the 2021 Director's Cut, whose main story is unchanged. It does not cover the sequel. Full spoilers. The ending's revival is intentionally treated as unexplained rather than converted into a universal rule.

很多年前，克利福德抱着一个婴儿逃离实验设施。

他没能逃出去。枪击中父亲，也击中了孩子。

那个孩子就是山姆。

一、最早的牺牲品

山姆是早期布桥婴实验的核心，也是最初准备接入开罗尔网络的牺牲品。克利福德试图把他从计划中救走，父子却一起中了致命枪伤。

亚美莉把死去的婴儿山姆从冥滩送回人间，使他成为遣返者。这个动作打乱了生死平衡，触发了后来席卷世界的死亡搁浅。

时间线需要说准：在山姆获救之前，搁浅者现象与早期虚空噬灭已经出现，曼哈顿灾难也已发生。亚美莉的遣返不是“世界上第一次死人爆炸”，而是把局部裂口推成了广泛、持续的灾变。

于是山姆的生命从一开始就带着无法结清的账。他被从一套把婴儿当组件的计划里救下；救他的那个人，恰恰也是批准计划并在混乱中误伤他的人。这个世界不是由一个纯粹善意或纯粹恶意的动作改变的，而是由拯救、利用、愧疚和权力缠在一起的动作改变的。

二、账算不清，不等于什么都一样

若只看后果，遣返山姆带来了巨大的灾难。若只看眼前，亚美莉面对的是一个被成人计划牺牲、又被自己射中的婴儿。

游戏没有替玩家做简单结算。它也没有说，只要出于爱，后果便不必负责。恰恰相反，亚美莉后来又利用山姆对她的信任，让他建设能够加速最后搁浅的网络。一次救人不能替她之后的操纵免罪；之后的操纵，也不能把当初那个婴儿重新写成“本来就该死”。

有些选择没有一张能提前算完的总账。但不能算完，不等于没有方向。至少有一个问题始终可以问：眼前这个生命，是不是被当成了计划里可替换的一格？

三、一个拥抱

最后，山姆来到亚美莉的冥滩。这里没有常规意义上的最终决战。游戏先给他枪，最终真正有效的动作却是靠近和拥抱。

这对山姆尤其重要。他长期害怕身体接触，能把货物、资料和整个国家送进网络，却始终把自己留在连接之外。

拥抱并不能证明人类会获救，也不能洗掉亚美莉做过的事。它只是拒绝把她压成必须被消灭的敌人，同时也拒绝接受她替所有人做出的终局决定。山姆承认她仍是一个可以被呼唤、被说服、也必须对自己的选择负责的人。

于是亚美莉把自己的冥滩与其他世界隔开，为人类争取时间。雨随后恢复成普通的雨。终结没有消失，只是没有在今天发生。

四、最后一份委托

回来以后，BB-28生命迹象消失，被判定为故障单元。山姆接到命令：把它送去焚化炉。

这条命令并非专门为了惩罚它“产生了自我意识”，而是延续布桥婴制度一贯的处置方式：不能运作的设备就被销毁。

但山姆在漫长旅程中早已不再叫它BB-28。他叫它Lou。他哄过它，依赖过它，也让它一次次看见自己的记忆。起名字和产生感情这两件被规程禁止的事，并非结尾突然发生；结尾只是让他不能再假装那些事没有改变关系。

他没有把Lou送进火里。

五、从“它”到“她”

山姆打开舱，把Lou抱出来，试图唤醒她。她最终恢复呼吸。游戏没有给这个复苏一个完整的技术说明，也没有证明所有BB只要被抱出舱就能存活。把它说成一条被实验推翻的旧规矩，会让结尾反而变小。

重要的是山姆作决定时并不知道奇迹会发生。他没有因为确信Lou能活才违抗命令。他先拒绝把她当作失效设备，之后才看见她呼吸。

这里的“路易斯”并不是刚刚赐下的名字，而是Lou作为一个“谁”终于得到公开确认。游戏直到结尾才明确她是女孩，后来完整的名字是Louise。名字没有制造她的生命，却让山姆无法再用编号躲开自己的责任。

六、没有总胜利，仍然有真实的改变

结尾没有告诉我们布桥婴制度已经废除，也没有证明开罗尔网络从此清白。山姆救下的是一个孩子，不是所有孩子。

因此这不是宏大的制度胜利。但也不能把它缩成一次私人心软。

制度之所以能长期运作，靠的不只是命令，也靠每一个执行者继续把眼前的生命看成类别、编号和功能。山姆无法在那一刻重写整套制度，却可以停止替它完成最后一个动作。

整部游戏都在让他“把东西送到”。最后一份委托仍然如此：把失效的设备送去焚化。

他第一次没有送到。

他把她抱出舱，走进恢复正常的雨里。世界没有因此被彻底修好，可至少有一个人不再被送往别人替她决定的终点。
