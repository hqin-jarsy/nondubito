Refusing to Let Go

不肯放手

Sep 4, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)Independent English edition中文原作 · 简 / 繁

Edition note: An independent English rewriting of the Chinese argument for English-language readers. Plot claims have been checked and interpretive claims are marked as such.

Death Stranding is crowded with people who cannot let go.

One holds a dead child. Another dies every twenty-one minutes to search for his family. A soldier stalks battlefields assembled from memory, trying to recover the son taken from him.

The game neither canonizes them as monuments to devotion nor dismisses them with the instruction to “move on.” It asks a prior question: what, exactly, does letting go release?

Mama’s umbilical cord

Mama gives birth beneath the rubble of a destroyed hospital. Her baby dies and remains with her as an infant BT, visible only to Mama. An umbilical cord binds them. It also keeps Mama’s damaged body attached to the world of the living.

She cannot leave the ruins simply because grief has made her unwilling to walk out. Death Stranding makes attachment literal and ontological. Mama keeps the child with her, and the child keeps Mama here. She feeds, cradles, and speaks to a baby nobody else can see. The gestures are love, but they are also the conditions of her continued existence.

When the cord is finally cut, the moment is not the exposure of a delusion. Both sides pay. The child can depart, while Mama’s body rapidly fails.

“Letting go” therefore does not mean deleting the past and returning intact to ordinary life. Some relationships change us so completely that their ending also ends the person we were inside them.

Heartman’s twenty-one minutes

Heartman lost his wife and daughter in a voidout. He lives for twenty-one minutes, arrests his heart for three, searches the Beach for them, and is automatically resuscitated. Meals, research, and conversation all submit to the timer. He politely excuses himself, dies, and returns to finish his sentence.

He understands how poor his chances are. He does not conscript other people into his grief. That lucidity makes his fixation more striking, not less. A life can be orderly, productive, even charming while its entire order remains arranged around a loss.

Eventually Heartman receives evidence that his family has moved beyond the Beach he has been searching. He does not conclude that the years of searching were foolish. He permits his living minutes to acquire another use. Letting go is not admitting that he loved wrongly. It is releasing love from the demand that it must repeat one action forever.

Cliff’s battlefields

Sam is repeatedly pulled into war-torn spaces and hunted by Clifford Unger. The violence is real. Cliff attacks Sam, and knowing his motive later does not make those encounters harmless.

But conquest is not what drives him. Believing BB-28 to be the child taken from him, Cliff is trying to recover his son. He has become trapped in spaces where war, memory, and Beach overlap, replaying a rescue that failed in life.

Only near the end does he recognize that the son he has been seeking is Sam himself.

That recognition changes our understanding without converting combat into a misunderstanding. Love does not automatically make conduct gentle. The more desperately Cliff tries to recover the past, the more easily he reduces people in the present to doors leading back to it.

Higgs and Amelie

Higgs treats extinction as inevitable and chooses to accelerate it. His nihilism can sound philosophical, but his conduct includes deliberate cruelty and coercion. He is not simply the calm spokesman for acceptance.

Amelie is more difficult. As an Extinction Entity, she knows that an ending will come. She also manipulates Sam into completing the chiral network and works through Higgs, bringing the Last Stranding closer. It would be false to say she merely waits in honesty for Sam to decide.

Yet at the final Beach she does open a possibility: extinction now, or isolation and delay—more human time without any guarantee of ultimate rescue.

Sam cannot prove that delay will redeem humanity. His answer is more modest. Finite time is still time. An ending that cannot be prevented does not make every moment of its arrival equivalent.

The game does choose a direction

Death Stranding understands the humiliation and pain of being unable to let go, but it does not flatten every response into an equally valid option.

Mama cuts the cord. Heartman accepts that his family has moved on. Cliff recognizes Sam and ceases to mistake another BB for his child. Sam embraces Amelie and asks her to postpone the end.

The direction is subtler than “hold on” or “move on.” What are you preserving: the other person, or the role you require that person to keep performing?

Refusing to discard a life is not the same as refusing to let that life leave, change, or become itself. The first may be care. The second can become another form of possession.

No year is simply wasted

Nor does change retroactively empty the years of fixation.

Mama reconnects with Lockne. Heartman’s research helps others understand chiral matter and timefall. The child Cliff seeks survives and completes a journey his father could not.

People rarely recover exactly what they set out to save. Their unfinished devotion can nevertheless leave something behind—knowledge, a bond, a path—that another person later carries in a different direction.

The final essay turns to that surviving child, and to the baby on his chest who has long since ceased to be only a number.

Based on Kojima Productions' 2019 Death Stranding and the 2021 Director's Cut, whose main story is unchanged. It does not cover the sequel. Full spoilers.

《死亡搁浅》里，到处都是不肯放手的人。

有人抱着已经死去的孩子，有人每隔二十一分钟死一次，有人在结束多年的战争里寻找被夺走的儿子。游戏没有急着赞美他们，也没有把他们都诊断成“应该走出来”。它先问另一件事：放手究竟意味着什么？

一、芒姆的脐带

医院坍塌时，芒姆刚刚生产。孩子死了，成为只有她能看见的婴儿搁浅者；一条脐带把母女连在一起，也把芒姆的身体留在生者这边。

所以她不能离开那片废墟，不只是因为悲伤使她不愿走。游戏把依恋变成了一个字面上的生死结构：孩子被她留住，她也被孩子留住。她照顾、喂养、拥抱这个别人看不见的婴儿，那些动作既是爱，也是她存在于此的条件。

最终剪断脐带，不是识破一场自我欺骗，而是两边都付出代价。孩子得以离去，芒姆的身体也很快崩坏。所谓“放手”，并不是把过去删掉以后恢复正常，而是承认有些关系一旦结束，原来的自己也不会完整保留下来。

二、心人的二十一分钟

心人的家人在虚空噬灭中死去。他给自己装上自动除颤装置：活二十一分钟，心跳停止三分钟，在冥滩上寻找妻女，再被拉回来。

他把生活切成了一个搜索循环。吃饭、研究、谈话都得服从倒计时，说到一半会礼貌地躺下，三分钟后再接着说。

他清楚希望渺茫，也没有用自己的悲伤绑架别人。正因为一切如此理性，这份执着才更醒目：一个人可以把日子安排得井井有条，而整套秩序仍围绕着一件失去的事旋转。

后来，他得到迹象，家人已经离开那片冥滩。他没有因此否定过去多年的寻找；他只是终于能够把活着的时间用在别处。放手在这里不是承认爱错了，而是停止要求爱永远保持同一种动作。

三、克利福德的战场

山姆数次被卷入战争般的空间，被克利福德·昂格追击。那些枪火是真的，克利福德也确实攻击山姆；不能因为后来知道他的动机，就把危险抹掉。

但他的目标不是征服，更不是单纯杀死山姆。他以为BB-28是自己被夺走的孩子，想把孩子抢回来。他被困在由记忆、战争与冥滩叠成的空间里，一遍遍重演营救失败。

直到最后，他才认出自己一直寻找的孩子其实就是山姆。

这个认出没有让过去的战斗变成误会。它只是改变了我们看待那份暴力的方式：爱并不会自动使行动无害。一个人越无法接受失去，也可能越把眼前的人压成通往过去的工具。

四、希格斯与亚美莉

希格斯相信灭绝不可避免，于是选择加速它。他的话里有虚无的诱惑，但他的行动也包含清楚的操纵与伤害，不能只当成一种平静的哲学立场。

亚美莉更复杂。身为灭绝体，她知道终局会来，也曾利用山姆重建开罗尔网络，与希格斯合作，让最后搁浅更快接近。她不是从头到尾诚实地站在远处等待答案。

可到了最后的冥滩，她又把可能性放到山姆面前：让灭绝现在完成，或者把自己隔离起来，为人类争取一段无法保证结局的时间。

山姆不能证明延迟一定有用。他只能回答：有限的时间仍然是时间，注定结束不等于现在结束没有区别。

五、作品其实给了方向

《死亡搁浅》理解不肯放手的痛苦，却没有把所有选择摆成毫无差别的五个答案。

芒姆最后剪断脐带。心人接受搜索对象已经离开。克利福德认出山姆以后，不再把另一个BB当作自己的孩子。山姆抱住亚美莉，请她把终局延后。

作品给出的方向不是“抓紧”或“放下”四个字，而是一个更难的区分：你守住的，究竟是对方，还是你要求对方继续扮演的角色？

不肯把一个生命当作可以随手丢弃的东西，与不肯允许他离开、变化、成为自己，并不是同一回事。前者可能是照料，后者也可能成为另一种占有。

六、留下来的并不等于白费

那些卡住的岁月也没有因此变成纯粹的浪费。

芒姆与洛克妮重新连接；心人的研究帮助人们理解时间雨与开罗尔物质；克利福德一次次寻找的孩子活了下来，并走完了他未能走完的路。

人很少按原计划救回失去的一切。可他的执着仍可能留下一点什么，后来被另一个人捡到，改造成新的方向。

下一篇写山姆，也写他胸前那个早已不只是编号的BB。
