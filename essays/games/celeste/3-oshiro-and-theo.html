Essay III of IV · Celeste《蔚蓝》解读 · III / IV

Oshiro and Theo

奥希罗和希欧

Sep 4, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)Independent English edition中文原作 · 简 / 繁

Edition note: An independent English rewriting of the Chinese argument for English-language readers. Plot claims have been checked and interpretive claims are marked as such.

The Celestial Resort is in ruins. Its roof is broken, its corridors are clogged with debris, and its doors hang crooked in their frames.

Mr. Oshiro welcomes Madeline with a formal bow. Business has been quiet, he explains, but the hotel remains excellent. A guest has finally arrived.

Madeline wants to help.

Kindness can reinforce the room

She initially agrees to clean. The player crosses room after room removing laundry, books, and accumulated clutter. The labor is substantial, and Madeline’s generosity is sincere.

Oshiro does not read it as help facing the hotel’s decline. He reads it as proof that reopening is possible. The more Madeline assists, the more elaborate his expectations become.

This is one danger of compassionate participation. We dislike puncturing the account that keeps someone going, so we enter it and begin repairing its walls. Both people may feel better for a while. The helper works hard; the recipient is less alone. Yet the effort makes the original room harder to leave.

Understanding why somebody needs an account does not require endorsing everything that account claims.

A truth with nowhere to land

Madeline eventually tries to leave and tells Oshiro what is plainly visible: the hotel is a wreck. He hears rejection and humiliation, transforms into a furious monster, and chases her out.

The lesson is not that truth should never be spoken. It is that accuracy and receivability are different conditions.

For Oshiro, the hotel is not merely a mistaken proposition. It contains his remaining identity: host, caretaker, someone whose service is still wanted. To remove the fantasy at once is also to remove the floor on which he stands.

That does not justify his attack, and it does not oblige Madeline to stay. Help has boundaries; departure can be one of them. “I told the truth” simply cannot guarantee that truth will become a path for the listener.

The gondola stops

Later, Madeline rides a gondola with Theo. It stalls above the mountain. She has a panic attack; breathing becomes difficult and dark shapes close around her.

Theo does not diagnose her, order her to calm down, or promise that everything will certainly be fine. He offers a small exercise: imagine a feather and use each breath to keep it afloat.

The player controls the feather. Too much force sends it high; lost rhythm lets it fall. No explanation repairs the world. Attention receives one immediate action it can perform.

Theo offers not a conclusion but a practice.

Help that can be carried away

The contrast between Oshiro and the gondola is not simply blunt truth versus soothing language.

Madeline does labor for Oshiro while tacitly accepting his goal. Theo does not breathe for Madeline. He gives her a method she can enact herself.

When somebody is falling, a theory of her whole life may be impossible to hold. The next breath may still be available. Assistance need not require agreement with the helper’s entire interpretation, and it need not transfer control of the person along with the problem.

The best help often remains usable after the helper leaves. The feather becomes Madeline’s. Theo does not make himself an indispensable piece of equipment for every future crisis; he leaves her with one more capacity.

Companionship is not repair

After the panic recedes, a photograph catches something of their shared terror, and the two can laugh.

Theo does not turn the episode into a polished story of growth. He stays, acknowledges the awkward fear, and does not reduce Madeline to a damaged object awaiting his repair.

Oshiro supplies the other limit. Not every attempt to help succeeds. Greater tact cannot guarantee rescue. Madeline ultimately leaves the hotel not because Oshiro has no worth, but because she cannot manufacture in him the place from which he might face its ruin.

Companionship has weight both in refusing to disappear and in refusing to take another person’s life away from them.

Based on Chapters 1–8 of the 2018 game Celeste, directed and written by Maddy Thorson with music by Lena Raine. It does not cover the 2019 Farewell chapter. Full spoilers.

山上的旅馆已经破败。屋顶塌了一半，走廊堆满杂物，房门歪在框上。

店主奥希罗先生仍穿得整整齐齐，向玛德琳鞠躬，说这里一切都好，只是很久没有客人。

玛德琳想帮他。

一、陪着演并不总是温柔

她先答应打扫旅馆。玩家跑过一个个房间，把书、衣物和垃圾清走。劳动很实在，善意也是真的。

可奥希罗从中读出的不是“我需要面对旅馆已经荒废”，而是“重新开业果然可行”。玛德琳越帮忙，他越确信客人马上会回来。

这是好心的一种风险。我们不忍戳破一个人的说法，于是进入那套说法替他补墙。双方当下都可能舒服一些：帮助者付出了，被帮助者也不再孤单；但所有努力只让原来的房间更难离开。

理解一个人的痛苦，不等于必须赞同他对痛苦的全部解释。

二、真话也可能没有落脚处

玛德琳后来失去耐心，指出旅馆已经是一片废墟，自己必须离开。她说的大部分事实没有错，奥希罗却把拒绝听成羞辱，变成愤怒的怪物一路追赶她。

这段情节并不是劝人永远别说真话。它提醒我们，真话准确与对方当下能否接住，是两件不同的事。

奥希罗的旅馆不只是一个错误判断。它保存着他对自己是谁的全部想象：主人、服务者、一个仍然被需要的人。直接抽掉旅馆，也等于突然抽掉他的立足点。

这不使他的攻击正当，也不说明玛德琳有义务永远留下。帮助有边界，离开有时正是边界。只是“我说的是事实”不能保证事实会自动成为对方的路。

三、缆车停在半空

后来，玛德琳与希欧乘坐缆车。缆车停在高空，她的恐慌发作，呼吸变得困难，黑色触手般的影子从四周逼近。

希欧没有替她诊断，没有命令她冷静，也没有许诺一切肯定没事。他提出一个很小的练习：想象一片羽毛，用呼吸让它保持漂浮。

玩家亲自控制羽毛。吸气，呼气，太急就会把它推高，失去节奏又会让它下坠。世界没有被一句话修好，注意力却得到一件眼前可以做的事。

希欧给的不是结论，而是动作。

四、能带走的帮助

奥希罗那一段与缆车这一段，不是“说真话”与“说好听话”的简单对比。

玛德琳替奥希罗劳动，也替他默认了目标；希欧没有替玛德琳呼吸，只提供一个她可以自己完成的方法。

这一区别很重要。有人正在下坠时，未必接得住关于人生的总结，却可能接得住下一口气。帮助不一定先要求他同意你的完整解释，也不必把选择权一并接管。

更好的帮助，往往能在帮助者离开后继续使用。羽毛属于玛德琳，她以后可以自己想起。希欧没有把自己变成她每次恐慌时不可缺少的设备，而是让她手上多了一样东西。

五、陪伴不是修理

恐慌过去以后，两个人看到刚才留下的照片，笑了起来。

希欧没有把发作讲成一段漂亮的成长故事，也没有假装害怕并不存在。他留在旁边，承认刚才很狼狈，同时不把玛德琳缩成一个等待他修好的人。

奥希罗也提醒我们另一面：并非每一次帮助都能成功，并非说得更委婉就能救一个人。玛德琳最终离开旅馆，不是因为奥希罗不值得，而是因为她不能替他准备好面对现实的位置。

陪伴的分量，既在没有走，也在不把另一个人的人生接管过来。

本系列依据二〇一八年Maddy Makes Games开发的《蔚蓝》第一至第八章，导演与编剧Maddy Thorson，音乐Lena Raine；不含二〇一九年追加的第九章《再见》。创作者其后公开确认自己与玛德琳都是跨性别女性。全文含剧透。
