Two essays on a genuinely happy city, the child made to bear its cost, and what walking away refuses when innocence and an adequate alternative are both unavailable.

Ursula K. Le Guin's 1973 work The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas. The opening essay can serve as an introduction; the remaining 1 essays contain full-story spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Why Does a Happy City Need a Suffering Child?

Omelas makes one child’s misery the condition of collective flourishing, converting a preventable relation into a necessity citizens are trained to accept.

What Do Those Who Walk Away from Omelas Refuse?

The walkers reject participation and the city’s moral vocabulary, yet departure leaves the child behind and therefore cannot be mistaken for completed justice.
