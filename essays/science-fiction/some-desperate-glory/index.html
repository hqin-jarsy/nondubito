Five essays on Gaea’s apocalypse education, the gender of glory, Wisdom’s optimized worlds, an alternate life, and a girl raised for war learning not to need the end of everything.

Emily Tesh's 2023 work Some Desperate Glory. Every essay in this series discusses the complete story and contains full spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
How Does Gaea Turn a Lost War into a Child’s Entire World?

Gaea makes catastrophe the source of identity, converting real grief and scarcity into a closed story in which every child’s future already belongs to revenge.

Why Does Kyr’s Reproductive Assignment Reveal the Gender of “Glory”?

Gaea promises girls status through military excellence while reserving the authority to redirect their bodies toward reproduction, exposing equality as a method of mobilization rather than freedom.

III
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
If Wisdom Chooses the Best World, Why Does It Still Produce Intolerable Sacrifice?

Optimization can compare civilizations while treating particular lives as exchangeable, and extraordinary intelligence becomes dangerous when no affected person may challenge the scale on which “best” is computed.

IV
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
How Can Kyr Face the Fact That She Might Have Become an Oppressor?

The alternate life of Val shows that suffering does not guarantee moral clarity and that awakening becomes credible only when a beneficiary gives up the advantages that supported her comfort.

2026Three reading modes
V
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
After Destroying Gaea, Why Does Kyr Say She No Longer Needs the End of the World?

Kyr’s final freedom is not one last triumphant battle but the ability to enter ordinary time, where courage serves other people’s futures rather than an identity built on permanent catastrophe.
