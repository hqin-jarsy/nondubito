Five essays on a war against speech mistaken for noise, Mahit and Three Seagrass, collective personhood, a child emperor, and an empire that calls devastation peace.

Arkady Martine's 2021 work A Desolation Called Peace. The opening essay can serve as an introduction; the remaining 4 essays contain full-story spoilers.

When the Enemy’s Language Sounds Like Noise

The alien speech is treated as meaningless because it does not fit imperial expectations; the failure to hear becomes military permission.

III
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Can a Collective Life Be Recognized as “a Person”?

The aliens unsettle the empire’s assumption that a person must be one bounded body, forcing diplomacy to meet a subject whose unity has another form.

2026Three reading modes
IV
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Eight Antidote Is Still a Child. Why Must He Already Embody the Empire?

The young heir is trained to turn every fear and curiosity into imperial judgment, making his personhood disappear inside the continuity he represents.

V
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Calling Desolation Peace: Why Conversation Is Harder Than Firing

Military victory can remove the visible enemy and name the silence peace; communication preserves the harder fact that another claimant remains.
