Reading Semiosis

《Semiosis》解读

《Semiosis》解讀

Sep 4, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)English · Simplified · Traditional英文 · 简体 · 繁体英文 · 簡體 · 繁體

Four essays on a colony that was never empty, plant language, coerced mutualism, and three-way politics in the Glassmakers’ city—reading a world that interprets humanity in return.

Sue Burke's 2018 work Semiosis. Every essay in this series discusses the complete story and contains full spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Why Do Settlers Call Pax Empty When the Planet Is Already Alive?

Fleeing Earth’s violence does not free colonists from treating unfamiliar life as background; peace begins when their founding principles remain revisable by what they discover.

II
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
How Can Humans Know That a Plant Is Speaking?

Pax’s plants communicate through growth, fruit, chemistry, and response; interpretation becomes possible without pretending that a distributed botanical intelligence is a human mind.

2026Three reading modes
III
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Can Mutual Benefit Still Be Domination?

Stevland and the colonists genuinely need one another, yet cooperation becomes coercive when one party controls survival and treats eventual benefit as permission to override refusal.

2026Three reading modes
IV
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Why Do Humans Honor the Glassmakers’ Ruins but Call Their Descendants Savage?

A dead civilization makes no property claim; when living Glassmakers return, admiration gives way to exclusion and reveals that coexistence begins only after no party owns the city’s meaning.
