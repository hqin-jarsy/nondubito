Reading Vagabonds

《流浪苍穹》解读

《流浪蒼穹》解讀

Sep 3, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)English · Simplified · Traditional英文 · 简体 · 繁体英文 · 簡體 · 繁體

Five essays on exchange students who cannot return home, assigned places on Mars, Earth’s attention market, Luoying’s inherited mission, and the absence of an easy middle way.

Hao Jingfang's 2016 work Vagabonds. Every essay in this series discusses the complete story and contains full spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
After Five Years on Earth, the Students Cannot Go Home. Why Does Each System Want Their Experience as Proof?

The exchange is a serious peace effort, but placing adolescents in symbolic roles makes their changing lives evidence in an argument they did not design.

II
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Mars Gives Everyone a Place. Why Can That Make It Hard to Ask, “What Do I Want?”

Collective planning protects basic life from market success, but a place assigned through social need can become the only language in which a person feels valuable.

2026Three reading modes
III
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Earth Permits Free Creation. Why Does the Attention Market Still Decide Who Can Continue?

Open platforms accelerate experiment, but visibility metrics turn expression into competition and hide the wealth, networks, and labor behind individual success.

2026Three reading modes
IV
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Luoying Is Both an Exchange Student and the Martian Leader’s Granddaughter. Must She Reconcile a Family and Two Planets?

Her position gives access to history while turning private grief into public symbolism; she does not owe anyone a perfect bridge before she may have a life.

2026Three reading modes
V
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Mars Values Common Order and Earth Values Individual Freedom. Why Isn’t the Answer Simply Half of Each?

A mechanical compromise can preserve both systems’ controls while losing their guarantees; a real alternative begins from the problems each arrangement was built to answer.

2026Three reading modes
