Reading Harmony

《和谐》解读

《和諧》解讀

Sep 3, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)English · Simplified · Traditional英文 · 简体 · 繁体英文 · 簡體 · 繁體

Four essays on health transformed into public duty, bodies under WatchMe, death as refusal of benevolent control, and whether a conflictless harmony still has room for persons.

Project Itoh's 2008 work Harmony. The opening essay can serve as an introduction; the remaining 3 essays contain full-story spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
When Health Changes from a Right into a Duty

Harmony’s medical order defeats much preventable suffering, then turns the gift of care into a demand that each citizen maintain the body society has invested in.

II
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Cared for Until There Is No Escape: The Body under WatchMe

WatchMe makes bodily condition continuously legible to institutions, eliminating neglected illness while also eliminating the private interval in which refusal can form.

2026Three reading modes
III
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Why Do They Use Death to Refuse a Benevolent World?

The girls’ suicide pact seeks an exit from total care, but the need to destroy the self exposes how completely the system has removed ordinary forms of refusal.

2026Three reading modes
IV
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Does a “Harmony” Without Conflict Still Need People?

The final project promises to end destructive conflict by removing the inner plurality from which disagreement arises, saving human organisms by abolishing human authorship.
