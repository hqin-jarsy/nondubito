Reading Walkaway

《Walkaway》解读

《Walkaway》解讀

Sep 2, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)English · Simplified · Traditional英文 · 简体 · 繁体英文 · 簡體 · 繁體

Five essays on the infrastructure of exit, unwanted labor, possessive parental love, digital persons, and restraint under attack—asking how a new commons avoids becoming another home no one may leave.

Cory Doctorow's 2017 work Walkaway. Every essay in this series discusses the complete story and contains full spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Why Is Walking Away More Than an Individual Act?

Automation weakens the material need for wage obedience, but departure becomes freedom only because other people have built places, knowledge, and protection that can receive it.

II
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Who Does the Unwanted Work in a Community Without Bosses?

Shared production removes some wage coercion but not maintenance, care, conflict, or status; freedom requires rules that make necessary labor visible and refusable.

2026Three reading modes
III
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Why Does Natalie’s Father Call Her Kidnapping “Protection”?

Jacob interprets an adult daughter’s disagreement as manipulation because wealth, parental care, and institutional force let him turn his judgment of her interests into captivity.

2026Three reading modes
IV
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Is an Uploaded Mind a Resurrection, a Copy, or a New Member?

Digital Dis preserves memory and relationship while beginning a distinct future; backup does not erase local death or permit communities to manufacture expendable persons.

2026Three reading modes
V
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
Does a Commitment to Peace Still Matter After the Default World Attacks?

Walkaway settlements cannot protect themselves by declaring nonparticipation, yet defense remains different from conquest when its purpose is to preserve exit and limit new command.

2026Three reading modes
