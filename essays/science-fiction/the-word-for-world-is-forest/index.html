Sep 2, 2026Han Qin (秦汉)English · Simplified · Traditional英文 · 简体 · 繁体英文 · 簡體 · 繁體

Three essays on a forest renamed timber, Selver’s terrible knowledge of killing, and a victory that must end in withdrawal rather than reverse conquest.

Ursula K. Le Guin's 1972 work The Word for World Is Forest. Every essay in this series discusses the complete story and contains full spoilers.

I
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
What Is Lost When a Forest Is Renamed Timber?

Terran language converts a lived world into inventory and its people into labor, showing that naming becomes colonial when superior force decides which description may govern action.

II
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
What Changes When Selver Brings His People the Knowledge of Killing?

Armed resistance breaks a system with no court or appeal, but even necessary violence changes its users and must remain answerable to the purpose of ending domination.

2026Three reading modes
III
English · 简 / 繁 · Science fiction
After Victory, How Can Colonialism Actually End?

Selver seeks withdrawal rather than a better rank within occupation, protects surrendering enemies, and makes any future contact conditional on the hosts’ continuing power to refuse it.
