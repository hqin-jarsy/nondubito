为什么还需要制度

Why Institutions Become Necessary

Han Qin (秦汉)

如果人人都成熟，还要规则吗

关于制度，常有两种相反想象。一种认为人性不可靠，所以必须用规则压住；另一种认为只要人人足够成熟、理性并相互尊重，制度便会自然消失。SAE 从一个更严格的问题开始：即使参与者都是成熟主体，是否仍存在善意无法解决的冲突？

两个人共享一处不可分割的水源，各自的需要都真实；父母分开以后，对孩子都负有不能退出的责任；两个社群使用同一片有限空间，彼此都不能简单离开。双方可以充分理解对方，也可以诚实检查自己，余项仍然存在。

制度不是因为主体失败才出现，而是因为多个主体共享有限世界。承认彼此为目的，并不会让所有资源自动可分，也不会让所有不得不自然兼容。

从临时裁决到稳定框架

一次冲突可以请一位共同信任的人判断。但当相似冲突反复出现，临时安排会产生新的问题：谁有资格裁决？依据什么？同类案件是否一致？裁决者如何被质疑？

制度就是被稳定下来的仲裁。它把一次性的共同接受转化为公开、可预期、可重复并可问责的框架。人不必等到冲突最激烈时才重新谈判全部条件，也不必每次都把力量差异伪装成共识。

在这个意义上，制度表面上限制主体，深层却是主体间自限的共同工具。每个人都放弃在交集处单方面作最终裁判，换取其他人也不能单方面裁决自己。

制度的目的不是制度

制度一旦稳定，便容易把自身延续当作最高目的。程序为了维持程序而存在，组织要求人服务组织，法律把可管理性置于生活之上。SAE 因此坚持一条次序：存在先于建构。主体先于制度，制度是处理主体间余项的工具。

这不意味着任何个人愿望都高于规则。若共同规则一遇到不满便失效，它就无法保护其他主体。更准确的说法是，制度必须能把自己的每项增量追溯到交集中的需要：这一条规则处理什么余项？保护谁继续作为目的？为什么不能用更薄的办法完成？

没有理由的增量，不应因为“制度通常如此”便被继承。我们不能把现有国家、公司或婚姻中的惯例倒过来当成制度的本质。

权利与义务

共同仲裁之所以可行，还因为权利与义务成对出现。制度保护我的发声、边界和退出，我也承担不把别人当作工具、遵守共同裁决并为共同框架贡献必要成本的义务。

只有权利而没有义务，保护会成为无法兑现的支票；只有义务而没有权利，制度则成为剥削。参与越深、拥有的权力越大，承担的义务通常也应越厚。权力最大者若义务最少，共构很快会退化为统治。

制度的必要性并不来自对人的绝望。恰恰因为人是目的，冲突中的任何一方都不能被另一方的善意、力量或确定感吞掉。制度是承认的余项：当我们无法只靠彼此解决问题时，仍共同建造一个不让任何人独占最后一句话的位置。

Would Perfectly Mature People Need Rules?

Two opposing stories dominate accounts of institutions. One says that human beings are unreliable and must be constrained by rules. The other imagines that sufficiently mature, rational, respectful people would no longer need institutions. SAE begins with a harder question: even among developed subjects, can conflicts remain that goodwill cannot solve?

Two people share an indivisible water source and both needs are genuine. Separated parents remain responsible for a child from whom neither can simply exit. Two communities occupy finite common space. Each party may understand the other and examine its own claims honestly, yet a remainder persists.

Institution does not arise only because subjects fail. It arises because multiple subjects inhabit a finite world. Mutual recognition does not make every resource divisible or every cannot-not compatible.

From a Decision to a Framework

A single dispute can be submitted to a trusted third party. Repeated conflicts introduce further questions. Who may decide? According to what reasons? Will similar cases be treated alike? How can the decision maker be challenged?

An institution is stabilized arbitration. It transforms one episode of common acceptance into a framework that is public, predictable, reusable, and accountable. Parties need not renegotiate every condition at the moment of greatest conflict, nor mistake an imbalance of force for agreement.

In this sense, institutions appear as external constraints while functioning more deeply as instruments of inter-subjective self-limitation. Each person gives up unilateral final judgment within an intersection in exchange for the assurance that no other person may judge unilaterally either.

The Institution Is Not Its Own End

Once stabilized, a construct is tempted to make its own continuation supreme. Procedures exist to preserve procedures, organizations demand service to the organization, and manageability outranks life. SAE therefore preserves an order: existence precedes construct. Subjects are prior to institutions; institutions are tools for handling remainders among subjects.

This does not mean any private desire overrides a common rule. A framework that dissolves whenever someone dislikes its result cannot protect other subjects. The point is that every institutional increment must be traceable to a condition in the shared intersection. Which remainder does this rule handle? Whose continued status as an end does it protect? Why can a thinner arrangement not do the work?

An increment without such a reason should not be inherited merely because familiar institutions contain it. Features of states, corporations, or marriages cannot be projected backward as the essence of institution.

Rights and Obligations

Common arbitration also depends on the pairing of rights and obligations. An institution protects my voice, boundaries, and exit; I accept duties not to instrumentalize others, to comply with legitimate common judgment, and to contribute necessary costs to the shared framework.

Rights without obligations become promises the institution cannot fulfill. Obligations without rights become exploitation. The deeper one's participation and the greater one's power, the thicker one's obligations generally should be. A construct in which the powerful carry the fewest duties rapidly ceases to be co-construction and becomes rule.

Institutional necessity does not begin in despair about human nature. It begins in respect for persons as ends. No party to a conflict may be absorbed by another's goodwill, strength, or certainty. Institution is the remainder of recognition: when we cannot solve a shared problem through relationship alone, we build a position from which no one owns the final word.
