Essay 11 of 15

两个目的相遇以后怎么办

When Two Ends Meet

Han Qin (秦汉)

他者不是另一版本的我

人们常说，理解他人要“换位思考”：如果我是他，我会怎样感受？这个方法有用，却藏着一项风险——我仍然用自己的欲望、恐惧和价值替他生成答案。真正困难的情况恰恰是：如果我是他，我也不会成为他。他所不得不做的事，在我的生命里可能完全没有对应物。

SAE 所说的主体之间，不是两个观点经过充分沟通后必然统一，而是两个目的来源彼此相遇。承认他者，首先承认对方不能被我的尺度穷尽。理解可以增长，不可化约性不会因此消失。

这也是共情与接受的差别。共情尝试进入另一个人的参照系；接受则可能只是说“好吧，你就这样”，把无法理解的部分留在门外。反过来，高度共情若缺乏自我边界，也可能变成让渡：为了感受对方，我取消自己的不得不。

互凿

当两个都把自己和对方视为目的的人发生冲突，第一步不是谁压服谁，而是互凿。每个人把自己的“构”放到对方的参照系前，检查哪些部分是核心不得不，哪些只是习惯、恐惧、身份表演或未经检验的普遍化。

互凿可能发现，表面冲突来自错误表达，底层目的其实兼容；也可能发现双方确实不可通约。后一种结果不代表沟通失败。能清楚知道“我不能放弃这一点，同时我承认你也不能放弃那一点”，本身就是一种成熟结果。

互凿不是无限妥协。它要求每一方都允许自己被追问，却不要求为了证明开放而交出核心目的。没有自我保存的互凿只剩迎合，没有被他者改变可能性的坚持则只是独白。

离开

如果一方拒绝承认另一方为目的，或冲突无法形成共同生活，离开可能是最诚实的选择。Walk away 不是辩论失败，而是分叉权在关系中的实现：我的目的不需要通过征服你或获得你的批准才成立。

离开也保护对方。继续以教育、拯救或惩罚为名纠缠，常把“我尊重你”变成“直到你同意我”。两个主体不能共构，并不必然要求其中一个消失。

但离开不是总能做到。共同抚育的孩子、不可分割的资源、对第三方的责任、共同领土或已经形成的高退出成本，会使 walk away 本身造成新的伤害。

余项

当互凿不能消除冲突，离开又不可行，就留下主体间余项。此时诉诸外部仲裁不是主体性的失败，而是主体承认自己的边界：我们不能同时作自己的法官，又要求对方把判决视为共同有效。

所以两个目的相遇后的道路可以分成三层：能重新检查彼此时互凿；不必继续纠缠时离开；有不可退出的交集时，共同接受仲裁。第三条路正是制度出现的位置。

主体之间之所以可能，不是大家最终变得相同，而是每个人内部已有复数目的，因而知道“不可化约”并不等于“必须消灭”。最深的承认不是我终于完全理解你，而是即使理解仍有边界，我也不把边界解释成你不配存在。

The Other Is Not Another Version of Me

Empathy is often described as putting oneself in another person's place: how would I feel if I were there? The exercise is useful but carries a risk. My desires, fears, and values still generate the answer. The hardest cases begin when, even in the other's circumstances, I would not become the other. What she cannot-not do may have no analogue in my life.

For SAE, an inter-ontological encounter is not a meeting of opinions destined to converge after enough communication. It is a meeting of two sources of ends. Recognition begins by accepting that another subject cannot be exhausted by my scale. Understanding may grow without abolishing irreducibility.

This also separates empathy from acceptance. Empathy attempts to enter another reference frame; acceptance may merely say “that is how you are” while leaving the unintelligible outside. Yet empathy without self-differentiation can become surrender: in order to feel what the other feels, I cancel my own cannot-nots.

Mutual Chiseling

When two people who recognize themselves and each other as ends enter conflict, the first response is mutual chiseling rather than conquest. Each places a construct before the other's reference frame and asks which parts are genuine cannot-nots and which are habits, fears, identity performances, or unexamined attempts at universality.

Mutual chiseling may reveal that a surface conflict arose from poor expression and that the underlying ends are compatible. It may also establish that the two ends are genuinely incommensurable. The second result is not automatically a failure of dialogue. To know clearly that “I cannot abandon this, and I acknowledge that you cannot abandon that” is itself a mature achievement.

Mutual chiseling does not mean compromise without limit. Each party must permit examination without surrendering a core end merely to prove openness. Chiseling without self-preservation becomes appeasement; conviction without the possibility of alteration becomes monologue.

Walking Away

If one party refuses to recognize the other as an end, or if the conflict cannot sustain a common life, departure may be the honest response. Walking away is not necessarily a failed argument. It is Fork Rights expressed in relationship: my end does not require your approval or defeat in order to exist.

Departure can protect the other as well. Continuing to educate, save, or punish someone often turns “I respect you” into “I will respect you once you agree with me.” The failure of co-construction does not require one subject to disappear.

But exit is not always available. Shared children, indivisible resources, responsibilities to third parties, common territory, and high costs already created by a relationship can make walking away a new source of harm.

The Remainder

When mutual chiseling cannot dissolve the conflict and exit is infeasible, an inter-subjective remainder persists. Turning to external arbitration is not a defeat of subjecthood. It is an acknowledgment of its boundary: the parties cannot each remain judge in their own case while asking the other to treat the decision as common.

The encounter between two ends therefore has three paths. They chisel mutually where examination can change the conflict. They depart where continued entanglement is unnecessary. Where an intersection cannot be exited, they jointly accept arbitration. The third path is the point at which institution appears.

Inter-ontological life is possible not because everyone eventually becomes alike, but because internally plural persons already know that irreducibility does not imply extermination. The deepest recognition is not complete understanding. It is the refusal, when understanding reaches its limit, to treat that limit as evidence that the other should not exist.
