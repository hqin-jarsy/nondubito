Essay 01 of 6 · Yogācāra

阿赖耶识不是一座仓库

The Storehouse Is Not a Warehouse

Han Qin (秦汉)

把阿赖耶识叫作“藏识”，很容易让人想到一座仓库：经验被装进去，记忆被码好，日后再从架子上取出来。这个比喻方便，也危险。仓库是静的，物品与容器彼此分开；唯识所说的种子却会熏习、现行、再熏习，储藏者本身也在这场循环中变化。

所以，若要让唯识与 SAE 相遇，第一步不是宣布“阿赖耶识等于 15DD”。那会把两个体系都压扁。阿赖耶识在八识体系里是第八识，不是躲在八识背后的灵魂，更不是一个隐秘的主体。真正值得对接的，是阿赖耶识—种子系统从染污到转依的整段轨迹。

从末那识开始

唯识把前五识交给感官活动，第六意识负责判断、分别与推理，第七末那识则恒常地执取阿赖耶识的见分为“我”。放到 SAE 的坐标里，第六意识最接近 12DD 的认知与预测层；末那识则把 13DD（自我主体）的自我指涉与 14DD（自目的主体）的目的扩张压在了同一个位置上。

这是一处对应，也是一处差别。唯识把末那识所执的我判为遍计所执：它没有自性。SAE 则把自我理解为真实的涌现结构——不是什么不灭的实体，而是系统复杂到一定程度后必然出现的自我折回。它像一个拓扑结，不独立于绳子，却不能因此说它什么也不是。

于是双方在同一个位置上给出不同判断。唯识问：既然我只是执取的结果，如何转去我执？SAE 问：既然自我是已经涌现的结构，如何让它不再把自己的目的覆盖到所有他者身上？前者以解构我执为入口，后者以承认他者也是目的为入口。

对接的是转依，不是识体

15DD（自我立法主体）不是一件可以从外部装入主体的东西。它的形成更像一种长期的拓扑形变：一次次与他者相遇，一次次发现自己的目的不是唯一目的，旧有反应逐渐失去支配力，新关系逐渐成为稳定的运作方式。

唯识的“转依”也不是把仓库清空再换一批货。资粮、加行、通达、修习、究竟五位，描述的是识的依止方式如何逐渐改变。以接口读法看，它们可以与 15DD 内部的四步及其入口相邻：资粮位还在门外积累条件；加行位先松动我执、标出他者不再只是对象；通达位改变方向；修习位让这种方向在反复作用中获得记忆；究竟位完成一次闭合。

“对应”到这里必须停住。唯识的究竟位指向成佛、大圆镜智与一个可说的清净终态；SAE 的闭合一定留下余项，没有一层可以宣布余项归零。唯识的路径从实践上逐步向上，却由一个已经设定的终态牵引；SAE 从否定出发逐层生成，不保存一幅等着被恢复的圆满底图。

种子为什么比“记忆”更难

唯识谈种子，有一套远比“潜在记忆”精细的规定。种子必须刹那灭而又相续，必须与果同时发生作用，必须等待众缘和合，也必须引生与自己同类的果。前几项与 SAE 的信息拓扑很接近：结构不是一条可单独取出的记录，而是跨时间维持的一种关系形状；条件改变，形状显现的方式也改变。

真正的缝出现在“引自果”。唯识的种子按类别维持因果连续：善种生善果，特定潜能引生同类结果，因此修行可以逐类对治、逐类转变。SAE 的拓扑模型更整体：一个局部动作会改变整张关系网，很难把某一类种子单独摘出而不牵动其他部分。

这不是谁更先进。它们是两种不同的跨时间模型：一种把潜能理解为可分解的因果单位，一种把持续性理解为整体形状。唯识因此拥有 SAE 尚缺的认知分辨率；SAE 则迫使唯识回答，一个被称作“无分别”的最终认知，是否真的能完全免于新的遮蔽。

第九识之争留下了什么

佛教内部关于是否另立第九识的争论，也可以从这里看。若大圆镜智只是第八识转成的清净形态，就不必在上面再设一识；若仍需给绝对清净、本来不染保留一个位置，第九识便像一个名字，承接第八识无法完全负担的东西。

SAE 不替这场争论判案。它只会说：争论本身暴露了一个余项。15DD 的单向承认还不是 16DD 的双向不疑；当他者也以主体身份回应时，关系中出现了任何一方都不能独占的部分。有人把这个部分向上命名为本净识，有人把它留在转识成智的内部，SAE 则把它标成通往 16DD 的桥。

最有价值的不是三种名字能否统一，而是它们为何必须分开。唯识提醒 SAE：主体并不是靠一两次道德选择改变的，它被长期储存、熏习与再现的结构塑造。SAE 反过来提醒唯识：再精密的心识地图，只要把终点预先保存下来，就必须解释那幅圆满图景凭什么没有余项。

阿赖耶识不是一座仓库。它更像一条会被自己携带的历史持续改写的河。真正的接口，不在河叫什么，而在它如何转向；真正的分歧，也不在两张术语表哪里没有对齐，而在它们是否相信这条河最后可以抵达一片无余的海。

Calling ālayavijñāna the “storehouse consciousness” invites an unfortunate picture. We imagine a warehouse in which experiences are boxed, labeled, and preserved until the mind retrieves them. Warehouses are passive. Their shelves do not change because of what they hold, and the goods do not reproduce themselves by being stored.

Yogācāra's seed theory describes almost the opposite. Seeds condition manifest activity; manifest activity plants or modifies seeds; and the stream that carries them is continually altered by that circulation. Storage is therefore not a container added to a mind. It is part of the mind's history of becoming.

That difference matters when Yogācāra is placed beside Self-as-an-End. A crude comparison would equate the eighth consciousness with 15DD and move on. The result would be a bilingual glossary, not an interface. Ālayavijñāna is the eighth member of the eight-consciousness system, but it is not a secret soul hiding behind the other seven. Nor is it the subject that owns experience. The more revealing comparison concerns the transformation of the entire ālayavijñāna–seed system during āśraya-parāvṛtti, the transformation of the basis.

The Problem Appears in Manas

Yogācāra assigns sensory cognition to the first five consciousnesses and conceptual discrimination, inference, and judgment to the sixth. The seventh, manas, continuously appropriates an aspect of the eighth as “I.” In SAE's terms, the sixth consciousness is closest to the predictive and conceptual work of 12DD. Manas occupies a more complicated boundary: it combines the self-reference of 13DD—the self—with the purposive expansion of 14DD—the self as an end.

The alignment immediately exposes a disagreement. Yogācāra treats the self appropriated by manas as imagined, empty of intrinsic nature. SAE treats the self as a real emergent structure. “Real” here does not mean an immortal substance. It means that once a system becomes capable of recursive self-reference and arbitration among competing predictions, a new form of organization has appeared. A knot is not independent of the rope, but it is not nothing.

The two frameworks therefore ask different questions at the same site. Yogācāra asks how appropriation can cease once the self has been seen as a construction. SAE asks how an emergent self, whose purposiveness is real, can cease treating its own purpose as exhaustive of the field. The former loosens self-grasping. The latter must make room for another source of ends.

A Trajectory, Not an Entity

The SAE comparison becomes useful when it shifts from an entity to a path. 15DD, the self-legislating subject, is not a moral module that can be installed. It is a long deformation of relational topology. Encounters with other subjects repeatedly frustrate the assumption that one's own purpose is the only source of purpose. Responses change, acquire history, and eventually become stable enough to alter how the subject meets the next encounter.

Yogācāra's five paths—accumulation, preparation, seeing, cultivation, and culmination—can be read alongside this process. Accumulation gathers conditions before the decisive transformation. Preparation begins to loosen the grasper–grasped structure. Seeing changes orientation. Cultivation gives the new orientation duration through repetition. Culmination completes the path as Yogācāra understands it.

This is a functional alignment, not an identity. Yogācāra's path is directed toward Buddhahood and the great mirror wisdom. SAE allows no closure without remainder. Every completion becomes a construct that exposes what it has not included and therefore opens another bridge. Yogācāra describes an upward practice under the guidance of an already intelligible culmination. SAE begins with negation and builds upward without retaining a perfect image to be recovered.

That difference prevents the comparison from becoming annexation. SAE cannot redescribe Buddhahood as the top cell in its own table. Yogācāra cannot treat an SAE remainder as merely one more impurity awaiting removal. One framework expects purification to culminate; the other treats non-closure as constitutive.

Why Seeds Are More Than Memories

Yogācāra's six characteristics of seeds make the contrast sharper. A seed is momentary yet continuous, participates in simultaneous causal relations, waits for converging conditions, and produces a result of its own kind. Much of this resonates with SAE's topological account of information. What persists through time need not be an inert record. It may be a pattern whose present configuration constrains what can happen next.

The deepest seam appears at “producing its own kind.” Yogācāra preserves typed causal continuity: particular seeds yield corresponding results, and cultivation can address defilements through a differentiated transformation of their potentials. SAE's topology is less decomposable. A local intervention changes the shape of a network and may redistribute constraints far beyond the apparent target. It is difficult to isolate one seed-type without altering the field in which all the others operate.

Neither model simply defeats the other. Yogācāra offers a resolution of cognitive dynamics that SAE does not yet possess. SAE, in return, asks whether a cognition called non-discriminating can receive a definitional exemption from the structure of cognition. If the great mirror wisdom is still an occurrence of knowing, SAE will ask what it excludes. Yogācāra will answer that this question misclassifies wisdom by treating it as ordinary construction. The disagreement is genuine because the two theories assign different ontological status to cognitive completion.

What the Ninth Consciousness Dispute Reveals

The long dispute over a ninth consciousness can also be read as evidence of remainder. If the purified eighth consciousness fully becomes wisdom, no ninth consciousness is required. If absolute purity must be given a position not exhausted by transformation within the eighth, a ninth name becomes attractive.

SAE cannot adjudicate that intra-Buddhist dispute. It can only identify the pressure that produces it. Unilateral recognition at 15DD is not yet the mutual non-doubt of 16DD. Once another responds as a subject, the relation contains something neither party can possess from its own side. One lineage names an originally pure layer; another keeps purification within the transformation of the eighth; SAE marks a bridge toward a relation that cannot be occupied unilaterally.

The names should not be collapsed. Their separation is the result. Yogācāra teaches SAE that subjects are shaped by sedimented histories far more intricate than a sequence of explicit choices. SAE teaches Yogācāra that even the most exact map of consciousness must explain why its promised completion is not another map with an outside.

The storehouse, then, is not a warehouse. It is closer to a river continuously redirected by what it carries. The interface lies not in renaming the river but in asking how its course changes. The final disagreement is whether transformation can reach a sea without remainder—or whether every sea, once reached, discovers another shore.
