Essay 06 of 6 · Pure Land

他力从哪里进入

Where Can Other-Power Enter?

Han Qin (秦汉)

前五篇虽然彼此差得很远，却共享一个不太显眼的前提：修行发生在主体这一边。唯识要转依，禅要破住，中观要松开自性执，天台要因机施教，华严要入法界。外部条件可以帮助，但最后那一步仍由主体自身的结构发生变化。

净土把这条前提翻了过来。

至少在善导一系的强读法里，凡夫不能靠自力出离生死。真正完成横超的，不是行者积累到足够高的修行能力，而是阿弥陀佛本愿的他力。信、愿、称名是行者的行；使行者越过生死、往生报土的力，却不在行者这一边。

这让净土与 SAE 在一个前五篇都没有正面建立的轴上相遇：力的来源。

竖出与横超

龙树《十住毘婆沙论》以难行道、易行道区分求不退转的两条路；昙鸾把它固定成自力与他力的分判，后来又成为圣道门与净土门的区别。难行道像陆路步行，靠自力逐步前进；易行道像乘船，由佛的愿力载度。

若放在 SAE 坐标里，圣道门与 DD 阶梯共享一种形式：自下而上，逐层而升，每一构由前一构经否定而自凿出来。这里的相似只是操作方向，不是说佛教各修行位等于某个 DD。

净土门不是少走几级台阶。它是横超：不是在外力帮助下更快地爬同一架梯子，而是力的来源已经在梯子之外，逐层计算因而失效。若把他力解释成“SAE 涵育得特别强”，就已经把净土改写成自力理论了。

涵育为什么不是他力

涵育是他力在 SAE 里最像的近邻。一个老师、一段关系、一套制度，都可以改变主体能够看见什么、承受什么，为主体搭出以前没有的桥。

但涵育只提供条件，不替对方完成构。桥可以由别人搭，过桥仍由主体自己过；对方能够自凿，才说明涵育成功。涵育的终点是外部调节者变得不再必要。

净土的他力不是这样。它不只改善条件，而把“越过”本身归于本愿。行者称名，但往生不被理解为称名这个动作靠自身功效积累出来的结果。净土不是给了一座更好的桥，而是以愿力载人横过。

因此两者的差别不在有没有外部帮助，而在帮助落在哪个位置：涵育帮助条件，成办仍属主体；他力帮助成办，越过属于本愿。

行不是过

净土并不因此等于被动。三心、五念门、称名都要求行者有所作为。问题是，行动与完成不在同一个归属里。

“南无阿弥陀佛”可以被两种框架读成两件不同的事。SAE 会把称名登记为一个主体侧的操作：它改变注意、重组习惯、形成一个带桥的构。净土则可以把同一次称名读成受纳：不是靠自己的构造积功，而是乘名号与本愿之力。

这两个读法不能拼成“我先自凿九成，佛替我补最后一成”。那会把净土降成一套少了一步的 SAE。称名在两边都是完整的：在 SAE 中是完整的自凿操作，在净土中是完整的受纳之行。分歧不在现象，而在现象的力源。

十念往生把问题推到最尖。临终凡夫只得十声称名，自积之功被压到极薄，他力因而最清楚地显出；可从 SAE 看，哪怕一念仍然是一件发生在主体侧的事，仍可登记为构。净土越强调“不是自力成办”，SAE 越撞上自己的表征极限。

SAE 为什么无法表示纯受纳

SAE 有一条很强的发生学承诺：凡是能够被登记为主体状态的东西，都是某种构；凡构都由既有结构经否定而自凿形成。外部可以改变条件，却不能在主体内部完成一个完全没有主体起点的构。

所以“纯粹受纳、由外力完成越过”在 SAE 中没有位置。若受纳能够被说出，它已经是主体的一种状态；既然是状态，就已是构；既然是构，就不是零自力。

这不是 SAE 还没发明好一个术语，而是它的公理负担。增加“他力”维度并不能修补，因为那会改变整个凿构循环的发生学。同样，净土也不会接受把本愿降成主体内一套强大的心理机制；那会把救度重新收回自力。

两边必须在这里分手。

不能自救，是否反而成为入口

善导讲两种深信：深信自己是罪恶生死凡夫、无有出离之缘；同时深信弥陀本愿摄受众生。这里的“不能”不是训练尚不足，而是他力结构的入口。自力不能出离，恰恰使受纳本愿成为唯一真实的方向。

SAE 也常从“不能”开始：构不能无余闭合，所以余项成为下一轮的桥。但这两个不能只在形式上相邻。净土说的是凡夫不能靠自力完成救度；SAE 说的是任何构都不能穷尽其所蔽。前者把不能接向外部愿力，后者把不能接向下一次自凿。把罪恶凡夫翻译成“一个余项很多的主体”，既不尊重净土，也误解余项。

双方还能互相发问。净土问 SAE：若一切解脱都必须自凿，SAE 是否从公理上排除了全然受纳这种完整可能？SAE 问净土：若连信心都由本愿回向，一个没有主体侧起点的受纳，如何被登记为一次真实发生？

两问都成立，也都只能在自己的框架里成立。

两种完整，不是一种缺两步

净土不是一套自力不够强的 SAE；SAE 也不是一套还没学会信靠本愿的净土。净土的完整在于他力横超：行者有行，越过由愿力成办，终点是可指方、可往生的报土。SAE 的完整在于无外力竖出：每一构由主体自凿，16DD 也不是可被运送进去的处所，而是不可单方占据的关系极限。

前一篇华严说无外：一切都在法界整全之中。SAE 说有外而无力：余项永远在外，却不会替主体完成什么。净土说有外且有力：本愿不仅在主体之外，而且能够成办横超。这三种位置不能混在一起。

他力从哪里进入？在净土里，它从本愿进入，且正因为来自外部才是救度。在 SAE 里，它无处进入；凡能进入主体状态者已经成为构，凡构必须自凿。

这道缝不需要被弥合。它让两套哲学各自说出自己最不能放弃的东西：一边说，有一种完成不是我完成的；另一边说，凡发生在我这里的完成，都不能没有我的构。它们相向而立，也提醒我们，所谓“人的主体性”，也许从一开始就有不止一种完整的理解。

The first five encounters in this series differ radically, yet they share a quiet assumption: transformation occurs on the subject's side. Yogācāra transforms the basis, Chan breaks abiding, Madhyamaka loosens grasping at intrinsic nature, Tiantai adapts teaching to capacity, and Huayan enters the dharma-realm. External conditions may help, but the subject's structure undergoes the decisive change.

Pure Land reverses that assumption.

On a strong Shandao-lineage reading, the ordinary being cannot escape birth and death through self-power. The force that accomplishes crosswise transcendence is not an accumulated capacity of the practitioner but the other-power of Amitābha's Primal Vow. Faith, aspiration, and recitation are acts of the practitioner. The power of the crossing does not belong to those acts.

Pure Land therefore meets SAE on an axis that the previous five essays never had to isolate: the source of accomplishment.

Vertical Ascent and Crosswise Transcendence

Nāgārjuna's <em>Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā</em> distinguishes a difficult and an easy path to non-retrogression. Tanluan stabilizes this difference through self-power and other-power; later formulations distinguish the Gate of the Sages from the Pure Land gate. The difficult path is compared to walking overland. The easy path resembles being carried by boat.

Placed in an SAE coordinate, the Gate of the Sages shares a formal direction with the DD ladder: bottom-up, layered, and self-chiseled, each construct emerging through negation of a prior construct. This is an analogy of operational type, not a claim that Buddhist stages correspond rung by rung to DD levels.

Pure Land is not a shorter route up the same ladder. Crosswise transcendence is not assisted climbing. The source of the force lies outside the ladder, so the accounting of successive rungs no longer governs the passage. To explain other-power as unusually powerful SAE cultivation would already convert Pure Land into a self-power theory.

Why Cultivation Is Not Other-Power

Cultivation is the strongest SAE near-neighbor to other-power. A teacher, relationship, institution, or ritual can alter what a subject can perceive and bear. It can provide a bridge that did not previously exist.

But cultivation changes conditions; it does not construct on another's behalf. Someone else may build the bridge, but the subject still crosses. Successful cultivation increases the other's capacity for self-chiseling and ends by making the external regulator unnecessary.

Other-power occupies a different position. It does not merely improve the conditions of passage. It assigns the passage itself to the Vow. The practitioner recites, but rebirth is not the product of merit accumulated by recitation as a self-powered technique. Pure Land does not offer a better bridge. Vow-power carries the practitioner across.

The difference is therefore not whether help comes from outside. It is where help acts. Cultivation helps at the level of conditions while accomplishment remains with the subject. Other-power acts at the level of accomplishment itself.

The Act Is Not the Crossing

Pure Land is not simple passivity. The three minds, the five gates of mindfulness, aspiration, and recitation are activities. Yet activity and accomplishment belong to different domains.

Reciting “Namo Amitābha Buddha” can be read differently by the two frameworks. SAE registers recitation as a subject-side operation: attention changes, habit is reorganized, and a new construct forms. Pure Land can read the same recitation as reception—not merit-producing self-construction, but participation in the power of the Name and the Vow.

The readings cannot be combined as “I self-chisel ninety percent and the Buddha supplies the final ten.” That would turn Pure Land into an incomplete SAE. Recitation is complete in both frameworks: a complete self-chiseling operation for SAE and a complete act of reception for Pure Land. The divergence lies not in the observable event but in the source of its efficacy.

The promise of rebirth through ten recitations sharpens the problem. With the dying ordinary being's own accumulated capacity reduced almost to nothing, other-power becomes maximally visible. Yet from SAE's perspective, even one recitation is an event on the subject's side and can therefore be registered as a construct. The more Pure Land insists that the crossing is not self-accomplished, the more clearly SAE encounters its limit of representation.

Why SAE Cannot Represent Pure Reception

SAE contains a strong genetic commitment. Anything registered as a state of a subject is a construct. Every construct emerges from a previous structure through negation and self-chiseling. External relations can alter conditions, but they cannot complete inside the subject a construct with no subject-side genesis.

Pure reception by which an external power accomplishes the crossing therefore has no SAE position. If reception can be described as a state of the subject, it is already a construct. If it is a construct, it is not zero self-power.

This is not a missing term that future SAE vocabulary can easily supply. It is an axiomatic burden. Adding an “other-power layer” would alter the genesis of the entire chisel-construct cycle. Conversely, Pure Land cannot reduce the Primal Vow to an unusually effective psychological process within the practitioner. That would reclaim salvation for self-power.

The frameworks must part here.

When Inability Becomes the Entrance

Shandao's twofold deep faith holds together conviction that one is an ordinary being unable to escape birth and death and conviction that Amitābha's Vow receives beings. “Cannot” is not merely insufficient training. It is the entrance to the structure of other-power. The inability of self-power is precisely what makes reception of the Vow the real direction.

SAE also begins many transitions from a cannot: a construct cannot close without remainder, so remainder becomes the bridge to another chisel. The resemblance is formal and limited. Pure Land concerns the ordinary being's inability to accomplish liberation. SAE concerns the inability of any construct to exhaust what it occludes. The former turns inability toward external Vow-power; the latter turns it toward further self-chiseling. Translating the sinful ordinary being as “a subject with a great deal of remainder” would distort both traditions.

Each can now ask the other a serious question. Pure Land asks whether SAE, by requiring all transformation to be self-chiseled, excludes in advance the complete possibility of unconditional reception. SAE asks how a reception with no subject-side point of genesis can be registered as an event at all—especially when even faith is said to be transferred through the Vow.

Both questions are valid. Each is valid within its own framework.

Two Completions, Not One Theory Missing Two Steps

Pure Land is not SAE with insufficient self-power. SAE is not Pure Land before it learns to trust the Vow. Pure Land is complete in other-power and crosswise transcendence: the practitioner acts, the Vow accomplishes passage, and the terminus is a recompense land toward which one can be born. SAE is complete in ascent without external force: every construct self-chisels, and even 16DD is not a place into which anyone can be carried but a relational limit no side occupies alone.

The preceding Huayan essay described a whole without outside. SAE preserves an outside without force: remainder remains external but accomplishes nothing for the subject. Pure Land affirms an outside with force: the Vow is external to the practitioner and can accomplish the crossing. These positions cannot be collapsed.

Where can other-power enter? In Pure Land it enters from the Primal Vow, and its external source is precisely what makes it salvific. In SAE it has no point of entry. Whatever enters a subject's state has become construct, and every construct must self-chisel.

The seam need not be repaired. It allows each philosophy to state what it cannot surrender. One says that there is an accomplishment I do not accomplish. The other says that whatever is accomplished here cannot occur without my construction. Standing face to face, they suggest that “human subjectivity” may admit more than one complete interpretation from the beginning.
