太阳与不能被看的东西

The Sun and What Cannot Be Seen

Han Qin (秦汉)

那座城造到后来，问题变成谁来管它，管事的人得先学会什么。苏格拉底说，有一样学问比别的都大，护卫者里最拔尖的那些人非学不可，不学，别的学问再多也白搭。

这样一说，同座的人当然要追问：那到底是什么。

他说，是善的理念。

而且他还说过一句很扎人的话。在正义和体面这些事情上，人常常肯要一个看起来的。看起来正义就行，看起来体面就行，做不到真的，有那个名声也认了。唯独在好这一件上，没有人肯要一个看起来的。谁都想要真的好，假的好一钱不值。

所以善这个词在他那里不是随便哪一个词。它是唯一一个没有人肯拿赝品凑合的词。

这个答案等于没答，格劳孔立刻就顶上来。他问，你说的善究竟是什么，是知识吗，是快乐吗，还是别的什么东西。苏格拉底不肯说。他先说，只有意见而没有知识，是难看的，我不愿意那样；后来又说，善本身今天讲不了，这个题目高过我今天飞得起来的高度，硬讲怕出洋相。

格劳孔不放。苏格拉底于是想了个折中。他说，善本身我讲不了，我讲它的儿子给你听，那个儿子长得最像它。希腊文里儿子那个词也当利息讲，所以格劳孔顺着这个玩笑接了一句：本钱那一笔，你改天再还。

这是全书里最好看的一处讨价还价。一个人被问到他一辈子最重的那个词，他的回答是，本钱今天拿不出来，先付你一点利息。

利息就是太阳这个比方。

他说，眼睛和耳朵不一样。耳朵听声音，有声音有耳朵就够了，中间不缺什么。眼睛不行。眼睛再好，东西再有颜色，中间还得有第三样东西才行，那就是光。眼睛和看得见的东西，是被光连起来的，而光是太阳给的。太阳自己不是眼睛，也不是看这个动作，可是没有它，看这件事根本不发生。

善在可知的那一边，就是太阳在可见的这一边。

这个比方他推到了底。太阳做的是两件事，一件是让东西可以被看见，另一件是让眼睛有看的能力。善也做两件事，一件是让那些东西可以被认识，另一件是让认识的人有认识的能力。看的那一头和被看的那一头，都是它给的。

到这里，两边碰上了。

SAE 那边也有一个不能填的格子，位置一模一样，在最底下。那一格里的东西叫非，说白了就是否定这个动作本身。它有一条别的东西都没有的性质：你没法否定它。你要是说根本没有这回事，你说这句话的时候就已经在做这件事了。划一条线，把这一边和那一边分开，是否定；设一个前提，把别的排除在外，是否定；对一句话起疑，还是否定。所以一切认识的动作都要从它这里过。

可它自己一点内容也不给你。

说话要用气。你说的每一个字都得靠一口气送出去，一句也少不了。可这口气不决定你说什么。它是你说话的条件，不是你话里的内容。要是有人因为句句都得用气，就说气是一切话的源头，那就是把两件事弄混了。

这里得说准一点。苏格拉底说的是今天讲不了，不是原则上讲不得。他留的是一句改天再还，不是一张永远的欠条。

可是全书读完，那一笔他一次也没有还。

两边都在最底下留了一个位置，而且都空着。SAE 那一格是说不得，说不得就是它的性质；柏拉图那一格是今天没说，然后一直没说。理由不是一回事，空着的样子是一回事。这一处相遇不轻。

留空是难受的。一套说法讲到最后，总会有人问那最下面究竟是什么，答不上来是要丢分的。放一样东西进去，回答立刻就有了，而且从那以后每一层都有了着落。空着那一格的人等于永远欠着一个答案，还是当着所有人的面欠着。所以多数人走到那里都要放点什么进去，这不是他们不够老实，是那个位置本身在催人。

苏格拉底本来也是不肯放的。可格劳孔一直逼，他被逼过了那条线。

他说了这样一句：可知的那些东西，不但从善那里得到被认识的可能，连它们的存在和本质也是从它那里来的；而善自己不是本质，它在本质之外，在地位和能力上都超过本质。

格劳孔当场叫了出来，说天哪，这话说得没边了。

苏格拉底回他一句：这可怪你，是你逼我把心里想的说出来的。

可是仔细看他被逼出来的那一句，仍旧不是善是什么。他说的是它在哪儿，它管着什么，别的东西从它那里得到些什么。位置和职能他给了，那样东西本身，他一个字也没有描。

洞穴那一段里分量最重的词是被迫，被迫的是那个囚徒。到了这里，被迫的是苏格拉底本人，逼他的是坐在旁边的一个朋友。这一段两千四百年后还活着，多半就因为它把一个人被问到墙角的样子原样留了下来。

但那一句一出口，格子就被填上了。

填进去的是什么，值得看仔细。他说善是那些东西之所以能被认识的原因，也是它们之所以存在的原因。也就是说，那一格里坐着的是一个来源，别的东西都从它那里领东西。他还说过，每个人的灵魂都在追善，做一切事都是为着它，只是谁也说不清它究竟是什么。所以那个来源同时还是最想要的那个东西。

SAE 那一格里坐着的不是来源，是一个动作。你做任何事都要用它，它自己什么也不发给你。

家里那把菜刀就是这个样子。你做哪一道菜都要用它，可它不告诉你今天做什么。所有的菜都要经它的手，没有一道菜是从它里面长出来的。你要是问那这些菜到底从哪儿来，答案是从菜本身来，从今天冰箱里有什么来，从你想让谁吃得高兴来，就是不从刀来。刀只是那个不用它就什么都办不成的东西。

来源和动作，差别在哪里。差别在于来源可以被朝拜，动作只能被使用。

把一样天天都在用的东西当成万物的来处，是很容易犯的一个错，而且犯了以后特别舒服。因为它确实无处不在，你走到哪里都能指着它说，看，它又在这儿。无处不在很容易被读成万物之本，其实它只说明了一件事，就是你绕不过它。绕不过和生得出，是两回事。

还有更要紧的一层。他给的还不只是善这个字，是善的理念。理念在他那里是可以被认识的东西里最结实的一种。也就是说，那个说不出是什么的东西，被安顿成了一样可认识之物里的头一名。这件事本身就已经给它派了内容。善是一个内容词，而且是分量最重的那一个。一样东西一旦得了这个名字，别的一切立刻就知道该往哪边去了。这就是为什么那一格填上之后，整座建筑忽然变得齐整：有了朝向，有了先后，有了值得不值得。

而且既然没有人肯要一个看起来的好，那么把最底下那一格叫作善，拉力就大得没法比。换个别的名字都不行。叫它一，叫它存在，叫它必然，都太干，人不会为它起身。叫它善，每一个人当场就认得，而且当场就想要。

家长跟孩子说，你要做个好人。这句话听着什么都没说，其实什么都说了，因为好这个字后面拖着一整套具体的期待，说的人和听的人心里都清楚拖的是哪一套。

一个被造出来的东西，坐到了地基的位置上，而且坐得极稳，稳到后来两千年里很少有人觉得它是被造出来的。这是这一类事情里最漂亮的一个标本，漂亮到连动机都是干净的。他不是要蒙谁。他是真的走到了那里，被人逼着，说了一句连他自己都觉得过了头的话。

SAE 这边也有一个免检的位置。整套说法里只有一样东西不受审查，就是那个不能被否定的动作。别的全站在受审的位置上，随时可能被判负。而那唯一免检的一样，什么也不给你。

免检在这里不是一种荣誉。它是检验的结果，检验只放过了一条，就那一条。

他会盯着这个免检的位置看。

他那一格里坐的是个发东西的。他说得很清楚，那一个不但让别的东西被认识，也让它们成其为它们，它是原因。他还说过，每个灵魂都在追它，人做一切事都为着它，虽然谁也讲不清它是什么。

于是他会问：凡是被用的东西，总是被用来做成点什么。那些做成的东西，它们的根据从哪里来。我这边说得出，从那一个来。你那边呢。你那一个只出力气，不出根据。你留了一个免检的位置，往里面放了一个什么也不给的东西。那么这个位置留着做什么。一个不发东西的地基，和根本没有地基，分别究竟在哪里。

SAE 能说的只有一句：它不可否认。

这句话说的是它的地位，没说它的出产。地位和出产是两件事，而他问的正是后面那一件。

这个问法很难躲。他不是在说那一格里放错了东西，他是在问那一格凭什么还算一格。一个位置之所以叫地基，通常是因为上面的东西压在它身上，抽掉它，上面就塌。而这一个抽不掉，却也不承重。上面那些东西各自站着，各自随时可能倒，倒了也不牵连它。

回到他还没有被逼过线的时候。

苏格拉底躲了很久。他说过我怕讲砸了，说过只有意见没有知识是难看的，说过这个题目高过我今天飞得起来的高度。他一路往后退，退到退不动了才说那一句，说完还要抱怨一句是你逼我的。

后来两千多年里，站在那个位置上说话的人多得很，而且大多说得很顺，没有谁在开口之前先声明我怕讲砸了。

一个人在自己最重的那个词跟前打的那个磕巴，可能是他关于那个词讲得最准的一句话。

As the city takes shape, the question becomes who will govern it and what its governors must first learn. Socrates says there is a subject greater than all the others. The best of the guardians must learn it. Without it, all their other learning is of no use.

Naturally, his companions ask: what subject is that?

The Form of the Good, he says.

He has also said something rather piercing. In matters of justice and respectability, people will often settle for appearances. Looking just is enough; looking respectable is enough. If they cannot have the real thing, they will accept the reputation. Good is the one thing for which nobody will accept an appearance. Everyone wants what is really good. A counterfeit good is worth nothing.

So good is not just any word for him. It is the one word for which nobody will settle for a counterfeit.

The answer amounts to no answer, and Glaucon presses him immediately. What exactly is this Good: knowledge, pleasure, or something else? Socrates will not say. First he says that having only opinion without knowledge is an ugly thing, and he does not want that. Later he says that he cannot discuss the Good itself today. The subject lies higher than he can fly today; if he tries, he fears making a fool of himself.

Glaucon will not let go. Socrates finds a compromise. He cannot speak of the Good itself, he says, but he can tell them about its son, the son who most resembles it. In Greek, the word for offspring also means interest on a debt. Glaucon follows the joke: you can repay the principal another day.

It is one of the finest bargains in the book. Asked about the weightiest word of his life, a man answers: I cannot produce the principal today; let me pay you a little interest first.

The interest is the comparison with the sun.

Eyes, he says, are unlike ears. For hearing, a sound and an ear suffice. Nothing is missing between them. Eyes are different. However good the eyes, however colorful the objects, a third thing must come between: light. Light connects the eyes and visible things, and the sun supplies the light. The sun is neither the eye nor the act of seeing, yet without it seeing does not happen at all.

The Good on the intelligible side is what the sun is on the visible side.

He pushes the comparison all the way. The sun does two things: it makes objects visible and gives the eyes the power to see. The Good likewise does two things: it makes objects knowable and gives the knower the power to know. Both ends, the seeing and the seen, receive their capacity from it.

Here the two sides meet.

SAE too has a slot that cannot be filled, in exactly the same position, at the very bottom. What occupies it is called non, or, plainly, the act of negation itself. It has a property nothing else possesses: you cannot negate it. If you say there is no such thing, you are already doing it as you speak. Draw a line between this side and that: negation. Set a premise that excludes others: negation. Doubt a sentence: still negation. Every act of knowing must therefore pass through it.

Yet it gives you no content at all.

Speaking requires breath. Every word you say must be carried out on a breath; no sentence can do without it. But that breath does not decide what you say. It is a condition of speaking, not the content of your speech. If someone claimed that breath was the source of every utterance because every utterance needed it, they would be confusing two different things.

We should be precise here. Socrates says he cannot explain it today, not that it cannot in principle be explained. He leaves a promise to repay another day, not an eternal IOU.

But read to the end of the book, and he never repays it once.

Both sides leave a place at the bottom, and both leave it empty. SAE’s slot cannot be given content; that inability belongs to its nature. Plato’s slot is not explained today, and then remains unexplained. The reasons differ; the appearance of an empty place is shared. This is no trivial meeting point.

Leaving a place empty is uncomfortable. Whenever an account reaches the bottom, somebody asks what is down there, and failing to answer costs you points. Put something in the slot and an answer is immediately available. From then on every level has somewhere to rest. Whoever leaves it empty permanently owes an answer, in front of everybody. So most people, when they get there, put something in. It is not that they are insufficiently honest. The position itself exerts pressure.

Socrates too was initially unwilling to put anything there. But Glaucon keeps pressing, and he is pressed across the line.

He says this: knowable things receive from the Good not only the possibility of being known, but also their existence and essence. The Good itself is not essence. It lies beyond essence and exceeds it in rank and power.

Glaucon cries out: heavens, that goes beyond all bounds!

Socrates answers: you are to blame; you forced me to say what I thought.

Yet look closely at what he has been forced to say. It still does not tell us what the Good is. It tells us where it stands, what it governs, and what other things receive from it. He supplies position and function. Of the thing itself, he offers not a word of description.

In the cave passage, the weightiest word is forced, and the prisoner is the one being forced. Here it is Socrates himself, pressed by a friend sitting beside him. If this passage is still alive twenty-four centuries later, it is probably because it has preserved exactly how a person looks when questioned into a corner.

But once that sentence is spoken, the slot has been filled.

What has been put there deserves a close look. He says the Good is the reason those things can be known and the reason they exist. In other words, the occupant of the slot is a source, from which other things collect what they receive. He has also said that every soul pursues the Good and does everything for its sake, though nobody can clearly say what it is. So this source is also what is most desired.

The occupant of SAE’s slot is not a source but an act. You must use it in everything you do. It hands out nothing itself.

The kitchen knife at home works like this. You need it for every dish, but it does not tell you what to cook today. Every dish passes through its hands; no dish grows out of it. If you ask where the dishes do come from, the answer is: from the ingredients themselves, from what is in the refrigerator today, from whom you want to please with the meal. Just not from the knife. The knife is simply that without which nothing can get done.

Where does the difference between a source and an act lie? A source can be worshipped. An act can only be used.

Mistaking something used every day for the origin of everything is an easy error, and a particularly comforting one. It really is everywhere. Wherever you go, you can point and say: look, there it is again. Ubiquity is easily read as the root of all things. In fact, it shows only that you cannot get around it. Being unavoidable and being able to generate things are different matters.

There is a more important layer still. What he supplies is not merely the word good, but the Form of the Good. For him, Forms are the most solid kind of knowable thing. So this thing whose nature cannot be stated is installed at the very head of knowable things. That already assigns it content. Good is a content-bearing word, and the weightiest one. Once something receives this name, everything else immediately knows which way to go. That is why the whole building suddenly falls into order once the slot is filled. There is direction, there is precedence, there is worth and unworthiness.

And since nobody will accept a merely apparent good, naming the bottom slot Good gives it an incomparable pull. No other name will do. Call it One, Being, or Necessity: all are too dry. People will not rise for them. Call it Good, and everyone recognizes it immediately and immediately wants it.

A parent tells a child: be a good person. It sounds as though nothing has been said. In fact, everything has been said, because the word good trails a whole set of concrete expectations, and both speaker and listener know which set it is.

Something made takes the place of the foundation and sits there so securely that, for the next two thousand years, few people notice that it was made. It is the finest specimen of its kind, so fine that even the motive is clean. He is not trying to deceive anybody. He really has reached that place, been pressed by others, and said something that even he feels goes too far.

SAE too has a position exempt from examination. In the entire account, only one thing is not subject to scrutiny: the act that cannot be negated. Everything else stands under examination and may at any moment receive a negative verdict. And the one exempt thing gives you nothing.

Exemption here is no honor. It is the result of the test. The test lets only one thing through, and only that one.

He would look hard at this exempt position.

His slot contains something that distributes things. He is clear about that: it not only lets other things be known but makes them what they are. It is a cause. He has also said that every soul pursues it and that people do everything for its sake, although nobody can clearly say what it is.

So he would ask: whatever is used is used to accomplish something. Where do the grounds of those accomplishments come from? On my side, I can answer: from that one thing. What about yours? Your one thing supplies effort but no grounds. You reserve an exempt position and put into it something that gives nothing. What is the position being kept for? What, exactly, distinguishes a foundation that supplies nothing from no foundation at all?

SAE can say only this: it is undeniable.

That states its status, not its output. Status and output are different things, and he is asking about the latter.

The question is hard to evade. He is not saying that the wrong thing has been put into the slot. He is asking what entitles it to count as a slot at all. A position is usually called a foundation because the things above bear down on it: remove it and they collapse. This one cannot be removed, yet it bears no load. The things above stand separately, each liable to fall at any time, and their fall does not implicate it.

Return to the moment before he was pressed across the line.

Socrates evaded the question for a long time. He said he feared making a mess of it. He said that having opinion without knowledge was ugly. He said the subject lay higher than he could fly today. He kept retreating until there was no more room, and only then spoke that sentence. After speaking it, he still complained: you forced me.

Over the next two thousand years, a great many people spoke from that position, and most spoke smoothly. None began by announcing that they feared making a mess of it.

The stammer a person makes before the weightiest word in their life may be the most exact thing they say about that word.
