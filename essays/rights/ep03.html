第三篇：权利为何不预设义务

Essay 3: Why Rights Presuppose No Duty

Han Qin (秦汉)

西方权利理论里有一条几乎从不被质疑的公理：每一项权利都预设一个与它对应的义务。我有言论自由的权利，就意味着别人（或者国家）背上了不去阻止我说话的义务；我有受教育的权利，就意味着有人背上了提供教育的义务。权利和义务，像一枚硬币的两面，密不可分。这一篇要直接挑战这条公理。

一、只有一项义务，而且朝内不朝外

上一篇提到，权利体系的最上层——所有主体都彼此承认为目的的那个透明状态——每个主体的自身之法已经把别人考虑在内了。这不是因为外面有一条规则要求它这么做，而是因为一套真正彻底、真正自洽的自我立法，只要推到底，就没法在不自相矛盾的情况下随意践踏别人——这不是被要求出来的，是被推导出来的。

这个系统里，其实只有一项义务，而且这项义务是朝内的，不是朝外的：一个主体不能不为自己立法。除此之外，没有任何"你欠别人什么"式的、朝向他人的义务作为基本构件存在。这一篇之前讨论过的三类权利位置——基础权利、权益（下面会解释）、承认性权利——没有一类是靠"对面站着一个欠着义务的人"来撑起来的。

基础权利是关系发生之前就已经站住的东西，谈不上对应谁的义务。承认性权利对应的是权力，不是义务——它要的是权力的支撑，不是某个具体的人主动欠你什么。还有一种更边缘的位置，叫权益：某个不一定是主体的对象（一片海岸线、一只宠物、一个还在襁褓里的孩子）被某个主体给予、照顾、保护，但这个对象自己并不因此持有任何东西——这种关系完全建立在给予者那一端，后面会有专门一篇讲它。这三种位置，没有一种的对面站着一个"背着义务"的人。

二、意志论的睡眠反例

一种理论认为，权利建立在一个人主动的意志之上——你之所以有某项权利，是因为你能够行使它、放弃它、执行它，权利就是你意志所能触及的那片空间。

这个说法有一个非常直接的反例：睡着的人。一个人熟睡的时候，没有在行使任何意志——他没有在同意什么，没有在拒绝什么，没有在主张什么。但显然不能因为他睡着了，就可以随便拿走他的财产、伤害他的身体。他的权利在他睡着的时候，一点都没有减少。

这说明一件事：一个主体"有资格持有"一项权利，和这个主体"眼下正在行使"这项权利，是两个完全不同的问题。前者是一种稳定的站位，不随清醒还是昏睡而波动；后者是一种随时都在变化的带宽——醒着的时候带宽全开，睡着的时候带宽归零，但站位一直都在。婴儿和儿童，正是这种情况的另一个版本：他们的站位是完整的，只是展开这个站位的带宽还没有全部打开——不是缩水版的权利，是还在展开中的权利。

三、利益论借错了register

另一种理论认为，权利之所以存在，是为了保护某种特别重要的利益。这个说法上一篇已经指出过一次范畴错误——利益衡量的是"多少"，权利建立的是一种结构性的位置——这里要补充一层：利益这套语言，本来就属于另一个register，一个交换、权衡、投入换回报的register，跟一个主体自身立法的那个register根本不是同一个。把权利建立在利益之上，等于是把一套市场式的语言，硬套进了一个它原本不属于的地方。

这也顺带解开了利益论一直很难处理的一个难题：动物、树木明明也有"利益"（一棵树"受益于"充足的水分），为什么它们没有权利？答案不是利益不够重要，而是"利益"从一开始就不是权利成立所需要的那个register——非主体的利益完全可以在道德考量里占据重要位置（上一篇已经讨论过这一点），但这从来不会因此变成一项"权利"，因为"利益值得被在意"和"持有一项权利"，本来就是两个不同register里的东西，不是同一个刻度尺上的两个点。

四、消极权利与积极权利，换一条线来切

传统上，权利常常被切成"消极"和"积极"两类：消极权利只要求别人别插手（比如言论自由），积极权利要求别人主动做点什么（比如某种福利）。这条切法，切的角度是"这项权利给别人施加了哪种形式的义务"。既然这一篇已经取消了朝外的义务这个概念，这条切法自然就站不住了。

换一条线来切：不看这项权利要求别人做什么，看这项权利这一部分内容，到底是从主体自身固有的站位里长出来的（基础的），还是完全依赖制度机器和社会协议才能存在的（承认性的）。一项看起来"积极"的权利——比如某种福利——拆开来看，往往是混合的：里面可能确实包着一个基础权利的内核（跟生存、身体完整相关的部分是真实存在的），外面裹着一层完全依赖制度机器的承认性外壳（具体的发放方式、资金来源，这些纯粹是制度安排）。所谓"政府欠你一份义务"，更准确的说法是：某套制度性的权力机器，接下了让某个位置真正运作起来的工作，不是某个具体官员在道德上欠了你什么。

五、历时展开：三代人权只是一个具体案例

权利这整件事，从来不是一次性定好、永远不变的清单。历史上，被承认的权利范围一直在扩张——这一篇借用一个常见的说法来举例：公民与政治权利，接着是经济与社会权利，再接着是集体与团结性权利，通常被称为"三代人权"。

但这个具体的历史顺序，只是一个更普遍的结构性动力在人类历史上的一次具体呈现，它本身不是理论——这个更普遍的动力究竟是什么，留给几篇之后专门讨论。这里只想先标记一件事：不要把某一次具体的历史展开，误当成理论本身。

还有一个边界案例值得一提：一个彻底的无国籍者，被排除在所有能让他持有承认性权利的制度之外，他依然需要某种位置，让他有可能重新进入这整套体系——这个位置本身不是某一项具体的权利，而是"有资格拥有权利"这件事的最底层保证。这正是这一篇开头提到的那种朝内的义务、以及基础权利本体论上先于制度这件事，在最极端情况下的体现：即使一个人被所有制度抛弃，基础权利的核心依然完整，被抛空的只是承认性权利那一层。

结语

取消朝外的义务之后，权利理论反而变得更干净：只剩一项朝内的义务——自身立法；三种位置各自站在自己的根据上，不需要对面站着一个欠债的人；消极积极的区分换了一条更准确的线来切；历史上的扩张只是一个更深层动力的具体案例。这样清理干净之后，下一篇可以放心地进到两条脊柱各自内部的细节，而不用担心里面藏着一个没有被检查过的义务概念。

Western rights theory carries an axiom almost never questioned: every right presupposes a correlative duty. If I have a right to free speech, someone — or the state — thereby carries a duty not to stop me from speaking. If I have a right to education, someone carries a duty to provide it. Rights and duties, on this view, are two faces of one coin, inseparable. This essay challenges that axiom directly.

1. Only One Duty, and It Points Inward

The previous essay mentioned that at the very top of the rights spectrum — the transparent state in which every subject recognizes every other as an end — each subject's own law has already taken the others into account. Not because some external rule required it, but because a truly complete, truly self-consistent act of self-legislation, followed all the way through, cannot trample on others without contradicting itself. This isn't demanded from outside; it falls out of the reasoning itself.

In this system, there is, in fact, only one duty, and it points inward, not outward: a subject cannot not legislate for itself. Beyond that, there is no outward-facing "you owe someone something" duty built in as a basic component anywhere. None of the three positions discussed in this series so far — basic rights, entitlement (explained below), and recognition-based rights — is held up by someone standing opposite it, carrying a debt.

Basic rights are already standing before any relation exists at all; there is no one's duty for them to correspond to. Recognition-based rights correspond to power, not duty — what they need is power's backing, not some specific person actively owing you something. There is also a more peripheral position, entitlement: some object that need not even be a subject — a stretch of coastline, a beloved pet, an infant still in its cradle — is given to, cared for, and protected by a subject, without that object itself holding anything as a result. This relation lives entirely on the giving end; a later essay is devoted to it in full. None of these three positions has someone standing opposite it "carrying a duty."

2. Will Theory's Sleep Problem

One theory grounds rights in a person's active will — you have a right because you are capable of exercising it, waiving it, enforcing it; a right is the space your will can reach.

There is a very direct counterexample: a sleeping person. While someone is fast asleep, they are exercising no will at all — they are not consenting to anything, not refusing anything, not asserting anything. But no one thinks that being asleep licenses taking their property or harming their body. Their rights have not diminished one bit while they slept.

This shows something: a subject's being eligible to hold a right, and that same subject's currently exercising it, are two entirely different questions. The first is a stable standing that does not fluctuate with wakefulness or sleep. The second is a bandwidth that is constantly changing — fully open while awake, at zero while asleep, while the standing underneath never moves. Infants and children are another version of exactly this: their standing is complete; only the bandwidth for exercising that standing has not yet fully opened. Not a diminished right — a right still unfolding.

3. Interest Theory Borrows the Wrong Register

Another theory holds that rights exist to protect some especially important interest. The previous essay already flagged one category error here — interest measures "how much," a right establishes a structural position — and there is a further layer worth adding: the language of interest belongs to an entirely different register to begin with, a register of exchange, trade-off, input for return, which simply is not the same register as a subject's own self-legislation. Grounding rights in interest forces a market-style vocabulary into a place it was never built for.

This also resolves a puzzle interest theory has always struggled with: animals and trees plainly have "interests" too — a tree "benefits" from adequate water — so why don't they hold rights? The answer isn't that their interests don't matter enough. It's that "interest" was never the right register for this question in the first place. A non-subject's interests can occupy a fully legitimate place in moral consideration, as the previous essay already argued — but that never, on its own, amounts to a "right," because "an interest worth caring about" and "holding a right" are simply different registers, not two points on the same scale.

4. Negative and Positive Rights, Cut Along a Different Line

Rights are traditionally split into "negative" (requiring only that others not interfere, like free speech) and "positive" (requiring others to actively do something, like some form of welfare). That cut runs along the question of what kind of duty a right imposes on others. Having just removed outward-facing duty from the picture entirely, that line no longer holds.

Cut along a different line instead: not what a right asks others to do, but whether this component of the right grows out of a subject's own inherent standing (basic) or depends entirely on institutional machinery and social agreement to exist at all (recognition-based). A seemingly "positive" right — some form of welfare, say — usually turns out, on inspection, to be mixed: it may genuinely contain a basic-rights kernel (something real about survival and bodily integrity), wrapped in a recognition-based shell that depends entirely on institutional machinery (the specific disbursement scheme, the funding mechanism — purely institutional arrangements). What looks like "the government owes you a duty" is better read as: an institutional power-machine has taken on the job of making a certain position actually operative, not that some specific official morally owes you something.

5. Diachronic Expansion: The Three Generations as One Instance

Rights, as a whole, were never fixed once and settled forever. Historically, the recognized range of rights has kept expanding — one commonly cited example: civil and political rights first, then economic and social rights, then collective and solidarity rights, often called the "three generations" of rights.

But this specific historical sequence is only one concrete instance of a more general structural dynamic playing out in human history; it is not, itself, the theory. What that more general dynamic actually is gets its own full treatment several essays from now. For the moment, it's worth just marking the point: don't mistake one specific historical unfolding for the theory itself.

One boundary case is worth mentioning here: a person rendered entirely stateless, excluded from every institution that would let them hold recognition-based rights, still needs some position that makes it possible for them to re-enter that whole system at all — a position that isn't itself any specific right, but the deepest-level guarantee that having rights at all remains possible. This is exactly the inward-facing duty mentioned at the start of this essay, and the ontological priority of basic rights over institutions, showing up in their most extreme form: even when every institution has abandoned a person, the core of their basic rights remains whole. What has been hollowed out is only the recognition-based layer.

Conclusion

Once outward-facing duty is removed, rights theory actually gets cleaner: only one duty remains, and it points inward — self-legislation; each of the three positions stands on its own ground, with no one needing to stand opposite it carrying a debt; the negative/positive distinction is cut along a more accurate line; and historical expansion turns out to be one concrete instance of a deeper dynamic. With this cleared away, the next essay can safely go inside the two spines' own inner workings, without worrying that an unexamined concept of duty is hiding somewhere inside them.
