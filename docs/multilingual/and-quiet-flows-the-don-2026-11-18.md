## Source-review decisions

- Civil-war and Soviet-power vocabulary must not imply the later agricultural collectivization campaign.
- Petro's death after surrender, Aksinia's fatal shooting during flight, and deaths through illness or abortion cannot be reduced to a contradictory tally of battlefield deaths.
- Polyushka dies of diphtheria, not scarlet fever. Dunyashka remains alive at the end. The father-and-son closing image is not a census of the only survivors.
- The final return includes discarding the rifle, Nagant revolver and cartridges, crossing the March ice, and a son who recognizes his father but is frightened. The novel does not narrate Grigori's subsequent execution or pardon.
- The novel expressly describes Grigori's hardening and loss of his early pity (Part IV, Chapter IV). Claims that he never becomes numb, always looks at every victim's face, or never crosses a moral boundary must not survive as purported plot facts. Specific episodes of pain remain specific episodes, not a new universal psychological law.
- Dunyashka maintains the sibling relationship and supports Grigori marrying Aksinia; she reports their mother also supported it late in life (Part VIII, Chapters VIII–IX). Political hostility makes home insecure but does not literally extinguish kinship. Longstanding absence of institutional protection is not the same as never receiving anyone's approval.
- Preserve the author's attention to persons, violence, damaged relationships and the final embrace without inventing a flawless protagonist, unbroken motive or definitive ending beyond the text.

Original-text witnesses and exact local revisions are recorded with the source audit and correction ledger. Internal AI editorial review is not external native-speaker or scholarly certification, nor a claim to collate a physical printed edition.
