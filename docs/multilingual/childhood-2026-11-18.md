## Source and interpretation boundaries

- The infant brother is born before the river journey and dies during it. Tsyganok is nineteen in the original, not seventeen or eighteen. Unverified precise childhood ages should not become invented certainty.
- Tsyganok gives a reason for shielding the child: pity and the hope that the rod will break. He shows his injured arm. Care can have a reason and be known without being an exchange; the essays' non-exchange argument is retained without the false premise of reasonless secrecy.
- The grandfather has heard the mother's account of a father who did not beat the child. The account of his own success can still be read as a mistaken causal inference; it cannot be defended by claiming he had never encountered a counterexample.
- The grandfather is alive at the book's close and still has money/lending activities. His damaged relations are not converted into an invented death in absolute poverty. Yakov, not the grandfather, fatally beats Yakov's wife.
- The grandmother's God is experienced as kinder, but not wholly without punishment. She does criticize her husband. The child's distinction draws on what he hears and sees, not on literally no input.
- The hairpins penetrate her scalp during blows and kicks, not because the grandfather attacks her with a hairpin. The fire scene involves sulfuric acid, a burning horse blanket and her expressed pain; courage does not require invented insensibility.
- The cross accident is reported to the child rather than witnessed by him. Family members do accuse the uncles; this is not identical to omniscient proof of intentional murder. Neither the accusations nor the evidentiary limit should disappear.
- The adult narrator's backward-looking description of an excellent person is not a child's prophecy. The two autobiographical volumes connect narratively, not through an identical final/first sentence.

The author's core remains the coexistence of violence, instruction, care and stories without reducing a person to one total verdict; the difference between explaining a motive and pricing an act; and what survives outside a household's calculation of usefulness. Verified facts and directly dependent sentences are repaired without replacing this with a new thesis.
