## Material Chinese corrections

- The workhouse does physically strike Oliver. Its ration includes onions twice weekly, not extra bread. Gamfield agrees a price; a magistrate, not failed bargaining, blocks the apprenticeship. Rumors about former apprentices are not converted into proved recent killings.
- Fagin deceives as well as feeds and welcomes the child; he physically confines as well as destroys routes back. Brownlow's uncertainty and the bookseller's eyewitness testimony are distinct.
- Nancy participates in wrongdoing but is also pressured. She delivers Oliver to Sikes, not accompanies the burglary. Charley Bates eventually reforms. Nancy's exact six-year/young-adult age is not invented from the novel's relative-age statements.
- Rose's first offer of care precedes the full disclosure, and the hotel conversation and later bridge meeting are separate. The essay's non-exchange reading is retained without falsely making all assistance conditional on information.
- Nancy refuses money but asks for a keepsake. She speaks about love and entanglement, and her final plea is for survival and separate distant lives, not a joint future. She can seek her own life and still urge Sikes to turn back.

The revisions do not replace the author's analysis with a new psychological thesis. Explanations of Nancy's action and Sikes' haunting remain literary readings, not independently proved mental laws. All touched short paragraphs, including repeated recaps of the same false premise, are logged; their count is not a count of distinct factual errors.
